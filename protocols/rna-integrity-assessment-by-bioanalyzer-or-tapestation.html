RNA Integrity Assessment by Bioanalyzer or TapeStation

1–1.5 h · 2026-11-23

Assess RNA concentration and integrity on the Agilent Bioanalyzer (RNA 6000 Nano) or TapeStation (RNA and High Sensitivity RNA ScreenTape): sample and chip preparation, run settings, and interpretation of RIN, RINe and DV200 before RNA-seq.

⬇ Word (.docx)⬇ PowerPoint (.pptx)

Purpose

To measure the concentration and integrity of total RNA before RNA-seq, microarray or RT-qPCR using the Agilent 2100 Bioanalyzer (RNA 6000 Nano) or Agilent TapeStation (RNA or High Sensitivity RNA ScreenTape), and to report RIN, RINe and DV200.

Principle

Microfluidic or ScreenTape gel electrophoresis separates RNA by size and detects it with a fluorescent dye. In intact eukaryotic total RNA the two ribosomal RNAs (18S and 28S) form sharp peaks, and as RNA degrades the 28S peak shrinks and fragments build up in the fast (small-size) region [4]. Software converts the whole electropherogram into the RNA integrity number (RIN, Bioanalyzer) or its equivalent (RINe, TapeStation) on a scale of 1 to 10, and also reports the percentage of fragments above 200 nt (DV200) [4][5]. The two scores are not interchangeable, so use one platform and threshold throughout a study [4][5].

Materials and reagents

ItemDetails
RNA sampleTotal RNA in RNase-free water (best) or a buffer within kit limits; Nano 25–500 ng/µL quantitative, High Sensitivity ScreenTape 500–10,000 pg/µL; thaw on ice [1][2][3].
RNA 6000 Nano kit (Bioanalyzer)Gel matrix, dye concentrate, marker, ladder and chips (cat. 5067–1511); equilibrate reagents to room temperature for 30 min [1].
RNA ScreenTape kit (TapeStation)ScreenTape devices, RNA sample buffer and ladder (cat. 5067–5576/5577/5578); for dilute samples use the High Sensitivity RNA ScreenTape (5067–5579/5580/5581) [2][3].
Electrode decontaminationRNaseZAP and RNase-free water (Bioanalyzer) [1].
DisposablesRNase-free, non-filter tips and tubes; 0.5 mL tubes; TapeStation tube strips or 96-well plates with caps or foil seals.
Gloves and RNase-free waterWear gloves; use RNase-free water for dilution and cleaning [1].

Equipment

  • 2100 Bioanalyzer, priming station and IKA vortexer
  • TapeStation 4150 or 4200 with mixer
  • Heat block or thermal cycler (70–72 °C)
  • Microcentrifuge (13,000 × g)
  • Ice bucket

Procedure

  1. All work: wear gloves, use RNase-free tips and tubes, and set reagents out for 30 min to reach room temperature, with the dye in the dark. Thaw samples on ice [1][2]. (30 min)
  2. Bioanalyzer: decontaminate the electrodes with 350 µL RNaseZAP (1 min) then 350 µL RNase-free water (10 s) on a cleaning chip, as the kit guide specifies [1]. (5 min)
  3. Bioanalyzer, first use of the kit: heat the ladder 2 min at 70 °C, cool on ice and store as RNase-free aliquots at −28 to −15 °C [1]. (5 min)
  4. Bioanalyzer, first use: spin 550 µL gel through the spin filter at 1,500 × g for 10 min and store 65 µL aliquots at 2–8 °C [1]. (15 min)
  5. Bioanalyzer: vortex the dye 10 s, add 1 µL to 65 µL filtered gel, vortex, and spin 10 min at 13,000 × g at room temperature. Use within 1 day [1]. (15 min)
  6. Bioanalyzer: put a chip on the priming station, add 9 µL gel-dye mix to the marked well, press the plunger (from 1 mL) for exactly 30 s, release, wait 5 s, then draw back to 1 mL [1]. (3 min)
  7. Bioanalyzer: add 9 µL gel-dye mix to each of the two other marked wells. Add 5 µL marker to the ladder well and all 12 sample wells (fill unused wells with marker and 1 µL buffer) [1]. (3 min)
  8. Bioanalyzer: heat the ladder and samples for 2 min at 70 °C, then keep on ice. Add 1 µL ladder and 1 µL of each sample to the ladder and sample wells [1]. (5 min)
  9. Bioanalyzer: vortex the chip horizontally for 60 s at 2,400 rpm, load it in the instrument and start the Eukaryote or Prokaryote Total RNA Nano assay within 5 min (run time 30 min) [1]. (35 min)
  10. Bioanalyzer: remove the chip right after the run and clean the electrodes with 350 µL fresh RNase-free water (10 s) [1]. (3 min)
  11. TapeStation RNA ScreenTape: mix 5 µL sample buffer with 1 µL RNA sample (25–500 ng/µL); ladder tube: 5 µL buffer + 1 µL ladder in position A1 [2]. (10 min)
  12. TapeStation High Sensitivity RNA: mix 1 µL HS buffer with 2 µL sample; dilute the ladder with 10 µL RNase-free water and use 2 µL with 1 µL buffer in A1 [3]. (10 min)
  13. TapeStation: cap, vortex 1 min at 2,000 rpm, spin down 1 min, heat at 72 °C for 3 min, put on ice 2 min and spin down 1 min [2][3]. (10 min)
  14. TapeStation: load the ScreenTape and the tubes (ladder in A1, caps off, liquid at the bottom), then click Start. Run time is under 20 min for 16 samples [2]. (20 min)
  15. Review the gel and electropherogram, then record concentration, RIN or RINe, and DV200 (region 200–10,000 nt, % of total). Note any DNA or fast-region peaks [4][5]. (10 min)
  16. Compare with the project threshold (see Expected results). Do not compare RIN with RINe or RQN directly [4][5]. (5 min)

Pause points

  • Prepared and filtered gel can be stored at 2–8 °C for about 4 weeks; gel-dye mix is good for 1 day [1].
  • Denatured ladder aliquots can be stored at −28 to −15 °C [1]. Do not pause after sample denaturation: run immediately (Bioanalyzer within 5 min) [1][2].

Expected results

  • Intact eukaryotic total RNA: sharp 18S and 28S peaks, flat baseline, RIN or RINe of about 8–10; one study cited in the Agilent white paper recommends RQN ≥ 7 for RNA-seq, so set a project-specific threshold [4][6].
  • Degraded RNA: smaller or absent 28S peak, a rising signal in the fast region, and lower RIN, RINe and DV200 [4].
  • DV200 above about 70% is commonly used as a cut-off for fragmented RNA such as FFPE [5].
  • Ladder electropherogram on the Bioanalyzer shows one marker and six RNA peaks [1].

Controls

  • Run the supplied ladder with every chip or tape [1][2].
  • Include a known intact RNA (for example, a universal reference RNA) and a heat-degraded aliquot as positive and negative integrity references [3][4].
  • Run a water blank in an unused well or lane to check for contamination.
  • Repeat any sample with a flagged or NA score after fresh dilution [5].

Troubleshooting

ProblemLikely causeSolution
No peaks or failed runEmpty wells, air in the chip, or missed sampleFill all wells (use marker in unused wells); re-prime with a new chip [1]
Peak above 28SGenomic DNA contaminationTreat with DNase and re-purify; check on the gel [3][4]
Distorted traces or low RIN despite fresh RNASalts, phenol, guanidine or detergent in the sampleRe-purify RNA, or elute in RNase-free water, and stay within the buffer limits [1][2]
Chloroplast peaks in plant RNAPlant tissue contains chloroplast rRNAUse the plant assay and interpret RIN with care [4]
Sample spills when vortexingDetergent-containing bufferReduce Bioanalyzer vortexing to 2,000 rpm [1]
RIN and RINe disagreeThey are different scoring algorithmsUse one platform per study [5]

Safety and waste

  • The Bioanalyzer dye concentrate contains DMSO and a DNA-binding dye; wear gloves and dispose of chips, tips and dye as chemical waste per the safety data sheet [1].
  • RNaseZAP is a chemical irritant; use it in a ventilated area.
  • Remove chips immediately after the run; do not leave them in the instrument for more than 1 h [1].
  • Human or other primary samples can carry pathogens; follow local biosafety rules.
