C. elegans Crossing and Male Maintenance

About 10 days · 2026-11-19

Generate and maintain C. elegans males by heat shock, then use them in small-spot mating crosses and confirm cross progeny by their ~50% male fraction and markers.

⬇ Word (.docx)⬇ PowerPoint (.pptx)

Purpose

To produce and maintain C. elegans males and use them to cross strains, so that alleles from two genotypes can be combined in cross progeny.

Principle

C. elegans is a self-fertilizing hermaphrodite species (XX); males (XO) arise spontaneously at only about 0.2% because of X chromosome loss, so they must be enriched [2]. A 4–6 h heat shock at 30 °C of L4 hermaphrodites raises the F1 male fraction to roughly 2–5% [1][2]. Males are then propagated by mating them with hermaphrodites at excess male ratios on small food spots, which increases encounters [3][4]. Successful cross progeny are recognized because mating yields about 50% male offspring, whereas self-progeny are almost all hermaphrodites [4].

Materials and reagents

ItemDetails
NGM agar plates (60 mm)Per liter: 3 g NaCl, 2.5 g peptone, 17 g agar, 975 mL water. Autoclave, cool to 55 °C, add 1 mL 1 M CaCl₂, 1 mL 5 mg/mL cholesterol in ethanol, 1 mL 1 M MgSO₄, 25 mL 1 M potassium phosphate (pH 6.0). Pour about 11 mL per 6 cm dish [4]; dry 2–3 days.
E. coli OP50 cultureOvernight LB culture at 37 °C. For stock plates, spread about 250 µL and dry covered for 2–3 days [4]. For mating plates, spot 20 µL in the center and leave to dry [3][4].
Hermaphrodite strainWild-type N2 or the mutant strain to be crossed, as synchronized L4 larvae
Male strainN2 males, or a high-incidence-of-males strain such as him-5(e1490) or him-8, obtained from the Caenorhabditis Genetics Center
Worm pickPlatinum wire (about 0.2–0.3 mm) mounted in a glass Pasteur pipette or holder; flame-sterilize before each strain

Equipment

  • 30 °C incubator (heat shock)
  • 20 °C incubator (standard growth)
  • Stereomicroscope with transmitted-light base
  • Bunsen burner or electric sterilizer for the pick
  • Autoclave and 55 °C water bath for media
  • Laminar flow hood or clean bench

Procedure

  1. Pour NGM plates (about 11 mL per 6 cm dish), dry them for 2–3 days at room temperature, then seed with about 250 µL of OP50 and dry again for 2–3 days [4]. (4–6 days)
  2. Set an incubator to 30 °C. Pick 5–10 L4 hermaphrodites onto each of 5–6 seeded stock plates [1][2]. (15 min)
  3. Heat shock the plates at 30 °C for 4–6 h; longer exposure (8–9 h) works but yields few progeny [1][2][3]. (4–6 h)
  4. Move the plates to 20 °C and let the animals lay eggs; expect a few males (about 2–5%) among the F1 [1][2]. (3–4 days)
  5. Pick the males onto a plate with a small OP50 patch and add L4 hermaphrodites, for example 5 males with 2 hermaphrodites per plate [3]. (20 min)
  6. Maintain the stock: every 3–4 days repeat the mating, transferring an excess of males with a few hermaphrodites onto fresh plates [1][3]. (every 3–4 days)
  7. For a cross, set up small-spot plates: spot 20 µL of concentrated OP50 in the plate center and let it dry [3][4]. (10 min)
  8. Pick L4 males to a hermaphrodite-free plate the day before the cross to keep them virgin; keep them at 20 °C for 18–24 h [4]. (18–24 h)
  9. Pick 1–2 L4 hermaphrodites (the strain to be crossed, ideally carrying a recessive marker) onto the spot, then add young adult males at about 3 males per hermaphrodite [3][4]. (15 min)
  10. Incubate the mating plate at 20 °C overnight (about 12–18 h) to allow mating [4].
  11. Transfer each hermaphrodite singly to a fresh seeded plate. Move her daily for 2–3 days to separate cohorts of cross progeny. (2–3 days)
  12. Check plates 3–4 days later. A successful cross shows about 50% male F1 progeny, which tells you that mating occurred [4]; plates with only hermaphrodites are self-progeny. (3–4 days)
  13. Pick several F1 hermaphrodites (cross progeny) to separate plates; let them self and score F2 for the marker phenotype or genotype by PCR. (4–5 days)
  14. Record the cross date, genotypes and plate IDs in a log; keep a backup stock plate of both parent strains at 15–20 °C.

Pause points

  • After heat shock and return to 20 °C, plates can stay at 20 °C until F1 males appear.
  • The male stock can be kept at 20 °C and re-mated every 3–4 days.
  • After picking F1 hermaphrodites, plates can be left until the F2 stage for scoring.

Expected results

  • F1 from heat-shocked hermaphrodites contain about 2–5% males; established male stocks contain a substantial male fraction.
  • A successful cross yields about 50% males among the F1; self-progeny only give hermaphrodites.
  • F2 segregation of the marker confirms that the cross carried the allele.

Controls

  • Heat-shocked hermaphrodites without males, to estimate spontaneous self-progeny
  • A cross with unmarked wild-type N2 males to check mating efficiency
  • Hermaphrodites alone on a small-spot plate to confirm no males appear

Troubleshooting

ProblemLikely causeSolution
Few or no males after heat shockShock too short, wrong stage, or plates not at 30 °CUse L4 worms, verify the incubator temperature, and extend toward 6 h; recover at 20 °C [1][2]
No male progeny after the crossMales unhealthy or too few, or a large lawn lowering encountersUse more males and a smaller food spot (20 µL); use fresh virgin males [3][4]
Males crawl off the plateNo hermaphrodites present or the lawn is too thinAdd unmated hermaphrodites so they condition the plate and keep the spot small [4]
Male stock declines over timeToo few males propagated each generationTransfer an excess of males with a few hermaphrodites every 3–4 days [1][3]
Plates contaminated or driedOld or unevenly poured platesUse plates that are about 1 week old and stored cold, with thoroughly dried lawns [3][4]
All progeny look like self-progenyHermaphrodite was too old or self-fertilized before matingUse L4 hermaphrodites and transfer them away early to identify cross progeny

Safety and waste

  • C. elegans is a Biosafety Level 1 organism; wash hands and decontaminate the bench afterward.
  • Flame sterilization of the pick: keep ethanol away from open flame and let the wire cool.
  • Dispose of plates and worms by autoclaving or bleach; media is hot after autoclaving, so use heat-protective gloves.
