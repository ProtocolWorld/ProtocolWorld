DNase Treatment of RNA Samples

1.5–2 hours · 2026-11-24

Remove contaminating genomic DNA from purified total RNA with RNase-free DNase I or TURBO DNase, inactivate the enzyme, and verify the RNA is DNA-free before RT-qPCR or RNA-seq.

⬇ Word (.docx)⬇ PowerPoint (.pptx)

Purpose

To remove trace genomic DNA (gDNA) from 1–10 µg of purified total RNA in a 50 µL reaction, so that RT-qPCR and RNA-seq signals reflect RNA rather than contaminating DNA.

Principle

Residual gDNA can be amplified by primers that do not span introns, inflating apparent transcript levels in RT-qPCR; the no-RT control reveals it [5]. DNase I cleaves DNA endonucleolytically and needs Mg²⁺ plus Ca²⁺ for activity; salts above about 30–50 mM inhibit it [2][6]. TURBO DNase tolerates more RNA and DNA and is removed with an inactivation reagent that binds the enzyme and divalent cations [1][6]. Conventional DNase I is stopped with EDTA (with or without 65 °C) and then removed by cleanup, because carryover enzyme or Mg²⁺ can damage RNA or interfere with RT [2][3][6]. On-column digestion is an alternative inside silica-column RNA purification [4].

Equipment

  • Heat block or thermal cycler (37 °C and 65 °C)
  • Refrigerated microcentrifuge (≥10,000 × g)
  • RNase-free, low-bind tubes and filter tips
  • Spectrophotometer or fluorometer
  • Automated electrophoresis system for RIN (e.g., Bioanalyzer or TapeStation)
  • qPCR instrument
  • Ice bucket

Procedure

  1. Wipe the bench and pipettes with an RNase-decontaminating solution. Wear fresh gloves, and use RNase-free tubes and filter tips throughout [2]. (10 min)
  2. Quantify the RNA (A260 or fluorometry) and note A260/280 (about 2.0). Dilute to ≤200 µg/mL, i.e., ≤10 µg in 44 µL nuclease-free water, so it fits a 50 µL reaction [1].
  3. Thaw RNA, 10× TURBO DNase Buffer and water on ice. Keep TURBO DNase at −20 °C until use, then place on ice [1].
  4. Routine reaction, 50 µL, on ice in this order: water to 44 µL final RNA volume, then 5 µL 10× TURBO DNase Buffer (1×) and 1 µL TURBO DNase (2 U) [1].
  5. Mix gently by flicking, spin briefly, and incubate at 37 °C for 20–30 min. Do not vortex RNA [1]. (20–30 min)
  6. For stubborn gDNA (>200 µg/mL or >2 µg DNA per 50 µL): dilute RNA if possible, add 0.5 µL TURBO DNase, incubate 30 min at 37 °C, add 0.5 µL more and incubate 30 min [1]. (60 min)
  7. If dilution is impossible, use 2–3 µL TURBO DNase (4–6 U) in the 50 µL reaction. Very concentrated samples (up to 500 µg/mL) may still retain detectable DNA [1].
  8. Resuspend DNase Inactivation Reagent by flicking. Add 5 µL (0.1 volume; at least 2 µL) for routine treatment, or 10 µL (0.2 volume) after rigorous treatment [1].
  9. Incubate 2 min at room temperature, flicking the tube 2–3 times to keep the reagent dispersed [1]. (2 min)
  10. Centrifuge at 10,000 × g for 1.5 min. Transfer the supernatant (RNA) to a fresh tube without disturbing the pellet, which binds Mg²⁺ and can carry over [1]. (2 min)
  11. Conventional alternative: 100 µL on ice with 10 µL 10× DNase I buffer, 1 µg RNA, 1 µL DNase I (2 U) and water; add 2.5 µL murine RNase inhibitor if desired [2].
  12. Incubate conventional DNase I (NEB) at 37 °C for 10 min. For RQ1 (Promega), use about 1 U per µg in 10 µL for 30 min [2][3]. (10–30 min)
  13. Stop conventional reactions: add EDTA to 5 mM final (e.g., 1 µL of 0.5 M per 100 µL), or add 1 µL RQ1 Stop Solution per 10 µL reaction [2][3].
  14. Incubate at 65 °C for 10 min only after EDTA or Stop Solution is present. Heating RNA with free Mg²⁺ causes degradation, so never heat-inactivate without chelator [3][5][6]. (10 min)
  15. Re-purify EDTA-stopped digests with a spin-column cleanup, acid phenol–chloroform extraction, or ethanol precipitation; elute in 10–20 µL nuclease-free water [2][4].
  16. On-column alternative (RNeasy): after loading RNA, wash with 350 µL Buffer RW1; apply 10 µL DNase I stock plus 70 µL Buffer RDD to the membrane; incubate 15 min at 20–30 °C [4]. (15 min)
  17. Finish the on-column procedure with a second 350 µL Buffer RW1 wash, then the standard RPE washes and elution. For heavy DNA load, digest in solution instead and clean up [4].
  18. Measure RNA yield and A260/280 (about 2.0). Check integrity (RIN, or 28S:18S about 2:1 on a gel); RIN should be unchanged from the untreated RNA [5].
  19. Verify DNA removal: run qPCR on treated RNA without reverse transcriptase (no-RT), using intron-spanning or gDNA-sensitive primers (e.g., rDNA primers) in triplicate [5].
  20. Interpret: no amplification in no-RT reactions indicates adequate removal. Otherwise repeat with the rigorous protocol or re-treat [1][5].
  21. Use treated RNA in RT (≤20% of RT-PCR volume, up to 40% [1]) or store at −80 °C in aliquots.

Pause points

  • After step 2: diluted RNA can be stored at −80 °C.
  • After step 15 (cleanup): purified RNA can be stored at −80 °C in aliquots.
  • After step 18: treated RNA can be kept at −80 °C until the no-RT check.

Expected results

  • No qPCR product in no-RT controls (or a Cq far later than +RT) shows gDNA removal [1][5].
  • A260/280 about 2.0 and RIN unchanged from untreated RNA; some RNA loss during cleanup is expected, so re-quantify before RT.
  • Intact 28S and 18S bands at about 2:1 on a denaturing gel [5].

Controls

  • No-RT control per RNA sample and primer pair, run with each qPCR [5]
  • Untreated RNA run in parallel to compare gDNA signal and RIN
  • No-template (water) control for qPCR
  • Mock digest without DNase (buffer only) to detect any RNA loss from handling

Troubleshooting

Degraded RNA (low RIN) after treatmentRNase contamination, or heating RNA with Mg²⁺ without EDTAUse RNase-free consumables; add EDTA before 65 °C; avoid heat-only inactivation [3][6]
Poor DNase activityMissing Ca²⁺/Mg²⁺ or high salt (>30–50 mM) in RNA sampleUse the supplied 10× buffer; desalt RNA or switch to a salt-tolerant DNase [2][6]

Low RNA recoveryLoss during cleanup or precipitation of small amountsUse carrier for ethanol precipitation, or a spin column sized for the RNA amount [2]
Incomplete on-column digestionDNase mix touched the column wall or O-ringPipette directly onto the membrane; or digest in solution [4]

Safety and waste

  • Phenol and chloroform are toxic and corrosive: work in a fume hood with gloves and eye protection, and dispose of as hazardous waste.
  • Wear gloves and a lab coat; DNase I is an enzyme preparation and a possible respiratory sensitizer, so avoid aerosols and check the SDS.
  • Handle 65 °C blocks with care; EDTA solutions and guanidine-containing lysis buffers in spin-column kits are irritants.
  • Treat RNA from biological samples according to your institutional biosafety rules.
