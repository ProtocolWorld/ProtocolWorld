Plasmid Sequence Verification

2–4 days incl. core turnaround · 2026-11-27

Confirm a plasmid by Sanger sequencing: clean miniprep, primer design, core submission amounts, trace review, trimming, alignment to the reference and both-strand checks.

⬇ Word (.docx)⬇ PowerPoint (.pptx)

Purpose

To confirm by Sanger sequencing that a plasmid from E. coli carries the intended insert, junctions and mutations, with no unwanted changes in the region of interest.

Principle

Sanger sequencing reads one strand per primer, giving about 700–900 good-quality bases per reaction; the first 25–35 bases and the tail are unreliable, so primers must sit at least 50 nt before the region to check [1][4]. Clean, accurately quantified DNA and one primer per tube give clean traces [1][2][3]. The .ab1 trace is aligned to the expected map and read by eye, not only as a text file [1][2][4].

Materials and reagents

ItemDetails
Plasmid miniprep DNASilica-column prep eluted in water or 10 mM Tris pH 8–9, not TE with EDTA [2][5]. A260/280 about 1.8 (1.7–1.9) [2][5].
Sequencing primerCustom or vector primer, 18–20 nt or longer, 40–60% GC, Tm above 50 °C, a 3′ GC clamp, no runs of three or more G/C, no hairpins, dimers or degenerate bases, and a single binding site on the plasmid [5][4]. Submit at the core's stated concentration (5–10 µM) [1][5].
Common vector primersM13F(−21) TGTAAAACGACGGCCAGT; M13 Rev AACAGCTATGACCATG; T7 promoter TAATACGACTCACTATAGGG; T3 AATTAACCCTCACTAAAGGG. Check that they match your vector [2][5].
Template quantity (typical core rules)MSU: 1,000 ng plasmid with 3 µL of 10 µM primer in 12 µL [1]. Cornell: 1 µg plasmid, 25 pmol primer in 18 µL [2]. Nevada: plasmid size (bp) ÷ 20 in ng, up to 1 µg, 2–10 pmol primer [3]. UAB: 100 ng/µL plasmid and 5 µM primer, 10 µL of each [5]. Always follow your core's instructions.
QuantificationFluorometer (Qubit, PicoGreen) preferred; a NanoDrop can overestimate concentration, by about 4-fold [2] or 10-fold or more [1] in the cores' experience.
Nuclease-free water or 10 mM Tris pH 9For dilution; EDTA can inhibit the sequencing reaction [2].
Reference sequencePlasmid map (GenBank or SnapGene/Benchling file) of the expected construct.

Equipment

  • Fluorometer or spectrophotometer
  • Microcentrifuge and 0.2–1.5 mL tubes (or a 96-well plate)
  • Trace viewer: SnapGene Viewer, 4Peaks, FinchTV, Chromas or Sequence Scanner [1][4]
  • Alignment software (SnapGene, Benchling, Geneious or NCBI BLAST)

Procedure

  1. Pick 2–3 colonies, grow each overnight in LB with antibiotic, and purify plasmid with a miniprep kit. Elute in water or 10 mM Tris pH 8–9 [2][5]. (~1 day)
  2. Measure concentration by fluorometer and A260/280 (target 1.7–1.9) [2][5]. If the column carried over resin, spin 5 min at full speed and take the top of the eluate [5]. (10 min)
  3. Choose primers that flank the region of interest and bind at least 50 nt, and no more than about 300 nt, before it [4]. Add internal primers for inserts longer than about 800 bp [4]. (15 min)
  4. Plan one reaction per primer. Sequence both strands for important regions. Never put two primers in one tube [1]. (5 min)
  5. Dilute DNA and primer to the core's amounts (see Materials). Example [1]: 1,000 ng plasmid plus 3 µL of 10 µM primer in 12 µL. Mix gently. (15 min)
  6. Label tubes clearly, submit the order with the primer name, plasmid name and size, and ship or deliver on the core's schedule. (10 min)
  7. Download the .ab1 and text files when ready (typically 1–3 days). (1–3 days)
  8. Open each .ab1 in a trace viewer. Check signal (about 175–10,000 RFU in the MSU report [1]), quality values (QV 20 is a 1% error rate [1]), and single clean peaks. (15 min)
  9. Trim the poor first 25–35 bases and the noisy tail beyond about 900 bases [1][4]. (5 min)
  10. Align the trimmed read to the expected sequence. Check the insert, both junctions, reading frame and every region you changed. (20 min)
  11. For every mismatch or N call, return to the trace and decide whether it is a real change or a mis-call. Reads from both strands should agree [4]. (15 min)
  12. Keep the verified colony's glycerol stock, record the clone ID, and discard clones with real mutations. If results are poor, see troubleshooting [1][2]. (15 min)

Pause points

  • Miniprep DNA can be stored at −20 °C before submission.
  • Raw .ab1 files can be stored indefinitely; analyze when convenient.

Expected results

  • A good reaction gives about 300–900 bp of usable sequence [4].
  • Peaks are single, evenly spaced and well above background [1][2].
  • The read matches the reference across the insert and both junctions, on both strands.

Controls

  • A known-good plasmid and primer pair as a positive control for the reaction
  • Sequence a second colony to rule out a single bad clone
  • Use both strands (forward and reverse primers) for key regions [1]
  • Compare with an empty-vector read to find vector-specific artifacts

Troubleshooting

ProblemLikely causeSolution
Weak or failed reactionToo little DNA, bad primer, template–primer mismatchQuantify by fluorometer; re-check primer sequence, Tm and binding site [1][2]
Noisy or deteriorating traceSalt, ethanol, protein or other contaminantsRe-purify; check A260/280 of 1.8 or more [2][5]
Mixed peaks from the startTwo primers in one tube, two plasmids or a mixed colonyUse one primer per reaction; re-streak and re-pick [1][2]
Signal stops or drops suddenlyHairpin or secondary structure; end of a PCR productAsk the core about a hairpin protocol; sequence from the other strand [1][2]
Slippage after a homopolymer runPolymerase slippageSequence the opposite strand or place a new primer beyond the run [1][2]
Short readPoor template quality, primer too near the targetRe-prep the DNA; use a primer at least 50 nt upstream [1][4]

Safety and waste

  • Wear gloves and a lab coat when handling E. coli cultures; autoclave or bleach cultures and plates.
  • Antibiotic stocks: avoid skin contact and dispose as chemical waste.
  • Ethidium bromide or other gel stains (if you check DNA on a gel) are hazardous: use gloves and dispose per institutional rules.
