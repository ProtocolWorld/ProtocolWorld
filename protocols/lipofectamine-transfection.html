Lipofectamine Transfection

≈ 1.5 h hands-on over 2 days + 24–72 h expression · 2026-11-26

Transfect adherent mammalian cells with plasmid DNA using Lipofectamine 3000 and P3000: seed to 70–90% confluence, form lipid–DNA complexes in Opti-MEM, add dropwise and assay at 24–72 h.

⬇ Word (.docx)⬇ PowerPoint (.pptx)

Purpose

To deliver plasmid DNA into adherent mammalian cells (for example HEK293, HeLa or similar) with Lipofectamine 3000 so that a gene of interest or a reporter is expressed transiently.

Principle

Lipofectamine 3000 is a cationic lipid reagent. It is diluted in serum-free Opti-MEM and mixed with DNA that has been primed with the P3000 enhancer reagent, which forms lipid–DNA complexes. The complexes are added to cells in growth medium, taken up by endocytosis and release the DNA, which is expressed from the nucleus. Efficiency depends mainly on cell health, cell density (70–90% confluent), DNA quality and the lipid-to-DNA ratio, so two reagent doses are tested first [1][2][4]. The numbers below are for a 24-well plate and scale to other formats in the table.

Equipment

  • Biosafety cabinet (Class II)
  • CO₂ incubator (37 °C, 5% CO₂)
  • Multi-well tissue-culture plates
  • Hemocytometer or automated cell counter
  • Micropipettes (0.1–1000 µL)
  • Inverted fluorescence microscope or plate reader for the readout

Procedure

  1. Day 0: detach healthy, subconfluent cells, count them, and confirm viability above 90% [1][2]. (20 min)
  2. Seed 0.5–2 × 10⁵ cells in 500 µL antibiotic-free medium per 24-well well so they are 70–90% confluent the next day [1]. (10 min)
  3. Incubate overnight at 37 °C, 5% CO₂. Check by microscope that the wells are 70–90% confluent and evenly spread before continuing [1][2]. (16–24 h)
  4. Day 1: warm Opti-MEM and the DNA to room temperature, mix the Lipofectamine 3000 vial by gentle inversion, and prepare a DNA master mix for all wells plus 10% extra [1]. (10 min)
  5. Tube A (per well): dilute 0.75 µL or 1.5 µL Lipofectamine 3000 in 25 µL Opti-MEM and mix gently. Prepare both doses the first time you use a cell line [1][4]. (5 min)
  6. Tube B (per well): mix 0.5 µg DNA and 1 µL P3000 in 25 µL Opti-MEM (2 µL P3000 per µg DNA). Mix gently [1]. (5 min)
  7. Add the diluted DNA from tube B to tube A (1:1, 50 µL per well), mix gently by pipetting, and do not vortex [1]. (2 min)
  8. Incubate the mixture for 10–15 min at room temperature. A fine granular haze is normal [1][4]. (10–15 min)
  9. Add 50 µL of complex dropwise to each well, spreading the drops across the well, then rock the plate gently so the complexes distribute evenly [1][4]. (5 min)
  10. Return the plate to the incubator. No medium change is needed after adding the complexes [1]. (—)
  11. For stable lines, add the selective antibiotic only 24–48 h after transfection [4]. (24–48 h)
  12. Analyze the cells (fluorescence, Western blot, luciferase, and so on) 24–72 h after transfection; peak expression is usually at 48 h [1][2][3]. (24–72 h)

Pause points

  • After seeding (step 3): cells wait overnight at 37 °C.
  • After step 10: cells stay in the incubator until the chosen time point (24–72 h).

Expected results

  • With a GFP reporter, a healthy line such as HEK293 typically shows bright, widespread fluorescence at 24–48 h; efficiency varies by cell line (the manufacturer's example runs from 32% to 62% in two lung cancer lines) [2][3].
  • Cells stay attached and keep a normal shape; there is little rounding or floating debris at 24 h.

Controls

  • Untransfected cells (background and baseline health)
  • Cells plus DNA only and cells plus lipid only (no complexes), to show that neither alone causes toxicity or expression [1]
  • Positive control: reporter plasmid such as CMV–GFP
  • Mock transfection with Opti-MEM only
  • Empty-vector control when a gene of interest is expressed

Troubleshooting

ProblemLikely causeSolution
Low efficiencyCells too sparse or too confluent; serum or antibiotics present in complex mix; poor DNAAim for 70–90% confluence; prepare complexes in Opti-MEM only; use endotoxin-free DNA with A260/A280 of 1.8–2.0 [1][4]
Many cells die or round upToo much reagent or DNA; low cell density; endotoxinTest the lower Lipofectamine dose (0.75 µL per 24-well well); seed more cells; use endotoxin-free DNA [1][4]
Heavy precipitate in the complexEDTA or other salts in the DNA stockDilute DNA in water or keep EDTA below 0.3 mM [4]
Uneven expression across the wellComplexes not spreadAdd dropwise across the well and rock the plate gently [4]
Performance suddenly dropsHigh passage number or contaminated lineThaw a fresh vial; test for mycoplasma [4]

Safety and waste

  • Work in a biosafety cabinet with a lab coat, gloves and eye protection; treat cells and medium as potentially biohazardous.
  • Follow your institution's rules for recombinant DNA; check whether the plasmid or the cell line needs a registered biosafety level.
  • Collect used medium and tips as biohazardous waste and disinfect liquid (for example 10% bleach for at least 20 min) before disposal.
  • The reagent is for research use only; consult the product safety data sheet for handling.
