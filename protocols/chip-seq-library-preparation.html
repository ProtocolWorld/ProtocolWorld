ChIP-seq Library Preparation

1.5 days (about 8–9 h hands-on) · 2026-11-24

Build barcoded Illumina libraries from 1–10 ng of ChIP and input DNA: end prep, adaptor titration, USER ligation, bead cleanup, input-based PCR cycle choice, QC and ENCODE depth/quality guidelines.

⬇ Word (.docx)⬇ PowerPoint (.pptx)

Purpose

To convert 1–10 ng of immunoprecipitated (ChIP) DNA, and a matched input control, into barcoded Illumina sequencing libraries with minimal amplification bias.

Principle

ChIP DNA fragments are end-repaired, given a single 3′ A overhang and ligated to Illumina adapters; USER digestion opens the hairpin adapters. Magnetic-bead cleanups remove enzymes and adapter dimers, and a limited PCR adds indexes and enriches adapter-ligated molecules [1][2][4]. Fewer PCR cycles and low adapter concentration protect library complexity [1][3]. Library quality and depth are then checked against ENCODE guidelines [5].

Materials and reagents

ItemDetails

Magnetic beadsSPRIselect or AMPure XP, at room temperature for 30 min before use [2]

Equipment

  • Thermocycler with heated lid
  • Magnetic rack for PCR strips or tubes
  • Qubit fluorometer
  • Bioanalyzer or TapeStation
  • Multichannel pipette
  • Vortex mixer and microcentrifuge
  • Ice bucket

Procedure

  1. Measure ChIP and input DNA with Qubit HS. Use matched amounts (for example 5 ng each) in 50 µL of 0.1× TE or water. Include a no-template control [2][5]. (20 min)
  2. End prep: mix 50 µL DNA, 3 µL End Prep Enzyme Mix and 7 µL Reaction Buffer (60 µL). Run 20 °C for 30 min, 65 °C for 30 min, hold at 4 °C, lid at 75 °C [2]. (1 h)
  3. Dilute the adaptor: 1:25 (0.6 µM) for under 5 ng DNA or 1:10 (1.5 µM) for 5–100 ng. Add 2.5 µL adaptor to the end-prep mix and mix before the enzymes [2][6]. (5 min)
  4. Add 30 µL Ligation Master Mix and 1 µL Ligation Enhancer (93.5 µL total). Incubate 15 min at 20 °C with the heated lid off [2]. (15 min)
  5. Add 3 µL USER enzyme, mix and incubate at 37 °C for 15 min with the lid at 47 °C. This opens the hairpin of the NEBNext adaptor [2]. (15 min)
  6. At 50 ng or less skip size selection (above 50 ng use the manual's table). Add 87 µL (0.9×) beads to the 96.5 µL reaction, mix, 5 min, then magnet [2]. (15 min)
  7. Discard the supernatant. Wash the beads twice with 200 µL fresh 80% ethanol on the magnet (30 s each). Air-dry for up to 5 min, avoiding cracks [2]. (10 min)
  8. Elute in 17 µL 0.1× TE, wait 2 min and move 15 µL to a new PCR tube. Stop here if needed: store at −20 °C [2].
  9. PCR: 15 µL library, 25 µL Q5 master mix, 10 µL index primer mix (50 µL). Run 98 °C 30 s; N × (98 °C 10 s, 65 °C 75 s); 65 °C 5 min; 4 °C hold [2]. (30 min)
  10. Choose N from the input: 10 ng 6–7 cycles; 5 ng 7–8; 1 ng 9–10; 0.5 ng 10–11. Use the lowest number that yields enough library; extra cycles skew results [2][3].
  11. Post-PCR cleanup: add 45 µL (0.9×) beads, wash twice with 80% ethanol, dry and elute in 33 µL 0.1× TE; transfer 30 µL to a LoBind tube [2]. (25 min)
  12. If a sharp peak near 127 bp (adapter dimers) remains, repeat one more 0.9× bead cleanup [2][6].
  13. QC: dilute 1–2 µL and run a High Sensitivity DNA chip or TapeStation HS D1000. Expect one smooth peak and little 127 bp adapter dimer. Quantify by Qubit or qPCR [2]. (1 h)
  14. Pool libraries in equimolar amounts and sequence. Aim for at least 10 million uniquely mapped reads per replicate (20 million per factor, mammalian point-source) [5].

Pause points

  • After end prep and before ligation, proceed immediately; avoid overnight storage at low input [6].
  • Washed and eluted libraries (before or after PCR) can be stored at −20 °C.
  • Final pooled libraries are stable at −20 °C for months.

Expected results

  • Final library yield is sufficient to pool (measure by Qubit); a Bioanalyzer trace shows a smooth peak (insert plus adapters, roughly 300–400 bp) with little adapter dimer.
  • Input library and ChIP library show similar fragment-size distributions.
  • After sequencing and mapping, library complexity is high (non-redundant fraction ≥ 0.8 at 10 million reads) and FRiP is at least 1% for a point-source factor [5].

Controls

  • Matched input DNA library (or IgG) sequenced at least as deeply as ChIP [5]
  • No-template control through PCR
  • Two independent biological replicates [5]
  • ChIP-qPCR enrichment at positive and negative loci before library prep [4]
  • Reference ChIP of a well-characterized target (e.g., H3K4me3 or CTCF)

Troubleshooting

ProblemLikely causeSolution
Adapter dimer peak (about 127 bp)Too much adaptor for the input, or self-ligationTitrate adaptor (1:25 under 5 ng); add adaptor to DNA before the ligase mix; repeat 0.9× cleanup [2][6]
Low library yieldPoor ChIP recovery, bead loss or enzyme inhibitionCheck Qubit before and after; avoid over-drying beads; add 1–2 PCR cycles, not more than needed [2]
High duplication, low complexityToo much amplification or too little DNAIncrease input, reduce cycles and consider a low-input method [3]
Broad or large-sized libraryUnder-sheared chromatinOptimize sonication to a modal size of about 200 bp [3]
Weak peak enrichment (low FRiP)Antibody or ChIP qualityValidate the antibody; check ChIP-qPCR before library prep [5]
Carryover of beads or ethanolIncomplete drying or washingRemove all ethanol; air-dry 5 min only

Safety and waste

  • Wear gloves and a lab coat; formaldehyde used earlier in ChIP is toxic, so handle cross-linked samples in a hood.
  • Ethanol is flammable; keep away from heat.
  • Dispose of used tips, tubes and beads as laboratory waste; follow institutional rules for Illumina flow-cell waste.
  • Work with human or animal samples under approved protocols.
