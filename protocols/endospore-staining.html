Endospore Staining

Endospore Staining

Estimated time: About 1 h (plus 24–72 h culture growth) | Protocol World

To detect bacterial endospores and distinguish them from vegetative cells, and to record whether spores are free or inside the cell and where they sit.

Endospores have a dehydrated core and a tough, poorly permeable coat, so ordinary dyes do not enter them. Steaming drives malachite green into the spores, where it is retained during a water rinse, whereas vegetative cells lose the dye and take up the safranin counterstain [1][5]. Spores appear green and vegetative cells pink-red [1][3]. Escherichia coli does not sporulate, so it serves as a negative control; Bacillus subtilis sporulates readily and serves as the positive control [3]. Older cultures (24–72 h) contain more spores than young ones [1][6].

  • Bunsen burner or slide warmer
  • Hot plate with 100–250 mL beaker and wire rack (steam bath)
  • Inoculating loop and wooden clothespins or forceps
  • Staining rack and tray with waste container
  • Bright-field microscope with 100× oil-immersion objective (1,000× total)
  • Biohazard and chemical waste containers
  • Nitrile gloves, lab coat, safety glasses
  • Grow B. subtilis for 24–72 h and E. coli for 18–24 h; older B. subtilis cultures give more spores [1][6]. (24–72 h)
  • Clean and label two slides (or two halves of one): B. subtilis and E. coli. Use a wax pencil, not a marker [2][3].
  • Place a drop of water or saline on each slide; mix in a small loopful of culture to a thin, even smear [3].
  • Air dry completely, then heat fix by passing the slide through a flame about three times. Cool before staining [1][3]. (5 min)
  • Heat water in a beaker on a hot plate to a gentle boil (do not boil dry). Lay a rack over it [2][3].
  • Cover each smear with a paper strip slightly smaller than the slide and saturate it with 0.5% malachite green [1][2].
  • Steam about 5 min, adding dye dropwise so the paper never dries out [1][3]. Some manuals steam longer (up to 15 min) [2]. (5 min)
  • Pause point: lift the slide with a clothespin, discard the paper in solid waste, and rinse until no green runs off [1][2].
  • Flood with safranin for 30 s to 1 min over a waste container [1][3]. Longer times (to 2 min) also appear in manuals [4]. (30–60 s)
  • Rinse gently with water, blot (do not wipe) with bibulous paper, and air dry fully [3][4].
  • Pause point: slides may be examined later once fully dry. Find the smear at 10×, add oil, and examine at 100× [1][3].
  • Record spore color, shape, and position (central, subterminal, terminal; free or within the cell) [1][2].
  • Optional Dorner method: steam carbolfuchsin 5–10 min, decolorize with acid-alcohol for 1 min, rinse, and overlay nigrosin [1]. (15 min)
  • Disinfect the bench, discard slides and wastes as directed, and wash hands [2][4].
  • The protocol can be paused after sterilization and labeling; store solutions as described.
  • B. subtilis: bright green oval spores, free or inside pink-red rods [1][2].
  • Spores may be central, subterminal, or terminal, helpful for species identification [3].
  • E. coli: all cells pink-red; no green structures [1].
  • Dorner method: vegetative cells colorless, spores red, background black [1].
  • Positive control: B. subtilis, 24–72 h on sporulation-promoting medium, on the same run [1][4][6].
  • Negative control: E. coli (non-spore-former) stained on the same slide or run.
  • Reagent control: a smear of a known spore-former confirms that the stains and steaming worked.

  • Malachite green stains skin and clothing for days; wear gloves, a lab coat, and eye protection. Collect stain runoff as hazardous chemical waste [2].
  • Safranin and carbolfuchsin (with phenol) are irritants; carbolfuchsin phenol and acid-alcohol HCl are corrosive. Consult the SDS for each reagent [1].
  • Steam, hot plates, and open flame: handle hot slides with clothespins, never let the beaker boil dry, and keep paper strips small so they do not ignite or drip [1][2].
  • Treat B. subtilis and E. coli K-12 as biosafety level 1 organisms; use aseptic technique. Do not use Bacillus anthracis, B. cereus, or Clostridium species without proper biosafety level approval, since spores resist disinfection.
  • Autoclave cultures and contaminated materials; place slides in the designated container and wash hands [4].
