Endospore Staining

About 1 h (plus 24–72 h culture growth) · 2026-11-21

Differentially stain bacterial endospores green and vegetative cells pink-red with malachite green and safranin using the Schaeffer–Fulton method, with B. subtilis as the spore-forming positive control and E. coli as the non-spore-forming negative control.

⬇ Word (.docx)⬇ PowerPoint (.pptx)

Purpose

To detect bacterial endospores and distinguish them from vegetative cells, and to record whether spores are free or inside the cell and where they sit.

Principle

Endospores have a dehydrated core and a tough, poorly permeable coat, so ordinary dyes do not enter them. Steaming drives malachite green into the spores, where it is retained during a water rinse, whereas vegetative cells lose the dye and take up the safranin counterstain [1][5]. Spores appear green and vegetative cells pink-red [1][3]. Escherichia coli does not sporulate, so it serves as a negative control; Bacillus subtilis sporulates readily and serves as the positive control [3]. Older cultures (24–72 h) contain more spores than young ones [1][6].

Equipment

  • Bunsen burner or slide warmer
  • Hot plate with 100–250 mL beaker and wire rack (steam bath)
  • Inoculating loop and wooden clothespins or forceps
  • Staining rack and tray with waste container
  • Bright-field microscope with 100× oil-immersion objective (1,000× total)
  • Biohazard and chemical waste containers
  • Nitrile gloves, lab coat, safety glasses

Procedure

  1. Grow B. subtilis for 24–72 h and E. coli for 18–24 h; older B. subtilis cultures give more spores [1][6]. (24–72 h)
  2. Clean and label two slides (or two halves of one): B. subtilis and E. coli. Use a wax pencil, not a marker [2][3].
  3. Place a drop of water or saline on each slide; mix in a small loopful of culture to a thin, even smear [3].
  4. Air dry completely, then heat fix by passing the slide through a flame about three times. Cool before staining [1][3]. (5 min)
  5. Heat water in a beaker on a hot plate to a gentle boil (do not boil dry). Lay a rack over it [2][3].
  6. Cover each smear with a paper strip slightly smaller than the slide and saturate it with 0.5% malachite green [1][2].
  7. Steam about 5 min, adding dye dropwise so the paper never dries out [1][3]. Some manuals steam longer (up to 15 min) [2]. (5 min)
  8. Pause point: lift the slide with a clothespin, discard the paper in solid waste, and rinse until no green runs off [1][2].
  9. Flood with safranin for 30 s to 1 min over a waste container [1][3]. Longer times (to 2 min) also appear in manuals [4]. (30–60 s)
  10. Rinse gently with water, blot (do not wipe) with bibulous paper, and air dry fully [3][4].
  11. Pause point: slides may be examined later once fully dry. Find the smear at 10×, add oil, and examine at 100× [1][3].
  12. Record spore color, shape, and position (central, subterminal, terminal; free or within the cell) [1][2].
  13. Optional Dorner method: steam carbolfuchsin 5–10 min, decolorize with acid-alcohol for 1 min, rinse, and overlay nigrosin [1]. (15 min)
  14. Disinfect the bench, discard slides and wastes as directed, and wash hands [2][4].

Pause points

  • The protocol can be paused after sterilization and labeling; store solutions as described.

Expected results

  • B. subtilis: bright green oval spores, free or inside pink-red rods [1][2].
  • Spores may be central, subterminal, or terminal, helpful for species identification [3].
  • E. coli: all cells pink-red; no green structures [1].
  • Dorner method: vegetative cells colorless, spores red, background black [1].

Controls

  • Positive control: B. subtilis, 24–72 h on sporulation-promoting medium, on the same run [1][4][6].
  • Negative control: E. coli (non-spore-former) stained on the same slide or run.
  • Reagent control: a smear of a known spore-former confirms that the stains and steaming worked.

Troubleshooting

ProblemLikely causeSolution
No green spores in B. subtilisCulture too young or lacking sporulation conditionsUse 24–72 h cultures or a sporulation medium (e.g., DSM) [1][6]
Entire smear stains greenOver-steaming, thick smear, or inadequate rinsingMake a thinner smear and rinse until runoff is clear [2]
Cells washed off the slideUnder-heat-fixing or vigorous rinsingHeat fix about three passes; rinse gently [3]
Stain precipitate or dry, reddish-gold patchesPaper dried out during steamingKeep the paper moist with dye; add drops as needed [1][4]
Vegetative cells stay green or look paleSafranin too short or rinse too longExtend safranin to 1 min; rinse briefly [1][3]
Spores appear colorlessInsufficient steamingSteam a full 5 min with a steady vapor [1]

Safety and waste

  • Malachite green stains skin and clothing for days; wear gloves, a lab coat, and eye protection. Collect stain runoff as hazardous chemical waste [2].
  • Safranin and carbolfuchsin (with phenol) are irritants; carbolfuchsin phenol and acid-alcohol HCl are corrosive. Consult the SDS for each reagent [1].
  • Steam, hot plates, and open flame: handle hot slides with clothespins, never let the beaker boil dry, and keep paper strips small so they do not ignite or drip [1][2].
  • Treat B. subtilis and E. coli K-12 as biosafety level 1 organisms; use aseptic technique. Do not use Bacillus anthracis, B. cereus, or Clostridium species without proper biosafety level approval, since spores resist disinfection.
  • Autoclave cultures and contaminated materials; place slides in the designated container and wash hands [4].
