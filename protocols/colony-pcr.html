Colony PCR

Estimated time: ≈ 3 h | Protocol World

To quickly check, without a plasmid miniprep, which bacterial colonies from a transformation carry the desired insert (or plasmid) and whether the insert has the expected size.

A small amount of a colony is used as the PCR template. The first heating step (95 °C, several minutes) lyses the cells and releases plasmid DNA, which is then amplified with primers that flank the insert (vector primers) or with one vector primer and one insert primer. A product of the expected size indicates a positive clone, while the empty vector gives a shorter product or none, depending on the primer pair [1][2][3]. Too many cells inhibit the reaction, so only a trace of the colony should be used [3][4]. Each colony is also patched onto a fresh plate so that positive clones can be recovered afterward [1][4].

  • Gel electrophoresis system and power supply
  • Gel imager or UV transilluminator
  • Microcentrifuge (for brief spins)
  • PCR tubes or a 96-well PCR plate
  • Marker pen and tube rack
  • 37 °C incubator
  • Number a grid of 8–24 spaces on a selective agar plate and label PCR tubes to match. Include a tube for an empty-vector colony and one no-template tube [4]. (5 min)
  • Prepare a master mix on ice for n + 1 reactions: 10 µL 2× Taq master mix, 0.4 µL each of forward and reverse primer (10 µM), and 9.2 µL water per reaction (19 µL total) [1][2]. (5 min)
  • Dispense 19 µL of master mix into each PCR tube. Keep the tubes on ice until the colonies are added.
  • Touch a sterile toothpick or tip to the center of one colony, picking only a small amount of cells. A visible clump is already too much [3][4]. (1 min)
  • Streak the same toothpick on its numbered grid space of the patch plate, then swirl it in the PCR tube until the liquid just turns faintly cloudy [1][2].
  • Add 1 µL of water to the no-template tube instead of a colony. Close all tubes and pulse-spin them briefly.
  • Cycle: 95 °C 5 min; 30 × (95 °C 30 s, 50–55 °C 30 s, 68–72 °C at 1 min/kb); then 68–72 °C 5–10 min and a 4 °C hold. Set the anneal about 5 °C below primer Tm [1][2]. (≈ 1.5 h)
  • Incubate the patch plate upside down at 37 °C for 4–16 h while the PCR runs, then store it at 4 °C once colonies are visible [1]. (overnight)
  • Cast or use a 1% agarose gel. Load 5 µL of each reaction (the master mix contains loading dye if it is a Quick-Load type; otherwise mix with loading dye) and 5 µL of ladder [1][3]. (10 min)
  • Run at 100 V for 30–40 min, until the dye front has traveled about two-thirds of the gel, then image it [3]. Compare each product with the empty-vector and positive controls. (45 min)
  • Pick positive clones from the matching patch spaces and inoculate 3 mL selective LB for a miniprep. Confirm important constructs by Sanger sequencing [1][4]. (overnight)
  • After the reactions finish, keep PCR products at 4 °C for up to 1 h or at −20 °C for longer before running the gel [2].
  • Patch plates can be incubated overnight at 37 °C and stored at 4 °C for about a week.
  • Positive clones give a single band of the expected size (insert plus the vector sequence between the primers).
  • Empty-vector colonies give a smaller band, or no band if one primer anneals inside the insert.
  • The no-template lane is clean. Products up to several kilobases can be amplified with common Taq mixes [1][3].
  • Empty-vector colony as the negative control for insert presence [3][4]
  • Known positive clone or purified plasmid as the positive control
  • No-template reaction (water only) to detect contaminated reagents
  • Ladder in every gel to confirm product size

  • Wear gloves and a lab coat; E. coli strains used in cloning are normally Biosafety Level 1, so follow your institution's rules.
  • Ethidium bromide is a mutagen; prefer a safer nucleic-acid stain, and wear gloves. UV light damages eyes and skin: use a shield.
  • Dispose of toothpicks, tips and plates as biohazardous waste (autoclave or bleach). Dispose of gels and stain waste according to local rules.
