Illumina RNA-seq Library Preparation

2 days (about 10 h hands-on) · 2026-11-17

Poly(A) capture, fragmentation, dUTP strand marking, adapter ligation, PCR and QC for strand-specific Illumina RNA-seq libraries.

⬇ Word (.docx)⬇ PowerPoint (.pptx)

Purpose

To convert total RNA into a strand-specific, poly(A)-selected, indexed cDNA library ready for sequencing on an Illumina instrument.

Principle

Poly(A) mRNA is captured on oligo-dT beads, fragmented with heat and divalent cations, and copied into first-strand cDNA with random primers. During second-strand synthesis dUTP replaces dTTP, so the second strand can later be suppressed in PCR and the read orientation is preserved [1][3]. After end repair, A-tailing and ligation of indexed adapters, size-selective bead cleanups remove adapter dimers, and a few PCR cycles enrich only adapter-ligated molecules [1][2].

Equipment

  • Thermal cycler with heated lid
  • Magnetic stand for 96-well plates or tubes
  • Plate or tube centrifuge
  • Multichannel pipettes and filter tips
  • Agilent Bioanalyzer or Fragment Analyzer
  • Qubit fluorometer and qPCR instrument

Procedure

  1. Quantify RNA fluorometrically and check integrity on a Bioanalyzer or Fragment Analyzer; proceed only if RIN ≥ 8. DNase-treat if genomic DNA is present [1][2]. (45 min)
  2. Adjust 0.1–1 µg total RNA to 50 µL with nuclease-free water. Add 50 µL oligo-dT beads, heat at 65 °C for 5 min, then bind for 5 min at room temperature [1]. (15 min)
  3. Wash the beads twice with 200 µL wash buffer. Elute at 80 °C for 2 min in 50 µL elution buffer, rebind with 50 µL binding buffer, and wash once more [1]. (25 min)
  4. Add fragmentation/priming mix (19.5 µL in the Illumina guide) and heat at 94 °C for 8 min for a median insert of about 150 bp (NEB: 94 °C for 15 min for about 200 nt) [1][2]. (15 min)
  5. Place on a magnet, move 17 µL supernatant to a new tube, add 8 µL first-strand mix with enzyme, and run 25 °C for 10 min, 42 °C for 15 min, 70 °C for 15 min [1]. (45 min)
  6. Add 20 µL second-strand mix (dUTP in place of dTTP) to the 25 µL first strand, and incubate at 16 °C for 1 h with the lid off or not above 40 °C [1][2]. (1 h)
  7. Clean up with 90 µL beads (about 1.8×), wash twice with 200 µL fresh 80% ethanol, dry briefly, and elute in 17.5 µL resuspension buffer; move 15 µL on [1].
  8. A-tail and end-repair per the kit: Illumina uses 30 µL at 37 °C for 30 min then 70 °C for 5 min; NEB uses 20 °C for 30 min then 65 °C for 30 min [1][2]. (1 h)
  9. Ligate indexed adapters, diluted to match input (NEB: 5-fold at 250–1,000 ng, 25-fold at 100–249 ng, 100-fold at 10–99 ng): 30 °C for 10 min (Illumina) or 20 °C for 15 min (NEB) [1][2]. (30 min)
  10. If the kit includes USER, add it and incubate at 37 °C for 15 min to cut the uracil-containing strand [2][3].
  11. Clean up the ligation: Illumina uses two bead rounds (42 µL then 50 µL); NEB uses 0.9× beads. Elute in 20 µL (Illumina) or 15 µL of 0.1× TE (NEB) [1][2]. (40 min)
  12. Amplify with index primers using the fewest cycles possible. NEB lists 7–8 cycles at 1 µg, 11–12 at 100 ng, 14–15 at 10 ng; the Illumina program uses 15. Follow your kit [1][2]. (40 min)
  13. Clean up the PCR with 0.9× beads, wash twice with 80% ethanol, and elute in 20–23 µL buffer. Store at −20 °C [2].
  14. Run 1 µL on a Bioanalyzer DNA 1000 chip. Expect a narrow peak around 260–300 bp; peaks near 80 or 128 bp mean primers or adapter dimers [1][2]. (1 h)
  15. Quantify by Qubit and qPCR, normalize each library to 10 nM in 10 mM Tris-HCl pH 8.5 with 0.1% Tween 20, and pool immediately before sequencing [1].

Pause points

  • After second-strand cleanup: store sealed at −25 to −15 °C for up to 7 days [1].
  • After ligation cleanup: store at −25 to −15 °C for up to 7 days [1].
  • After PCR cleanup: store at −20 °C [2].

Expected results

  • A single narrow library peak around 260–300 bp on the Bioanalyzer, with little signal near 80 or 128 bp.
  • Yield depends on input and cycle number; confirm by Qubit and qPCR before pooling, and use fewer cycles at higher input [1][2].
  • Mapped reads should show strand orientation consistent with antisense Read 1 for dUTP libraries [3].

Controls

  • Include a no-RNA (water) library to detect contamination.
  • Add synthetic spike-in RNA to track efficiency.
  • Run a reference RNA sample (e.g., standard human or mouse RNA) alongside new batches.

Troubleshooting

ProblemLikely causeSolution
Adapter-dimer peak near 128 bpToo much adapter for the input or low inputDilute adapter per the input table; repeat a 0.9× bead cleanup [2]
Low yieldDegraded RNA, bead loss or over-dried beadsCheck RIN; do not over-dry beads; add 1–2 PCR cycles only if needed [1][2]
Second peak near 1,000 bpOver-amplificationReduce PCR cycles [2]
Broad size distributionFragmentation too short or variableAdjust the 94 °C time and re-optimize [1][2]
Poor strand specificityActinomycin D missing or UNG/USER step skippedCheck reagents against the kit and include the dUTP strand-marking steps [3]

Safety and waste

  • Actinomycin D in the first-strand mix is toxic; wear gloves and dispose as hazardous waste.
  • Ethanol is flammable; keep 80% ethanol away from ignition sources.
  • Handle RNA with RNase-free tips and surfaces; dispose of reagents per your institution's rules.
