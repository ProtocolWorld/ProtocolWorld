Dual-Luciferase Assay

2–3 days · 2026-11-24

Sequential firefly and Renilla luciferase readout from one lysate of co-transfected HEK293T cells, using Promega DLR reagents or an in-house alternative, with ratio normalization to correct well-to-well variation.

⬇ Word (.docx)⬇ PowerPoint (.pptx)

Purpose

To quantify the activity of a firefly luciferase reporter in transfected mammalian cells, normalized to a co-expressed Renilla luciferase, by reading both enzymes sequentially from the same lysate.

Principle

Firefly luciferase (Photinus pyralis) uses luciferin, ATP and Mg²⁺; Renilla luciferase uses coelenterazine and needs no ATP. In the DLR format, LAR II starts the firefly reaction, then Stop & Glo® quenches firefly light by at least 10⁵-fold and starts the Renilla reaction in the same well [1]. Dividing firefly by Renilla in each well corrects for differences in transfection efficiency, cell number and viability [2]. The control plasmid is kept low (about 1:20–1:50 versus the reporter) because strong control promoters can suppress the experimental reporter in trans [1][2]. An in-house reagent set can replace the kit [3].

Equipment

  • CO₂ incubator (37 °C, 5% CO₂)
  • Luminometer or plate reader with two reagent injectors (e.g., GloMax)
  • Orbital shaker or rocking platform
  • Multichannel pipette and low-retention tips
  • Refrigerated microcentrifuge
  • Water bath at room temperature for thawing LAR II
  • Biosafety cabinet

Procedure

  1. Seed cells the day before: about 1.5 × 10⁵ HEK293T cells per 24-well well in DMEM + 10% FBS, so they are roughly 70–80% confluent at transfection [4]. (Day 1)
  2. Plan the plate: triplicate wells for each condition, plus untransfected cells (background), a Renilla-only well and a promoterless firefly vector control [1].
  3. Set DNA per 24-well well to 500 ng total: reporter, Renilla control at 1:20–1:50 of reporter mass (e.g., 50 ng + 1–2.5 ng) and carrier or effector DNA [1][2][4].
  4. Prepare a master mix per condition so each triplicate shares one DNA–lipid mix; this keeps well-to-well variation low [2].
  5. Dilute the DNA and 1.5 µL Lipofectamine 2000 in Opti-MEM (0.1 mL final per well), combine and incubate 15–20 min at room temperature [4]. (20 min)
  6. Add the complexes dropwise and swirl. Incubate at 37 °C, 5% CO₂ for 24–48 h; the best time depends on the promoter and treatment, so optimize [1][4]. (24–48 h)
  7. Apply treatments (drug, cytokine, cotransfected effector) for the planned period before harvest. Keep cells at no more than 95% confluence at lysis [1].
  8. Make 1× Passive Lysis Buffer: 1 volume of 5× PLB plus 4 volumes of distilled water. Prepare fresh when possible [1].
  9. Aspirate the medium, wash once with PBS and remove all rinse fluid. Add 1× PLB: 100 µL per 24-well well, 20 µL per 96-well well (250 µL for 12-well) [1]. (5 min)
  10. Rock or shake at room temperature for 15 min (20 min at 200 rpm in the in-house protocol). Extra lysis time does not harm activity [1][3]. (15–20 min)
  11. Use lysate directly, or freeze at −20 °C for up to 1 month (−70 °C long term). Lysates are stable 6 h at 22 °C or 16 h on ice; limit freeze–thaw to 2–3 cycles [1].
  12. Thaw LAR II (Luciferase Assay Substrate in 10 mL Buffer II) and make Stop & Glo®: 1 volume of 50× substrate to 50 volumes of buffer, i.e., 20 µL + 1 mL for 10 assays [1]. (Day 2–3)
  13. Equilibrate LAR II, Stop & Glo® and lysates to room temperature (about 30 min, protected from light); do not vortex LAR II [1][3].
  14. Set the luminometer for two injectors: inject 100 µL LAR II, read firefly with a 2 s delay and 10 s integration; inject 100 µL Stop & Glo®, read Renilla the same way [1].
  15. Dedicate one injector to each reagent and prime both with at least 3 shots. A Stop & Glo®-primed injector later used for LAR II will inhibit firefly signal [1].
  16. Load 20 µL lysate per well into a white 96-well plate (up to 20 µL per reaction) [1]. For a tube luminometer, add lysate to 100 µL LAR II and mix by pipetting 2–3 times; do not vortex [1].
  17. Run the plate. Record firefly (LAR II) and Renilla (Stop & Glo®) values for every well; check for warnings that signal exceeds the detector’s linear range [1]. (5–10 min)
  18. In-house option: per well, inject 75 µL solution A (DTT, CoA, ATP, luciferin in buffer B), then 112 µL solution B (PTC124, coelenterazine h in Renilla salts); 1 s wait, 5 s integration [3].
  19. For the in-house option, load 30 µL lysate in a white plate, spin 30 s, protect solutions from light and equilibrate them 30 min at room temperature [3].
  20. Subtract background: mean of untransfected-lysate readings (5–10 reads) for each channel, taken with the same reagents [1].
  21. Calculate the ratio per well: background-corrected firefly ÷ background-corrected Renilla [2].
  22. Average technical replicates, then express each condition relative to the control (set to 1) to obtain fold change. Average fold changes across independent experiments [2].
  23. Check that the Renilla signal itself does not change across treatments; if it does, the control is not neutral and the ratio is unreliable [1][2].

Pause points

  • After lysis: freeze lysate at −20 °C (up to 1 month) or −70 °C [1].
  • After preparing LAR II and Stop & Glo®: aliquot and freeze (LAR II 1 month at −20 °C; Stop & Glo® 15 days at −20 °C) [1].
  • After transfection: cells can be held 24–48 h before treatment or lysis [4].

Expected results

  • Firefly and Renilla signals well above untransfected background and within the instrument’s linear range [1].
  • Renilla counts that are similar across conditions (a stable control), so ratios reflect reporter activity rather than transfection differences [2].
  • Replicate ratios with lower variation than raw firefly values alone; report fold change versus control, mean ± SD of at least three independent experiments [2].
  • Residual firefly signal after Stop & Glo® should be negligible (quench at least 10⁵-fold) [1].

Controls

  • Untransfected cells: background for both channels [1]
  • Promoterless or empty firefly vector: basal reporter activity
  • Positive control: a known strong promoter or inducer, scaled to 1 and the negative control to 0 for relative response [2]
  • Renilla-only transfection: checks cross-reading of firefly into the Renilla channel
  • Several Renilla promoters or doses, tested in a pilot, to confirm the control does not alter the experimental reporter [1][2]

Troubleshooting

ProblemLikely causeSolution
Low firefly signalPoor transfection, low DNA quality, or LAR II contaminated with Stop & Glo® in the injectorUse endotoxin-free DNA; dedicate and clean injectors (water, 70% ethanol soak, water) [1]
Renilla signal too high or too lowControl plasmid dose or promoter not matched to the cellsTitrate control (1:20–1:50 or lower); use TK rather than CMV when a strong promoter squelches [1][2]
Firefly activity falls when control is addedTrans effects or squelching from a strong control promoter or high doseReduce control DNA or weaken its promoter; run a no-control comparison [1][2]
High well-to-well variabilityPipetting, uneven confluence, edge effectsUse master mixes; normalize to Renilla; avoid outer wells; pipette lysate consistently [2]
High background or cross talkClear or translucent plates, static, light exposureUse opaque white plates; shield tubes from light and static [1]
Signal exceeds linear rangeVery strong expression or too much lysateDilute lysate in 1× PLB or lower input DNA; shorten integration if validated [1]
Incomplete Renilla quench or lysisPoor mixing, overgrown cellsMix thoroughly; add more PLB or lyse longer [1]

Safety and waste

  • Wear gloves, lab coat and eye protection; handle transfected cells at the biosafety level required by your institution (BSL-2 for HEK293T in many sites).
  • Dispose of Stop & Glo®, LAR II and in-house reagents as chemical waste per local rules; check each SDS [1].
  • Coelenterazine and PTC124 stocks are made in ethanol and DMSO; ethanol is flammable and DMSO carries solutes through skin, so wear gloves [3].
  • Lipofectamine and DNA waste should be treated as biohazardous waste.
