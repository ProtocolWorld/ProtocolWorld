C. elegans CRISPR Genome Editing

About 2–3 weeks · 2026-11-22

Edit a C. elegans gene by injecting preassembled Cas9–guide RNA complexes and a short single-stranded repair template into the gonad, using dpy-10 co-conversion to enrich edited F1 progeny, then genotype and homozygose the edit.

⬇ Word (.docx)⬇ PowerPoint (.pptx)

Purpose

To introduce a precise point mutation, small insertion, or deletion at a chosen locus in the C. elegans genome by gonad microinjection of Cas9 ribonucleoprotein (RNP) complexes and a single-stranded oligodeoxynucleotide (ssODN) repair template, and to recover a homozygous, sequence-verified line.

Principle

Cas9 protein loaded with a tracrRNA:crRNA guide cuts the genome next to an NGG PAM; the break is repaired from a co-injected repair template that carries about 35 nt of homology on each side of the edit [1][2]. Injecting preassembled RNPs avoids the need to express Cas9 from a plasmid and works well with short ssODN donors [2][3]. Because only a small fraction of F1 animals carry the desired edit, a second guide and ssODN that convert dpy-10 to the dominant cn64 allele are co-injected: F1 animals with a Rol (or Dpy) phenotype have been through an editing event and are enriched for edits at the target locus [1][2][5]. Edited candidates are identified by single-worm PCR (size shift or restriction digest) and confirmed by Sanger sequencing [1]. Efficiency varies widely between loci (about 2–70% of screened Rol F1s in one protocol) [1], and Cas9 concentration should be kept moderate because high RNP doses reduced the number of Rollers in a titration [3].

Equipment

  • Dissecting stereomicroscope
  • Microinjection rig (inverted DIC microscope, micromanipulator, pressure injector)
  • Needle puller
  • 37 °C heat block or incubator
  • Refrigerated microcentrifuge (up to 17,000 × g)
  • Thermal cycler
  • Gel electrophoresis system and imager
  • 20 °C and 25 °C incubators
  • Worm pick

Procedure

  1. Design the edit: choose an NGG PAM within about 10 bp of the intended change, order the crRNA, and design a ssODN with about 35 nt homology on each side; mutate the PAM or seed in the template [1][2]. (1–2 days (ordering))
  2. Design flanking genotyping primers and, for edits under 50 bp, a restriction site gained or lost by the edit to allow digest-based screening [1].
  3. Resuspend lyophilized crRNAs, tracrRNA, and oligos as listed in Materials; spin crRNA tubes briefly (17,000 × g, 1 min) before opening. Store RNAs at −80 °C [1].
  4. At least one day before injection, pick L2–L3 larvae onto fresh OP50 plates and grow at 20 °C so that healthy young adults are available [1]. (Overnight)
  5. On ice, combine the injection mix in the order given in Materials (20 µL total); mix by pipetting. A 5 µL scaled mix is also workable [1].
  6. Assemble RNPs by incubating the mix at 37 °C for 15 min [1][4].
  7. Centrifuge the mix at 17,000 × g for 5 min at 4 °C to pellet debris that could clog needles; keep on ice and load the needle from the top of the supernatant [1]. (5 min)
  8. Pause point: the mix can be kept at 4 °C and reused for repeat sessions [1].
  9. Mount young adults with fewer than about 10 embryos in the uterus on an agarose pad under halocarbon oil [1].
  10. Inject the mix into the gonad, aiming for the distal syncytial germline core; inject both arms where possible. Inject about 30 worms per experiment [1][4]. (1–2 h)
  11. Move each worm to a 60 mm OP50 plate and recover about 1 h at room temperature [1].
  12. Transfer each injected P0 to its own 35 mm plate; after 24 h move it to a fresh plate so that early and later broods can be screened separately [1]. (24 h)
  13. Incubate plates at 20 °C (some labs use 25 °C after injection to boost edit rates) [1][5]. (3–4 days)
  14. Identify plates with the most Rol or Dpy F1s under the dissecting scope. Single 50–100 F1 Rollers (one per plate) from the best plates and let them lay eggs for 1–2 days [1][3].
  15. Lyse each F1 mother in 2.5 µL lysis buffer plus proteinase K: freeze at −30 °C for 20 min, 60 °C for 1 h, 95 °C for 15 min, then hold at 4 °C [1].
  16. Set up PCR: 2.5 µL lysate, 12.5 µL 2× mix, 1 µL of each 10 µM primer, water to 22.5 µL. Cycle: 95 °C 1 min; 35 × (95 °C 15 s, 55 °C 15 s, 72 °C 1 min); hold 4 °C [1]. (About 2.5 h)
  17. Screen: for edits under 50 bp, digest 10 µL of PCR product with 2–4 units of enzyme at 37 °C for 2 h and run on a 1–2% agarose gel; larger edits are read as a size shift [1].
  18. From F1s with a candidate edit, pick 8–12 non-Rol wild-type-looking F2s to individual plates; about 25% should be homozygous for a non-lethal edit [1]. (3–4 days)
  19. Lyse and PCR the F2 mothers as above to find homozygotes. Confirm edits by Sanger sequencing (at least three PCR reactions per line; sequencing primer at least 50 bases from the edit) [1].
  20. Outcross the verified line to wild type at least twice to remove the dpy-10(cn64) allele and off-target changes; keep frozen stocks.

Pause points

  • Design the edit: choose an NGG PAM within about 10 bp of the intended change, order the crRNA, and design a ssODN with about 35 nt homology on each side; mutate the PAM or seed in the template [1][2] (1–2 days (ordering)).
  • At least one day before injection, pick L2–L3 larvae onto fresh OP50 plates and grow at 20 °C so that healthy young adults are available [1] (Overnight).
  • Incubate plates at 20 °C (some labs use 25 °C after injection to boost edit rates) [1][5] (3–4 days).
  • From F1s with a candidate edit, pick 8–12 non-Rol wild-type-looking F2s to individual plates; about 25% should be homozygous for a non-lethal edit [1] (3–4 days).

Expected results

  • Plates from successful injections show Rol and Dpy F1 progeny 3–4 days after injection; efficiency of target edits among Rollers ranges widely (about 2–70% reported) [1].
  • Edited F1s show a shifted band or an altered digest pattern; homozygous F2s show only the edited pattern, and sequencing confirms the intended change without extra mutations.

Controls

  • Wild-type genomic DNA PCR (and digest) as the unedited reference
  • Injection mix with dpy-10 crRNA and ssODN alone to verify that Cas9 RNPs are active (Rol and Dpy progeny appear)
  • No-template water PCR control
  • Unlysed worm-free lysis buffer blank

Troubleshooting

ProblemLikely causeSolution
Few or no Rol/Dpy F1sInactive Cas9 or degraded RNA, poor injection into the gonad, unhealthy wormsUse fresh aliquots, keep RNA at −80 °C, inject young adults into the germline core, confirm worms are well fed
Many Rollers but no target editsPoor target guide or template designTest a second crRNA near the edit, mutate PAM or seed in the template, keep homology arms at about 35 nt
Needle clogsDebris or precipitate in mixRe-spin at 17,000 × g for 5 min at 4 °C and load from the top
Reduced brood or sick P0Cas9 concentration too high or injection damageLower RNP dose (high doses reduced Roller numbers in one titration) [3]; use a finer needle
PCR fails from lysatesProteinase K not inactivated or too much lysateMake sure the 95 °C step is complete; use only 2.5 µL of lysate
Homozygotes not foundEdit is lethal or sterile when homozygousMaintain as a heterozygote or use a balancer

Safety and waste

  • Wear gloves and eye protection when handling halocarbon oil and DNA stains; follow your institution's rules for chemical hazards.
  • Use sharp glass needles with care and dispose of them in a sharps container.
  • Wear gloves with proteinase K, NP-40, and DNA stains; follow local rules for recombinant DNA and CRISPR use under institutional biosafety approval.
  • Dispose of worm plates and agar as biological waste; autoclave per local policy.
