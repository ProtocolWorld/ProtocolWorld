Native PAGE

General Lab Methods · 6–8 h · 2026-11-19

Non-denaturing Tris-glycine polyacrylamide gel electrophoresis separates folded proteins and complexes by charge, size and shape. Run cold without SDS, reductant or heat, then stain, blot or assay in the gel.

Native PAGE

Estimated time: 6–8 h | Protocol World

To resolve proteins and protein complexes in their folded, non-denatured state on a Tris-glycine polyacrylamide gel (typically 4–20% gradient or 7.5–12% fixed) so that oligomeric state, complex formation or enzyme activity can be assessed.

Without SDS, reductant or heating, proteins keep their fold and interactions, so mobility depends on net charge at the gel pH, size and shape rather than mass alone [2][4]. In the Tris-glycine system, chloride leads, glycine trails and Tris is the common ion; native gels sit near pH 8.65 and the separation zone operates near pH 9.5 [1]. Proteins whose pI is above the gel pH carry little negative charge and migrate poorly or run toward the cathode; for such proteins, or for membrane complexes, Bis-Tris or Blue Native PAGE (Coomassie dye supplies charge, resolving complexes from under 100 kDa to about 10 MDa) is an alternative [3]. Run cold to protect labile complexes and limit heating [4][5]. Volumes and times are starting points to optimize.

  • Mini vertical gel casting and electrophoresis system with 0.75–1.5 mm spacers
  • Power supply (constant voltage)
  • Cold room or refrigerator at 4 °C, or ice-filled tank insert
  • Gradient mixer (optional, for 4–20% gradients)
  • Refrigerated microcentrifuge
  • Orbital shaker for staining
  • Wet transfer unit with cooling block (for blotting)
  • Gel imager or scanner
  • Assemble clean glass plates and spacers in the casting stand. Pre-chill running buffer to 4 °C for later [4]. (10 min)
  • Mix resolving gel, for example 7.5%: 10 mL 30% acrylamide, 10 mL 1.5 M Tris-HCl pH 8.8, water to 40 mL, then 400 µL 10% APS and 32 µL TEMED [2]. (10 min)
  • Pour the resolving gel to about 1 cm below the comb and overlay with isopropanol or water for a flat, smooth interface [4]. (5 min)
  • Polymerize, then pour off the overlay, rinse with water and dry the top with filter paper [4]. (45–60 min)
  • Prepare a 4–4.5% stacking gel (about 1.5 mL 30% acrylamide, Tris buffer, water, APS, TEMED per 10 mL), pour, insert the comb and let it set [2][4]. (45–60 min)
  • Remove the comb gently and rinse the wells with running buffer. Optionally pre-run the gel in cold 1× running buffer until the current is stable [2][4]. (15 min)
  • Spin samples at full speed for 1 min to remove aggregates. Mix with native sample buffer; do not add SDS or reductant and do not heat [1][2][5]. (5 min)
  • Load 5 µg purified protein or 30–50 µg lysate per lane, with a native marker lane. Fill unused wells with blank sample buffer [2][5].
  • Fill the upper and lower chambers with cold 1× running buffer so the wells and gel are fully covered, and remove trapped bubbles [1][4].
  • Run at 4 °C (cold room or ice) at constant 100–150 V; about 120 V for 2 h is typical for a mini gel [1][2][5]. (2–4 h)
  • Watch current and temperature. Expect roughly 6–12 mA falling to 3–6 mA per gel at 125 V; larger complexes may need slower, longer runs [1][4].
  • Stop when the dye front nears the bottom or the standards have resolved, then rinse the gel briefly in ddH₂O [2]. (5 min)
  • For Coomassie, fix and stain with Coomassie R-250 (detects about 40 ng per band), then destain until bands are clear [4]. (2 h to overnight)
  • For in-gel activity staining, incubate the unfixed gel in substrate or cofactor solution suited to your enzyme and stop when bands appear (enzyme-specific; optimize).
  • For western blotting, equilibrate the gel and PVDF in transfer buffer (no methanol, 0.04% SDS) and transfer 1 h at 100 V with cooling, or 16 h at 30 V [2]. (1–16 h)
  • Wash the PVDF membrane for 20 min in transfer buffer with 20% methanol to remove SDS, then block and probe in detergent-free blocking buffer [2]. (20 min)
  • Document the gel, annotate complex sizes against the native standards and discard gels and waste according to local rules.
  • For Coomassie, fix and stain with Coomassie R-250 (detects about 40 ng per band), then destain until bands are clear [4] (2 h to overnight).
  • Discrete bands at positions set by charge, size and shape, not by a simple mass ladder; apparent masses from native standards are estimates only.
  • Multiple bands or a ladder for a protein can indicate monomer, dimer and higher oligomers or complexes.
  • Sharp bands with minimal smear when samples are fresh, low in salt and run cold.
  • Activity stain or antibody signal at the same position as the Coomassie band confirms the active or specific complex.
  • Native molecular weight standards (e.g., thyroglobulin, ferritin, BSA) in a separate lane
  • Known monomer and known oligomer protein (e.g., BSA monomer and dimer) as mobility references
  • Same sample run on SDS-PAGE (reducing) to check subunit composition and purity
  • Buffer-only (blank sample buffer) lane to detect dye or buffer artifacts
  • For activity staining: heat-inactivated or inhibitor-treated sample as negative control

  • Acrylamide monomer is a neurotoxin and a likely carcinogen; wear nitrile gloves, goggles and a lab coat, and avoid powder or unpolymerized solution contact [4].
  • TEMED is flammable and corrosive and APS is an oxidizer and irritant; handle in a ventilated area.
  • Electrophoresis uses hazardous voltage: keep the lid on, power off before touching electrodes and keep buffer away from the power supply [4].
  • Methanol and Coomassie destain are flammable and toxic.
  • Collect unpolymerized acrylamide solutions and stain waste as hazardous chemical waste; fully polymerized gels follow local solid-waste rules [4].
