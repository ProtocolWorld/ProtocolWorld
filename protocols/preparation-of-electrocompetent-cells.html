Preparation of Electrocompetent Cells

Estimated time: About 6 hours + overnight culture | Protocol World

To prepare electrocompetent E. coli (for example DH10B, DH5α or TOP10) in a 100 mL batch that yields about 20 aliquots of 50 µL, ready for plasmid transformation by electroporation or for storage at −80 °C.

Electroporation briefly permeabilizes the cell envelope with a strong electric field so that DNA can enter. Cells must be harvested in early to mid-exponential growth (OD600 about 0.4–0.6), because efficiency falls sharply once cultures leave exponential phase [1][2]. Repeated cold washes in 10% glycerol remove the ions of the growth medium, which would otherwise cause arcing during the pulse [1]. Keeping cells, tubes and solutions at about 4 °C after harvest preserves viability and efficiency [2].

  • Shaking incubator at 37 °C
  • Spectrophotometer and cuvettes for OD600
  • Refrigerated centrifuge (4 °C) with 50 mL and 15 mL tube rotors
  • Ice-water bath and ice bucket
  • Pre-chilled sterile 50 mL and 15 mL conical tubes
  • Pre-chilled 0.5 mL or 1.7 mL microcentrifuge tubes
  • −80 °C freezer (liquid nitrogen for snap-freezing is optional)
  • Electroporator (for example Bio-Rad MicroPulser)
  • Day 1: inoculate 3–5 mL LB with a single colony and grow overnight at 37 °C with shaking [1][2]. (overnight)
  • Day 2: set the centrifuge to 4 °C about 30 min before use and chill tubes, 10% glycerol and (optionally) water on ice [1]. (30 min)
  • Inoculate 100 mL fresh LB in a 500 mL flask with the overnight culture to a starting OD600 of about 0.05 [1]. (5 min)
  • Grow at 37 °C with vigorous shaking, checking OD600 every 20–30 min, until it reaches 0.4–0.6 (about 2–3 h). Do not let the culture go past 0.6 [1][2]. (2–3 h)
  • Transfer the flask to an ice-water bath and chill for 15–30 min, swirling now and then. From here on keep everything at 4 °C or on ice [1][2]. (15–30 min)
  • Split the culture into two pre-chilled 50 mL tubes and pellet the cells (6,000 rpm for 5 min at 4 °C in [1]; check the equivalent × g for your rotor) [1]. (5 min)
  • Pour off the supernatant and resuspend each pellet in 15 mL ice-cold 10% glycerol by swirling or gentle vortexing; move to chilled 15 mL tubes [1]. (5 min)
  • Pellet again (5 min, 4 °C), decant, and resuspend in 15 mL ice-cold 10% glycerol. Repeat until you have done four washes in total [1]. (3 × 10 min)
  • Remove every trace of liquid from the last pellet with a pipette tip; leftover salts cause arcing [1]. (2 min)
  • Resuspend each pellet in about 500 µL ice-cold 10% glycerol, a roughly 100-fold concentration of the starting culture [1]. (3 min)
  • Dispense 30–50 µL aliquots into pre-chilled tubes on ice, then freeze promptly at −80 °C (snap-freezing in liquid nitrogen is also used) [1][2]. (10 min)
  • Test one aliquot: thaw on ice, mix with about 100 pg pUC19 and pulse (see Electroporation settings below) to estimate efficiency [5]. (1 h)
  • Electroporation: mix 1–3 µL DNA (under 100 ng) with 30–50 µL cells in a pre-chilled tube and leave on ice for 1–10 min [1]. (5 min)
  • Transfer to a chilled 0.1 cm cuvette, wipe off condensation, tap to the bottom, and pulse at 1.8 kV (about 18 kV/cm). For a 0.2 cm cuvette use 2.5 kV (12.5 kV/cm) [1]. (1 min)
  • Add 500–1,000 µL pre-warmed SOC or LB immediately, move to a 1.5 mL tube and shake at 37 °C for 30–60 min with the tube on its side [1][5]. (30–60 min)
  • Plate about 50 µL on pre-warmed selective plates and incubate overnight at 37 °C [1]. (overnight)
  • After the last wash and aliquoting, the cells can be stored at −80 °C for months; they do not need to be used the same day [1][2].
  • The overnight culture on day 1 is the natural stopping point before the main preparation.
  • Clear, white cell suspension with no visible clumps at the final resuspension, at roughly 100 times the cell density of the starting culture [1].
  • Many colonies from the pUC19 test transformation and none on a no-DNA control plate; no sources in this protocol quote a benchmark, so record your own CFU per µg and compare it with your strain's specification.
  • An electroporation pulse that completes without arcing (no loud crack, and a time constant reported by the instrument).
  • No-DNA control: cells plus pulse only, plated on the selective plate (checks for contamination and background growth)
  • Positive control: about 100 pg pUC19 to calculate CFU per µg [5]
  • Unpulsed cells plated on non-selective LB to check viability and sterility
  • Optional arcing test: pulse 40 µL of cells with test DNA before freezing a large batch [2][5]

  • Wear gloves and a lab coat. E. coli K-12 derivatives such as DH5α, DH10B and TOP10 are generally handled at biosafety level 1; follow your institution's rules.
  • Electroporators deliver high voltage: keep hands away from the cuvette chamber while the pulse is applied and use only the manufacturer's cuvettes.
  • Autoclave or disinfect bacterial cultures, tubes and plates before disposal.
  • If using liquid nitrogen for snap-freezing, wear cryo gloves and a face shield.
