Bacteriophage Lysate Preparation

2 days (plate lysate) or ≈ 1 day (liquid lysate) · 2026-11-27

Prepare a high-titer, cell-free bacteriophage lysate from E. coli by plate (web-lysis) or liquid-culture amplification: buffers, plating density, clarification, filtration, storage, titer check and troubleshooting.

⬇ Word (.docx)⬇ PowerPoint (.pptx)

Purpose

To produce a cell-free stock of an E. coli bacteriophage with a titer of about 10⁹–10¹¹ plaque-forming units (PFU)/mL for infections, P1 transduction or DNA extraction.

Principle

In a plate lysate, phage and host are mixed in soft agar at a density that gives nearly confluent lysis (a lacy web); the phage are then eluted into buffer. In a liquid lysate, early-log cells are infected with a small inoculum and phage amplify over several cycles until the culture clears [2][3][4]. Divalent cations (Ca²⁺, Mg²⁺) help adsorption; chloroform kills surviving bacteria; clarification and 0.22 µm filtration remove debris [1][2][3]. Chloroform can inactivate lipid-containing phages and reduce titer on long storage [2][3].

Materials and reagents

Equipment

  • 37 °C (or phage-optimal 30 °C) incubator and shaker at ≈ 200 rpm
  • 50–56 °C water bath or heat block
  • Refrigerated centrifuge (≥ 10,000 × g)
  • Fume hood
  • Spectrophotometer (OD₆₀₀)
  • Biosafety cabinet or clean bench
  • 4 °C storage

Procedure

  1. Plate lysate, day 1: make 10-fold dilutions of the phage in SM buffer. Aim for nearly confluent lysis, about 10⁴–10⁵ PFU per plate; overestimating is safer [1][5]. (20 min)
  2. Mix 250 µL overnight host and 10 µL phage dilution (with 10 mM CaCl₂ if needed); include a no-phage control with 10 µL SM. Adsorb 10–20 min at room temperature or 37 °C [1][3]. (20 min)
  3. Add 3–4 mL molten top agar (50–56 °C) to each tube, mix gently, pour onto a pre-warmed LB plate immediately, tilt to spread, and let it set 5 min [1][2][5].
  4. Incubate overnight at the phage's preferred temperature (30–37 °C), top agar upright or inverted as the lab protocol allows [1][2][3]. (12–16 h)
  5. Day 2: pick plates showing near-confluent lysis, record the dilution, and flood each with 4–6 mL SM buffer. Keep the top agar intact where possible [1][2][5].
  6. Let phage diffuse 15 min with gentle shaking at room temperature (or ≥ 1 h to overnight at 4 °C), then collect the liquid and loosened top agar into a tube [1][2].
  7. Optional: add a few drops of chloroform (about 0.1 volume), vortex 30 s, and rest 10 min in a fume hood [2][3]. (10 min)
  8. Clarify by centrifuging 5,000–10,000 × g for 5–10 min. Decant the supernatant, leaving the chloroform and pellet behind [1][3][5]. (10 min)
  9. Filter the supernatant through a new 0.22 µm filter into a sterile tube; store at 4 °C and titer by plaque assay [1][2][5].
  10. Liquid lysate: dilute an overnight culture 1:100 into LB with 0.2% glucose and 5 mM CaCl₂ (or 1 mM CaCl₂/MgCl₂); grow 30–60 min to OD₆₀₀ ≈ 0.2 [2][3][4]. (30–60 min)
  11. Keep an uninfected control culture. Add about 10⁶ PFU (or 100 µL of ≥ 10⁸–10⁹ PFU/mL stock for a 5–100 mL culture) and shake at ≈ 200 rpm [2][3][4]. (3–5 h)
  12. When the culture clears and shows clumpy debris (control stays turbid), add 50–100 µL chloroform per 10 mL and mix [3][4].
  13. Centrifuge 4,000–9,000 × g for 10–20 min at 4 °C; filter the supernatant (0.22 µm) and store at 4 °C without chloroform [2][3][4].
  14. Titer: spot or plate serial 10-fold dilutions; PFU/mL = plaques ÷ volume plated (mL) × dilution factor [2][3].

Pause points

  • Eluted plates can sit in SM buffer overnight at 4 °C before collection [1].
  • Finished lysates keep at 4 °C; re-titer after storage.

Expected results

  • Plate lysate typically about 10¹⁰ PFU/mL (sequencing-grade preps from one plate reach 10⁶–10⁸ in [1]) [5].
  • P1 lysates should reach ≥ 10⁹ PFU/mL [3].
  • Liquid T4 lysates 10¹⁰–10¹² PFU/mL; T5/T6 10⁷–10⁹ [4].
  • Clear, cell-free lysate with no colonies on a sterility plate.

Controls

  • No-phage plate or culture (lawn or turbidity control)
  • Phage-only and cells-only plates to confirm no surviving bacteria [3]
  • Sterility check: plate a drop of final lysate on LB
  • Titer before and after storage

Troubleshooting

ProblemLikely causeSolution

Plate not confluent or cleared too earlyWrong phage densityUse a dilution series and choose the best plate
Top agar sets in the tubePouring too slowlyAdd top agar to one tube at a time and pour at once [1]

Low titer (< 10⁹ for P1)Old or weak inoculumStart from a fresh plaque or high-titer stock

Safety and waste

  • Chloroform: use only in a fume hood with gloves, lab coat and eye protection; collect as halogenated waste.
  • E. coli K-12/B hosts are typically BSL-1; follow your institution's rules.
  • Decontaminate phage-containing liquids, plates and tips with fresh 10% bleach for 10–30 min or autoclave.
  • Prevent phage contamination of host cultures: use filter tips and separate bench areas; wipe surfaces with 70% ethanol or bleach.
