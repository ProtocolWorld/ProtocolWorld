Antibiotic Susceptibility Testing by Disk Diffusion

E. coli & Bacteria · 1 h + overnight · 2026-11-22

Standardized Kirby-Bauer disk diffusion on Mueller-Hinton agar: prepare a 0.5 McFarland inoculum, swab a uniform lawn, apply antibiotic disks, incubate at 35 °C, measure inhibition zones and interpret them against CLSI or EUCAST breakpoints with E. coli ATCC 25922 quality control.

Antibiotic Susceptibility Testing by Disk Diffusion

Estimated time: 1 h + overnight | Protocol World

To determine whether a bacterial isolate (here E. coli) is susceptible, intermediate or resistant to antibiotics by measuring zones of growth inhibition around antibiotic-impregnated paper disks on Mueller-Hinton agar (Kirby-Bauer method).

Each disk releases its antibiotic into the agar, creating a concentration gradient. A standardized lawn of bacteria grows except where the drug exceeds the inhibitory concentration, leaving a clear zone whose diameter is read in millimeters [1][2]. Because zone size depends on inoculum density, agar depth and pH, incubation and disk potency, every one of these variables must be standardized, and a reference strain of known susceptibility is run alongside the test isolate [1][2][3]. Zone diameters are converted to susceptible (S), intermediate (I) or resistant (R) categories using the current breakpoint tables of CLSI (M100) or EUCAST; the breakpoints themselves change yearly and are not reproduced here [2][3].

ItemDetails
Mueller-Hinton agar (MHA) plates4 ± 0.5 mm deep (about 25–31 mL in a 100 mm plate, about 60–71 mL in a 150 mm plate); pH 7.2–7.4 after gelling; dried and at room temperature before use [1][2]
Test isolateFresh (18–24 h) isolated colonies on non-selective agar, for example E. coli grown overnight on blood or nutrient agar [1][3]
Quality-control strainsE. coli ATCC 25922 (always); S. aureus ATCC 25923 or ATCC 29213 and P. aeruginosa ATCC 27853 as needed for the drugs tested [1][2]
Sterile 0.85% saline2 mL per tube for the suspension (sterile saline or broth)
0.5 McFarland standardBaSO₄ turbidity standard; absorbance at 625 nm of 0.08–0.13 (1 cm path); vortex before use [1][2]
Antibiotic disksCommercial disks at the labeled potency, stored sealed with desiccant, protected from light; let the cartridge reach room temperature before opening [2][3]
Sterile cotton swabsOne per isolate
Forceps and 70% ethanol, or disk dispenserWipe forceps with alcohol between disks, or use a dispenser or template [1][3]
Ruler or caliperReads to the nearest millimeter [1][2]
  • Biosafety cabinet or clean bench
  • 35 °C incubator (air, no CO₂)
  • Spectrophotometer or densitometer (optional; calibrated to 0.5 McFarland)
  • Vortex mixer
  • Dark non-reflective surface for reading plates
  • Biohazard waste container and autoclave
  • Subculture the isolate and the QC strain onto non-selective agar the day before so that 18–24 h colonies are available [1][3]. (Day 1)
  • Bring MHA plates and the disk cartridges to room temperature. If the agar surface is wet, dry the plates with lids ajar for 10–30 min [1][2][3]. (30 min)
  • Touch 4–5 well-isolated colonies with a loop and suspend them in 2 mL of sterile saline. Vortex to make a smooth suspension [1]. (3 min)
  • Adjust the turbidity to 0.5 McFarland (about 1–2 × 10⁸ CFU/mL for E. coli): add colonies if too light, saline if too heavy. Check by eye against a card or at A625 = 0.08–0.13 [1][2]. (5 min)
  • Use the suspension within 15 min (never longer than 60 min) [2]. (≤ 15 min)
  • Dip a sterile swab in the suspension and press and rotate it against the inside of the tube above the liquid to remove excess fluid [1][2][3]. (1 min)
  • Swab the entire MHA surface evenly in three directions, turning the plate about 60° each time, then run the swab around the rim. Leave no gaps [1][3]. (2 min)
  • Leave the lid slightly ajar for 3–5 min (no more than 15 min) so the surface dries before disks are applied [1][3]. (3–5 min)
  • Place disks with alcohol-wiped forceps or a dispenser, at least 24 mm apart center to center and off the edge. Maximum 5 per 100 mm plate, 12 per 150 mm plate [1][2]. (5 min)
  • Press each disk gently for full contact with the agar and do not move it afterward. Apply all disks within 15 min of inoculating the plate [1][2][3]. (2 min)
  • Invert the plates and incubate within 15 min of disk application at 35 ± 2 °C in air for 16–20 h (EUCAST specifies 18 ± 2 h). Stack no more than 5 plates [1][2][3]. (16–20 h)
  • Do not use CO₂, which lowers the agar pH. Some drug–organism pairs need a full 24 h, so follow the current CLSI or EUCAST table [1][2]. (as needed)
  • Read plates from the back over a black non-reflective surface in reflected light. Measure each zone to the nearest millimeter, including the disk diameter [1][3]. (15 min)
  • If a zone edge is unclear, measure from the disk center to the point of complete inhibition and double that radius [1]. (as needed)
  • Ignore the thin veil of Proteus swarming and, for trimethoprim and sulfonamides, faint growth up to about 20% [1][2]. (as needed)
  • Check the QC strain zones against the acceptable ranges in the current CLSI M100 or EUCAST QC tables before accepting any result [2][3]. (10 min)
  • Convert the test zones into S, I or R using the current CLSI M100 or EUCAST breakpoint table and record the standard and edition used [2][3]. (10 min)
  • Autoclave all plates, swabs and tubes before disposal [1][3]. (Day 2)
  • Plan the work so that colonies are 18–24 h old when the suspension is made [1][3].
  • After step 8 the plates must not wait: disks must go on within 15 min of inoculation and incubation must start within 15 min of disk application [2][3].
  • Read plates at the end of the recommended incubation time; do not extend incubation beyond the limits of the standard [2].
  • A uniform, near-confluent lawn of growth with circular inhibition zones around the disks of active drugs.
  • Zone diameters (mm) are recorded per drug and classified S, I or R using the current breakpoint table.
  • The E. coli ATCC 25922 zones fall within the published range (for example, ciprofloxacin 5 µg about 30–40 mm in the Omani SOP, which cites CLSI) [3]. Always check the current M100 table.
  • E. coli ATCC 25922 on every batch of plates and disks, tested each day of testing (or on a reduced schedule once the lab has demonstrated stable performance) [2][3].
  • S. aureus ATCC 25923 or 29213 and P. aeruginosa ATCC 27853 for drugs where they are the designated QC strains [1][2].
  • Run each new lot of MHA and disks against the QC strains before routine use [1][2].

Isolated colonies instead of a lawnInoculum too light or uneven swabbingRepeat with a 0.5 McFarland suspension and swab in three directions [1][2]
Zones too smallInoculum too heavy, agar too deep or disk potency lostStandardize turbidity, check 4 mm depth, use in-date disks stored dry and cold [1][2]
Zones too largeAgar too thin, inoculum too light or stacks too highPour plates to 4 mm and limit stacks to 5 [1][2][3]
QC zone out of rangeWrong disk, wrong medium, pH, cations or thymidine in the batchLook for the error, retest, check MHA and disk lot, then follow CLSI/EUCAST corrective action [1][3]
Fuzzy zone edges or hazeMoisture on the surface or old disksDry plates properly and use fresh disks [2]
Colonies inside a zoneMixed culture or resistant mutantsCheck purity and repeat; report according to the standard [1][2]
  • Treat the isolate as a potential pathogen; work in a biosafety cabinet or on a clean bench with gloves and a lab coat. E. coli ATCC 25922 is a biosafety level 1 strain, but clinical isolates need higher containment [1][3].
  • Wipe forceps with 70% ethanol; ethanol is flammable, so keep it away from open flames when flaming forceps.
  • Autoclave all contaminated plates, swabs and tubes before disposal; never discard live cultures in the regular trash.
