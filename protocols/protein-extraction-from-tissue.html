Protein Extraction from Tissue

About 3 h · 2026-11-15

Homogenize frozen tissue in ice-cold RIPA buffer with protease and phosphatase inhibitors, clarify, quantify by BCA, and prepare samples for Western blotting.

⬇ Word (.docx)⬇ PowerPoint (.pptx)

Purpose

To prepare a clarified total-protein lysate from animal tissue (liver, muscle, brain, adipose) for protein assays, Western blotting and other downstream analysis.

Principle

Tissue is disrupted mechanically in an ice-cold RIPA buffer, whose non-ionic and ionic detergents solubilize cellular and many membrane proteins. Protease and phosphatase inhibitors preserve protein integrity and phosphorylation. Centrifugation pellets insoluble debris, and the supernatant is quantified and denatured for SDS-PAGE [1][2][3][4].

Materials and reagents

ItemDetails
RIPA lysis buffer50 mM Tris-HCl pH 7.4, 150 mM NaCl, 1% Triton X-100 or NP-40, 0.5% sodium deoxycholate, 0.1% SDS, 1 mM EDTA, 10 mM NaF [3]
Protease inhibitor cocktailAdded fresh, for example 1:2000 of a commercial cocktail, or PMSF to 1 mM [2][3]
Phosphatase inhibitor cocktail1:2000 when phosphoproteins will be analyzed [2][3]
Tissue20–50 mg, snap-frozen in liquid nitrogen and stored at −80 °C [1]
Ice-cold PBS137 mM NaCl, 2.7 mM KCl, 10 mM Na₂HPO₄, 1.8 mM KH₂PO₄, pH 7.4 [3]
BCA assay kit and BSA standardsFor quantification [2][4]
2× Laemmli sample buffer125 mM Tris-HCl, 4% SDS, 20% glycerol, 10% 2-mercaptoethanol, 0.004% bromophenol blue [4]
Dry ice and glass plateFor cutting frozen tissue [1]

Equipment

  • Bead mill (for example TissueLyser) with stainless-steel beads, or a rotor-stator homogenizer
  • Refrigerated microcentrifuge at 4 °C
  • Ice bucket
  • Heat block at 95 °C
  • Plate reader (562 nm) for BCA
  • −80 °C freezer

Procedure

  1. Pre-cool the centrifuge to 4 °C. Add inhibitors to RIPA just before use and keep the buffer on ice [1][2]. (10 min)
  2. Cut frozen tissue on a glass plate on dry ice, weigh 20–50 mg into a pre-chilled round-bottom tube, and return the rest to −80 °C [1]. (10 min)
  3. Add ice-cold RIPA: 20 µL per mg for liver or muscle, or 2–3 µL per mg for adipose tissue [1]. A range of 50–60 µL per mg is also used for firm tissues [3][4]. (2 min)
  4. Add one stainless-steel bead and homogenize: 3 min at 25 Hz for liver and fat, 5 min at 50 Hz for muscle [1]. With a rotor-stator, use short bursts on ice. (5 min)
  5. Incubate on ice for 30 min with occasional mixing; some protocols agitate for up to 2 h at 4 °C [3][4]. (30–120 min)
  6. Centrifuge at 10,000–16,000 × g for 10–20 min at 4 °C [1][3][4]. (20 min)
  7. Move the supernatant to a clean tube, avoiding the pellet; for adipose tissue take the middle layer beneath the fat cake and re-spin if cloudy [1]. (5 min)
  8. Measure protein with a BCA assay: 5 µL sample plus BSA standards, 200 µL working reagent, 25 min at 37 °C, read at 562 nm [2]. (45 min)
  9. Equalize protein among samples with RIPA (for example 2 mg/mL), then mix 1:1 with 2× Laemmli buffer [1][3]. (10 min)
  10. Heat 5 min at 95 °C (one lab uses 85 °C for 2 min), spin briefly, and load or freeze [1][3]. (10 min)
  11. Snap-freeze unused lysate and gel samples in small aliquots and store at −80 °C; avoid repeated freeze–thaw cycles [1][2]. (5 min)

Pause points

  • After step 7: clarified lysate can be snap-frozen and stored at −80 °C.
  • After step 10: SDS-PAGE samples can be kept at −20 °C or −80 °C until loading [2].

Expected results

  • Typical protein concentrations are 1–5 mg/mL in the clarified lysate [4].
  • A linear BCA standard curve covering the sample readings.
  • On a Coomassie-stained gel or Western blot, a smooth lane without smearing or loss of high-molecular-weight bands.

Controls

  • A loading control antibody such as β-actin, GAPDH or tubulin for Western blots.
  • A lysis buffer blank in the protein assay.
  • For phosphoproteins, compare treated and untreated tissue prepared with identical inhibitors [2].

Troubleshooting

ProblemLikely causeSolution
Low yieldIncomplete homogenization or too little bufferIncrease buffer volume, extend homogenization or add sonication [3][4]
Degraded proteins or laddersWarm tissue or missing inhibitorsKeep everything on ice and add fresh protease and phosphatase inhibitors [2][3]
Fat layer or cloudy lysateLipid-rich tissueRe-spin, and take the clear phase below the lipid cake [1]
Membrane proteins missingRIPA may not solubilize themUse hot 1% SDS lysis instead of RIPA [4]
Phosphoprotein signal lostPhosphatase activityAdd phosphatase inhibitors and extract promptly [2][3]

Safety and waste

  • Liquid nitrogen and dry ice cause cold burns: wear cryo gloves and eye protection.
  • 2-mercaptoethanol and SDS are hazardous; open Laemmli buffer in a fume hood and wear gloves.
  • PMSF is toxic and unstable in water; handle with gloves.
  • Treat animal tissue as potentially biohazardous; dispose of tissue as biological waste and buffers as chemical waste per local rules.
