Blocking and Antibody Incubation for Western Blots

≈ 5–6 h, or overnight · 2026-11-26

Block a western blot membrane with 5% milk or BSA in TBST, incubate primary and HRP or fluorescent secondary antibodies, wash, and detect, with troubleshooting for background and weak signal.

⬇ Word (.docx)⬇ PowerPoint (.pptx)

Purpose

To block a protein-transfer membrane and probe it with a specific primary antibody and a labeled secondary antibody, giving clean, quantifiable bands for the target protein.

Principle

After electrotransfer, the membrane still has free protein-binding sites. A blocking protein (non-fat milk or BSA) occupies these sites so that antibodies bind only through their epitope [1][4]. The primary antibody binds the target; a secondary antibody against the primary's host species, conjugated to HRP or a fluorescent dye, reports it. Washes in buffer with 0.1% Tween-20 remove loosely bound antibody and lower background [1][2]. The membrane must never dry during these steps [3].

Materials and reagents

ItemDetails
TBST (1×)20 mM Tris base, 150 mM NaCl, 0.1% (v/v) Tween-20, pH 7.4–7.6; make 1 L from a 10× TBS stock and add Tween-20 fresh [1]
Blocking solution5% (w/v) non-fat dry milk in TBST, or 5% (w/v) BSA in TBST; make fresh and filter if clumpy [1][3]
Antibody diluentTBST with 1% milk or BSA, matching the blocker [1]
Primary antibodyDilute per datasheet; typical working range 1:500–1:5,000, or 1–2 µg/mL; titrate starting near 1:1,000 [1][4]
HRP- or dye-conjugated secondary antibodyAnti-species matched to the primary; use the datasheet dilution (HRP conjugates are often used between 1:2,000 and 1:20,000) [1][2]
Membrane with transferred proteinPVDF (activate in methanol for 15–30 s, rinse in water, equilibrate in transfer buffer) or nitrocellulose (wet in buffer); low-fluorescence PVDF for fluorescent detection [2][1]
Ponceau S (optional)0.1% (w/v) in 5% acetic acid, to confirm transfer; destain in water or TBST
ECL substrateAbout 2 mL of each reagent per mini blot (4 mL for a large blot) [3]
Sodium azideDo not add to any buffer used with HRP; it inhibits the enzyme [3]

Equipment

  • Rocker or orbital shaker (low speed)
  • Clean incubation trays or 50 mL tubes for roller incubation
  • Fume hood (methanol)
  • Chemiluminescence imager, film cassette, or fluorescence scanner
  • Forceps, gloves and a timer

Procedure

  1. After transfer, keep the membrane wet. Optionally stain with Ponceau S for 1–5 min, image the lanes, and destain completely in TBST [4]. (10 min)
  2. Place the membrane protein-side up in a tray with about 10 mL blocking solution for a mini blot, enough to cover it freely [3]. (2 min)
  3. Block on a rocker for 1 h at room temperature, or overnight at 4 °C. Do not exceed about 2 h at room temperature [1]. (1 h)
  4. Dilute the primary antibody in TBST with 1% milk or BSA (about 10 mL per mini blot) and replace the blocking solution [1][3]. (5 min)
  5. Incubate with gentle rocking for 1 h at room temperature or overnight at 4 °C; overnight often gives more signal with less background [1]. (1 h–overnight)
  6. Wash 3 × 10 min in a generous volume of TBST with gentle rocking (use 5 × 5 min if background is high) [1][4]. (30 min)
  7. Dilute the secondary antibody in TBST (1% milk or BSA optional) and incubate 1 h at room temperature with gentle rocking, protected from light for dye conjugates [1][2]. (1 h)
  8. Wash again, 3 × 10 min in TBST, then rinse briefly in TBS to remove residual Tween-20, which can raise ECL background [1][3]. (35 min)
  9. Chemiluminescence: drain, cover with ECL substrate for 1 min, blot off the excess, wrap in clear film and image or expose film for 15 s to 5 min [2][3]. (5–10 min)
  10. Fluorescence: scan the damp or dry membrane on the imager; do not wrap in ordinary plastic film, which can quench the signal [2].
  11. Record exposure settings, marker positions and antibody lots, then strip or re-probe for the loading control if needed.

Pause points

  • After blocking, the membrane can stay in blocking solution overnight at 4 °C.
  • After the primary antibody and washes, the membrane can be kept in TBST at 4 °C for a day.
  • A finished blot can be dried and stored; re-wet in methanol (PVDF) before re-probing.

Expected results

  • One distinct band at the expected molecular weight for the target, a clean background, and even loading across lanes.
  • A loading-control band of similar intensity in every lane.
  • Secondary-only blot: no bands at the target size.

Controls

  • Molecular-weight marker on every gel
  • Loading control (actin, tubulin, GAPDH) or total-protein stain
  • No-primary (secondary-only) membrane to expose nonspecific secondary binding
  • Positive and negative lysates when available

Troubleshooting

ProblemLikely causeSolution
High backgroundInsufficient blocking or washing; antibody too concentrated; membrane dried outDilute antibodies; extend washes; try the other blocker; keep the membrane wet [1][3]
Weak or no signalPoor transfer; antibody too dilute; azide in buffers; wrong secondary speciesCheck with Ponceau S; incubate the primary overnight at 4 °C; remove azide; match species [1][3]
Extra bandsAntibody too concentrated; cross-reactivity; degraded proteinTitrate the primary; run a no-primary control; use fresh protease inhibitors
Uneven or patchy stainingPoor agitation or too little liquidUse more volume and a level rocker; keep the membrane fully submerged

Safety and waste

  • Methanol is flammable and toxic: use it in a fume hood and collect it as solvent waste.
  • Unpolymerized acrylamide is a neurotoxin: wear gloves when handling gels and membranes.
  • Sodium azide is highly toxic; avoid it with HRP and collect it as hazardous waste.
  • Collect ECL substrate, Ponceau/acetic acid and used antibody solutions as chemical waste per institutional rules.
