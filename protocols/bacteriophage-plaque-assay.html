Bacteriophage Plaque Assay

Estimated time: ≈ 2–3 h hands-on + overnight incubation | Protocol World

To measure the concentration of infectious bacteriophage particles (plaque-forming units per mL, PFU/mL) in a lysate or environmental sample using E. coli as the host and the double agar overlay method.

Phage and a susceptible host are mixed in molten soft (top) agar and poured over a firmer bottom agar plate. The host grows into a continuous lawn, and each infectious phage particle infects one cell and spreads to neighbors, which leaves a clear zone (plaque) after overnight growth. Counting plaques on plates with 30–300 plaques and correcting for dilution and plated volume gives the titer [1][2][3].

ItemDetails
Host strainE. coli matching the phage (for example E. coli B for T4/T7 or K-12 for λ); overnight culture in LB, then subculture to early log phase (OD₆₀₀ ≈ 0.5, about 10⁸ cells/mL) [1][2]
LB broth10 g tryptone, 5 g yeast extract, 10 g NaCl per liter (or 5 g NaCl); autoclave
Bottom agar platesLB with 1.5% agar, about 25 mL per 100 mm plate; dry the plates at room temperature or 35–37 °C until the surface is free of moisture [1][2][3]
Top (soft) agarLB with 0.7% agar (7 g/L), 3 mL aliquots in tubes (or 2–3 mL per plate), melted and held at 47–50 °C in a water bath; do not use below 45 °C [1][2][3]
Divalent cations (optional)Some phages need them; one lab recipe adds 5 mM CaCl₂ and 10 mM MgSO₄ to the top agar for phage P1 [5]
SM buffer (phage diluent)100 mM NaCl, 8 mM MgSO₄·7H₂O, 50 mM Tris-HCl pH 7.5, 0.01% gelatin (per liter: 5.8 g NaCl, 2 g MgSO₄·7H₂O, 50 mL 1 M Tris-HCl pH 7.5, 5 mL 2% gelatin); autoclave [4]. Sterile saline or broth also works [2]
Phage sampleLysate or filtrate (0.22 µm), expected titer 10⁸–10¹⁰ PFU/mL for a fresh lysate
Sterile tubes, tips, spreadersMicrocentrifuge tubes (1.5 mL) for dilutions
  • Water bath at 47–50 °C
  • Incubator at 37 °C (35–37 °C)
  • Shaking incubator (37 °C, 200 rpm)
  • Spectrophotometer for OD₆₀₀
  • Vortex mixer
  • Biosafety cabinet or a clean bench with a Bunsen burner
  • Colony counter or a lightbox
  • Grow the host overnight in LB at 37 °C with shaking, dilute 1:100 into fresh LB, and grow at 37 °C, 200 rpm, to OD₆₀₀ ≈ 0.5 (early log) [1][2]. (2–3 h)
  • Melt top agar, place 3 mL aliquots in tubes in a 47–50 °C water bath, and warm bottom plates at 35–37 °C so the surface is dry [1][2][3]. (30 min)
  • Label plates for each dilution plus controls. Prepare 10-fold serial dilutions of the phage in SM buffer: 100 µL into 900 µL, vortexing between steps, down to 10⁻⁸ [2][4]. (15 min)
  • For each plate, combine 0.3 mL host culture with 0.1 mL of one phage dilution in a sterile tube. Use fresh tips for every dilution and start from the most dilute tube [2][4]. (10 min)
  • Optional adsorption: leave the mixture at room temperature or 37 °C for 8–15 min, but no longer than 15 min [1][2]. (8–15 min)
  • Add the mixture to a tube of 3 mL top agar at 47–50 °C, roll the tube between your palms 2–3 s to mix without bubbles, and pour at once onto a dry bottom plate [2][3]. (1 min per plate)
  • Tilt or swirl the plate in a figure-eight so the top agar covers the surface evenly, then leave it level on the bench until set [1][2]. (10–15 min)
  • Invert the plates and incubate at 37 °C. Check for plaques after 6 h and count after 12–18 h [1][2]. (6–18 h)
  • Choose plates with 30–300 plaques, count every clear zone (mark them on the lid), and calculate the titer [2][4]. (15 min)
  • Titer (PFU/mL) = plaques counted ÷ (volume plated in mL × dilution factor). For example, 150 plaques from 0.1 mL of a 10⁻⁶ dilution = 1.5 × 10⁹ PFU/mL [4]. (—)
  • Average duplicate plates of the same dilution, and discard plates with fewer than 30 or confluent lysis for the final value [2][4]. (5 min)
  • Store the phage stock at 4 °C in SM buffer (a few months); freeze with 15–25% glycerol for long-term storage. Autoclave or bleach used plates. (10 min)
  • After the dilution series (step 3): the dilutions can be kept at 4 °C for a few hours.
  • After step 8: plates can sit at 4 °C for a few hours once plaques are visible.
  • On the host-only control, a smooth, uniform lawn.
  • Phage plates show clear, round plaques about 0.5–3 mm across on the lawn, with their number falling about 10-fold per dilution.
  • A fresh phage lysate commonly gives 10⁸–10¹⁰ PFU/mL, so countable plates usually appear at 10⁻⁶–10⁻⁸ dilutions.
  • Host only plus top agar: confluent lawn with no plaques (shows the host is healthy and no prophage is induced) [3]
  • Phage only plus top agar (no host): no growth, so the phage stock is not contaminated with bacteria [3]
  • Top agar only: sterility of the medium [3]
  • Duplicate plates per dilution
ProblemLikely causeSolution
No lawn (plates clear)Over-dilution, culture too old (OD₆₀₀ above 0.8) or top agar too hotUse a fresh log-phase culture at OD₆₀₀ ≈ 0.5; keep the top agar at 45–50 °C [3]
Irregular or merged plaquesTop agar set before spreading; too much phage platedPour immediately and swirl quickly; use higher dilutions [3]
Plaques absent at all dilutionsWrong host, phage dead or needs Ca²⁺/Mg²⁺Check host range; add 5 mM CaCl₂ and 10 mM MgSO₄ to the top agar [5]
Pitted or patchy lawn, too many bubblesWet plates or vigorous mixingDry plates before use; roll rather than vortex the top agar tube
Pinpoint plaquesTop agar too concentrated, so phage cannot diffuseLower the top agar concentration (0.3–0.7%) [1][3]
  • Wear gloves and a lab coat; phage aerosols can contaminate a lab, so work in a biosafety cabinet (or on a clean bench) and avoid vortexing open tubes [3].
  • The E. coli host is usually BSL-1, but follow your local rules, and use BSL-2 practice when the host is a pathogen [4].
  • Molten agar at 47–50 °C can scald; use gloves and tongs.
  • Soak phage-contaminated tubes, tips and plates in 10% bleach or autoclave them; wipe surfaces with 70% ethanol and bleach.
