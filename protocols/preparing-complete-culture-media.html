Preparing Complete Culture Media

1 h hands-on + overnight serum thaw · 2026-11-25

Prepare 500 mL of complete growth medium: thaw and optionally heat-inactivate FBS at 56 °C for 30 min, aliquot, add 10% FBS, pen-strep and L-glutamine as needed, label, store at 2–8 °C and quality-check.

⬇ Word (.docx)⬇ PowerPoint (.pptx)

Purpose

To prepare sterile complete growth medium (basal medium + 10% heat-inactivated fetal bovine serum + penicillin-streptomycin ± L-glutamine) for routine culture of adherent mammalian cell lines.

Principle

Basal media such as DMEM or RPMI supply salts, amino acids, vitamins and glucose, but most cell lines also need serum for growth factors, attachment factors and carrier proteins [4]. Serum is thawed gently, optionally heat-inactivated at 56 °C for exactly 30 min to destroy complement, aliquoted to avoid freeze–thaw cycles, and then added aseptically to the basal medium together with antibiotics and, where the basal medium lacks it, labile L-glutamine [1][2][3][4]. Overheating or overlong heating damages growth-promoting serum components [3].

Materials and reagents

ItemDetails
Basal medium, 500 mL bottleDMEM or RPMI-1640, as specified for your cell line; check whether it already contains L-glutamine [1][4]
Fetal bovine serum (FBS)Stored at −20 °C; 56 mL is added per 500 mL bottle for a final 10% (56 ÷ 561 mL) [1]
Penicillin–streptomycin, 100× stockTypically 10,000 U/mL penicillin and 10,000 µg/mL streptomycin; 5 mL per 500 mL bottle gives about 100 U/mL and 100 µg/mL, within the 50–100 range given by ATCC [1][4]
L-Glutamine, 200 mM stock (only if the basal medium lacks it)Add 1 mL per 100 mL for about 2 mM, the test concentration suggested by ATCC; it is labile, so add shortly before use [4]
70% ethanolFor wiping bottles before they enter the biosafety cabinet [1]
Sterile bottles or tubes100 mL glass or polypropylene bottles for serum aliquots; 50 mL conical tubes for medium aliquots [2][3]
Labels and markerName, date, medium type, serum lot and supplements

Equipment

  • Biosafety cabinet (class II)
  • Water bath at 37 °C and a second at 56 °C (± 2 °C) with a thermometer
  • Refrigerator (2–8 °C) and freezer (−20 °C)
  • Sterile serological pipets and pipet controller
  • Ice bucket
  • pH or osmolality meter (optional quality control)

Procedure

  1. Thaw the frozen serum bottle overnight at 4 °C so that it thaws evenly. Do not use partly thawed serum for heat inactivation [2][3]. (overnight)
  2. Move the bottle to a 37 °C water bath for about 15 min and swirl to mix. Remove it as soon as it is fully thawed [2][3]. (15 min)
  3. Set a second water bath to 56 °C and verify it with a thermometer in a bottle of water. The water must cover the serum but stay below the cap [3]. (15 min)
  4. Heat-inactivate (skip if your cell line or supplier does not require it): once the bath is back at 56 °C, incubate the serum for 30 min and swirl every 5 min [3][4]. (30 min)
  5. Never exceed 56 ± 2 °C or 30 min; overheating reduces cell growth [3]. Cool the bottle on ice immediately afterward. (10 min)
  6. In the biosafety cabinet, divide the serum into sterile 50–60 mL aliquots, avoiding sediment. Label with date and lot and store at −20 °C [2][3]. (10 min)
  7. Warm the 500 mL basal medium to 37 °C. Thaw one serum aliquot and the pen-strep aliquot, spray bottles with 70% ethanol, wipe dry and place in the cabinet [1]. (20 min)
  8. Work aseptically: add 5 mL pen-strep to the basal medium and, if required, 5 mL of 200 mM L-glutamine (about 2 mM) [1][4]. (2 min)
  9. Add 56 mL heat-inactivated FBS to reach 10% of the 561 mL total [1]. Alternatively, first remove 55 mL of basal medium so the volume stays at 500 mL. (2 min)
  10. Close the bottle and mix by gentle inversion without foaming. The phenol red should be a bright red-orange color at pH 7.4 [4]. (1 min)
  11. Label the bottle with your name, date, medium, FBS percentage and supplements. Store at 2–8 °C protected from light; never freeze complete medium [1][4]. (2 min)
  12. Before use, warm only the volume you need to 37 °C and use within about one month, or sooner if a supplement expires earlier [4]. (20 min)

Pause points

  • After serum aliquoting: store aliquots at −20 °C for months [2][3].
  • After mixing: store the complete medium at 2–8 °C protected from light for about one month [4].

Expected results

  • A clear, bright red-orange medium with no turbidity, particles or color change to yellow [4].
  • Medium osmolality in the 260–320 mOsm/kg range expected for mammalian culture [4].
  • Cells plated in the new batch attach and grow as in the previous batch.

Controls

  • Compare growth of a reference cell line in the new batch against the previous lot before switching
  • Incubate a 5 mL aliquot of complete medium at 37 °C for 2–3 days and check for turbidity as a sterility test
  • Record the serum lot number on every bottle

Troubleshooting

ProblemLikely causeSolution
Serum has flocculent precipitate or cloudy clumps after heatingUneven or too-hot heating, or incomplete thawingThaw completely, swirl during heating and stay at 56 ± 2 °C for no more than 30 min [3]
Cells grow poorly in the new mediumOver-heated serum, old L-glutamine or a different serum lotUse fresh heat-inactivated serum, add glutamine just before use and test serum lots [3][4]
Medium turns yellow quicklyBacterial or fungal contamination or excess CO₂Discard the bottle; re-check aseptic technique and incubator CO₂ [4]
Medium looks purple or pinkpH too high from lid loose or CO₂ lowTighten the cap and check the CO₂ level [4]
Antibiotics hide a low-grade infectionPen-strep masks mycoplasma and resistant bacteriaTest cultures for mycoplasma regularly, and culture without antibiotics when possible [4]

Safety and waste

  • Wear gloves, a lab coat and eye protection; handle serum as potentially infectious because bovine serum may carry adventitious agents [4].
  • Use water-bath hygiene: keep the 56 °C bath clean, because contaminated water can enter bottles.
  • Dispose of used medium, pipets and gloves as biological waste according to your institution's rules.
