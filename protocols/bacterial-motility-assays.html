Bacterial Motility Assays

1–2 days (media 3–4 h + incubation) · 2026-11-26

Measure swimming, swarming and twitching motility on semi-solid agar. Agar concentrations, plate drying, stab and spot inoculation, incubation, ImageJ measurement and controls for Pseudomonas, E. coli, Bacillus and Salmonella.

⬇ Word (.docx)⬇ PowerPoint (.pptx)

Purpose

To compare swimming, swarming and twitching motility of bacterial strains on semi-solid agar, using Pseudomonas aeruginosa as the worked model and giving agar adjustments for E. coli, Bacillus and Salmonella.

Principle

Bacteria move in different ways depending on agar concentration. Swimming through the water-filled channels of soft agar (about 0.3%) uses flagella; swarming over the surface of moist 0.4–0.7% agar is a coordinated, flagella-driven movement; twitching under 1% agar or at the agar–plastic interface depends on type IV pili [1][2][3][4]. Each mode is measured as the size or shape of the zone that forms around the inoculation point, always compared against a wild-type and a non-motile mutant [1][2].

Materials and reagents

ItemDetails
Overnight cultureFrom a single colony on a plate less than 5–7 days old, grown 16–18 h at 37 °C (30 °C for some species) with shaking [1][2]
Swim agar (0.3%)3 g agar per L; for P. aeruginosa, M8 salts with 0.2% glucose, 0.5% casamino acids and 1 mM MgSO₄; for E. coli use LB or tryptone-based 0.3% agar [1][3]
5× M8 salts64 g Na₂HPO₄·7H₂O, 15 g KH₂PO₄, 2.5 g NaCl per L (warm to ~55 °C before adding) [1]
Swarm agarP. aeruginosa: FAB-minus (NH₄)₂SO₄ medium, 0.45% Noble agar, 12 mM glucose, 0.1% casamino acids; or 0.8% nutrient broth, 0.5% dextrose, 0.5% agar; B. subtilis and Salmonella about 0.6%; E. coli about 0.5% [2][3][4]
Twitching agarLB with 1% agar, poured as a thin layer [3]
PlatesSterile 100 × 15 mm Petri dishes
ControlsA wild-type strain and a non-motile strain (for example ΔflgK, ΔfliC or ΔmotAB for swimming; ΔpilA or ΔpilB for twitching) [1][3]
Toothpicks or pipette tipsSterile

Equipment

  • Autoclave and 2 L flask with stir bar
  • Incubator (30 or 37 °C) with humidity or a water tray
  • Level pouring surface
  • Flatbed scanner or camera with ruler
  • ImageJ or Fiji for area measurements
  • Flame or burner (optional, to remove bubbles)

Procedure

  1. Prepare swim agar: autoclave 3 g agar in 800 mL water for 45 min, cool to ~55 °C, and add 200 mL warm 5× M8 and the glucose, casamino acids and MgSO₄ [1]. (1 h)
  2. Stir gently with a magnetic bar, avoiding foam. Pour about 25 mL per 100 × 15 mm plate on a level surface [1]. (20 min)
  3. Let swim plates set at room temperature for 3–4 h with lids on, stacking fewer than four high; wipe off lid condensation before use [1]. (3–4 h)
  4. For swarm plates, pour about 25 mL per 100 mm dish of medium cooled to ~50 °C and dry them 30–60 min uncovered or overnight on the bench; Noble agar works best [2][3][4]. (overnight)
  5. Test the swarm plate: a 10 µL drop of 0.5% ink should hold its shape; if it spreads, dry longer [4]. (5 min)
  6. For twitching, pour thin LB 1% agar plates [3]. (30 min)
  7. Swim inoculation: dip a sterile toothpick lightly into the culture and stab it into the agar about halfway down, without touching the base; avoid surface inoculum [1][3]. (5 min)
  8. Swarm inoculation: spot 2 µL of culture at the center of the plate, or poke with a toothpick; let the drop absorb [3][4]. (5 min)
  9. Twitching inoculation: push a tip loaded with 2 µL of culture through the agar to the plastic and release 2 µL there [3]. (5 min)
  10. Incubate swim plates upright at 37 °C for 16–24 h (or 30 °C for 12–14 h); do not invert, as soft agar can detach [1][3]. (16–24 h)
  11. Incubate swarm plates at 30 or 37 °C for 16–24 h in moist air; twitching plates at 37 °C for 24 h [3][4]. (16–24 h)
  12. Image plates with a ruler. Measure the swim or swarm area with a freehand selection in ImageJ, calibrated to the plate diameter [1][4]. (20 min)
  13. Repeat with at least 3 replicate plates per strain, and compare to wild type and non-motile controls [1]. (—)

Pause points

  • Poured swim plates can be used the same day after setting; prepare them fresh for consistent moisture.
  • Overnight cultures can be held at 4 °C for a few hours but fresh cultures are best.

Expected results

  • Swimming: a large, circular halo around the stab point for wild type; very small halo for flagellar mutants [1][3].
  • Swarming: tendrils or sub-colonies radiating from the center in P. aeruginosa; a broader mat for Bacillus or Salmonella [3][4].
  • Twitching: a thin, hazy zone around a smaller, thicker colony in pili-positive strains [3].

Controls

  • Wild-type strain on every plate batch
  • Non-motile mutant (ΔflgK, ΔfliC, ΔmotAB) for swimming and swarming; pili mutant (ΔpilB or ΔpilA) for twitching [1][3]
  • Compare strains on the same batch of plates, the same day
  • Remember that swim size cannot separate a flagellar defect from a chemotaxis defect; use microscopy for that [1]

Troubleshooting

ProblemLikely causeSolution
No spreadingPlates too dry or too much agarShorten drying time; check agar percentage [4]
Smeared or uncontrolled spreadingPlates too wetDry longer; do the ink test; control humidity (~80%) [4]
Swarming appears on a swim plateSurface inoculation or stray dropsStab cleanly into the agar and avoid dripping [1]
Twitching appears on a swim plateStab reached the plate bottomStop halfway through the agar [1]
Large replicate variationDifferent agar thickness, drying or humidityPour equal volumes, dry uniformly, test on the same day [4]
Agar detachesInverted plate or rough handlingKeep plates upright [1]

Safety and waste

  • Handle bacteria under the appropriate biosafety level; P. aeruginosa is BSL-2.
  • Wear gloves and a lab coat; work away from open flames when alcohol is used.
  • Autoclave used plates and cultures before disposal; handle low-agar plates carefully to avoid spills.
