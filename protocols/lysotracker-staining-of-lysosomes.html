LysoTracker Staining of Lysosomes

1.5–3 h · 2026-11-24

Label acidic lysosomes in live cells with 50–75 nM LysoTracker probes, load 30 min to 2 h at 37 °C, and image promptly with appropriate controls.

⬇ Word (.docx)⬇ PowerPoint (.pptx)

Purpose

To label acidic organelles (mainly lysosomes and late endosomes) in living cultured cells with a LysoTracker fluorescent probe and image them by fluorescence microscopy.

Principle

LysoTracker dyes are fluorophores linked to a weak base. The neutral form crosses membranes freely; inside acidic compartments the amine is protonated, and the charged dye is trapped and concentrates, so these organelles light up at nanomolar probe concentrations [1][2]. The probes label any acidic compartment and are not specific for lysosomes, so identity should be confirmed with a marker such as LAMP1 [2]. Long incubation or high concentration can raise lysosomal pH and distort the organelle, so loading should be mild and imaging prompt [1][3].

Materials and reagents

ItemDetails
LysoTracker Red DND-991 mM solution in anhydrous DMSO, 50 µL vials; store at −20 °C, desiccated and dark, in single-use aliquots; avoid repeated freeze–thaw and frost-free freezers [3][4][5]
Other LysoTracker colorsGreen DND-26 (Ex/Em 504/511 nm), Blue DND-22 (373/422 nm), Deep Red (647/668 nm); Red DND-99 is 577/590 nm [2][3][4]
Cells on imaging dishesAdherent cells at 50–70% confluence on glass-bottom dishes or coverslips
Pre-warmed complete growth medium or HBSSAt 37 °C; HBSS with Ca²⁺ and Mg²⁺ is also suitable [4]
Working solution50–75 nM LysoTracker in pre-warmed medium: dilute 1 mM stock 1:1,000 in medium (1 µM intermediate), then 1:20 for 50 nM; prepare fresh and keep dark [3][4]
Hoechst 33342 (optional)1 µg/mL for nuclei, added separately, since the dyes should not be mixed in one staining solution [1]
Optional control agentsBafilomycin A1 (100 nM, 1–2 h) or NH₄Cl (10–20 mM) to neutralize lysosomes

Equipment

  • 37 °C, 5% CO₂ incubator
  • Fluorescence or confocal microscope with TRITC/Texas Red filters (for Red DND-99)
  • Live-cell chamber at 37 °C (preferred)
  • Biosafety cabinet
  • Pipettes and aluminum foil

Procedure

  1. Plate cells on glass-bottom dishes 24 h earlier so they are 50–70% confluent and healthy on the day of staining [3]. (24 h before)
  2. Warm the medium to 37 °C. Thaw a stock aliquot at room temperature, spin briefly, and keep it dark [4]. (10 min)
  3. Dilute the 1 mM stock to a working concentration of 50–75 nM in pre-warmed medium; use the lowest concentration that gives a clear signal [3][4]. (5 min)
  4. Remove the growth medium and add enough working solution to cover the cells [4]. (2 min)
  5. Incubate at 37 °C, 5% CO₂ in the dark. A typical load is 30 min; 30 min to 2 h is the range given in the manuals, and 1–5 min can suffice for bright, fast-loading cells [1][3][4]. (30 min–2 h)
  6. Replace the loading solution with fresh pre-warmed medium or imaging buffer without phenol red [3][4]. (2 min)
  7. Optional: add Hoechst 33342 at 1 µg/mL for 5–10 min and wash once with fresh medium. (10 min)
  8. Image immediately with a TRITC filter set (Ex 577 nm, Em 590 nm for Red DND-99), keeping excitation low to limit bleaching [2][5]. (15–30 min)
  9. Capture bright-field and fluorescence images of 5–10 fields per dish, using identical exposure for every condition. (20 min)
  10. Complete imaging within 1–2 h of staining; signal fades and cells may bleb in dye-free medium [3].

Pause points

  • After loading, the cells can be kept in fresh medium at 37 °C for a short period before imaging, but signal decreases over time [3].

Expected results

  • Bright punctate red or green vesicles in the perinuclear cytoplasm, with low diffuse background [1][2].
  • Bafilomycin A1 or NH₄Cl treatment strongly reduces staining, confirming acidification dependence [2].

Controls

  • Unstained cells imaged with the same settings to measure autofluorescence
  • Bafilomycin A1 or NH₄Cl–treated cells to neutralize acidic compartments
  • Co-staining with LAMP1 antibody or LAMP1-GFP (after fixation or in transfected cells) to confirm lysosomal identity [2]
  • Vehicle (DMSO) control at the same final concentration

Troubleshooting

ProblemLikely causeSolution
Weak signalToo little dye or time, old stock, or poorly acidified lysosomesRaise to 75–100 nM or lengthen loading; use a fresh aliquot [3]
Diffuse cytoplasmic stainDye too high, or long loading that raises lysosomal pHLower concentration, shorten incubation to 15–30 min [1][2]
Signal lost after fixationThe probe is not reliably retained after fixationImage live; if fixing is unavoidable, use methanol-free formaldehyde briefly, or a fixable marker such as LAMP1 antibody [2][5]
Rapid photobleachingHigh excitation or long exposureReduce light, image quickly, use a sensitive camera [2]
Cells round up or blebPhototoxicity or long incubation in dye-free mediumImage sooner, lower light and keep cells at 37 °C [3]

Safety and waste

  • DMSO carries dissolved dyes through skin: wear nitrile gloves and a lab coat.
  • Bafilomycin A1 is toxic; handle stock solutions in a hood and dispose of as chemical waste.
  • Collect dye-containing medium and DMSO waste as chemical waste; treat cell waste according to your biosafety level.
