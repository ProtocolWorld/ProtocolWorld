Touchdown PCR

≈ 2.5 h · 2026-11-27

Run touchdown PCR to boost specificity: Tm calculation, hot-start reaction setup, 12 cycles stepping the annealing temperature down 1 °C each cycle, a plateau phase, gel check, optimization for GC-rich and degenerate primers.

⬇ Word (.docx)⬇ PowerPoint (.pptx)

Purpose

To amplify a target with high specificity when standard PCR gives smears or extra bands, for example with GC-rich templates, primers with imperfect matches, degenerate primers, or complex genomic DNA.

Principle

In touchdown (TD) PCR the annealing temperature starts above the calculated primer Tm and is lowered by a small step every cycle or every few cycles until it reaches a more permissive value [2][3]. Early cycles allow only the most perfectly matched primer–template pairs to anneal, so the correct product gains a head start of about twofold per cycle for each degree of Tm advantage over spurious products [2]. Later cycles at the lower temperature amplify the enriched target efficiently [1][3]. The method should be run with a hot-start polymerase [1][3] and is an efficient single-step route to good conditions rather than a way to find the exact optimum [1].

Materials and reagents

ItemDetails
Template DNAGenomic DNA 1 ng–1 µg per 50 µL reaction; plasmid or viral DNA 1 pg–1 ng [4]
Forward and reverse primers10 µM stocks; 18–25 nt, 40–60% GC, 3′ G/C clamp, similar Tm [1][4]; final 0.2 µM each (range 0.05–1 µM) [4]
Hot-start Taq DNA polymerase with 10× bufferStandard buffer gives 1.5 mM Mg²⁺ (1.5–2.0 mM is optimal for most reactions) [4]; 1.25 U per 50 µL reaction [4]
dNTP mix10 mM each; 200 µM final of each [4]
Nuclease-free waterTo 50 µL
Optional additivesDMSO 2–5% for GC-rich templates [1]; MgCl₂ in 0.5 mM steps if needed [1][4]
Agarose gel reagents1–2% agarose in 1× TAE, DNA stain, loading dye and a DNA ladder

Equipment

  • Thermocycler with heated lid and programmable temperature decrement
  • PCR tubes or plates
  • Microcentrifuge
  • Gel electrophoresis system and imager
  • NEB Tm Calculator or similar for primer Tm

Procedure

  1. Calculate the Tm of each primer with a nearest-neighbor calculator (for example, the NEB Tm Calculator) using your polymerase buffer. Take the lower Tm of the pair [4]. (10 min)
  2. Choose the program: start the annealing 5–10 °C above Tm and end 2–5 °C below Tm (for example Tm 60 °C: 68 °C down to 56 °C) [3]. (5 min)
  3. Thaw reagents and set up on ice. For one 50 µL reaction: 5 µL 10× buffer, 1 µL dNTPs, 1 µL each primer (10 µM), template, 0.25 µL Taq (1.25 U), water to 50 µL [4]. (10 min)
  4. Mix gently, spin briefly, and keep on ice. Include a no-template control and, if available, a standard (non-touchdown) reaction for comparison. (5 min)
  5. Preheat the block to 95 °C and load the tubes (hot start). Initial denaturation: 95 °C for 30 s for pure templates, 2–4 min for GC-rich or genomic DNA [1][4]. (2–4 min)
  6. Touchdown phase, 12 cycles: 95 °C 15–30 s; annealing starting at Tm + 8 °C for 30 s, lowered 1 °C per cycle; 68 °C extension at 1 min per kb [1][3][4]. (≈ 25 min)
  7. Amplification phase, 20–25 cycles: 95 °C 15–30 s; annealing at the final (lowest) temperature for 30 s; 68 °C for 1 min per kb [1][4]. (≈ 40 min)
  8. Final extension at 68 °C for 5 min, then hold at 4–10 °C [4]. (5 min)
  9. Run 5 µL of each reaction with loading dye on a 1–2% agarose gel next to a ladder. Image the gel [4]. (45 min)
  10. If extra bands remain, raise the starting temperature, use 3–4 cycles per degree instead of 1, or remove cycles from the final phase [1]. (As needed)
  11. If no product forms, extend the initial denaturation to 5 min or add 10 cycles at a constant annealing temperature [1]. Label and store the product at −20 °C. (As needed)

Pause points

  • Reactions can be held at 4 °C overnight after the final extension.
  • Purified PCR product can be stored at −20 °C for months.

Expected results

  • A single band of the expected size, with fewer smears and nonspecific bands than a standard PCR run at one annealing temperature [2][3].
  • No band in the no-template control.

Controls

  • No-template control to detect contamination
  • Standard PCR at a single annealing temperature to compare specificity
  • Positive control template with a known amplicon, if available
  • Ladder to confirm product size

Troubleshooting

ProblemLikely causeSolution
Multiple bands or smearFinal annealing temperature too low or too many cyclesRaise the starting and final temperatures; use 3–4 cycles per degree; remove final-phase cycles [1]
No productAnnealing too high for the primers, or template poorLower the end temperature; lengthen denaturation to 5 min; add 10 cycles at constant annealing [1]
Primer-dimerPrimers too concentrated or complementary at 3′ endsReduce primers toward 0.05–0.2 µM; redesign primers [1][4]
Weak bandToo few cycles or Mg²⁺ not optimalAdd 3–5 cycles; adjust MgCl₂ in 0.5 mM steps [1][4]
Degenerate or cross-species primers failMismatches lower the effective TmUse a lower range (e.g., 50 °C declining to 35 °C) while keeping the last 15 cycles above about 50 °C [1]

Safety and waste

  • Wear gloves and eye protection when handling DNA stains and running gels; follow local rules for stain disposal.
  • Use UV protection if a transilluminator is used.
  • Dispose of PCR products and tips as molecular-biology waste; clean the workspace to prevent amplicon contamination.
