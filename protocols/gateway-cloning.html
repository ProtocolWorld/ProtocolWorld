Gateway Cloning

E. coli & Bacteria · About 4 days · 2026-11-25

Two-step recombinational cloning: an attB-flanked PCR product is moved into a pDONR vector by a BP reaction, then into a destination vector by an LR reaction, with ccdB negative selection at both steps.

Gateway Cloning

Estimated time: About 4 days | Protocol World

To build an entry clone from an attB-flanked PCR product and then transfer the insert into a destination vector, without restriction digestion or ligation, while keeping the reading frame and orientation.

Gateway uses the lambda phage integration and excision machinery. The BP reaction (attB × attP, catalyzed by integrase and IHF) produces an attL-flanked entry clone and an attR by-product. The LR reaction (attL × attR, catalyzed by integrase, IHF and excisionase) moves the insert into a destination vector, giving an attB-flanked expression clone [2][3]. Because attB1 pairs only with attP1/attR1 sites and attB2 only with the 2 series, the insert keeps its orientation [2]. Donor and destination vectors carry the toxic ccdB gene between the att sites. ccdB poisons DNA gyrase, so unreacted vectors cannot grow in ordinary hosts such as DH5α or TOP10; only recombined plasmids, which have lost ccdB, give colonies [2][3]. Entry clones carry kanamycin resistance, and most destination vectors carry ampicillin resistance, so each step is selected on a different antibiotic [1][2][3]. Proteinase K treatment after each reaction improves colony yield [4][5].

  • Design primers with four 5′ G residues, the 25 bp attB1 or attB2 site, and the gene-specific sequence in frame; amplify with a high-fidelity polymerase [3]. (1 h)
  • Purify the PCR product (PEG precipitation or gel extraction), check size on a gel, and measure its concentration; aim for about 10 ng/µL [1][3]. (1 h)
  • Calculate molar amounts: ng = fmol × bp × 660 fg/fmol ÷ 10⁶. For products over 4 kb, use at least 100 fmol (no more than 500 ng) [3][4]. (5 min)
  • At room temperature, combine 15–150 ng attB PCR product (1–7 µL) and 1 µL pDONR (150 ng/µL) in a 1.5 mL tube; add TE (pH 8.0) to 8 µL [1][4]. (5 min)
  • Thaw BP Clonase II on ice for 2 min, vortex twice briefly, and add 2 µL to the tube (10 µL total). Mix by brief vortexing and return the enzyme to the freezer promptly [1]. (5 min)
  • Incubate at 25 °C for 1 h; use 16–18 h (overnight) for inserts over 5 kb, which gives roughly 5–10 times more colonies [1][4]. (1–18 h)
  • Add 1 µL proteinase K solution, mix, and incubate at 37 °C for 10 min to stop the reaction [1]. Reactions can be stored at −20 °C for up to 1 week [3]. (10 min)
  • Thaw one 50 µL aliquot of ccdB-sensitive chemically competent cells on ice and add 1 µL of the BP reaction. Mix gently without pipetting up and down [1][3]. (5 min)
  • Incubate on ice for 30 min, heat-shock at 42 °C for 30 s without shaking, then return to ice for 2 min [1][3]. (35 min)
  • Add 250–450 µL room-temperature SOC and shake at 37 °C and 200 rpm for 1 h [1][3]. (1 h)
  • Plate 20 µL and 100 µL on prewarmed LB plates with 50 µg/mL kanamycin. Incubate inverted at 37 °C for 16–20 h [1][3]. (16–20 h)
  • Screen 4–8 colonies by colony PCR (M13 or vector primers) for the expected insert size; streak positives on a fresh kanamycin plate [3]. (2 h)
  • Inoculate 3–5 mL LB with 50 µg/mL kanamycin from each positive colony and grow at 37 °C and 220 rpm for 12–16 h; prepare minipreps [3]. (12–16 h)
  • Verify the entry clone by diagnostic restriction digest and by Sanger sequencing across both attL sites and the full insert [3]. Measure DNA concentration. (1–2 days)
  • For the LR reaction, combine 50–150 ng entry clone (1–7 µL) and 1 µL destination vector (150 ng/µL) in a tube at room temperature; add TE (pH 8.0) to 8 µL [1][5]. (5 min)
  • Thaw LR Clonase II on ice, vortex twice briefly, and add 2 µL (10 µL total). Mix briefly; if small colonies appear later, lower the entry clone to about 50 ng [1][5]. (5 min)
  • Incubate at 25 °C for 1 h. Extend to 16–18 h (overnight) when the entry clone or destination vector is larger than 10 kb [1][5]. (1–18 h)
  • Add 1 µL proteinase K, mix, and incubate at 37 °C for 10 min. Skipping this step can lower colony numbers up to 10-fold [1][5]. (10 min)
  • Transform 1 µL into 50 µL competent cells as before: 30 min on ice, 42 °C for 30 s, ice, add 250–450 µL SOC, then shake at 37 °C for 1 h [1][3]. (2 h)
  • Plate 20 µL and 100 µL on LB plates with 100 µg/mL ampicillin (or the antibiotic your destination vector carries). Incubate at 37 °C for 16–20 h [1][3]. (16–20 h)
  • Count colonies and compare them with the no-enzyme control; a full LR reaction typically gives thousands of colonies [1][3][5]. (10 min)
  • Pick 4–8 colonies, confirm by colony PCR or restriction digest, and grow overnight in 3–5 mL LB with 100 µg/mL ampicillin [3]. (12–16 h)
  • Prepare minipreps, digest to confirm the expected pattern, and sequence the insert junctions (attB sites) and any tag or promoter junctions [2][3]. (1 day)
  • Make glycerol stocks (500 µL culture plus 500 µL 50% glycerol) of verified entry and expression clones and store at −80 °C. (15 min)
  • Store pDONR and destination vectors as purified DNA at −20 °C. Grow stocks only in ccdB-resistant cells (DB3.1 or ccdB Survival 2 T1R) with the proper antibiotics [3][6]. (15 min)
  • After step 7, the BP or LR reaction can be stored at −20 °C for up to 1 week [3].
  • After steps 11 and 20, plates can be kept at 4 °C for a few days before picking colonies.
  • Verified minipreps and glycerol stocks can be stored indefinitely at −20 °C and −80 °C, respectively.
  • The BP reaction typically gives hundreds to over a thousand kanamycin-resistant colonies when the full reaction is plated [1].
  • The LR reaction typically gives several thousand ampicillin-resistant colonies [1][3][5].
  • Most colonies from correctly designed reactions carry the expected plasmid; one study found nearly all minipreps tested had the predicted pattern [2].
  • Entry clones and expression clones show the expected band sizes on restriction digestion, and sequencing confirms intact attL or attB sites and an in-frame insert.
  • No-enzyme control: repeat the reaction without Clonase mix; it should give essentially no colonies [3][4].
  • Positive control: pEXP7-tet (BP) or pENTR-gus (LR) from the kit; confirm tetracycline resistance on plates [3].
  • Transformation control: supercoiled plasmid such as pUC19 to check competent-cell efficiency.
  • ccdB check: transform 10–50 pg destination vector into DH5α and DB3.1; DB3.1 should give about 10,000-fold more colonies [3].
  • Untransformed cells plated on the selection antibiotic to confirm the plates work.
ProblemLikely causeSolution
Few or no colonies after BP or LRInactive enzyme, wrong att sites, wrong antibiotic, or missing proteinase K stepUse a fresh enzyme aliquot, recheck att site sequences and the antibiotic, treat with proteinase K, and extend incubation to 16–18 h [3][4][5]
Many colonies in the no-enzyme controlMutated or deleted ccdB, F′ host strain, or contaminated reagentsUse a new donor or destination vector, switch to an F′-free strain such as DH5α or DH10B, and remake solutions [3][4][5]
Large and small colonies after LRSmall colonies are often unreacted entry clone (kanamycin-resistant carry-over) or ampicillin degradationReduce the entry clone to 50–100 ng, transform less volume, or raise ampicillin to 300 µg/mL if the destination vector is ampicillin-resistant [3][5]
Large inserts (over 5 kb) give few clonesLow recombination efficiency or insert toxicityUse at least 100 fmol of PCR product, incubate overnight, plate at 30 °C, or use Stbl2 cells [3][4]
Wrong or truncated insertsPCR errors, primer-dimers, or primer synthesis errorsUse a high-fidelity polymerase, purify the PCR product, order HPLC- or PAGE-purified primers, and sequence several clones [3]
Entry or destination vector will not grow in your strainccdB is toxic in standard cloning strainsUse DB3.1 or ccdB Survival 2 T1R cells with chloramphenicol where required [3][6]
Low colony counts at BP with excess donor vectorToo much pDONR leaves intact ccdB plasmid and inhibits recombinationKeep pDONR at or below 500 ng per 20 µL (about 150 ng in a 10 µL reaction) and use roughly equimolar attB DNA [3][4]
  • Wear gloves, lab coat, and eye protection; work aseptically and follow your institution's biosafety level for recombinant DNA.
  • Ethidium bromide is mutagenic; use a safer gel stain where possible and dispose of stained gels as chemical waste.
  • Heat blocks, water baths, and melted agar can cause burns; handle with care.
  • Ampicillin and kanamycin can cause allergic reactions in sensitive people; avoid dust and skin contact when weighing powders.
  • Waste: autoclave or chemically decontaminate (for example, 10% bleach for at least 30 min) all bacterial cultures, plates, tips, and tubes before disposal; collect antibiotic stock solutions and gel waste as chemical waste.
