Small RNA-seq Library Preparation

About 3–4 days · 2026-11-20

Build Illumina-style small RNA (miRNA) sequencing libraries by gel size selection, 3′ and 5′ adapter ligation, reverse transcription, minimal-cycle PCR and gel purification, with QC and troubleshooting.

⬇ Word (.docx)⬇ PowerPoint (.pptx)

Purpose

To convert the 18–30 nt small RNA fraction (miRNA, siRNA, piRNA) of a total RNA sample into a PCR-amplified, adapter-flanked cDNA library ready for Illumina-type sequencing.

Principle

Small RNAs carry a 5′ phosphate and a 3′ hydroxyl, so they can be ligated to adapters directly. A pre-adenylated 3′ adapter is joined by truncated T4 RNA ligase 2 without ATP, which prevents the RNA from circularizing and limits adapter concatemers. A 5′ adapter is then added with T4 RNA ligase 1 and ATP. After reverse transcription from a primer complementary to the 3′ adapter, the cDNA is amplified with as few PCR cycles as possible and the correct-sized product is cut from a gel to remove adapter dimers [1][2][3]. Ligation order and enzyme choice bias the library, so all samples to be compared must be prepared the same way.

Materials and reagents

ItemDetails
Total RNA (high quality)Intact RNA from cells or tissue; 1–10 µg per library is typical (1–20 µg for gel-size-selected plant libraries) [1][3]. Check integrity on a gel or analyzer.
RNA size markers19, 24, 28 and 32 nt RNA oligonucleotides (end-labeled or fluorescent) and a Decade-type marker [2].
15% denaturing polyacrylamide gel15% acrylamide (19:1), 7–8 M urea, 1× TBE, 1.0 mm thick; formamide or Gel Loading Buffer II for loading [1][2].
3′ adapter, 50 µMPre-adenylated, 3′-blocked (ddC or amine) DNA/RNA adapter; the CSH protocol uses 1 µL of 50 µM per 20 µL reaction [2].
5′ adapter (linker), 50 µMRNA oligonucleotide with a 3′-OH; 1 µL of 50 µM per 20 µL reaction [2].
Ligases and buffersTruncated T4 RNA ligase 2 with ATP-free 10× buffer; T4 RNA ligase 1 with 10× buffer and 10 mM ATP; DMSO [2][3].
Reverse transcription reagentsRT primer complementary to the 3′ adapter (5 µM), SuperScript III or equivalent, 5× buffer, DTT, dNTPs [2].
PCR reagentsTaq or high-fidelity polymerase, 10× buffer, 10 mM dNTPs, forward and reverse library primers at 100 µM [2].
Precipitation and elution reagents0.3–0.4 M NaCl, acid phenol:chloroform (pH 4.5), 100% and 80% ethanol, GlycoBlue or glycogen carrier (20 µg), 3 M sodium acetate pH 5.2 [2][3].
2% low-melt agarose or 6% native polyacrylamide gel1× TAE or TBE with SYBR Gold; 50 bp ladder or pBR322-MspI marker [2][3].
Nuclease-free water, low-bind tubesRNase-free throughout; use low-binding tubes for pellets.

Equipment

  • Vertical gel apparatus and power supply (10 W constant)
  • Thermal cycler
  • Heat block or water bath (37, 42, 70, 95 °C)
  • Refrigerated microcentrifuge
  • UV transilluminator or blue-light imager
  • Fluorometer (Qubit) and Bioanalyzer or TapeStation
  • Rotator for gel elution
  • −80 °C freezer, dry ice for freezing eluates

Procedure

  1. Check RNA integrity and measure concentration. Use the same input mass for every sample (for example 1–5 µg total RNA per library) [1][2][3]. (30 min)
  2. Size selection of the input RNA: run RNA with 19, 24, 28 and 32 nt markers on 15% urea PAGE at 10 W for 1–1.5 h. Stain, then excise the 20–28 nt region [2]. (3 h)
  3. Elute the slice overnight at 4 °C in 420 µL 0.4 M NaCl. Filter, add 20 µg GlycoBlue and 2.5 volumes ethanol, precipitate at −20 °C for 6 h or more and resuspend in 13 µL water [2]. (overnight)
  4. 3′ ligation: mix 13 µL RNA, 2 µL ATP-free 10× buffer, 2 µL DMSO, 1 µL 50 µM 3′ adapter and 2 µL truncated T4 RNA ligase 2 (20 µL). Incubate at 37 °C for 1 h [2]. (1 h)
  5. Add 20 µL gel loading buffer, heat at 95 °C for 5 min and run on 15% urea PAGE at 10 W for 1.5–2 h. Excise the ligated product about 18 nt above the unligated RNA (about 38–47 nt) [2]. (3 h)
  6. Elute and ethanol-precipitate as in step 3 and resuspend in 13 µL water [2]. (overnight)
  7. 5′ ligation: mix 13 µL RNA, 2 µL 10× T4 RNA ligase buffer with ATP, 2 µL DMSO, 1 µL 50 µM 5′ adapter and 2 µL T4 RNA ligase 1 (20 µL). Incubate at 37 °C for 1 h [2]. (1 h)
  8. Gel-purify again: 15% urea PAGE at 10 W for 2 h or more. Excise the product about 32 nt above the 3′-ligated RNA (about 69–79 nt), then elute and precipitate as before [2]. (1 day)
  9. Reverse transcription: anneal 6.3 µL RNA with 4.2 µL of 5 µM RT primer (72 °C, 2 min; ice 2 min). Add RT mix and 1 µL SuperScript III; 50 °C 1 h, 70 °C 15 min [2]. (1.5 h)
  10. Set up a 100 µL PCR: 5 µL cDNA, 10 µL 10× buffer with MgCl₂, 2 µL 10 mM dNTPs, 1 µL each of 100 µM primers, 80 µL water, 1 µL Taq. Prepare a −RT control in parallel [2]. (20 min)
  11. Cycle: 94 °C 2 min; 5 cycles of 94 °C 15 s, 54 °C 30 s, 72 °C 30 s; then 15–25 cycles of 94 °C 15 s, 60 °C 30 s, 72 °C 30 s; 72 °C 2 min. Use the fewest cycles that give a visible band [2]. (1 h)
  12. Run the PCR on 2% low-melt agarose (80 V) with a 50 bp ladder. Cut the library band near 120 bp and not the lower adapter-dimer band [2][3]. (3 h)
  13. Dissolve the gel slice at room temperature or 70 °C in 0.5 M NaCl, extract with phenol and chloroform, ethanol-precipitate at −20 °C and resuspend in 20 µL water. Dilute to about 10 nM [2]. (3 h)
  14. Quality control: check size and purity on a Bioanalyzer DNA High Sensitivity chip (one distinct peak near 110–130 bp) and quantify by fluorometry. Re-purify if adapter dimer remains [3]. (1 h)

Pause points

  • Ligation reactions in the NEB workflow can be frozen at −20 °C for up to 72 h [3].
  • Ethanol precipitates can stay at −20 °C overnight or longer.
  • The final purified library is stable at −20 °C until sequencing.

Expected results

  • A distinct ligated-RNA band shifted by about 18 nt after the 3′ ligation and by about 32 nt more after the 5′ ligation [2].
  • A final library peak of about 110–130 bp (a 21–23 nt miRNA plus both adapters) with little or no adapter-dimer peak [2][3].
  • The −RT control shows no product of that size.

Controls

  • −RT control in the PCR to detect DNA or ligation-product carry-over [2]
  • Spiked end-labeled marker oligonucleotides to follow size selection and ligation yield [2]
  • Water-in-place-of-RNA library to see the adapter-dimer background
  • Use identical RNA input, enzymes and cycle numbers across samples to be compared

Troubleshooting

ProblemLikely causeSolution
Strong adapter-dimer bandToo little RNA, excess adapter, over-cyclingReduce adapter, avoid heating ligations above recommended temperatures, use fewer PCR cycles and cut only the library band [2][3]
No product after PCRLigation or RT failed, or RNA lost during gel stepsCheck radiolabeled marker shifts, use fresh ATP and enzymes, use more carrier and precipitate longer [2]
Smear or high-molecular-weight bandsOver-amplificationReduce cycles; run a cycle-number test and choose the lowest that gives a visible band [2]
Biased miRNA representationLigase sequence preferencesUse truncated ligase 2 without ATP and keep the workflow identical across samples; degenerate adapter ends can reduce bias [1][3]
Poor recovery from gelInsufficient elution time or small pelletElute overnight, add carrier, use dialysis elution for higher yield [2]
Bioanalyzer peak off targetWrong gel sliceRun a second size selection of the library and re-check [3]

Safety and waste

  • Acrylamide is a neurotoxin before polymerization: wear gloves and avoid dust and spills.
  • Urea gels, phenol and chloroform: use a fume hood; phenol causes severe skin burns.
  • SYBR Gold and ethidium bromide are mutagens: wear gloves and dispose of gels and buffers as chemical waste.
  • If you use 32P-labeled markers, follow your institution's radiation safety rules; otherwise use fluorescent markers.
  • Keep the workspace RNase-free and wear gloves; treat human or animal samples at the required biosafety level.
  • Dispose of organic phases and gel slices as hazardous chemical waste, not down the drain.
