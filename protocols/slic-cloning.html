SLIC Cloning

≈ 1 h hands-on + overnight growth · 2026-11-26

Join a PCR insert into a linearized vector without restriction enzymes or ligase: chew back ends with T4 DNA polymerase for 2.5 min, anneal 10 min on ice, and transform E. coli; includes a two-step dCTP variant.

⬇ Word (.docx)⬇ PowerPoint (.pptx)

Purpose

To join a PCR-amplified insert (or several fragments) into a linearized plasmid at any chosen site, without restriction sites or ligase, and recover the recombinant plasmid in E. coli.

Principle

Sequence and ligation-independent cloning (SLIC) uses the 3′→5′ exonuclease activity of T4 DNA polymerase. In the absence of dNTPs the enzyme chews back the 3′ ends of the vector and insert, exposing single-stranded 5′ overhangs that are complementary because the PCR primers carried 20–30 bp of homology to the neighboring fragment [1][3][4]. The overhangs anneal in vitro, and the nicked, annealed molecule is sealed by the E. coli host after transformation. In the one-step version, a very short chew-back at room temperature followed by 10 min on ice is enough, and no dNTP stop or RecA is needed [1]. In the original two-step version each fragment is chewed for about 30 min and the reaction is stopped with dCTP [2].

Materials and reagents

ItemDetails
Linearized vectorRestriction digest or inverse PCR, purified with a PCR clean-up kit and eluted in 10 mM Tris-Cl, pH 8.5; about 100 ng per reaction [1]
Insert PCR product(s)High-fidelity PCR with primers carrying 20–30 bp vector homology (about 40 bp for 3+ fragments); purified; if the template is a plasmid, treat with DpnI or gel-purify [3][4]
T4 DNA polymerase0.3–0.6 U per 100 ng vector (one-step) [1]; 0.5 U per 1 µg DNA for the 30-min two-step version [2]
10× NEBuffer 2 (+ BSA)1× = 50 mM NaCl, 10 mM Tris-HCl pH 7.9, 10 mM MgCl₂, 1 mM DTT; BSA as supplied with the polymerase [1]
10 mM dCTPOnly for the two-step method; 2 µL stops a 20 µL chew-back [2]
Competent E. coliChemically competent DH5α (100 µL) or TOP10 (50 µL), ≥ 10⁷ CFU/µg [1]
LB broth and LB agar10 g/L tryptone, 5 g/L yeast extract, 10 g/L NaCl; agar 15 g/L; add antibiotic (for example ampicillin 100 µg/mL) after cooling to ≈ 50 °C
Nuclease-free water, iceUse low-retention tubes

Equipment

  • Thermocycler (PCR amplification and optional 37 °C incubation)
  • Heat block or water bath at 42 °C
  • Ice bucket and timer accurate to seconds
  • Agarose gel system and NanoDrop or Qubit
  • 37 °C shaking incubator and plate incubator
  • Biosafety cabinet or clean bench for plating

Procedure

  1. Design primers so each fragment ends with 20–30 bp that match its neighbor; avoid hairpins in the overlap and shared homology between non-neighboring pieces [3][4].
  2. Linearize the vector (digest or inverse PCR), gel-purify or column-clean it, and measure the concentration. Amplify inserts with a high-fidelity polymerase and clean them up [1][3]. (2–3 h)
  3. On ice, mix 100 ng vector, insert at a 1:2 to 1:4 molar ratio, 1 µL 10× NEBuffer 2 (+ BSA), 0.3–0.6 U T4 DNA polymerase and water to 10 µL [1]. (5 min)
  4. Incubate at room temperature (22–26 °C) for exactly 2.5 min. Chew-back longer than 5 min sharply lowers efficiency [1]. (2.5 min)
  5. Move the tube to ice immediately and hold it there for 10 min so the single-stranded overhangs anneal [1]. (10 min)
  6. Add 1 µL of the reaction to 100 µL thawed competent DH5α (or 50 µL TOP10). Keep on ice for 20 min [1]. (20 min)
  7. Heat shock at 42 °C for 45 s, then return to ice for 2 min [1]. (3 min)
  8. Add 0.9 mL LB without antibiotic and recover at 37 °C with shaking for 1 h [1]. (1 h)
  9. Plate 10 µL and 100 µL on selective LB agar, and incubate the plates inverted at 37 °C for about 16 h [1]. (16 h)
  10. Screen 4–8 colonies by colony PCR or miniprep and restriction digest, then sequence across every junction before using the clone [1].
  11. Alternative for multi-fragment or long-overlap work (two-step): chew 1 µg of each fragment with 0.5 U T4 DNA polymerase in 1× buffer, 20 µL, 30 min at room temperature [2]. (30 min)
  12. Stop each chew-back with 2 µL 10 mM dCTP on ice. Combine 100 ng vector with equimolar chewed fragments in 15 µL, anneal 30 min at 37 °C, chill, then transform 5 µL [2]. (45 min)

Pause points

  • Purified vector and insert can be stored at −20 °C for weeks.
  • The annealed reaction can rest on ice or at −20 °C without a change in efficiency after the 10-min anneal [1].
  • Plates can be moved to 4 °C after 16 h of growth.

Expected results

  • With ≥ 10 bp of perfect homology the one-step method gave more than 5 × 10⁵ CFU per µg vector, and 22 of 22 single-insert colonies were correct in the original report [1].
  • Typical plates carry tens to hundreds of colonies; three- and four-fragment assemblies give fewer, with about 88–96% correct among those screened [1].
  • Reported efficiency falls with fragment number in the original method (about 80% correct for five pieces with 40 bp overlaps) [3].

Controls

  • Vector only, treated with T4 DNA polymerase: shows background from uncut or re-closed vector
  • Vector + insert without polymerase: shows carry-over of template and un-chewed vector
  • Uncut plasmid (10 pg): confirms competent-cell efficiency
  • No-DNA plate: confirms antibiotic activity and sterility

Troubleshooting

ProblemLikely causeSolution
No or few coloniesChew-back too long or too short; anneal under 10 min; dirty DNA or carried-over dNTPs; weak cellsKeep one-step chew-back ≤ 5 min; anneal 10 min on ice; re-purify; use high-efficiency cells and a 1:4 ratio
Many colonies, mostly empty vectorIncomplete linearization or plasmid template carry-overRe-digest and gel-purify the vector; add DpnI to PCR products
Wrong or rearranged clonesHairpins, repeats or shared homology in overlapsRedesign overlaps; assemble hierarchically; screen more colonies
Low yield with several fragmentsLow DNA concentration; short overlapsIncrease DNA; use 40 bp overlaps; consider a two-step reaction [3]

Safety and waste

  • E. coli K-12 strains are BSL-1: decontaminate cultures and plates by autoclaving or bleach before disposal.
  • Ampicillin and other antibiotics are sensitizers: wear gloves and dispose of stock as chemical waste.
  • Use a UV shield or a safer DNA stain for gel checks; the 42 °C bath is a burn hazard.
  • Dispose of kit buffers (chaotropic salts) as chemical waste per institutional rules.
