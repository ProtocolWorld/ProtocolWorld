Strand-Specific RNA-seq

Estimated time: 2 days (about 12–14 h hands-on and incubation) | Protocol World

To prepare Illumina sequencing libraries that preserve the original transcript strand, so that antisense transcripts, overlapping genes and non-coding RNAs can be assigned correctly.

Total RNA is enriched for mRNA (poly(A) selection) or depleted of rRNA, fragmented and reverse transcribed. The second cDNA strand is made with dUTP in place of dTTP, so it carries uracil [1][3]. After adapter ligation, a uracil-excising enzyme (UNG or a USER-type mix) destroys that strand, and only the first strand, which is complementary to the RNA, is amplified [1][2]. Reads then map in a fixed orientation relative to the transcript. A multi-method comparison ranked dUTP marking among the best-performing approaches [3]. Verify strandedness computationally after sequencing [5].

  • Thermocycler with heated lid
  • Magnetic rack for tubes or plates
  • Bioanalyzer or TapeStation
  • Fluorometer (Qubit-type) and qPCR instrument
  • Low-bind tubes, filter tips
  • Ice bucket and 4 °C benchtop cooler
  • Quantify RNA fluorometrically and check integrity on a Bioanalyzer; require RIN ≥7–8 for poly(A) selection, or use rRNA depletion for lower RIN [2]. (30 min)
  • Remove genomic DNA if needed (DNase treatment, about 30 min at 37 °C), then clean up the RNA [1]. (45 min)
  • Enrich mRNA on oligo(dT) beads (bind at 65 °C for 5 min, then room temperature) or deplete rRNA with probes and RNase H, per the kit manual [2]. (1–2 h)
  • Fragment RNA at 94 °C for about 5–15 min in the presence of divalent cations; use shorter times for degraded RNA, longer for larger inserts [2]. (5–15 min)
  • Prime with random hexamers; make first-strand cDNA at 25 °C for 10 min, 42 °C for 15–50 min, then 70 °C for 15 min. Actinomycin D is optional [1][2]. (40–75 min)
  • Clean up first-strand product, or proceed directly in a one-pot kit, to remove dNTPs that contain dTTP [1][2]. (20 min)
  • Make second strand with dATP, dCTP, dGTP, dUTP, DNA polymerase I and RNase H at 16 °C for 1–2 h [1][2]. (1–2 h)
  • Purify double-stranded cDNA with 1.8× SPRI beads; wash twice with 80% ethanol and elute in low-EDTA TE or water [2]. (20 min)
  • End repair and A-tail: about 30 min at 20 °C, then 30 min at 65 °C [2]. (1 h)
  • Ligate diluted Y-adapters at 20 °C for 15 min; dilute adapters more for lower input to limit adapter dimers [2]. (15 min)
  • Digest the uracil-containing strand with UNG or USER mix at 37 °C for 15 min [1][2]. (15 min)
  • Clean up ligated DNA with 0.9× SPRI beads (about 0.9× retains insert near 200 nt); elute in 15–17 µL [2]. (20 min)
  • Amplify with indexed primers: 98 °C for 30 s; 8–16 cycles of 98 °C for 10 s and 65 °C for 75 s; 65 °C for 5 min. Use fewer cycles for more input [2]. (30–45 min)
  • Purify PCR product with 0.9× SPRI beads and elute in about 20–23 µL of 0.1× TE [2]. (20 min)
  • Check size on a Bioanalyzer (expect a peak near 300 bp); repeat 0.9× cleanup if adapter dimers near 128 bp appear [2]. (1 h)
  • Quantify by qPCR and fluorometry, normalize and pool equimolar libraries before sequencing. (1–2 h)
  • After sequencing and alignment, run RSeQC infer_experiment.py on the BAM with a gene model to measure read orientation [5]. (30 min)
  • After double-stranded cDNA purification (step 8): store at −20 °C overnight.
  • After adapter ligation and cleanup (step 12): store at −20 °C.
  • Final purified library (step 14): store at −20 °C for weeks.
  • A dominant, narrow library peak near 300 bp with little or no primer or adapter-dimer peak [2].
  • infer_experiment.py shows one orientation class at about 0.9 or higher and the other at a few percent or less; an unstranded library gives about 50:50 [5].
  • Antisense (wrong-strand) reads are low, typically a few percent or less for dUTP libraries [1][3].
  • No-RT or no-template control to detect contamination
  • External RNA spike-ins (for example ERCC) to measure strand specificity and sensitivity
  • A well-characterized reference RNA such as universal human reference RNA
  • Unstranded library from the same sample for comparison [1]

Adapter-dimer peak near 128 bpAdapter excess for the inputDilute adapters more and repeat a 0.9× bead cleanup [2]

Low yieldDegraded RNA, bead loss or too few cyclesCheck RIN, avoid bead over-drying, add 1–2 cycles [2]
High rRNA readsPoor depletion or poly(A) carryoverRepeat selection with fresh beads or switch to rRNA depletion [2]
Mixed strand signalGenomic DNA contaminationTreat RNA with DNase and verify with no-RT control [1]
  • Actinomycin D is toxic and a suspected mutagen: handle in a fume hood with gloves and dispose of as hazardous waste.
  • Wear gloves and use RNase-free technique; heated blocks reach 94–98 °C.
  • Ethanol is flammable; keep away from heat sources.
