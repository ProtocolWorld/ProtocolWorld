CUT&RUN

About 8 h (1–2 days) · 2026-11-25

Profile histone marks or transcription factors from up to 500,000 mammalian cells: bind digitonin-permeabilized cells to ConA beads, target pAG-MNase with an antibody, cut with Ca²⁺ at 0 °C, purify released fragments with spike-in and controls.

⬇ Word (.docx)⬇ PowerPoint (.pptx)

Purpose

To map where a histone modification, transcription factor or other chromatin protein sits on the genome of up to 500,000 mammalian cells, using antibody-targeted cleavage in situ instead of crosslinking and sonication.

Principle

CUT&RUN (Cleavage Under Targets and Release Using Nuclease) binds permeabilized cells to concanavalin A (ConA) magnetic beads, adds an antibody against the target, and then a protein A/G–micrococcal nuclease fusion (pAG-MNase) that attaches to the antibody. MNase is inactive without Ca²⁺, so adding CaCl₂ at 0 °C cuts DNA only next to the antibody-bound protein. The short fragments diffuse out of the intact nuclei, and EDTA/EGTA stops the reaction. They are purified and sequenced [1][2]. Because background is very low, good profiles need far fewer reads and cells than ChIP-seq; the original work reports usable data from 100 cells for a histone mark and 1,000 cells for a transcription factor [3]. Digitonin permeabilizes the cholesterol-rich plasma membrane but leaves the nuclear envelope intact [1].

Materials and reagents

ItemDetails

Wash Buffer (100 mL)20 mM HEPES-KOH pH 7.5, 150 mM NaCl, 0.5 mM spermidine, 0.1% BSA; add EDTA-free protease inhibitor fresh on the day [2]. Spermidine offsets Mg²⁺ loss [1]

DNA purification10% SDS, 20 mg/mL Proteinase K, phenol:chloroform:isoamyl alcohol 25:24:1, chloroform, phase-lock tubes, 20 mg/mL glycogen, 100% ethanol, 1 mM Tris-HCl pH 8.0 with 0.1 mM EDTA [1]

Equipment

  • Magnetic rack for 1.5 mL tubes
  • Tube rotator or nutator (4 °C and room temperature)
  • Heater block set in wet ice (0 °C)
  • 37 °C and 50 °C heat blocks or water baths
  • Microcentrifuge (16,000 × g, 4 °C)
  • Qubit fluorometer and TapeStation or equivalent

Procedure

  1. Prepare and chill the buffers (Dig-Wash, Antibody Buffer, 2× STOP). Activate beads: wash 10 µL ConA slurry per sample twice in 1.5 mL Binding Buffer, then resuspend in 10 µL [1]. (20 min)
  2. Harvest and count up to 500,000 cells at room temperature. Pellet at 600 × g for 3 min and remove the liquid. Avoid vigorous vortexing [1]. (15 min)
  3. Wash the cells three times with 1.5 mL room-temperature Wash Buffer (600 × g, 3 min each) to remove sugars that compete for ConA, then resuspend in 1 mL [1]. (15 min)
  4. While vortexing gently, add 10 µL activated beads. Rotate for 5–10 min at room temperature to bind the cells [1]. (10 min)
  5. Place on the magnet and remove the liquid. At a low vortex angle, add 150 µL ice-cold Antibody Buffer along the tube wall and tap to free the beads [1].
  6. Add primary antibody at 1:100. Set up the H3K27me3 positive control and a rabbit IgG negative control in parallel [1]. (5 min)
  7. Nutate for about 2 h at room temperature, or overnight at 4 °C [1]. (2 h to overnight)
  8. Quick-spin (no more than 100 × g), clear on the magnet and wash twice with 1 mL Dig-Wash Buffer. For mouse or goat primaries, add 1:100 secondary in 150 µL Dig-Wash, nutate 1 h at 4 °C and wash twice [1][2]. (15 min–1.5 h)
  9. Add 150 µL pAG-MNase at 700 ng/mL in Dig-Wash Buffer. Nutate at 4 °C for about 1 h [1]. (1 h)
  10. Wash twice with 1 mL Dig-Wash Buffer, then resuspend in 100 µL Dig-Wash Buffer at a low vortex angle. Chill tubes at 0 °C in a block set in wet ice [1]. (20 min)
  11. Remove one tube at a time, add 2 µL 100 mM CaCl₂ (about 2 mM) with gentle vortexing and return it to 0 °C immediately. Digestion at room temperature or above gives high background [1][2]. (1 min)
  12. Digest at 0 °C for 30 min (a good default; yield rises with time, signal-to-noise changes little) [1][3]. (30 min)
  13. Stop by adding 100 µL 2× STOP Buffer (with RNase A, glycogen and spike-in DNA) and mixing gently [1][2]. (2 min)
  14. Incubate for 30 min at 37 °C to release fragments. Clear on the magnet and move about 200 µL supernatant to a fresh tube. Freeze the bead pellet for troubleshooting [1]. (35 min)
  15. Add 2 µL 10% SDS and 2.5 µL Proteinase K (20 mg/mL). Mix by inversion and incubate 1 h at 50 °C [1]. (1 h)
  16. Add an equal volume of phenol:chloroform:isoamyl alcohol, vortex about 2 s, transfer to a phase-lock tube and spin 5 min at 16,000 × g, room temperature [1]. (10 min)
  17. Add an equal volume of chloroform, invert about 10 times and spin 5 min at 16,000 × g. Move the top phase to a tube containing 2 µL of 2 mg/mL glycogen [1]. (10 min)
  18. Add 500 µL 100% ethanol, mix, chill on ice, then spin 10 min at 4 °C and 16,000 × g. Pour off the supernatant [1]. (25 min)
  19. Rinse the pellet with 1 mL 100% ethanol (1 min, 4 °C, 16,000 × g), pour off, air-dry 5 min and dissolve in 30 µL of 1 mM Tris-HCl pH 8.0, 0.1 mM EDTA [1]. (15 min)
  20. Quantify with Qubit and check sizes on a TapeStation. The H3K27me3 control should show a nucleosomal ladder; then build libraries [1][2]. (30 min)

Pause points

  • After the antibody step, tubes can stay overnight at 4 °C while rotating [1].
  • After step 17 the ethanol precipitate can be stored at −20 °C for several days [2].
  • Purified DNA in 1 mM Tris-HCl/0.1 mM EDTA can be stored at −20 °C before library preparation [2].

Expected results

  • The H3K27me3 positive control shows a nucleosomal ladder (about 150 bp, 300 bp and so on) on a TapeStation, with yield roughly proportional to cell number [1].
  • Transcription factor samples may give too little DNA to see on a gel or TapeStation; check them after PCR-amplified library preparation [1].
  • Libraries are sequenced paired-end (25 × 25 bp is enough); about 5 million read pairs usually suffice for a histone mark or transcription factor [1].
  • Fragments of 120 bp or less come from transcription factor footprints; fragments of 150 bp or more come from nucleosomes [1][4].

Controls

  • IgG (non-specific rabbit) negative control with pAG-MNase, to show untargeted cutting [1][2]
  • Positive control antibody (H3K27me3) to confirm the workflow before sequencing precious samples [1]
  • Spike-in DNA or E. coli carry-over DNA in every sample, to normalize between samples [1]
  • Same cell number, bead amount and digestion time across the samples being compared [2]

Troubleshooting

ProblemLikely causeSolution
Beads stick to the tube or are lostLow cell number or slippery beadsUse 0.6 mL LoBind tubes with 300 µL volumes; invert or push beads down with a tip [1]
No ladder in the positive controlPoor permeabilization, inactive pAG-MNase or lost Ca²⁺ controlTest digitonin with trypan blue; titrate pAG-MNase; keep the digest at 0 °C [1][2]
High background or smearWarm digestion, excess pAG-MNase or too much digitoninDigest on wet ice, shorten the time, lower digitonin [1]
Very low DNA yieldToo few cells or a weak antibodyCheck the antibody works in immunofluorescence; use more cells; use phenol–chloroform instead of columns [1][3]
Large complex does not diffuse outFragments stay in the nucleusExtract total DNA and remove large DNA with AMPure XP negative size selection [1]

Safety and waste

  • Digitonin is toxic; weigh it in a fume hood or powder-safe enclosure and wear gloves. Dissolving it in DMSO speeds absorption through skin [1].
  • Phenol and chloroform are toxic and volatile: work in a fume hood, wear nitrile gloves and collect all organic waste separately.
  • Dispose of cells according to your institution's biosafety rules and treat all liquid waste as chemical waste where it contains phenol, chloroform or digitonin.
