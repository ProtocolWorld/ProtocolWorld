C. elegans Lifespan Assay

Estimated time: ≈ 1 week of setup, then 3–6 weeks of scoring | Protocol World

To measure the survival of a synchronized population of C. elegans on solid medium at 20 °C so that the lifespans of different strains, RNAi treatments or compounds can be compared statistically.

A synchronized cohort of animals is kept on bacterial lawns, and living and dead worms are counted at regular intervals. An animal is scored dead when it does not respond to a gentle touch of the head with a platinum pick. Progeny production is blocked with the DNA-synthesis inhibitor FUdR (floxuridine) so that adults can be followed without being transferred away from their offspring, and the bacterial food is either UV-killed or treated with ampicillin to avoid bacterial effects on aging [1][3]. Animals that crawl off the agar, rupture or are lost are censored rather than counted as deaths. Survival curves are compared by Kaplan–Meier analysis and the log-rank test [2].

NGM agar (per 100 mL)0.3 g NaCl, 0.25 g peptone, 2 g agar; autoclave 40 min, cool to 55 °C, then add 100 µL 1 M MgSO₄, 100 µL 5 mg/mL cholesterol (in ethanol), 100 µL 1 M CaCl₂ and 1.625 mL 1.5 M potassium phosphate (KPi) pH 6.0 [1]
FUdR platesAt 55 °C add 33 µL of 150 mM FUdR (≈ 50 µM final) and 100 µL of 100 mg/mL ampicillin (≈ 100 µg/mL final) per 100 mL NGM [1]; some protocols use a higher dose (0.1 mg/mL ≈ 400 µM) [3], so keep one dose for the whole experiment
Plates60 mm Petri dishes, 10 mL NGM per plate [1]
OP50 E. coliOvernight culture grown at 37 °C, concentrated 10× (3,500 × g, 10 min, remove about 90% of the supernatant) [1]
M9 buffer22 mM KH₂PO₄, 42 mM Na₂HPO₄, 85 mM NaCl, 1 mM MgSO₄ (for washing worms)
Alkaline hypochlorite solutionOnly if synchronizing by bleaching; prepare fresh per your lab recipe [2]
Worm strainsWild type (N2) and a long-lived control such as a daf-2 mutant, maintained well fed for at least 3 generations after any starvation before the assay [2]
Platinum wire pickFor transfers and touch tests
  • 20 °C incubator
  • Dissecting stereomicroscope
  • UV crosslinker (e.g., Stratalinker), if killing OP50 by UV
  • Bacterial shaker incubator (37 °C)
  • Centrifuge for 15 mL tubes and 50 mL tubes
  • Flame or bead sterilizer for the pick
  • Counter or spreadsheet for daily tallies
  • Pour 10 mL NGM per 60 mm plate, with FUdR and ampicillin added at 55 °C for the experimental plates. Let the plates set and dry at room temperature [1]. (1 day)
  • Grow OP50 overnight at 37 °C. Pellet at 3,500 × g for 10 min, remove about 90% of the supernatant and resuspend to give a 10× concentrate [1]. (≈ 1 day)
  • Spot 100 µL of 10× OP50 in the center of each plate. Dry about 24 h at room temperature, then UV-treat for about 5 min if using killed bacteria [1]. (≈ 1 day)
  • Store plates upside down at 4 °C (up to 1 month). Warm to room temperature before use and check that no live bacterial colonies grow [1]. (—)
  • Synchronize by timed egg lay: place 10–15 gravid adults on a fresh plate for 6 h at 25 °C, then remove them [1]. (6 h)
  • Alternative: bleach gravid adults (4 mL M9 + 2 mL hypochlorite, about 3 min of vortexing), wash the eggs twice in M9 and hatch them overnight at 20 °C with rotation [2]. (≈ 1 day)
  • Grow the progeny at 20 °C until the L4 stage, about 2 days for wild type. Define day 0 as the day of egg lay and write it on every plate [1]. (≈ 2 days)
  • Pick L4 larvae onto the FUdR/ampicillin plates: 25–30 animals per plate and at least 4 plates per condition (≥ 100 animals). Label plates and keep them in a box at 20 °C [1][3]. (1–2 h)
  • Check the plates after 24 h. Transfer animals to fresh plates if the food is mostly eaten, larvae appear, or bacterial or fungal contamination is seen (1–2 times a week early on) [1]. (daily at first)
  • Score every 1–3 days (daily when deaths are frequent). Tap the plate; if there is no movement, touch the head with the pick. No response means dead; remove dead animals [1][2][3]. (5–15 min/plate)
  • Censor animals that crawl up the wall or burrow, rupture, or are lost. Record males and exclude them from the analysis. Score blind to genotype where possible [2]. (at each scoring)
  • Continue until all animals are dead. Enter the day of death and the censored animals for each plate in a spreadsheet [1][2]. (3–6 weeks)
  • Analyze with Kaplan–Meier curves and the log-rank test, including censored animals. Do not pool independent trials, and plot fewer than six conditions per graph [2]. (1–2 h)
  • Poured plates can be stored at 4 °C before seeding.
  • Seeded and UV-treated plates can be stored upside down at 4 °C for up to 1 month [1].
  • After the L4 transfer the assay cannot be paused, because scoring must continue on the planned schedule.
  • Wild-type N2 at 20 °C typically shows a median lifespan of about 25 days counted from egg on killed bacteria [1].
  • The long-lived daf-2 mutant lives clearly longer than N2 and shifts the survival curve to the right [1].
  • Survival curves fall from 100% to 0% over a few weeks; replicate plates of the same strain should overlap closely.
  • Wild-type N2 on every run, with the same plates and FUdR dose
  • A long-lived control (daf-2 mutant, or daf-2(RNAi) with an empty-vector L4440 control for RNAi experiments) [2]
  • A shortened-lifespan control such as daf-16(RNAi) where RNAi is used [2]
  • Identical FUdR and ampicillin concentrations, temperature and plate position for all conditions
ProblemLikely causeSolution
Many worms crawl off the agarDry or cracked agar, or too little foodPour fresh plates, avoid over-drying the lawn, and censor animals that leave the surface [2]
Fungal or bacterial contaminationNon-sterile handling or live bacteria on platesTransfer animals to fresh plates promptly; flame the pick; check lawns after UV treatment [1]
Larvae appear on the platesProgeny from eggs laid before FUdR took effect, or FUdR that is too diluteCheck the FUdR stock, make sure plates contain FUdR when L4 animals are transferred, and move adults to fresh plates [1]
Early or inconsistent deathsMated animals, males on the plate, or injury during transferRemove and record males; transfer gently with a clean pick [2]
Large differences between trialsDifferent temperatures, FUdR doses or day-0 definitionsStandardize and report the temperature, FUdR dose, bacteria and how day 0 was defined
  • Wear gloves and a lab coat. Hypochlorite solution is corrosive: avoid skin and eye contact and do not mix it with acid.
  • FUdR is a cytotoxic nucleoside analog: avoid skin contact and inhalation of powder; handle stock solutions with gloves.
  • UV light damages eyes and skin: operate the crosslinker with its door closed.
  • Discard worms, plates and bacteria as biological waste (autoclave); dispose of FUdR and ampicillin plates according to your institution's chemical waste rules.
