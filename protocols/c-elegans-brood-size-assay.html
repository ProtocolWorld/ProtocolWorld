C. elegans Brood Size Assay

C. elegans · 7–8 days (5 days of transfers + 2–3 days of counting) · 2026-11-27

Follow single C. elegans hermaphrodites from L4, transfer every 24 h for 5 days, count living progeny, dead eggs and unfertilized eggs, and compare brood size and embryonic lethality with wild type.

C. elegans Brood Size Assay

Estimated time: 7–8 days | Protocol World

To measure the number of self-progeny produced by individual C. elegans hermaphrodites, together with embryonic lethality and unfertilized eggs, so that fertility of mutants, RNAi treatments or drug exposures can be compared with wild type.

A self-fertilizing hermaphrodite makes a fixed pool of sperm during larval development and then lays fertilized eggs until sperm run out. Output is therefore set by sperm number, with later oocytes laid unfertilized, so each worm is followed individually from the L4 stage and moved to a fresh plate every 24 h until egg laying stops [1][2]. Progeny are scored a few days after each transfer, when hatched larvae and dead eggs are easy to count, and any animal that is lost, dies or bags is excluded entirely [1].

  • 20 °C incubator
  • Platinum pick and Bunsen burner or pick sterilizer

  • Plate marker pen
  • Spreadsheet or statistics software
  • Maintain all strains at 20 °C on OP50-seeded NGM for several generations without starvation so that animals are healthy and synchronized [2][3]. (days)
  • Prepare and label five plates per worm (D1–D5) with strain and worm number, and let them equilibrate to 20 °C. (30 min)
  • Pick L4 hermaphrodites (pale vulval crescent, no eggs) singly onto D1 plates, 10–12 per genotype; this is time zero [1][3]. (30 min)
  • Incubate at 20 °C for 24 h [1][3]. (24 h)
  • Move each adult to a fresh plate with a clean pick without carrying eggs; shorten to 12 h early on if you need a finer egg-laying profile [1][3]. (20 min)
  • Check each worm at every transfer. If one is missing, dead, or has bagged, exclude all of her data for the experiment [1]. (during transfers)
  • Repeat the 24 h transfer for 5 days in total, or until she lays no more fertilized eggs [1]. (5 days)
  • Return every old plate to 20 °C. Count 2–3 days after the worm was removed, before progeny become adults and lay eggs [1][3]. (2–3 days)
  • Count living progeny, dead (unhatched) eggs and unfertilized eggs separately; remove larvae as counted to avoid double counting [1]. (per plate)
  • Brood size = living progeny + dead eggs summed over all plates. Unfertilized eggs are scored but not included [1]. (15 min)
  • Embryonic lethality = dead eggs ÷ (living progeny + dead eggs); also tabulate daily output per worm [1][2]. (15 min)
  • Compare genotypes or treatments to wild type run in the same batch, e.g., with a t-test or Mann–Whitney U, or ANOVA with post-hoc tests for several groups [1][2]. (1 h)
  • Plates for counting can be held at 20 °C for the 2–3 day window; do not hold them longer or the progeny will mature.
  • Counting results can be saved and analyzed later.
  • Wild-type N2 at 20 °C gives roughly 290–330 progeny per hermaphrodite, depending on the protocol [1][2].
  • Egg laying peaks above 140 eggs/day, with about 90% of output in the first 3–4 days of adulthood [2][3].
  • Dead eggs should be rare in N2; unfertilized eggs appear at the end of the reproductive period once self-sperm are used up [1][2].
  • Wild-type N2 raised in the same batch and on the same plates
  • Same temperature (20 °C), food and plate type for every genotype
  • Vehicle-only or empty-vector plates for drug or RNAi tests
  • At least 10–12 animals per genotype, as used in published assays [1]
