MitoTracker Staining of Mitochondria

About 1.5 h (+1 h if fixed) · 2026-11-23

Label mitochondria in live cultured mammalian cells with MitoTracker dyes: stock preparation, working concentrations, staining and imaging, optional fixation, FCCP depolarization control and troubleshooting.

⬇ Word (.docx)⬇ PowerPoint (.pptx)

Purpose

To label mitochondria in living cultured mammalian cells with a MitoTracker dye so that mitochondrial morphology, network distribution and membrane potential can be examined by fluorescence microscopy, with an optional fixation step for co-staining.

Principle

MitoTracker probes are cell-permeant, mildly thiol-reactive dyes that carry a positive charge. They cross the plasma membrane, accumulate in mitochondria that maintain a negative membrane potential, and (for the chloromethyl and Deep Red variants) bond to protein thiols, which lets the signal survive aldehyde fixation [1][2][4]. Green FM is only weakly fluorescent in water and becomes bright in the lipid environment of mitochondria, but it is not retained after fixation [1][2]. Because uptake depends on potential, a loss of membrane potential lowers the signal, and high dye concentrations or long incubations stain other structures and harm cells, so the lowest effective concentration should be used [1][2][4].

Materials and reagents

Equipment

  • Biosafety cabinet
  • CO₂ incubator (37 °C, 5% CO₂)
  • Fluorescence or confocal microscope with matching filters and a 60× or 63× oil objective
  • Heated stage with CO₂ control for live imaging (recommended)
  • Micropipettes and amber microtubes
  • −20 °C freezer

Procedure

  1. Warm the vial to room temperature and dissolve 50 µg of dye in anhydrous DMSO to 1 mM (about 94 µL for CMXRos; see the label). Store at −20 °C, dark [1]. (10 min)
  2. Pre-warm medium and PBS to 37 °C. Prepare the staining solution by diluting the stock to 25–200 nM (for example, 1:5000 gives 200 nM) in warm medium and mix well [1][2]. (5 min)
  3. Prepare a vehicle control (same DMSO dilution, no dye) and, for the control dish, a depolarized sample treated with 5–10 µM FCCP for 10–15 min before imaging.
  4. Remove the medium from the dish and add enough staining solution to cover the cells (for a 35 mm dish, about 1 mL) [1]. (2 min)
  5. Incubate at 37 °C, 5% CO₂ for 15–45 min (20 min is a good starting point for 50 nM Deep Red FM) [1][3]. (15–45 min)
  6. Remove the staining solution and wash once with warm PBS (or replace with fresh warm medium) to lower background [1][3]. (3 min)
  7. Add fresh pre-warmed imaging medium (phenol red–free) and keep the dish at 37 °C [1][3]. (2 min)
  8. For live imaging, image soon after staining. Use the lowest laser power and shortest exposure that give a clear signal, and avoid repeated exposures [2][3]. (30–60 min)
  9. Optional fixation: add 3.7% formaldehyde in warm medium, 37 °C, 15 min. Use fixable dyes only (CMXRos, Deep Red FM) [1][2]; one vendor note reports poor retention [5]. (15 min)
  10. Rinse three times with PBS. For immunostaining, permeabilize with 0.2% Triton X-100 in PBS for 10 min, or ice-cold acetone for 5 min [1]. (15 min)
  11. Block and stain with antibodies if needed (for example, anti-TOM20 as a mitochondrial reference), then mount and image. Expect a lower signal-to-noise ratio after fixation [1].

Pause points

  • After fixation and permeabilization, samples can be stored in PBS at 4 °C in the dark for a few days before staining or mounting.
  • The 1 mM dye stock is stable at −20 °C or below, protected from light; avoid repeated freeze–thaw cycles [1].

Expected results

  • Healthy cells show a bright, filamentous or reticular network around the nucleus, extending toward the cell periphery.
  • Depolarized cells (FCCP) lose or redistribute the signal and mitochondria often become fragmented.
  • Unstained and vehicle-only cells show only background autofluorescence.

Controls

  • Unstained cells imaged with the same settings to define background
  • DMSO vehicle control (final DMSO at or below 0.1%)
  • Depolarization control: FCCP or CCCP, 5–10 µM, to show that uptake depends on membrane potential
  • Anti-TOM20 immunostaining or a mitochondria-targeted fluorescent protein to confirm localization [4]

Troubleshooting

ProblemLikely causeSolution
Weak signalDye concentration too low, old stock, depolarized cellsUse fresh stock, raise concentration within 25–200 nM, check cell health [1][2]
Diffuse or non-mitochondrial stainingToo much dye or too long an incubationLower the concentration and shorten incubation [1][2]
Signal lost after fixationNon-fixable dye (Green FM) or too low a dye concentrationUse CMXRos or Deep Red FM at 100–500 nM, or image live [1][2][5]
Cells round up or blebs form during imagingPhototoxicity or toxic dye levelReduce light dose; use a far-red dye; image soon after staining [2][3]
High backgroundDye left in medium or medium autofluorescenceWash once with PBS and image in phenol red–free medium [1][3]
Uneven staining between cellsDifferent membrane potentials or uneven dye mixingMix staining solution well; confirm with the FCCP control

Safety and waste

  • DMSO carries dissolved chemicals through the skin: wear nitrile gloves and a lab coat.
  • Formaldehyde and paraformaldehyde are toxic and a suspected carcinogen: use in a fume hood or biosafety cabinet and collect waste as hazardous chemical waste.
  • FCCP and CCCP are toxic if swallowed or on skin [3]; collect dye, DMSO and FCCP waste in labeled chemical containers.
  • MitoTracker dyes are for research use only; read the safety data sheet before use [1].
  • Treat cell lines as potentially infectious and follow your biosafety rules for liquid waste.
