Silver Staining of Protein Gels

General Lab Methods · 4–5 h · 2026-11-26

Step-by-step silver staining of SDS-PAGE mini-gels, using a mass-spectrometry-compatible thiosulfate/formaldehyde recipe with notes on the classic glutaraldehyde variant. Detects low-nanogram protein bands.

Silver Staining of Protein Gels

Estimated time: 4–5 h | Protocol World

To visualize proteins in SDS-polyacrylamide gels at low-nanogram sensitivity, with a variant that leaves bands usable for mass-spectrometric identification.

Proteins are fixed in the gel, then silver ions bind to the protein. Under alkaline conditions formaldehyde reduces the bound silver to dark metallic silver, and an acid stop ends the reaction [1][3]. Thiosulfate sensitizing improves contrast and limits background [3]. Classic protocols add glutaraldehyde, which cross-links proteins and boosts signal but blocks peptide recovery; omit it when bands will be identified by mass spectrometry [1][4][5].

ItemDetails
Fixer50% methanol, 12% acetic acid (about 100 mL per mini-gel); 5% acetic acid–50% methanol is also used [2][5][6]
Sensitizer0.02% (w/v) sodium thiosulfate pentahydrate in ultrapure water, made fresh (0.1 g per 500 mL) [3][6]
Silver solution0.1% (w/v) silver nitrate, chilled (0.5 g per 500 mL), prepared in glass and protected from light [6]
Developer2% sodium carbonate with 0.04% formalin (15 g carbonate plus 125 µL 37% formaldehyde per 500 mL); add formaldehyde just before use [1][6]
Stop solution5% acetic acid [6]; the classic glutaraldehyde protocol instead stops in 5% (w/v) Tris base with 2% (v/v) acetic acid [4]
Classic glutaraldehyde variant only0.5 M sodium acetate with 1% glutaraldehyde, then ammoniacal silver nitrate; not for mass spectrometry [4]
Ultrapure waterResistivity ≥15 MΩ·cm; no denatured alcohol [1]
Protein standardsMolecular-weight ladder and a BSA dilution series (for example 1, 5, 10, 50 and 100 ng per lane)
Staining traysClean glass or polypropylene dishes, rinsed with plenty of water after detergent [6]
  • Orbital or rocking shaker (30–60 cycles/min) [1]
  • Glass or polypropylene staining trays
  • Powder-free nitrile gloves [1]
  • Light box or white-light scanner
  • Fume hood for formaldehyde and acetic acid
  • Designated silver waste container
  • Run the SDS-PAGE gel with the ladder and a BSA dilution series. Wearing powder-free nitrile gloves, pry the plates apart and notch one corner of the gel [1][6]. (1–1.5 h)
  • Fix the gel in 100 mL fixer (50% methanol, 12% acetic acid) with gentle rocking for at least 1 h; overnight is preferred for best results [1][6]. (1 h–overnight)
  • Wash in 50% methanol for 20 min, then twice in ultrapure water for 20 min each to remove acid and SDS [6]. (60 min)
  • Sensitize in 100 mL of 0.02% sodium thiosulfate for exactly 1 min [3][6]. (1 min)
  • Rinse twice in ultrapure water for 1 min each. Use fresh water each time to remove excess thiosulfate [6]. (2 min)
  • Stain in chilled 0.1% silver nitrate for 20 min with gentle rocking, in the dark if possible [6]. Blum's classic version uses 0.2% silver for 45 min [3]. (20 min)
  • Rinse twice in ultrapure water for 1 min each. Prepare the developer now, adding formaldehyde just before use [1][6]. (2 min)
  • Develop in 100 mL of 2% sodium carbonate with 0.04% formalin. Watch closely; bands appear within about 1–10 min and the exact time varies [1][6]. (1–10 min)
  • When the faintest BSA band is visible and background is still pale, pour off the developer and add 5% acetic acid at once to stop the reaction [6]. (10 min)
  • Wash in ultrapure water for 2 × 10 min. Image the gel on a white-light scanner or light box, ideally within about 24 h [4]. (20 min)
  • Store the gel in 1% acetic acid at 4 °C, or in a sealed bag with a little water, away from light [4][6].
  • Classic variant (not mass-spec compatible): fix in 40% ethanol/10% acetic acid, then soak in 0.5 M sodium acetate with 1% glutaraldehyde for 30 min and wash three times for 10 min each [4]. (60 min)
  • Classic variant: stain in freshly made ammoniacal silver nitrate for 30 min, rinse four times for 4 min each, and develop in 0.01% citric acid with 0.1% formaldehyde for 5–10 min [4]. (45–60 min)
  • Classic variant: stop in 5% Tris with 2% acetic acid for 30 min, then rinse in water for at least 30 min [4]. (60 min)
  • For mass spectrometry, use the thiosulfate/formalin recipe above and avoid glutaraldehyde, which cross-links proteins and blocks peptide recovery [5][6]. (note)
  • To excise bands for digestion, cover each piece with a 1:1 mix of 30 mM potassium ferricyanide and 100 mM sodium thiosulfate until the brown color clears [1][3]. (5–10 min)
  • Rinse destained pieces five times in water, soak in 200 mM ammonium bicarbonate for 20 min, then proceed to reduction, alkylation and digestion [1][5]. (45 min)
  • After electrophoresis: the gel can stay in fixer overnight [1][6].
  • After step 3, a fixed gel can be held in water for a short time before sensitizing.
  • After the stop solution: the gel can be stored in 1% acetic acid at 4 °C [6].
  • Do not pause during sensitizing, silver, or development steps because they are time critical [3].
  • Bands from about 1–5 ng of BSA are visible, roughly 30–100 times more sensitive than colloidal Coomassie [1].
  • Bands are dark brown to black on a clear background, with the ladder visible.
  • The BSA series darkens with load, but staining saturates at higher amounts and is not linear.
  • BSA dilution series (for example 1–100 ng per lane) to judge sensitivity and linearity limits
  • Molecular-weight ladder in every gel
  • Blank lane with sample buffer only to reveal background and keratin streaks
  • A Coomassie-stained duplicate gel to compare staining patterns before silver
ProblemLikely causeSolution
High background or brown gelOverdevelopment, poor water quality, or incomplete washesStop earlier and wash thoroughly. Use ≥15 MΩ·cm water [1]
Silver mirror or metallic filmDirty plates or trays; finger pressure on the gelClean dishes well and handle gels minimally with powder-free gloves [1]
Weak or no bandsWrong silver or developer, cold solutions, or exhausted reagentsCheck recipes, use carbonate (not bicarbonate), warm to 18–25 °C. Rinse well and restart [1]
Yellow background or black developerThiosulfate in the developer is used upReplace thiosulfate with fresh stock [1]
Streaks at the top of lanesKeratin from skin or dustWear nitrile gloves, use dedicated trays and clean the plates [5][6]
Heavy overstainingDeveloper left too longDestain with ferricyanide–thiosulfate, rinse and restart sensitization. Mass-spec results will be poor [1]
  • Silver nitrate stains skin and is toxic to aquatic life: collect all silver waste for hazardous-waste pickup and never pour it down the drain.
  • Formaldehyde and formalin are toxic, irritating and carcinogenic: use in a fume hood with gloves and eye protection.
  • Glutaraldehyde is a sensitizer and toxic: use only in a fume hood and dispose of as chemical waste.
  • Methanol is toxic and flammable, and acetic acid is corrosive. Sodium thiosulfate is low hazard, but follow your institution's waste rules.
