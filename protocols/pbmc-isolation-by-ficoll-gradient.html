PBMC Isolation by Ficoll Gradient

2–3 h · 2026-11-26

Isolate PBMCs from human whole blood on Ficoll-Paque (1.077 g/mL): dilute 1:1, layer, spin 400 × g for 30 min with the brake off, harvest the interface, wash, count, and optionally cryopreserve.

⬇ Word (.docx)⬇ PowerPoint (.pptx)

Purpose

To isolate viable peripheral blood mononuclear cells (lymphocytes and monocytes) from human whole blood by density gradient centrifugation, free of most red cells, granulocytes and platelets.

Principle

Ficoll-Paque is a sterile solution of Ficoll and sodium diatrizoate with a density of 1.077 g/mL. After centrifugation, erythrocytes and granulocytes are denser and sediment through the medium, whereas mononuclear cells are lighter and collect as a band at the plasma–medium interface [1][2]. Keeping the layers undisturbed, using room-temperature reagents, and turning the centrifuge brake off preserve a sharp interface. Low-speed washes then remove Ficoll and platelets [3][4].

Equipment

  • Class II biosafety cabinet
  • Swing-out bucket centrifuge with adjustable brake and aerosol-tight lids
  • Hemocytometer or automated cell counter
  • Controlled-rate freezing container (optional)
  • Liquid nitrogen storage (optional)
  • Lab coat, gloves and eye protection

Procedure

  1. Bring Ficoll-Paque, PBS + 2% FBS and the blood to room temperature (18–25 °C). Work in a biosafety cabinet and wear gloves, lab coat and eye protection [1][4]. (15 min)
  2. Dilute the blood 1:1 with PBS + 2% FBS in a 50 mL tube (for example 10 mL blood + 10 mL buffer) and mix gently by inversion [1][3]. (5 min)
  3. Pipette 15 mL of Ficoll-Paque into a fresh 50 mL tube. For smaller batches use 3 mL of Ficoll under 4 mL of diluted blood in a 15 mL tube [1][3]. (5 min)
  4. Tilt the Ficoll tube about 45°. Add the 20 mL of diluted blood slowly along the tube wall so it floats on the Ficoll without mixing. Do not disturb the layers afterward [1][4]. (10 min)
  5. Centrifuge in a swing-out rotor at 400 × g for 30 min at 18–20 °C with the brake off and slow acceleration. Some protocols use 800 × g for 20–30 min [1][2][3]. (30–40 min)
  6. Remove the tubes carefully so the layers stay intact. From the top you see plasma (with platelets), a cloudy PBMC band, clear Ficoll, then a red granulocyte/erythrocyte pellet [1][4]. (2 min)
  7. Aspirate about half of the upper plasma layer and discard it, taking care not to touch the PBMC band. This lowers platelet carry-over [5]. (3 min)
  8. Insert a transfer pipette through the remaining plasma and collect the whole cloudy interface with as little Ficoll as possible. Excess Ficoll brings in granulocytes [1][2]. (5 min)
  9. Transfer the cells to a fresh 50 mL tube. Add PBS + 2% FBS to 45 mL (at least 3 volumes of the harvest) and mix gently by inversion to dilute residual Ficoll [1][4]. (3 min)
  10. Centrifuge at 300 × g for 10 min at room temperature with the brake on low. Pour off or aspirate the supernatant without disturbing the pellet [4]. (15 min)
  11. Loosen the pellet by flicking the tube, resuspend in 10 mL of PBS + 2% FBS, fill to 45 mL and repeat the spin. A third wash at 100–200 × g for 10 min removes more platelets [1][2][4]. (25 min)
  12. If the pellet is clearly red, resuspend in 6 mL of RBC lysis buffer, incubate 5 min at room temperature, dilute to 40–45 mL with PBS + 2% FBS and spin at 400 × g for 8 min [5]. (15 min)
  13. Resuspend the pellet in 1–5 mL of PBS + 2% FBS or culture medium. Mix 10 µL of cells with 10 µL of 0.4% trypan blue and count live and dead cells [4]. (10 min)
  14. For immediate use, adjust to the density required by your assay (for example 1–2 × 10⁶ cells/mL in RPMI + 10% FBS) and rest cells at 37 °C, 5% CO₂ if the downstream assay calls for it. (5 min)
  15. To freeze, spin at 300 × g for 10 min, resuspend at 5 × 10⁶ cells/mL in cold 90% FBS + 10% DMSO, put 1 mL per cryovial in a controlled-rate container at −80 °C [5][6]. (20 min)
  16. After at least 4 h to overnight at −80 °C, move the vials to the vapor phase of liquid nitrogen (below −135 °C) for long-term storage [3][6]. (overnight)

Pause points

  • After the first washes, cells can be held at 4 °C for a short time in PBS + 2% FBS, but process promptly to protect viability.
  • Frozen vials can be stored in liquid nitrogen for years once transferred from −80 °C.

Expected results

  • A visible, cloudy PBMC band at the plasma–Ficoll interface, with a red pellet below.
  • With a good draw and technique, Ficoll-Paque PLUS gives roughly 95% mononuclear cells, about 95% viability and about 60% recovery, with only minor granulocyte (about 3%), erythrocyte (about 5%) and platelet contamination [1].
  • Yield per mL of blood varies by donor and anticoagulant; record the count and viability for each donor.

Controls

  • Count and record viability before every downstream experiment
  • Process a blood sample from a healthy donor in parallel when validating a new batch of Ficoll or a new operator
  • Check a small aliquot by flow cytometry for lymphocyte and monocyte populations if purity matters

Troubleshooting

ProblemLikely causeSolution
Many red cells in the PBMC layerBlood column too tall, undiluted blood, or temperature too low or too highDilute the blood 1:1, use a wider tube, keep everything at 18–20 °C [1]
Layers mixed during loadingBlood poured too quickly or tube bumpedLayer slowly along the wall of a tilted tube; carry tubes gently into the centrifuge
Poor band formation or low yieldOld blood, cold or warm reagents, brake usedUse fresh blood, room-temperature reagents, brake off [1][2]
Platelet contaminationInsufficient washingAdd a low-speed wash (100–200 × g) or remove extra plasma before harvesting [1][5]
Granulocyte contaminationToo much Ficoll collected with the bandCollect only the cloudy interface and as little Ficoll as possible [1]
Low viability after thawingSlow freezing or long exposure to DMSOChill freezing medium, work quickly, use a controlled-rate container

Safety and waste

  • Human blood is a potential biohazard: use BSL-2 practices and universal precautions, and follow your institution's bloodborne pathogen training [4].
  • Use aerosol-tight centrifuge lids and open tubes only in a biosafety cabinet.
  • Decontaminate liquid waste and disposables with 10% bleach before disposal in biohazard containers, following your institution's biosafety rules [4].
  • DMSO penetrates skin; wear gloves. Liquid nitrogen causes cold burns: use cryo gloves and a face shield.
