Single-Cell RNA-seq Sample Preparation

Estimated time: About 2–4 h | Protocol World

To prepare a clean, viable single-cell suspension from fresh tissue (or cultured cells) at the concentration and purity needed for droplet-based single-cell RNA sequencing, such as the 10x Genomics Chromium platform.

Droplet-based scRNA-seq captures single cells in nanoliter droplets, so cell clumps, debris, dead cells and inhibitory buffer components directly reduce data quality. A good preparation uses gentle but effective dissociation, cold handling in a simple buffer (PBS with 0.04% BSA), filtration, removal of red blood cells and dead cells when needed, and an accurate count adjusted to the target concentration [1][2]. Fast processing limits stress-response gene induction and RNA degradation, and a pilot run on spare tissue is the best way to optimize the enzyme mix and time [1][3].

ItemDetails
Wash buffer1× PBS without Ca²⁺/Mg²⁺ plus 0.04% molecular-biology-grade (UltraPure) BSA; filter sterilize and keep on ice [1][2]
Dissociation enzyme mixFor soft mouse tumor tissue: 2.35 mL RPMI or DMEM + 100 µL Enzyme D + 50 µL Enzyme R + 12.5 µL Enzyme A per gentleMACS C tube (Miltenyi Tumor Dissociation Kit) [2]
Culture medium, coldRPMI or DMEM, with or without up to 10% FBS to quench enzymes [1][2]
RBC lysis solution1× RBC removal solution made in double-distilled water, chilled; or ACK buffer, 4 mL for 4 min at room temperature (alternative) [2][3]
Trypan blue0.4% solution, mixed 1:1 with the cell suspension [2][3]
Dead cell removal kit (optional)For example, Miltenyi Dead Cell Removal Kit, or a FACS viability dye such as DAPI [1]
Cell strainers70 µm (prewetted) and 40 µm strainers, or Flowmi 40 µm tip strainers [1][2]
RNase inhibitor (RNase-rich tissue or nuclei)0.2 U/µL or higher in the final buffer if recommended by your sequencing core [1]
Low-bind tubes1.5–2 mL tubes, kept on ice [1]
  • gentleMACS Octo Dissociator with Heaters, or a heated shaker for manual digestion
  • Refrigerated centrifuge with a swinging-bucket rotor (4 °C and room temperature)
  • Biosafety cabinet
  • Automated cell counter (for example Countess) or hemocytometer, and a microscope
  • Glass dish and scalpels for mincing
  • Ice buckets and prechilled racks
  • Agree on buffer, target concentration (about 1,000–1,500 cells/µL for 3′ v4 chemistry) and minimum volume (50 µL) with your sequencing core. Pilot the dissociation on spare tissue [1]. (Before the day)
  • Chill wash buffer, medium and tubes on ice. Prewet strainers, set the dissociator or heater to the enzyme program and thaw enzyme aliquots [2]. (20 min)
  • Collect tissue into cold PBS or medium on ice and start the dissociation as soon as possible; surgical samples should reach dissociation in under 1 h [3][4]. (Under 1 h)
  • Mince the tissue in a glass dish on ice with a scalpel into pieces of about 2–4 mm³. Avoid plastic dishes, which shed debris [1][2]. (5–10 min)
  • Transfer the pieces to a gentleMACS C tube with the enzyme mix (2.35 mL medium + 100 µL Enzyme D + 50 µL Enzyme R + 12.5 µL Enzyme A) and run the 37 °C program for the tissue type [2]. (30–45 min)
  • Stop the digestion with 10 mL cold medium. Pass the suspension through a prewetted 70 µm strainer and rinse the strainer with 10 mL medium [2]. (5 min)
  • Centrifuge at 300 × g for 7 min at room temperature. Discard the supernatant and resuspend the pellet gently in cold wash buffer. Do not vortex [2]. (10 min)
  • If the pellet is red, add 1 mL chilled RBC lysis solution for 10 min at 4 °C, add 10 mL cold wash buffer and centrifuge at 300 × g for 10 min at 4 °C [2]. (25 min)
  • Resuspend in 1–2 mL cold wash buffer and pass through a 40 µm strainer to remove clumps. Keep the cells on ice [1][3]. (5 min)
  • Mix 10 µL of cells with 10 µL of 0.4% trypan blue and count with an automated counter or hemocytometer. Record total cells, viability, clumps and debris [2][3]. (10 min)
  • If viability is below about 70%, remove dead cells with a magnetic kit or sort by FACS with a viability dye, then recount. Below 20% dead cells is a typical acceptance line [1][2]. (30–60 min)
  • Adjust the concentration: dilute with cold wash buffer, or concentrate by centrifugation (300 × g, 5 min) and leave a small volume. Recount and load immediately [1][2]. (15 min)
  • The tissue can wait on ice in cold buffer or medium for a short time, but dissociation should begin within 1 h of collection [4].
  • Once the final suspension is counted, keep it on ice and load it into the instrument right away; any hold time must have been tested in a pilot [1].
  • If tissue cannot be processed fresh, cryopreserve intact tissue or use nuclei isolation from frozen tissue (snRNA-seq) instead [1][3].
  • A final single-cell suspension of 1,000–1,500 cells/µL (older 10x guidance: 700–1,200 cells/µL) with at least 70–80% viable cells [1][2].
  • Few clumps and little debris on the hemocytometer or counter image.
  • After sequencing, a few percent doublets (about 0.4% at 1,000 cells and 4% at 10,000 recovered cells) are expected for droplet platforms [1].
  • Pilot dissociation on spare tissue to optimize enzyme mix and time before the real sample [1]
  • Count each sample twice (or by two methods) and compare; FACS counts often overestimate [1]
  • A healthy cultured cell line processed in the same buffer, as a viability and counting reference
  • A buffer-only tube carried through the same steps to check for debris or RNA contamination
ProblemLikely causeSolution
Low viabilityOver-digestion, rough pipetting or slow handlingShorten digestion, handle gently, keep cells cold and add dead cell removal [1][2]
Many clumpsFree DNA from dead cells or poor dissociationFilter through 40 µm, add a short DNase step if your core allows, and resuspend gently [1][3]
Lots of debrisOver-mincing or dead cellsAdd one or two extra washes, filter, or use FACS or a debris removal step [1][2]
Too few cellsCell loss in washes or low yieldCheck the first supernatant for lost cells and concentrate by gentle centrifugation [1]
Poor RNA capture after sequencingEDTA, serum or high-RNase tissueAvoid EDTA over 0.5 mM, wash serum, and use an RNase inhibitor for RNase-rich tissue [1]
  • Primary human cells may carry pathogens. Work at least at biosafety level 2 and follow institutional rules for human tissue handling and consent [1][3].
  • Use sealed double containers for transporting human tissue and treat all waste as biohazardous (autoclave or disinfect as your biosafety office specifies) [3].
  • Trypan blue is a suspected carcinogen; wear gloves and discard according to chemical waste rules.
  • Use scalpels carefully and discard them in a sharps container.
