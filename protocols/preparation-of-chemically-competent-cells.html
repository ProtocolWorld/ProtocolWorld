Preparation of Chemically Competent Cells

Estimated time: 2 days (4–5 h hands-on) | Protocol World

To prepare frozen, chemically competent E. coli (DH5α, TOP10 or XL1-Blue) by the CaCl₂ method, with the Inoue method as a higher-efficiency option, and to confirm quality with a pUC19 transformation (expected 10⁶–10⁸ cfu/µg).

Cells harvested in early to mid-log growth are chilled and washed in ice-cold divalent-cation buffer. Ca²⁺ (and, in the Inoue method, Mn²⁺, Ca²⁺ and K⁺ in PIPES buffer) neutralizes charge repulsion between DNA and the cell envelope, so plasmid DNA adheres to the cells on ice [1][2][6]. A brief 42 °C heat shock then promotes DNA uptake, and recovery in rich medium lets the antibiotic-resistance gene be expressed before plating [2][5]. Glycerol (CaCl₂ method) or DMSO (Inoue) protects cells during snap-freezing and −80 °C storage [1][3][4]. Efficiency depends strongly on growth density: the CaCl₂ method uses OD600 about 0.3–0.4, while the Inoue method uses slow growth at 18–22 °C to OD600 about 0.55 [1][3][4]. The Inoue method typically gives the highest efficiencies [1]. Cells are checked with a known quantity of supercoiled pUC19.

ConsumablesSterile 50 mL conical tubes or centrifuge bottles, pre-chilled 1.5 mL or 2 mL screw-cap tubes (thin-walled tubes give better heat shock [2]), sterile spreader beads, ice
  • Refrigerated centrifuge (4 °C) with rotor for 50 mL tubes or 250 mL bottles, able to reach 2,700 × g
  • Shaking incubator at 37 °C (and an 18–22 °C shaker or cool room for the Inoue method)
  • Spectrophotometer and cuvettes for OD600
  • 42 °C water bath (a dry block works less well [2])
  • Biosafety cabinet or clean bench, Bunsen burner or ethanol-flamed tools
  • Ice bucket, dry ice–ethanol bath or liquid nitrogen with a Dewar
  • −80 °C freezer with labeled boxes
  • 37 °C static incubator for plates
  • Calibrated micropipettes with filter tips
  • Streak E. coli from a glycerol stock onto antibiotic-free LB agar and grow overnight at 37 °C to get single colonies. Use fresh colonies for every batch [3]. (Day 0, overnight)
  • Pick one colony into 10 mL LB in a 50 mL tube. Shake at 37 °C and 200–250 rpm for 12–16 h (overnight) [4]. (12–16 h)
  • Pre-chill 100 mM CaCl₂, 100 mM CaCl₂ + 15% glycerol, centrifuge tubes, rotor and centrifuge to 4 °C. Place 1.5 mL aliquot tubes at −20 °C or on ice [4]. (Day 1, 30 min)
  • CaCl₂ method: dilute the overnight culture 1:100 to 1:200 into pre-warmed LB without antibiotic (for example 1 mL into 100 mL in a 500 mL flask) [4]. (5 min)
  • Shake vigorously at 37 °C and 200–250 rpm. Read OD600 from about 1.5 h; adjust the timing so cells are in early to mid-log phase [2][4]. (2–3 h)
  • Harvest at OD600 0.3–0.4 (a reading of 0.5 is the upper limit; later cultures lose competence). Place the flask on ice at once for 20 min, swirling now and then [4]. (20 min)
  • Transfer the chilled culture to sterile pre-chilled 50 mL tubes. Keep everything on ice from here on, and handle cells gently; never vortex [2][4].
  • Pellet the cells at about 2,700 × g (roughly 4,000 rpm in many rotors) for 10 min at 4 °C. Pour off the medium and blot inverted tubes on sterile paper, removing every drop [3][4]. (10 min)
  • Gently resuspend each pellet in 1/2 culture volume (about 20 mL per 50 mL tube) of ice-cold 100 mM CaCl₂ by swirling or slow pipetting with a wide-bore tip [4].
  • Incubate on ice for 30 min [4]. Cells tolerate a longer cold hold (1–2 h) in the related Rb/Mn protocol [2]. (30 min)
  • Pellet again at about 2,700 × g for 10 min at 4 °C. Discard the supernatant completely [4]. (10 min)
  • Resuspend very gently in 1/50 of the original culture volume (2 mL for 100 mL) of ice-cold 100 mM CaCl₂ + 15% glycerol. Pool tubes if you used several [4].
  • Hold the suspension on ice for 15–30 min. Cells can be used fresh at this stage, but freezing in aliquots is recommended. (15–30 min)
  • Dispense 50–100 µL into pre-chilled, labeled tubes. Work fast and keep tubes on ice until freezing; retain 2 aliquots for efficiency testing [3][4]. (10 min)
  • Snap-freeze the tubes in liquid nitrogen or a dry ice–ethanol bath for about 1 min, then move to −80 °C [2][3]. Do not use −20 °C: competence is lost [4].
  • Inoue variant, afternoon (about 6 pm): inoculate SOB (250 mL in a 1 L flask) with 2, 4 and 10 mL of a 6–8 h LB starter, in three separate flasks, and shake at 18–22 °C, 200–250 rpm [3]. (Overnight)
  • Inoue variant, morning: read OD600 of each flask every 45 min. Keep the flask that reaches OD600 0.55 (acceptable 0.5–0.6) and discard the others [1][3]. (1–2 h)
  • Chill the chosen flask in an ice bath for 10 min, then pellet at 2,500 × g for 10 min at 4 °C. Drain the bottle and remove the last drops [3]. (20 min)
  • Resuspend gently in ice-cold Inoue buffer at 0.32 × the original volume (80 mL for 250 mL). Spin again at 2,500 × g for 10 min at 4 °C and drain fully [3].
  • Resuspend in ice-cold Inoue buffer at 0.08 × the original volume (20 mL). Add DMSO to 7% (1.5 mL to 20 mL cells), mix by swirling, and chill on ice for 10 min [1][3]. (15 min)
  • Dispense 50 µL aliquots into pre-chilled tubes and snap-freeze in liquid nitrogen. Store at −80 °C; cells keep for at least a year [3][4]. (15 min)
  • Efficiency test: thaw one aliquot (50 µL) on ice for about 10 min. Add 1 µL of 100 pg/µL pUC19 (100 pg) and flick 4–5 times to mix; do not vortex [2][5]. (10 min)
  • Incubate on ice for 30 min without mixing [2][5]. (30 min)
  • Heat shock at 42 °C for 45 s (30–45 s depending on tube wall thickness [2][5]), then return to ice for 2 min, without shaking. (3 min)
  • Add 450 µL room-temperature SOC. Shake at 37 °C and 225 rpm (or rotate) for 1 h, with tubes lying on their side [2][5]. (1 h)
  • Make 10-fold dilutions in SOC (1:10 and 1:100). Spread 100 µL of the undiluted culture and each dilution on LB-ampicillin plates with beads [5].
  • Incubate plates inverted at 37 °C for 16–20 h, then count colonies on plates with 30–300 colonies (or the best countable plate) [5]. (16–20 h)
  • Calculate efficiency: cfu/µg = colonies ÷ pg DNA plated × 10⁶. Plating 100 µL of 500 µL from 100 pg gives 20 pg on the undiluted plate; 200 colonies there means 10⁷ cfu/µg.
  • Record the strain, method, OD600, date and measured efficiency on the box label and in the lab log; use cells within about 12 months [4].
  • Frozen aliquots can be kept at −80 °C for at least a year; avoid thawing and refreezing [3][4].
  • After the Day 0 streak, plates can wait 1–2 days at 4 °C.
  • LB starter, overnight culture and plates run overnight; the cell washes (steps 6–15) must be done in one session without breaks, as cells must stay at 0–4 °C throughout.
  • Transformed, recovered cells can stay at 4 °C for 24–48 h before plating with little loss [2].
  • CaCl₂ cells: usually about 10⁶–10⁷ cfu/µg of supercoiled pUC19, up to about 10⁸ with careful growth control and a fresh batch.
  • Inoue cells: usually 10⁷–10⁸ cfu/µg and sometimes higher, an order of magnitude or more above the CaCl₂ method [1][3]. Reported peak values are for the very best strain and batch.
  • Acceptable for routine subcloning of intact plasmids: at least 10⁶ cfu/µg; for ligations and libraries aim for 10⁸ cfu/µg or more [1].
  • The no-DNA plate has no colonies. Colonies on LB-ampicillin should be uniform in size and appearance (blue-white screening with DH5α needs X-gal and IPTG).
  • No-DNA control: cells with water or TE only, plated on LB-ampicillin (expected zero colonies; any growth means contamination or inactive antibiotic).
  • Uncut control plasmid: 100 pg pUC19 gives the efficiency value (positive control for competence).
  • Antibiotic plate control: plate untransformed cells on LB-ampicillin (no growth) and on plain LB (lawn) to check the antibiotic and cell viability.
  • Mock ligation or an uncut/cut plasmid control when testing a cloning protocol with these cells, so cell quality is separated from DNA quality.
  • Check sterility: plate 10 µL of one thawed aliquot on plain LB without DNA and confirm one colony type (the strain's expected morphology).

  • E. coli K-12 derivatives (DH5α, TOP10, XL1-Blue) are BSL-1; follow your institution's biosafety rules, work cleanly, and wear a lab coat, gloves and eye protection.
  • Autoclave all cultures, plates and contaminated plastic before disposal; decontaminate bench surfaces and spills with 10% bleach (freshly made, 10–20 min contact) or 70% ethanol.
  • Liquid nitrogen and dry ice cause severe cold burns: wear cryo gloves, a face shield and closed shoes. Never seal tubes in liquid nitrogen without rated cryo-vials (trapped liquid can burst them) and use only in ventilated areas.
  • DMSO carries solutes through skin, MnCl₂ is toxic if inhaled or ingested, and PIPES/KOH solutions are irritants: wear nitrile gloves and weigh powders carefully.
  • Ampicillin is a sensitizer: avoid inhaling powder and skin contact. Handle ethanol away from flames and used spreader tools carefully (flaming ethanol risk).
