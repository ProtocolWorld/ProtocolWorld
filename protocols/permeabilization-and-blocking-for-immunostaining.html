Permeabilization and Blocking for Immunostaining

Estimated time: About 3 h at the bench + overnight primary antibody | Protocol World

To make fixed cultured cells accessible to antibodies (permeabilization) and to saturate nonspecific binding sites (blocking), so that immunofluorescence signal reflects the target and background stays low.

Aldehyde fixation preserves structure but leaves the plasma membrane closed to antibodies. Intracellular targets therefore need a permeabilizing agent: detergents such as Triton X-100 and Tween-20 dissolve lipids non-selectively, saponin removes membrane cholesterol to leave pores, and cold methanol or acetone fixes and permeabilizes at once [1][3][5]. Stronger or longer permeabilization can extract proteins and damage morphology, while too little gives weak or no signal, so type, concentration and time are optimized per antigen [4]. Blocking with normal serum from the host species of the secondary antibody (or BSA) occupies sticky sites; serum from the primary host must be avoided [2][3].

ItemDetails
1× PBSPhosphate-buffered saline, pH 7.4; ice-cold PBS for the first rinse
Fixative4% paraformaldehyde in PBS (methanol-free formaldehyde at 2–4% also works), freshly prepared or thawed [2][3]
Permeabilization buffer (detergent option)0.1–0.2% Triton X-100 in PBS (harsher; 2–5 min) or 0.2–0.5% Tween-20 or saponin in PBS (milder; 2–5 min) [3]
Permeabilization (solvent option)100% methanol chilled to −20 °C, 5–10 min; fixes and permeabilizes together [3][5]
Blocking buffer5% normal serum from the host species of the secondary antibody + 0.1% Triton X-100 in PBS; for 10 mL: 0.5 mL serum, 10 µL Triton X-100, 9.5 mL PBS [2]. Alternative: 1–5% BSA in PBS
Antibody dilution buffer1% BSA + 0.1% Triton X-100 in PBS (or 1% BSA + 0.1% Tween-20 in PBS) [1][3]
Primary and fluorophore-conjugated secondary antibodiesDilute per the datasheet; use pre-adsorbed secondaries for multicolor work [3]
DAPI and antifade mounting mediumDAPI 1 µg/mL in PBS; antifade mountant
Cells on coverslipsAdherent cells grown to 50–70% confluence on sterile 12 mm glass coverslips in a 24-well plate
  • Fume hood for paraformaldehyde and methanol
  • Humidified staining chamber
  • Fine forceps and a dissecting needle
  • Orbital shaker (optional)
  • Fluorescence microscope
  • 4 °C refrigerator and −20 °C freezer
  • Aspirate the medium and rinse the coverslips once with PBS. Work gently so cells are not detached. (2 min)
  • Fix with 500 µL of 4% paraformaldehyde per well (about 2–3 mm deep) for 15 min at room temperature in a fume hood [2][3]. (15 min)
  • Rinse 3 × 5 min with PBS (500 µL per well) to remove fixative [1][2]. (15 min)
  • Permeabilize with 0.1–0.2% Triton X-100 in PBS for 2–5 min at room temperature, or use a milder agent (Tween-20 or saponin, 0.2–0.5%) [3][4]. (2–5 min)
  • Methanol option: skip steps 2–4 and fix cells in −20 °C methanol for 5–10 min instead. Then rinse 3 × 5 min with PBS [3][4]. (15 min)
  • Rinse once for 5 min with PBS, then place coverslips cell-side up in a humidified chamber. (5 min)
  • Block with 100–200 µL of blocking buffer per coverslip (5% normal serum, 0.1% Triton X-100) for 60 min at room temperature [2]. Some protocols block up to 2 h [3]. (60 min)
  • Aspirate the blocking buffer without rinsing. Add primary antibody diluted in antibody dilution buffer and incubate overnight at 4 °C [1][2]. (Overnight)
  • Rinse 3 × 5 min with PBS [1][2]. (15 min)
  • Add the fluorophore-conjugated secondary antibody in antibody dilution buffer. Incubate 1–2 h at room temperature in the dark [1][2][3]. (1–2 h)
  • Rinse 3 × 5 min with PBS in the dark. Stain nuclei with DAPI (1 µg/mL) for 5 min, then rinse once with PBS [2][3]. (20 min)
  • Mount coverslips cell-side down on a drop of antifade medium, let it cure as directed, and store flat at 4 °C in the dark until imaging [1].
  • After the 4% paraformaldehyde rinses (step 3), store coverslips in PBS at 4 °C for up to a few days.
  • After blocking and primary antibody addition (step 8), incubate overnight at 4 °C in a humidified chamber.
  • After mounting (step 12), store slides flat at 4 °C in the dark until imaging.
  • Specific signal at the expected location (for example, nuclear, cytoskeletal or Golgi pattern) with a dark, even background.
  • Nuclei are clearly visible with DAPI and cell morphology is intact; cells do not look swollen, blebbed or stripped.
  • Secondary-only control: omit the primary antibody to reveal nonspecific secondary binding.
  • Isotype or no-primary control at the same concentration as the primary antibody.
  • Unpermeabilized control for a suspected surface antigen: skip permeabilization and compare.
  • Known positive and negative cell lines (or knockdown cells) for the target.
ProblemLikely causeSolution
High backgroundInadequate blocking, serum cross-reactivity or too much antibodyIncrease serum to 5–10% or block longer; match serum to the secondary host; titrate antibodies [3]
Weak or no signalUnder-permeabilization, low antibody affinity, short incubation or photobleachingIncrease detergent time or concentration, lengthen the primary incubation, protect from light [3][4]
Poor morphology or lost antigenOver-fixation or harsh permeabilizationShorten fixation, use a milder detergent such as Tween-20 or saponin, or try methanol [3][5]
Membrane antigen not seenTriton X-100 extracted the membrane proteinUse a milder agent or skip permeabilization for a surface epitope [3]
Cells detachAspirating forcefully or drying outPipette against the well wall and never let coverslips dry
  • Paraformaldehyde and formaldehyde are toxic and carcinogenic: handle in a fume hood with gloves and collect as hazardous waste.
  • Methanol is flammable and toxic; keep away from ignition sources and dispose of as organic solvent waste.
  • DAPI is a DNA-binding dye: wear gloves and dispose of stained liquid waste per institutional rules.
  • Triton X-100 is toxic to aquatic life; do not pour it down the drain.
