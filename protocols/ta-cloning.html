TA Cloning

Estimated time: 1.5 h hands-on + overnight ligation and growth | Protocol World

To clone a Taq-amplified PCR product into a linearized T-vector (such as pGEM-T or pGEM-T Easy) by exploiting the single 3′-A overhang left by Taq polymerase, and to identify recombinant E. coli colonies by blue/white screening.

Taq polymerase lacks proofreading and adds a single, non-templated deoxyadenosine to the 3′ ends of PCR products. A T-vector is cut open and carries a single 3′-T at each end, so the two overhangs anneal and T4 DNA ligase seals them without restriction digestion [1][2]. The vector places the cloning site inside the lacZα gene. Colonies carrying an insert lose α-complementation and stay white on plates containing X-Gal, while empty vector gives blue colonies [2][3]. Products made with proofreading polymerases are blunt and must first be A-tailed [2].

ItemDetails
Purified PCR productMade with Taq (no 3′→5′ exonuclease); gel-purify if there are extra bands or primer-dimers; keep a 10 min, 72 °C final extension [1][2]
T-vectorpGEM-T or pGEM-T Easy, 50 ng/µL, supplied linearized with 3′-T overhangs [2]
2× Rapid Ligation Buffer60 mM Tris-HCl (pH 7.8), 20 mM MgCl₂, 20 mM DTT, 2 mM ATP, 10% PEG 8000; vortex before use [2]
T4 DNA ligase3 Weiss units/µL, 1 µL per reaction [2]; if your buffer lacks ATP, add ATP to 1 mM [1]
Competent E. coliHigh-efficiency lacZΔM15 strain such as JM109 (≥ 1 × 10⁸ cfu/µg), 50 µL per transformation [2]
SOC medium2% tryptone, 0.5% yeast extract, 10 mM NaCl, 2.5 mM KCl, then 20 mM Mg²⁺ and 20 mM glucose added after sterilization; room temperature [2]
LB/ampicillin agar platesLB with 15 g/L agar and 100 µg/mL ampicillin; use plates less than 1 month old [2]
X-Gal and IPTGX-Gal 50 mg/mL in DMF (−20 °C, dark); IPTG 100 mM in water. Per plate spread 20 µL X-Gal and 100 µL IPTG, or pour plates with 80 µg/mL X-Gal and 0.5 mM IPTG; IPTG can be omitted with the high-copy pGEM-T [2][4]
Control Insert DNA4 ng/µL, 2 µL, for the positive ligation control [2]
  • Thermal cycler or heat block (A-tailing at 70 °C)
  • Water bath or heat block at 42 °C
  • Ice bucket
  • Shaking incubator (37 °C, about 150 rpm)
  • 37 °C plate incubator and 4 °C refrigerator
  • Microcentrifuge
  • Agarose gel system and NanoDrop or Qubit for quantifying the insert
  • Check the PCR product on an agarose gel. If more than one band or primer-dimer is visible, gel-purify; otherwise clean up with a column kit [1][2]. (45 min)
  • Estimate insert mass: ng insert = (ng vector × insert kb × molar ratio) ÷ vector kb (3.0 kb). For 50 ng vector and a 0.5 kb insert at 3:1, use about 25 ng [2]. (5 min)
  • If the product was made with a proofreading enzyme, A-tail it: 1–7 µL product, 1× Taq buffer, 0.2 mM dATP, 5 U Taq, 10 µL total, 70 °C for 15–30 min [2]. (30 min)
  • Spin the 2× buffer and vector tubes briefly; on ice, set up each 10 µL ligation: 5 µL 2× buffer, 1 µL vector (50 ng), PCR product, 1 µL T4 ligase, water [2][4]. (10 min)
  • Prepare two controls: positive (2 µL Control Insert DNA in place of PCR product) and background (water instead of insert) [2]. (5 min)
  • Incubate 1 h at room temperature, or overnight at 4 °C for the most transformants. Do not exceed 28 °C, which raises background [2]. (1 h–overnight)
  • Thaw competent cells on ice. Add 2 µL of each ligation to a pre-chilled tube, then 50 µL cells; mix by flicking, not vortexing [2]. (10 min)
  • Keep on ice 20 min, heat-shock at exactly 42 °C for 45–50 s without shaking, and return to ice for 2 min [2][3]. (25 min)
  • Add 950 µL room-temperature SOC and shake at about 150 rpm at 37 °C for 1.5 h [2][3]. (1.5 h)
  • Plate 100 µL on duplicate LB/ampicillin/IPTG/X-Gal plates. For more colonies, spin 1,000 × g for 10 min, resuspend in 200 µL SOC and plate 100 µL on each of two plates [2]. (15 min)
  • Incubate plates inverted at 37 °C for 16–24 h, then place at 4 °C for 1–2 h so the blue color develops fully and white colonies are clear [2]. (16–24 h)
  • Pick several white colonies, choosing ones near blue colonies because X-Gal spreads unevenly [4]. Screen by colony PCR (T7 and M13 reverse primers) [4]. (1 h)
  • Grow positive colonies overnight in LB/ampicillin, prepare plasmid DNA, digest with a restriction enzyme that flanks the insert, and confirm size on a gel [1]. (overnight + 2 h)
  • Confirm the sequence and insert orientation by Sanger sequencing before further use; orientation is random in TA cloning [1][2]. (1–2 days)
  • After ligation: ligations can stay overnight at 4 °C (step 6).
  • After the 16–24 h incubation: plates can be held at 4 °C for a few days before picking colonies.
  • Verified plasmid DNA can be stored at −20 °C indefinitely.
  • The positive control gives about 100 colonies per plate, with more than 60% white and 10–40% blue [2].
  • The background (no-insert) plate has only a small number of blue colonies, roughly 10–30 at 1 × 10⁸ cfu/µg [2].
  • With your insert, most colonies should be white; the blue-to-white ratio varies widely between experiments (about 1:5 to 2:1 in one published account) [1].
  • Positive control: Control Insert DNA to check ligation and transformation efficiency [2]
  • Background control: vector with no insert to measure blue colonies from self-ligation [1][2]
  • Transformation efficiency control: 0.1 ng uncut plasmid, 100 µL cells, 900 µL SOC, plated at 1:10 [2]
  • Antibiotic control: untransformed competent cells on an ampicillin plate should give no growth

Mostly blue coloniesInsert lacks A-overhangs or is poorly ligatedUse fresh Taq products, include the 10 min 72 °C extension, or A-tail proofreading products [1][2]
Many white colonies without the right insertPrimer-dimers or non-specific products ligatedGel-purify the PCR band; adjust insert:vector ratio (try 3:1 to 1:3) [2]
White colonies hard to tell from blueOld plates or weak X-GalUse fresh plates, cold-develop at 4 °C, and check the strain carries lacZΔM15 [2]

  • X-Gal is dissolved in DMF, a reproductive toxin: wear gloves and use a fume hood.
  • Ampicillin may cause allergic reactions; avoid skin contact and inhaling powder.
  • Ethidium bromide and its substitutes need gloves; dispose of gels per local rules.
  • Autoclave or bleach all E. coli cultures, plates and tips before disposal, and follow BSL-1 practice.
