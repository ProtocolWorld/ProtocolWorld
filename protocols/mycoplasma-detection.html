Mycoplasma Detection

PCR: 1 day after at least 4 days of antibiotic-free culture. Hoechst indicator-cell test: 4–6 days · 2026-11-22

Test mammalian cultures for mycoplasma with universal 16S rRNA PCR and Hoechst DNA staining of Vero indicator cells, with positive, negative and internal controls and interpretation guidance.

⬇ Word (.docx)⬇ PowerPoint (.pptx)

Purpose

To test mammalian cell cultures for mycoplasma contamination with two complementary methods: PCR of cell DNA and DNA staining of an indicator cell line, so that a negative result is supported by more than one assay.

Principle

Mycoplasmas are tiny, wall-less bacteria that grow in cell cultures without turbidity and alter cell metabolism, growth and gene expression. Because no single test is perfect, at least two techniques should be used [1][2][3]. PCR with universal 16S rRNA primers amplifies a conserved region from most species and is rapid and sensitive, but it can also detect DNA from dead organisms [2]. DNA-binding dyes such as Hoechst stain mycoplasma DNA as small fluorescent dots or filaments around host nuclei; growing the test sample on Vero indicator cells lowers the detection limit from about 10⁶ to about 100 colony-forming units (cfu) per mL [3].

Materials and reagents

ItemDetails
Test cell cultureGrown for at least 4 days in antibiotic-free medium without a medium change, at high density in late log phase, so that any mycoplasma are abundant [2]
Cell lysis and DNA extraction reagentsSTE buffer (10 mM NaCl, 20 mM Tris-HCl, 1 mM EDTA, pH 8.0) with 1% SDS and 40 µg/mL proteinase K, or a commercial genomic DNA kit [2]
Universal mycoplasma primers (16S rRNA)Forward 5′-GTG GGG AGG AAA YAG GAT TAG A-3′; reverse 5′-GGC ATG ATG ATT TGA CGT CRT-3′ (Y = C/T, R = A/G). Expected product about 425 bp [2]
PCR reagents10× Taq buffer, dNTPs, MgCl₂ (about 1.5 mM final), Taq polymerase (1 U per 25 µL reaction), nuclease-free water
Agarose gel1% agarose in TAE or TBE with a DNA stain, plus a 100 bp ladder [2]
Positive control DNADNA from a known mycoplasma-infected culture, or a commercial positive control. Use a different source from your lab cultures to avoid cross-contamination
Vero indicator cells2 × 10⁴ cells per well in 12-well plates with sterile coverslips [3]
Carnoy's fixative1 part glacial acetic acid : 3 parts absolute methanol, prepared fresh [3]
Hoechst DNA stainPrepared from the supplier's instructions [3]
Mounting mediumAqueous or glycerol-based, for coverslips [2][3]

Equipment

  • Biosafety cabinet
  • Thermal cycler
  • Microcentrifuge (12,000 × g)
  • 37 °C incubator with 5% CO₂
  • Gel electrophoresis system and gel imager
  • Fluorescence microscope with a UV (DAPI) filter and 100× objective
  • 37 °C heat block or water bath
  • Separate pre-PCR and post-PCR work areas

Procedure

  1. Culture the test cells without antibiotics for at least 4 days without changing the medium, and use cells in late log growth [2]. (4+ days)
  2. Part A, PCR. Collect about 1 × 10⁵ cells, spin at 12,000 × g for 1 min, and remove the supernatant [2]. (5 min)
  3. Resuspend the pellet in STE buffer with 1% SDS and 40 µg/mL proteinase K and incubate for 2 h at 37 °C [2]. (2 h)
  4. Purify the DNA by phenol–chloroform extraction and ethanol precipitation as in [2], or use a genomic DNA kit. Resuspend the DNA in nuclease-free water. (1 h)
  5. Prepare a 25 µL reaction: 2.5 µL 10× buffer, dNTPs, 15 pmol of each primer, about 1.5 mM MgCl₂, 1 U Taq, 1 µL template DNA and water to volume [2]. (15 min)
  6. Set up the controls in the same run: a known positive DNA, a water (no-template) control, and a negative-culture DNA [2]. (5 min)
  7. Cycle: 94 °C for 3 min; 32 cycles of 94 °C for 60 s, 55–60 °C for 30 s and 72 °C for 1 min [2]. (2 h)
  8. Load the products on a 1% agarose gel next to a 100 bp ladder and image. A band of about 425 bp indicates mycoplasma DNA [2]. (1 h)
  9. Part B, DNA stain. Seed 2 × 10⁴ Vero cells per well on sterile coverslips in 12-well plates and let them attach for 2–24 h at 37 °C, 5% CO₂ [3]. (2–24 h)
  10. Scrape the test cells into the medium. Replace the Vero supernatant with 1 mL of the test sample, in duplicate wells [3]. (10 min)
  11. Include duplicate uninoculated wells as negative controls and, if you hold approved strains, wells with 100 cfu of positive control mycoplasma (for example M. hyorhinis, M. orale) [3]. (10 min)
  12. Incubate at 37 °C and 5% CO₂ for 3–5 days [3]. (3–5 days)
  13. Remove the medium and fix with at least 2 mL of Carnoy's fixative for 3 min, discard, and repeat once. Then add Hoechst stain for 3 min, shielded from light [3]. (10 min)
  14. Discard the stain into toxic waste. Mount each coverslip cell side down in a drop of mountant on a slide [3]. (5 min)
  15. Allow the mounting medium to set (15 min at 37 °C or 30 min at room temperature), then examine by UV epifluorescence at 100× [3]. (30 min)
  16. Score: nuclei only on a dark background is negative; extranuclear dots or filaments are positive. Confirm with the PCR result; repeat if they disagree [2][3]. (30 min)

Pause points

  • Extracted DNA can be stored at 4 °C for several weeks or at −20 °C for long periods before PCR [1].
  • Fixed and stained coverslips can be mounted and read the following day if stored in the dark.

Expected results

  • Negative: no 425 bp band by PCR (the positive control gives the band), and only Vero nuclei are visible under the UV filter with a dark background.
  • Positive: a band of about 425 bp, and small fluorescent dots or strands around nuclei and between cells.
  • Different species may give slightly different sizes with species-specific primers (about 300–335 bp) [2].

Controls

  • Positive control DNA or positive culture
  • Water (no-template) control to detect reagent contamination
  • Internal control: a host-DNA primer pair multiplexed with the mycoplasma primers, so that a negative reaction can be told from PCR failure [1]
  • Uninoculated indicator cells (negative) and cells inoculated with a known positive for the Hoechst assay [3]

Troubleshooting

ProblemLikely causeSolution
Faint or no band in the positive controlPoor DNA quality or PCR inhibitorsRe-extract the DNA, dilute the template, and check the cycling program [1][2]
Band in the water controlAmplicon or reagent contaminationUse fresh aliquots, separate pre-PCR and post-PCR areas, and repeat [2]
Primer dimers or smearingExcess primer or DNALower the primer concentration or template amount
Dots in the stain assay look like debrisDead cells, debris or other bacteria and yeastCompare with controls, look for dots in a regular pattern near nuclei, and confirm with PCR [3]
PCR positive but stain negativeDNA from dead organisms after antibiotic treatment, or low titerRetest after at least 2 passages without antibiotics; use two methods [2]
Weak or uneven Hoechst stainingToo few Vero cells or a stain that is too diluteCheck seeding density, prepare fresh stain, check filters [3]

Safety and waste

  • Treat any culture suspected of mycoplasma contamination as infectious. Work in a biosafety cabinet, wear gloves and a lab coat, and disinfect surfaces afterward.
  • Phenol, chloroform, methanol and acetic acid (Carnoy's fixative) are toxic, flammable or corrosive. Use a fume hood and dispose of them as hazardous chemical waste [3].
  • Hoechst, DNA gel stains and some PCR stains bind DNA and should be handled as potential mutagens. Wear gloves and discard stained waste according to local rules [3].
  • Mycoplasma-positive control strains should only be handled with institutional approval. Quarantine, test or discard contaminated lines, and do not share them.
