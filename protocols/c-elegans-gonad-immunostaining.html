C. elegans Gonad Immunostaining

Estimated time: 1.5–2 days | Protocol World

To visualize protein localization and chromosome behavior in the germline of adult C. elegans hermaphrodites by dissecting gonads, fixing them on slides, and staining with antibodies and DAPI.

Cutting an anesthetized adult behind the pharynx, or at the head or tail, extrudes one or both gonad arms, so germ nuclei are reachable by fixative and antibodies without the cuticle in the way [1][4][5]. The gonads are fixed briefly in formaldehyde or paraformaldehyde (PFA), pressed under a coverslip onto a charged slide, and frozen; flicking off the coverslip while frozen cracks the tissue open (freeze-crack). A cold methanol or ethanol step completes fixation and permeabilization [1][4][5]. Primary and fluorescent secondary antibodies and DAPI are then applied, and the gonads are mounted in antifade medium for imaging. Wild-type diakinesis nuclei show six DAPI-stained bivalents, which serves as a morphology check [4].

ItemDetails
WormsWell-fed hermaphrodites on NGM/OP50 at 20 °C. Pick late-L4 larvae 12–24 h before dissection to obtain young adults; use about 48 h post-L4 for diakinesis analysis [1][4]
Egg buffer, 10× stock250 mM HEPES-NaOH pH 7.4, 1.18 M NaCl, 480 mM KCl, 20 mM EDTA, 5 mM EGTA; filter-sterilize [1][5]
Dissection buffer1× egg buffer with 0.1% Tween-20 and an anesthetic: 20 mM sodium azide [1] or about 12 mM levamisole in an M9 base [4]. Make fresh
Fixative2% PFA (from 16% PFA ampule opened within 2 weeks) in 1× M9 [4]; added 1:1 to the drop for about 1% final. Sources range from 0.8% to 1.85% final [1][4][5]
PBST1× PBS pH 7.4 with 0.1% Tween-20 [1][5]
Blocking solution0.5% BSA in PBST, or normal serum diluted 1:10 in PBST; use BSA if a primary was raised in goat [1][3][5]
Primary and secondary antibodiesPrimary dilution must be found empirically [1][4]. Alexa Fluor secondaries at 1:200 [4] to 1:1,000 [5]; spin at maximum speed for 10 min and use the upper phase [5]
DAPI0.1–0.5 µg/mL in PBST for a wash [1][3], or about 2 µg/mL in a DAPI-containing mounting medium [4]
Mounting mediumCommercial antifade, or 450 µL NPG-glycerol (2 g N-propyl gallate in 50 g glycerol) plus 35 µL 2 M Tris base, made fresh [1]
Slides and coverslipsPositively charged slides and 18 × 18 mm No. 1 coverslips for dissection [1][4]
Methanol100%, pre-chilled to −20 °C [1][5]
  • Dissecting stereomicroscope
  • Scalpel (Feather #11) or two crossed syringe needles
  • Flat aluminum block chilled on dry ice for at least 30 min, or liquid nitrogen
  • Fresh single-edge razor blades
  • Coplin jars, humid chamber and Parafilm squares
  • Fume hood for PFA and methanol
  • Fluorescence microscope with DAPI and secondary-antibody channels
  • Place a 20 µL drop of dissection buffer on an 18 × 18 mm coverslip resting on a slide. Transfer 15–25 young adults into the drop [1][5]. (10 min)
  • Cut each worm once, behind the pharynx or at the tail, so the gonad extrudes. Finish all dissections within 5 min, before the anesthetic and evaporation degrade extrusion [1][4]. (5 min)
  • Add 20 µL fixative to the drop, mix by pipetting or flicking, and fix for 2–5 min at room temperature [1][4][5]. (5 min)
  • Lower a charged slide onto the coverslip, remove excess liquid from the edges, and leave about 15 µL under the coverslip so the gonads stick [1][5]. (2 min)
  • Freeze the slide on a dry-ice aluminum block for at least 5 min, or in liquid nitrogen. Frozen slides can wait 15–20 min before cracking [1][4]. (5–20 min)
  • Flick off the coverslip quickly with a fresh razor blade while the slide is still frozen [1][4][5]. (1 min)
  • Immediately immerse the slide in methanol at −20 °C for 1 min, then move it to a Coplin jar of PBST [1][5]. (2 min)
  • Wash three times for 10 min each in PBST at room temperature [1][5]. (30 min)
  • Block with 50–100 µL blocking solution under a Parafilm square in a humid chamber for 30 min at room temperature [1][5]. (30 min)
  • Apply 50–100 µL primary antibody in PBST under Parafilm. Incubate 2 h at room temperature or overnight at 4 °C, and never let the sample dry [1][4][5]. (2 h to overnight)
  • Float off the Parafilm in PBST and wash three times for 10 min each [1][5]. (30 min)
  • Apply fluorescent secondary antibody (1:200–1:1,000) in the dark, 2 h at room temperature or overnight at 4 °C [1][4][5]. (2 h to overnight)
  • Wash in PBST for 10 min, wash in DAPI (0.1–0.5 µg/mL) in PBST for 10 min, then wash in PBST for at least 10 min [1][5]. (35 min)
  • Blot around the sample, apply 10–15 µL antifade mounting medium, lower a 22 × 22 mm coverslip, wick off excess, and seal with nail polish [1][5]. (15 min)
  • Image after the polish dries. Store slides at 4 °C in the dark for 1–2 weeks, or at −20 °C for longer [4]. (variable)
  • After cracking, slides can be held in 95% ethanol at −20 °C for several days if you use the ethanol option [4].
  • Primary and secondary incubations can run overnight at 4 °C.
  • Sealed slides keep for 1–2 weeks at 4 °C in the dark, and longer at −20 °C [4].
  • DAPI shows clear germ nuclei from the mitotic zone through pachytene to diakinesis; wild-type diakinesis nuclei show six DAPI bodies [4].
  • Specific antibody signal localizes to the expected compartment (for example chromosome axes or foci in pachytene nuclei) and is absent from controls.
  • Well-attached gonads stay in place and do not flap during imaging.
  • No-primary (secondary-only) slide: should show no signal [4]
  • Wild-type versus a mutant lacking the target antigen, with age-matched worms [1][4]
  • DAPI as a positive control for tissue presence [4]
  • For tagged proteins, an anti-tag antibody rather than native fluorescence, which fixation can quench [4]

  • Formaldehyde, PFA and methanol are toxic: prepare and use them in a fume hood with gloves and eye protection.
  • Sodium azide is acutely toxic and can form explosive metal azides in drains; collect it as chemical waste.
  • Dry ice and liquid nitrogen cause cold burns; wear cryo gloves and eye protection.
  • Dispose of razor blades and scalpels in a sharps container and DAPI, fixative and antibody waste as chemical waste.
