Restriction-Ligation Subcloning

3 days (about 6 h hands-on) · 2026-11-21

Move a DNA fragment from a donor plasmid or PCR product into a recipient vector using restriction enzymes and T4 DNA ligase, then transform E. coli and verify clones.

⬇ Word (.docx)⬇ PowerPoint (.pptx)

Purpose

To insert a defined DNA fragment into a plasmid backbone at compatible restriction sites and recover verified recombinant clones in E. coli.

Principle

Restriction enzymes cut donor and recipient DNA to give compatible ends; T4 DNA ligase joins the 5′ phosphate and 3′ hydroxyl to form the recombinant plasmid. Two different enzymes give directional cloning, while a single enzyme requires vector dephosphorylation to limit self-ligation [1][2]. A vector:insert molar ratio near 1:3 is a typical start and varies from 1:1 to 1:10 [2][3]. Cohesive-end ligation proceeds at room temperature for 10 min or at 16 °C overnight [1][3]. Ligation products are transformed into recA− E. coli, selected on antibiotic plates and checked by diagnostic digest and sequencing [2][4].

Equipment

  • Thermal cycler or heat blocks (37 °C, 65 °C, 80 °C, 42 °C)
  • Water bath or incubator at 16 °C (optional)
  • Agarose gel system and blue-light or long-wave UV (360 nm) transilluminator
  • Microcentrifuge
  • NanoDrop or fluorometer
  • 37 °C shaking and static incubators
  • Ice bucket

Procedure

  1. Design the cloning: choose enzymes that flank the insert, cut the vector only in the multiple cloning site, and are not found inside the insert [2]. (1 h)
  2. Digest 1.5–2 µg donor and 1 µg vector separately: 1 µg DNA, 5 µL 10× buffer, 10 units of each enzyme, water to 50 µL [2][4]. (15 min setup)
  3. Incubate at the enzymes' optimal temperature (usually 37 °C) for 1 h, or up to overnight for complete vector cutting [2][4]. (1 h–overnight)
  4. If using one enzyme or blunt ends, add 1 µL rSAP (1 unit/µL) to the vector digest and incubate at 37 °C for 30 min, then 65 °C for 5 min [4]. (35 min)
  5. Add loading dye and run both digests on a 1% agarose gel with a ladder and uncut plasmid lanes, using a wide comb and spaced lanes [2]. (60 min)
  6. Excise the vector backbone and insert bands with a clean blade under long-wave UV (360 nm) and keep UV exposure brief [4]. (10 min)
  7. Purify each band with a gel extraction kit and elute in 10–30 µL; if reusing digests without a gel, remove enzymes by spin column [3][4]. (20 min)
  8. Measure DNA concentration by NanoDrop or fluorometer and calculate the insert mass for a 1:3 vector:insert molar ratio [1][2]. (10 min)
  9. Calculate: insert ng = 3 × vector ng × (insert kb ÷ vector kb); for 50 ng of a 4 kb vector and a 1 kb insert use 37.5 ng [1]. (5 min)
  10. On ice, set up a 20 µL ligation: 2 µL 10× T4 Ligase Buffer, 50 ng vector, calculated insert, water to 19 µL; add 1 µL T4 DNA Ligase last [1]. (10 min)
  11. Prepare a vector-only control (no insert) and a no-ligase control (vector and insert, water in place of ligase), each in 20 µL [2]. (5 min)
  12. Mix gently by pipetting, spin briefly, and incubate cohesive-end ligations for 10 min at room temperature or at 16 °C overnight [1][3]. (10 min–overnight)
  13. Heat-inactivate at 65 °C for 10 min, then chill on ice; skip this for Quick Ligation Kit or master-mix reactions [1][3][4]. (10 min)
  14. Thaw competent cells on ice and add 1–5 µL of each ligation (2 µL typical) to 50 µL cells; flick to mix and incubate on ice for 30 min [1][4]. (30 min)
  15. Heat shock at exactly 42 °C for 30 s, then return to ice for 5 min [4]. (6 min)
  16. Add 950 µL room-temperature SOC and shake at 37 °C and about 250 rpm for 60 min [4]. (60 min)
  17. Plate 50–100 µL (and the pelleted remainder on a second plate) on pre-warmed antibiotic plates; incubate inverted at 37 °C for 16 h [4]. (16 h)
  18. Compare plates: the vector + insert plate should show clearly more colonies than the vector-only and no-ligase plates [2]. (10 min)
  19. Pick 3–10 colonies; optionally run colony PCR with insert-flanking primers, then grow each in 3–5 mL LB plus antibiotic at 37 °C overnight [2]. (16 h)
  20. Miniprep each culture and digest 100–300 ng with the cloning enzymes for 1 h at 37 °C; resolve on a gel for vector and insert bands [2]. (2 h)
  21. Send positive clones for Sanger sequencing across both junctions and the insert; confirm orientation and frame, then make a glycerol stock [2]. (2–3 days)

Pause points

  • Digests can be held at −20 °C after heat inactivation or before gel purification.
  • Gel-purified fragments can be stored at −20 °C.
  • Ligations can sit at 4 °C overnight, and transformation plates can stay at 4 °C for a few days after colonies appear.

Expected results

  • The vector + insert plate shows many more colonies than the vector-only plate, often tens to hundreds.
  • Diagnostic digest of correct clones gives two bands, one at the vector size and one at the insert size.
  • Sequencing confirms intact junctions, correct orientation and reading frame, and no unwanted mutations.

Controls

  • Vector-only ligation (cut vector plus ligase, no insert) to measure self-ligation and background [2]
  • No-ligase control (vector and insert without ligase) to detect uncut parental plasmid [2]
  • Uncut plasmid lanes on the gel to confirm complete digestion [2]
  • Optional positive transformation control with 10 pg intact plasmid to check competent cell efficiency

Troubleshooting

ProblemLikely causeSolution
Many colonies on vector-only plateIncomplete digestion or vector self-ligationExtend digest, gel-purify vector, dephosphorylate with rSAP or Quick CIP and heat-inactivate it [2][4]
No coloniesInactive ligase or ATP, wrong antibiotic, poor cells, or leftover enzymesUse fresh buffer, run a positive control, confirm antibiotic, and remove restriction enzymes before ligation [2][3]
Colonies lack the insertPoor vector:insert ratio or contaminated DNATry 1:1, 1:3 and 1:10 ratios and re-purify DNA [2][3]
Few colonies after Quick LigationHeat inactivation or PEG carryoverDo not heat-inactivate; use less ligation mix or a spin-column cleanup before electroporation [3][4]
Faint or smeared gel bandsNicked DNA or UV damageUse long-wave UV, short exposure, and more starting DNA [2][4]
Insert in wrong orientationSingle-enzyme or compatible-end cloningScreen by an orientation-specific digest or switch to two different enzymes [2]

Safety and waste

  • Wear gloves and eye protection; UV light damages skin and eyes, so use a shield or blue-light source.
  • Ethidium bromide is a mutagen; use safer stains where possible and dispose of gel waste per local rules.
  • Handle E. coli as BSL-1 material, disinfect surfaces and discard cultures and plates by autoclaving or bleach.
  • Heat blocks and molten agarose cause burns.
