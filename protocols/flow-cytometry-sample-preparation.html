Flow Cytometry Sample Preparation

Mammalian Cells · 2–3 h · 2026-11-19

Prepare viable single-cell suspensions from mammalian cells, block Fc receptors, stain with a viability dye and fluorochrome-conjugated antibodies, and fix for flow cytometric analysis. Includes the controls needed for compensation and gating.

Flow Cytometry Sample Preparation

Estimated time: 2–3 h | Protocol World

To convert adherent, suspension or primary mammalian cells into a clean, viable, specifically stained single-cell suspension suitable for multicolor flow cytometry or sorting.

Flow cytometry measures light scatter and fluorescence of single cells passing a laser, so cells must be dispersed, viable and free of clumps [1]. Fc receptor blocking reduces nonspecific antibody binding, and a viability dye lets dead cells, which bind antibodies promiscuously, be excluded [2][3][4]. Cold staining in protein-containing buffer limits antibody internalization and nonspecific sticking, and fixable amine-reactive dyes must be applied before fixation [3][4]. Reliable interpretation depends on unstained, single-stain compensation, fluorescence-minus-one (FMO) and, where useful, isotype controls processed exactly like the samples [1][4][5].

Wash/staining bufferPBS (Ca²⁺/Mg²⁺-free) + 1–2% FBS or BSA, ± 2 mM EDTA to limit clumping; optional 0.05–0.1% sodium azide; keep at 4 °C
Single-cell suspensionAdherent or suspension culture cells; aim for ≥ 90% viability before staining [4]
Dissociation reagent (adherent cells)Enzyme-free dissociation buffer, trypsin-EDTA or Accutase; neutralize with serum-containing medium
Fc blockAnti-mouse CD16/32 (about 0.25–1 µg per 10⁶ cells) or human Fc blocking reagent, or 2–10% normal serum [2][3][4]
Fixable viability dyeAmine-reactive dye used in protein-free PBS; or 7-AAD, DAPI or propidium iodide for unfixed cells [3][4]
Fluorochrome-conjugated antibodiesTitrated beforehand; panel-matched, protected from light
Compensation particles (optional)Antibody-capture beads, plus beads/cells for negative populations [5]
Fixative1–4% paraformaldehyde (PFA) in PBS, freshly diluted [4]
Permeabilization reagent (optional)Saponin or commercial kit for intracellular targets; surface-stain first [4]
Brilliant/polymer dye stain bufferOnly if the panel contains several polymer dyes [3]
Tubes or plates12 × 75 mm round-bottom tubes with 35–70 µm cell-strainer caps, or V-/U-bottom 96-well plates
PBMC isolation medium (optional)Density-gradient medium for blood; RBC lysis buffer for spleen or whole blood [2][3]
  • Biosafety cabinet
  • Refrigerated centrifuge with swinging-bucket rotor
  • Flow cytometer (or cell sorter)
  • 35–70 µm nylon mesh or strainer-cap tubes
  • Cell counter or hemocytometer
  • Ice bucket
  • Vortex mixer
  • Micropipettes and filter tips
  • Plan the panel and controls; titrate each antibody beforehand and chill buffer, centrifuge and tubes to 4 °C [1]. (15 min)
  • Adherent cells: detach gently with enzyme-free buffer or Accutase, neutralize, and pipette to a single-cell suspension. Suspension cells: collect directly.
  • PBMC or tissue: isolate PBMC by density gradient, or dissociate tissue and lyse red cells if needed (about 3 mL lysis buffer, 5 min on ice) [2].
  • Pass cells through a 35–70 µm mesh to remove clumps, count, and confirm viability of about 90% or higher [4].
  • Pellet at 300–350 × g for 5 min at 4 °C [2]. Wash once in cold PBS if using an amine-reactive viability dye.
  • Resuspend 1–10 × 10⁶ cells/mL in PBS and add the fixable viability dye per manufacturer instructions; incubate in the dark, usually 15–30 min [3][4]. (20–30 min)
  • Wash once in cold staining buffer, then aliquot about 5 × 10⁵–1 × 10⁶ cells in 100 µL per tube or well [2].
  • Block Fc receptors: anti-mouse CD16/32 or human Fc block for 5–20 min on ice or at room temperature, per reagent. Do not wash before staining [2][3]. (10–20 min)
  • Add the antibody cocktail, mixed fresh in staining buffer, to each tube. Use polymer-dye buffer if needed [3]. (5 min)
  • Incubate 20–30 min at 4 °C in the dark (purified or biotinylated antibodies may need up to 1 h) [3][4]. (20–30 min)
  • Wash twice with at least 2 mL cold staining buffer (200 µL per well), 300–400 × g for 5 min, discarding the supernatant each time [2][3].
  • If secondary reagents are used, add them for 20–30 min on ice in the dark, then wash twice [2][3].
  • Optional intracellular targets: fix, then permeabilize (for example saponin), then stain; always surface-stain first [4].
  • Fix in 1–4% PFA for about 20 min at 4 °C, wash, and store in buffer at 4 °C in the dark. Fix control tubes identically [3][4]. (20 min)
  • Resuspend in 300–500 µL buffer and filter through 35–70 µm mesh again just before acquisition.
  • Prepare single-stain compensation controls, unstained and FMO controls (and isotype if used) in parallel, using the same fluorochromes and treatment [1][5].
  • Acquire on ice-cold or room-temperature samples at a low-to-medium flow rate; gate singlets and live cells, and collect at least 5,000 events per compensation population [5]. (30–60 min)
  • The protocol can be paused after sterilization and labeling; store solutions as described.
  • Single-cell suspension with few doublets and debris; viability dye-negative cells form a clear live gate
  • Clear separation of positive and negative populations in each channel, with spillover correctable by compensation
  • Unstained and FMO controls define positive gates; background in isotype or Fc-blocked controls is low
  • Stable event rate with no clogging during acquisition
  • Unstained cells (autofluorescence baseline) [5]
  • Single-stain compensation control for every fluorochrome, as bright as or brighter than the sample, using beads or cells [5]
  • FMO controls: all fluorochromes minus one, to set gates for dim or unknown antigens [1]
  • Isotype control (same isotype, fluorochrome and concentration) to assess nonspecific binding; not for gating [1]
  • Viability-dye control (heat-killed or ethanol-treated cells) to set the dead-cell gate
  • Biological positive and negative control cell lines or stimulated vs. resting cells
ProblemLikely causeSolution
Clogging or unstable flowCell clumps, DNA from dead cells, tissue debrisFilter through 35–70 µm mesh; add 2 mM EDTA and, if needed, DNase; reduce concentration
High background or nonspecific stainingFc receptor binding, dead cells, excess antibodyUse Fc block, viability dye and titrated antibody; wash more thoroughly
Weak or no signalLow antigen, epitope loss by enzyme or fixation, wrong dye or laserUse gentler detachment, stain before fixation, pair bright dyes with weak antigens, check configuration
Low viability or many dead cellsHarsh dissociation, over-spinning, long storageUse gentler dissociation, spin at 300–350 × g, keep cold, and acquire promptly [4]
Poor compensation or spillover spreadDim single-stain controls, mismatched fluorochrome or tandem lotUse bright, matched controls with at least 5,000 events per population; remake tandem controls [5]
Signal loss in fixed samplesProlonged fixation or light exposure of tandem dyesFix 20 min or less, store in the dark at 4 °C, and acquire within a few days [2][3]
  • PFA is toxic and a suspected carcinogen: handle in a fume hood or ventilated area with gloves and eye protection; collect as hazardous waste.
  • Sodium azide is highly toxic and forms explosive metal azides in drains; use dilute solutions and dispose as chemical waste.
  • Treat all human and primary cells as potentially biohazardous (BSL-2 practices); work in a biosafety cabinet.
  • Cell sorters can generate aerosols; use containment and validated decontamination of fluidics.
  • Dispose of liquid and solid waste per institutional biohazard and chemical rules; decontaminate with 10% bleach or per local policy.
  • Lasers: do not bypass instrument interlocks.
