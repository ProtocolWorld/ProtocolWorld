High-Fidelity PCR

Estimated time: About 2 h | Protocol World

To amplify DNA with a proofreading polymerase so that the product contains very few errors and is suitable for cloning, sequencing or mutagenesis.

High-fidelity polymerases such as Q5 and Phusion combine a thermostable polymerase with a 3′→5′ proofreading exonuclease, giving error rates tens of times lower than Taq (the manufacturer lists 4.4 × 10⁻⁷ for Phusion in HF buffer, about 50-fold below Taq) [2][3]. They tolerate short, hot cycles: denaturation at 98 °C for 5–10 s and annealing at about 3 °C above the lower primer Tm for primers over 20 nt [1][2][3]. Extension times are set by template complexity, and the products are blunt-ended [1][2][3].

ItemDetails
High-fidelity DNA polymeraseQ5 (2 U/µL stock; use 0.5 µL per 50 µL, about 1 U) [1] or Phusion (2 U/µL; 0.5 µL per 50 µL, 0.02 U/µL final) [3]
5X reaction bufferQ5 Reaction Buffer (2.0 mM Mg²⁺ at 1X) [1]; Phusion HF Buffer (1.5 mM MgCl₂ at 1X), or GC Buffer for difficult templates [3]
dNTP mix10 mM each; 1 µL per 50 µL gives 200 µM each; higher dNTP gives no benefit [1][2][3]
Primers10 µM stocks; 2.5 µL each per 50 µL gives 0.5 µM (range 0.2–1.0 µM); 20–40 nt, 40–60% GC [1][3]
Template DNAPlasmid or viral 1 pg–10 ng; genomic 50–250 ng (up to 1 µg for Q5) per 50 µL [1][2][3]
Optional additives5X Q5 High GC Enhancer (10 µL per 50 µL) [1]; DMSO 3% (1.5 µL per 50 µL) for Phusion [2][3]
Nuclease-free waterTo 50 µL
  • Thermal cycler with heated lid
  • Thin-wall PCR tubes
  • Ice block and pipettes with filter tips
  • Agarose gel system
  • PCR cleanup or gel extraction kit
  • Tm calculator (NEB or Thermo)
  • Calculate the Tm of each primer with the manufacturer's calculator (nearest-neighbor method); values differ between methods and between enzymes [1][2][3]. (10 min)
  • Set up reactions on ice with a master mix: water, 10 µL 5X buffer, 1 µL 10 mM dNTPs, 2.5 µL of each primer, template; add polymerase last [1][3]. (15 min)
  • Per 50 µL: 0.5 µL polymerase (about 1 U; keep between 0.5 and 2 U); mix gently by pipetting and spin down; add GC Enhancer or DMSO only if needed [1][3].
  • Preheat the block to 98 °C before placing the tubes in it to limit nonspecific products [1]. (3 min)
  • Initial denaturation: 98 °C for 30 s; up to 3 min for difficult or genomic templates [1][2][3].
  • Cycle 25–35 times: 98 °C for 5–10 s; anneal 10–30 s at 3 °C above the lower primer Tm (Tm for primers under 20 nt); extend at 72 °C [1][2][3]. (1–1.5 h)
  • Extension: 15 s/kb for plasmid or other simple templates, 20–30 s/kb for typical use and genomic DNA, up to 40 s/kb for cDNA or long products [1][2][3].
  • If primer Tm is 72 °C or higher, use two-step cycling with combined annealing and extension at 72 °C [1][2][3].
  • Final extension at 72 °C for 2 min (Q5) or 5–10 min (Phusion), then hold at 4 °C [1][3]. (10 min)
  • Analyze 5 µL on an agarose gel; purify the product with a cleanup or gel extraction kit before cloning [1][2].
  • For T/A cloning, remove all polymerase first and add A-tails afterward because the enzyme strips overhangs; blunt-end cloning avoids this step [2]. (20 min)
  • Reactions can be held at 4 °C in the cycler overnight after the final extension.
  • Purified amplicons can be stored at −20 °C.
  • A single sharp band of the expected size on the gel.
  • Enough yield for cloning from 25–35 cycles; low error rate in sequenced clones (Phusion HF buffer: 4.4 × 10⁻⁷) [3].
  • No-template control to detect contamination
  • Positive control template with known primers
  • Gradient of annealing temperatures when optimizing a new primer pair [2]
