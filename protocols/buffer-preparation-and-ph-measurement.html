Buffer Preparation and pH Measurement

45–60 min per buffer; meter calibration 10–15 min · 2026-11-15

Calculate, dissolve, titrate and sterilize common buffers (Tris-HCl, PBS, EDTA, HEPES) and calibrate a pH meter for accurate readings.

⬇ Word (.docx)⬇ PowerPoint (.pptx)

Purpose

To prepare accurate, reproducible laboratory buffers (Tris-HCl, PBS, EDTA and HEPES examples) and to measure and adjust their pH with a properly calibrated meter.

Principle

A buffer resists pH change because it contains a weak acid and its conjugate base; it works best within about one pH unit of its pKa [3]. The mass of each solid is calculated from molarity, formula weight and final volume [3]. Solids are dissolved in part of the final volume, the pH is adjusted, and only then is the solution brought to volume [1][6]. Because pKa depends on temperature, especially for Tris (about −0.028 pH units per °C), pH must be set at the temperature of use [3]. A glass electrode gives reliable readings only after calibration with fresh standards that bracket the target pH [4][5].

Equipment

  • pH meter with temperature probe
  • Magnetic stirrer and stir bars
  • Analytical balance
  • Graduated cylinders and volumetric flasks
  • Autoclave
  • Vacuum filtration or syringe filters
  • Fume hood for concentrated acid

Procedure

  1. Calculate the mass: grams = molarity (mol/L) × formula weight (g/mol) × final volume (L). For 1 L of 1 M Tris, weigh 121.1 g [3][6]. (5 min)
  2. Warm or cool the electrode and standards to the temperature at which the buffer will be used, and record that temperature [4].
  3. Rinse the electrode with deionized water and blot the glass gently with lint-free paper. Do not rub the tip [4][5].
  4. Calibrate with two or three standards that bracket the target (4.01, 7.00, 10.01). Stir gently, wait for a stable reading, and rinse between standards [4][5]. (10–15 min)
  5. Check the slope (95–105% is a common target; below about 90% means replace the electrode) and the offset (within ±30 mV) [4].
  6. Dissolve the solids in about 80% of the final volume of water in a beaker on a stirrer [1][2]. (10 min)
  7. 1 M Tris-HCl, pH 8.0: dissolve 121.1 g Tris base in about 400 mL water, add about 400 mL of 1 N HCl, then adjust dropwise to pH 8.0 [3][6].
  8. PBS, 1×: dissolve 8 g NaCl, 0.2 g KCl, 1.44 g Na₂HPO₄ and 0.24 g KH₂PO₄ in 800 mL water, then adjust to pH 7.4 with HCl [1].
  9. 0.5 M EDTA, pH 8.0: stir 186.1 g Na₂EDTA·2H₂O into 800 mL water and add about 20 g NaOH pellets. It dissolves only near pH 8.0 [2].
  10. 1 M HEPES-KOH, pH 7.5: add 238.4 g HEPES to about 400 mL of 1 N KOH, check the pH and add KOH until it reaches 7.5 [6].
  11. Add titrant slowly while stirring, concentrated at first and dilute near the target. Keep the electrode at the same depth for each reading [4][6]. (10 min)
  12. Transfer to a graduated cylinder or volumetric flask, bring to the final volume with water and mix well [1][6]. (5 min)
  13. Re-check the pH. For Tris, confirm it at the working temperature, as pH shifts with temperature [3].
  14. Sterilize by autoclaving (PBS and EDTA; 20 min, liquid cycle) or by 0.22 µm filtration [1][2][6]. (30 min)
  15. Label the bottle with name, concentration, pH, temperature, date and initials. Store as the recipe requires [1][6].
  16. Rinse the electrode and return it to storage solution. Do not store it in deionized water [4][5]. (2 min)

Pause points

  • Stop after sterilizing and labeling; sterile PBS and EDTA are stored at room temperature [1][2].
  • Finish pH adjustment and bringing to volume in one session, because pH changes with temperature [3].
  • Electrodes can rest in storage solution between uses [4][5].

Expected results

  • Final pH within about ±0.05 of the target at the stated temperature.
  • A clear, colorless solution; EDTA is fully dissolved.
  • Calibration slope of 95–105% with offset within ±30 mV [4].

Controls

  • Read a fresh pH 7.00 standard as an unknown after calibration
  • Record the pH and the temperature with every reading
  • Make a small test volume first when developing a new recipe

Safety and waste

  • Concentrated HCl and NaOH are corrosive: wear gloves, eye protection and a lab coat, and add acid or base slowly in a fume hood.
  • Wear a mask when weighing powders that irritate the airways.
  • Dispose of acids, bases and buffers containing hazardous additives according to your institution's waste rules.
