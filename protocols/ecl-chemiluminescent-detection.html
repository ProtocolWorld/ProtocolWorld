ECL Chemiluminescent Detection

≈ 1.5 h · 2026-11-27

Detect HRP-labeled antibodies on a western blot with enhanced chemiluminescence (ECL): final washes, substrate preparation at 0.1 mL/cm², and film or digital imaging with exposure series and troubleshooting.

⬇ Word (.docx)⬇ PowerPoint (.pptx)

Purpose

To visualize target proteins on a western-blot membrane by detecting horseradish peroxidase (HRP)-conjugated secondary antibodies with an enhanced chemiluminescent (ECL) substrate.

Principle

HRP catalyzes the oxidation of luminol by peroxide in the presence of an enhancer, and the oxidized luminol emits light at about 425 nm, which is captured on X-ray film or by a CCD camera [1]. Signal is proportional to the amount of enzyme only while substrate is in excess, so enough substrate and sensible exposure times are essential [2][3]. Promega likewise describes picogram-level detection, with repeated film exposures and reprobing possible [4]. Too much antibody or protein depletes substrate locally and causes oversaturated or white (“donut”) bands [1][3]. Substrates differ in sensitivity and duration: classic ECL gives low-to-mid picogram detection for about 0.5–2 h, while higher-sensitivity formulations detect femtograms and last for hours [1].

Materials and reagents

ItemDetails
Membrane with HRP secondary antibodyBlocked, probed with primary and HRP-conjugated secondary antibodies, kept moist
Wash buffer (TBST)20 mM Tris-HCl, 150 mM NaCl, pH 7.6, with 0.1% (v/v) Tween 20; PBST also works
ECL substrateTwo-component kit (luminol/enhancer and peroxide), mixed 1:1 immediately before use [1][3]
Mixed substrate volume0.1 mL per cm² of membrane: about 7 mL for a mini blot (7 × 8.5 cm), 12 mL for a midi blot (8.5 × 13.5 cm) [3]
Plastic sheet protector or cling wrapClear, wrinkle-free, to cover the wet membrane
Lint-free tissueTo blot excess substrate from the edge
Film and cassette (optional)X-ray film for darkroom exposure
Ponceau S stain (optional)0.1% in 5% acetic acid, to check transfer [2]
Mild stripping buffer (optional)Glycine-based, pH 2.2, with SDS and Tween 20, for reprobing [2]

Equipment

  • Rocker or orbital shaker
  • Clean tray or box for washes
  • CCD imager or X-ray film processor
  • Forceps (blunt-ended)
  • Timer
  • Pipettors and 15 mL tubes

Procedure

  1. After the secondary antibody, wash the membrane in at least 20 mL TBST (mini blot) or 100 mL (midi) on a shaker: 6 washes of 5 min each [3]. Remove all unbound antibody. (30 min)
  2. Make sure no sodium azide is in any buffer, since azide inhibits HRP [1][3]. Keep the membrane in buffer and never let it dry.
  3. Just before detection, mix equal volumes of the two substrate components in a clean tube (for example 3.5 mL + 3.5 mL for a mini blot) [1][3]. (1 min)
  4. Lift the membrane with forceps, let the buffer drain for a moment, and place it protein side up on a clean sheet of plastic [3].
  5. Pipette the substrate (0.1 mL/cm²) over the whole surface. Make sure it is covered evenly without bubbles [3]. (1 min)
  6. Incubate for 5 min at room temperature, and do not let the membrane dry [2][3]. (5 min)
  7. Lift the membrane, hold a corner on a tissue to drain extra liquid, and slide it into a sheet protector or wrap it in plastic [2][3]. Remove bubbles gently.
  8. Imager: capture a first image after about 30 s and continue every 30 s for up to 20 min; select the frame with no saturated pixels [2]. (5–20 min)
  9. Film: expose 10 s, then 30 s, 1 min, and 5 min; use 10 min only if no signal appears. Adjust to avoid black, overexposed bands [2]. (1–10 min)
  10. Image promptly: light output falls considerably after about 1 h for classic ECL, so finish all exposures within that window [2].
  11. When changing substrates, adjust exposure: Bio-Rad advises about 20-fold shorter exposure for Clarity than for classic ECL, or 5-fold shorter than for SuperSignal Pico [3].
  12. To reprobe, wash briefly, strip with the mild buffer for 5–10 min at room temperature, rinse 3 × 5 min in TBST, reblock, and repeat detection [2].

Pause points

  • After the final TBST washes the membrane can wait for up to 30 min in buffer if it never dries; do not delay after adding substrate.
  • Re-imaging is possible within the signal window of the substrate; classic ECL fades considerably after about 1 h [2].

Expected results

  • Sharp bands at the expected molecular weight, with low, even background.
  • Exposures show a graded increase in band intensity without white centers.
  • Loading controls show even signal across lanes.

Controls

  • Loading control antibody (such as β-actin or GAPDH) on the same membrane
  • Lane with a known positive lysate or recombinant protein
  • Secondary-antibody-only membrane to check non-specific signal
  • Ponceau S stain before blocking to confirm even transfer [2]

Troubleshooting

ProblemLikely causeSolution
No or weak signalPoor transfer, low antigen, old substrate, azide presentCheck transfer with Ponceau S, load 20–30 µg protein, use fresh substrate, remove azide [1][2][3]
High backgroundInsufficient blocking or washing, antibody too concentratedBlock longer, wash 6 × 5 min with Tween 20, dilute antibody further [2][3]
White (“donut”) bandsLocal substrate depletion by too much HRPDilute primary or secondary antibody, load less protein [1][3]
Black specklesClumps of blocking agentFilter or freshly dissolve milk or BSA [2]
Blank or white spotsTrapped air bubbles or dried areasRoll out bubbles; keep the membrane wet [2][3]
Signal fades quicklyToo much enzyme or an old substrateUse less antibody; repeat with fresh substrate and a faster imaging series [1][2]

Safety and waste

  • Wear gloves, a lab coat and eye protection; chemiluminescent substrates can irritate skin and eyes: read the supplier's safety data sheet.
  • Handle the membrane only with clean forceps. Dispose of used substrate and TBST as directed by your institution.
  • Film developer and fixer are chemical waste in many labs; collect them separately.
