Minimum Inhibitory Concentration (MIC) by Broth Microdilution

2 h setup + 16–20 h incubation + 30 min reading · 2026-11-24

Determine the lowest antibiotic concentration that stops visible growth of an aerobic bacterial isolate: CAMHB 96-well 2-fold dilution series, 0.5 McFarland inoculum to 5 × 10⁵ CFU/mL, 16–20 h reading, growth/sterility/QC controls and troubleshooting.

⬇ Word (.docx)⬇ PowerPoint (.pptx)

Purpose

To determine the lowest concentration of an antibacterial agent that prevents visible growth of an aerobic bacterial isolate in cation-adjusted Mueller-Hinton broth, using a 96-well plate and a 2-fold dilution series.

Principle

A standardized bacterial suspension is added to wells containing a 2-fold dilution series of the drug in broth. After 16–20 h at 35 ± 2 °C the MIC is the lowest concentration with no visible growth [1][2]. The result is valid only if the inoculum is close to 5 × 10⁵ CFU/mL, the growth control is clearly turbid, the sterility control is clear, and the quality-control strain falls inside its published range [1][2]. Plate material, solvents and medium cations can shift the MIC, so they must be fixed and reported [3].

Materials and reagents

ItemDetails
Cation-adjusted Mueller-Hinton broth (CAMHB)Commercial, certified for Ca²⁺ 20–25 mg/L and Mg²⁺ 10–12.5 mg/L (CLSI M07); pH 7.2–7.4 at room temperature; check each new lot with a QC strain [1]
Antibiotic powderReference-grade; correct for potency: mg powder = (volume × concentration) ÷ potency (µg/mg) [1]
Stock solventWater for most agents; DMSO only if needed, keeping the final concentration at or below 1% [4]
Test isolatesFresh 18–24 h colonies on a non-selective agar plate (blood or tryptic soy agar); never from broth or a slant [1]
QC strainsE. coli ATCC 25922, S. aureus ATCC 29213, P. aeruginosa ATCC 27853, E. faecalis ATCC 29212, chosen to match the drug [1]
Sterile 0.85% salineFor the 0.5 McFarland suspension and colony-count check
0.5 McFarland standardCommercial latex or BaSO₄ standard, about 1.5 × 10⁸ CFU/mL [1]
96-well platesSterile, untreated polystyrene, round or flat bottom, with lids; the same plate type throughout [3]
Optional: 0.002% polysorbate 80For lipophilic or sticky agents such as lipoglycopeptides and polymyxins; report if used [3]

Equipment

  • Biosafety cabinet
  • Incubator at 35 ± 2 °C, ambient air
  • Multichannel pipette (20–200 µL) and reservoirs
  • Turbidity meter or Wickerham card
  • Vortex mixer
  • Plate reader (optional, OD₆₀₀) or reading mirror
  • Analytical balance

Procedure

  1. Weigh the antibiotic and dissolve it to a 1 mg/mL active-drug stock using the potency from the certificate of analysis. Aliquot and store at −20 °C [1]. (20 min)
  2. Prepare the 4× top working solution in CAMHB. Example: 64 µg/mL gives a top well of 16 µg/mL. Make 500 µL of each 2-fold step down to 0.125 µg/mL (500 µL + 500 µL) [1]. (20 min)
  3. Label a plate. Put 50 µL of CAMHB in columns 1–10, 75 µL in column 11 (growth control) and 100 µL in column 12 (sterility control) [1]. (10 min)
  4. Add 25 µL of each 4× drug dilution to columns 1–10, highest concentration in column 1. Columns 11 and 12 receive no drug [1]. (10 min)
  5. From an 18–24 h plate, touch 3–5 similar colonies and suspend them in saline. Adjust to 0.5 McFarland (about 1.5 × 10⁸ CFU/mL) [1][2]. (10 min)
  6. Within 15 min, dilute the suspension 1:75 in CAMHB (for example, 10 µL in 740 µL) to reach about 2 × 10⁶ CFU/mL [1]. (5 min)
  7. Add 25 µL of the diluted inoculum to columns 1–11 only. Each well now holds 100 µL and about 5 × 10⁵ CFU/mL. Leave column 12 uninoculated [1][2]. (10 min)
  8. Streak 10 µL from the inoculum onto agar to check purity. Optional count check: 10 µL from a growth-control well into 10 mL saline, plate 100 µL; expect about 50 colonies [1][2].
  9. Cover the plate and incubate at 35 ± 2 °C in ambient air for 16–20 h (Acinetobacter 20–24 h; vancomycin and enterococci 24 h). Stack no more than four plates [1]. (16–24 h)
  10. Check controls first: column 11 must show a definite button or turbidity and column 12 must be clear. Reject the plate otherwise [1][2].
  11. Read the MIC as the lowest concentration with no growth to the unaided eye (use a mirror). A single skipped well: retest or report the higher value [2]. (20 min)
  12. Optional: read OD₆₀₀ against a broth blank. Any OD cutoff must be validated against visual reading before it replaces the eye [4][5].
  13. Compare the QC strain MIC with its range. Examples: E. coli ATCC 25922 colistin 0.25–2 µg/mL; S. aureus ATCC 29213 vancomycin 0.5–2 µg/mL [1]. Report the MIC in µg/mL.

Pause points

  • Drug stocks can be stored in single-use aliquots at −20 °C for 1–2 months and should not be refrozen [1].
  • Plates are not paused once inoculated; read them within the stated incubation window.

Expected results

  • The growth-control well is clearly turbid or has a definite button; the sterility-control well is clear.
  • Wells show an abrupt change from turbid to clear across 1–2 dilutions; the MIC is the first fully clear well.
  • The QC strain MIC is within its published range, and the purity plate shows a single colony type.

Controls

  • Growth control (no drug) to confirm the isolate grows in CAMHB
  • Sterility control (broth only, no inoculum) to detect contamination
  • QC strain with a known MIC range for every drug and every new broth or drug lot
  • Solvent control if DMSO or another solvent is used
  • Inoculum purity plate and, periodically, a colony count

Troubleshooting

ProblemLikely causeSolution
QC MIC out of rangeWrong inoculum, degraded drug, wrong cations or pHCheck potency and storage, remake broth or stock, recheck 0.5 McFarland, repeat the run
No growth in the growth controlInoculum too low, old colonies or toxic residueUse fresh colonies; confirm 1:75 dilution; repeat
Growth in the sterility controlContaminated broth, tips or plateDiscard results and repeat with fresh sterile reagents
Skipped wells or trailing growthPipetting error, contamination, or drug effectRetest; for trailing, follow the rule for that drug [2]
MIC higher than expected for a lipophilic drugDrug binding to plasticAdd 0.002% polysorbate 80 or switch plate type, and report it [3]
Edge wells dry outEvaporationUse lids and a humid tray; do not stack more than four plates

Safety and waste

  • Handle isolates in a biosafety cabinet at the level set by the organism (BSL-2 or higher for pathogens such as MRSA or carbapenem-resistant organisms).
  • Autoclave all plates, tips and cultures before disposal; decontaminate surfaces with 10% bleach or 70% ethanol.
  • Wear gloves and a lab coat; powdered antibiotics can sensitize, so weigh them in a fume hood or enclosure.
  • DMSO carries solutes through skin; wear gloves. Collect antibiotic stock waste according to institutional rules.
