Whole-Genome Bisulfite Sequencing

≈ 3–5 days (library) + sequencing · 2026-11-27

Prepare a methylated-adapter WGBS library for single-base methylation mapping: gDNA with lambda spike-in, shearing, end repair, A-tailing, ligation, bisulfite conversion, uracil-tolerant PCR, QC, sequencing depth and conversion-efficiency checks.

⬇ Word (.docx)⬇ PowerPoint (.pptx)

Purpose

To prepare an Illumina-compatible whole-genome bisulfite sequencing (WGBS, MethylC-seq style) library from genomic DNA so that cytosine methylation can be read at single-base resolution across the genome.

Principle

Sodium bisulfite deaminates unmethylated cytosine to uracil, which is read as thymine after PCR, whereas 5-methylcytosine is protected and still reads as cytosine. In the pre-conversion (MethylC-seq) workflow, genomic DNA is sheared, end-repaired, A-tailed and ligated to methylated adapters so that the adapters themselves survive conversion; the ligated library is then converted and amplified with a uracil-tolerant polymerase [1][2]. Unmethylated lambda phage DNA is spiked in to measure the conversion rate [1]. Because bisulfite fragments DNA, input losses are high and the library should be amplified with as few cycles as possible [1][2]. Post-bisulfite adapter tagging (for example SPLAT) is an alternative that ligates adapters after conversion [3]. Mapping uses bisulfite-aware aligners, and at least 20× CpG coverage is a common minimum for human genomes [4].

Materials and reagents

Equipment

  • Focused ultrasonicator (Covaris S- or E-series) with microTUBEs
  • Thermocycler with heated lid
  • Magnetic stand for 1.5 mL tubes and 0.2 mL strips
  • Thermomixer or heat block (60 °C)
  • Fluorometer (Qubit)
  • TapeStation or Bioanalyzer
  • Illumina sequencer (e.g., NovaSeq, paired-end 150)

Procedure

  1. Quantify gDNA by Qubit. Treat with RNase A if needed. Mix 2 µg gDNA with 10 ng unmethylated lambda DNA (0.5%) in 125 µL; remove 5 µL for a gel check [1]. (1.5 h)
  2. Shear in a Covaris microTUBE: 10% duty cycle, intensity 5, 200 cycles per burst, 3 × 60 s, for a ≈ 200 bp peak (settings differ by instrument) [1]. (15 min)
  3. Size-select with beads: add 72 µL beads to 120 µL DNA (0.6×), keep the supernatant, add 105 µL beads, wash twice with 500 µL 80% ethanol, elute in 34 µL Tris [1]. (30 min)
  4. End repair: 34 µL DNA, 5 µL 10× End-It buffer, 5 µL dNTP, 5 µL ATP, 1 µL enzyme mix (50 µL). Incubate at room temperature for 45 min [1]. (45 min)
  5. Clean up with 70 µL beads (1.4×), wash twice with 80% ethanol, and elute in 42 µL Tris [1]. (20 min)
  6. A-tail: 42 µL DNA, 5 µL 10× dA-tailing buffer, 3 µL Klenow exo− (5 U/µL). Incubate at 37 °C for 30 min [1]. (30 min)
  7. Clean up with 70 µL beads (1.4×), wash twice, and elute in 16.25 µL Tris [1]. (20 min)
  8. Ligate: 16.25 µL DNA, 2 µL methylated adapter (25 µM), 2.5 µL 10× ligase buffer, 1.25 µL T4 ligase (500 U), 3 µL water (25 µL). Incubate at 16 °C overnight [1]. (Overnight)
  9. Add 25 µL water, then 50 µL beads (1×). Wash twice, elute, and repeat the 1× cleanup once to remove adapter dimers. Final elution: 21 µL Tris [1]. (45 min)
  10. Check an aliquot by Qubit. If the library is below 3 ng/µL, stop: too little DNA survived to continue [1]. (15 min)
  11. Convert up to 450 ng (or 20 µL) with EZ Gold or MethylCode, following the kit manual for conversion, desulfonation and cleanup. Elute in 23 µL per PCR [1]. (≈ 3 h)
  12. Alternative kit workflow [2]: EpiTect Fast, 95 °C 5 min, 60 °C 20 min, 95 °C 5 min, 60 °C 20 min; desulfonate with Buffer BD for 15 min at room temperature. (≈ 3 h)
  13. Amplify: 23 µL converted DNA, 2 µL 25× primer mix, 25 µL 2× KAPA HiFi Uracil+ ReadyMix (50 µL) [1]. (10 min)
  14. PCR: 95 °C 2 min; 98 °C 30 s; 4 cycles of 98 °C 15 s, 60 °C 30 s, 72 °C 1 min; 72 °C 10 min. Use up to 6–8 cycles for low input [1][2]. (1 h)
  15. Clean up with 50 µL beads (1×) and elute in 16 µL Tris [1]. (20 min)
  16. Quantify by Qubit HS (typically 5–25 ng/µL after 4 cycles) and run a TapeStation or Bioanalyzer trace [1][2]. (45 min)
  17. Confirm a broad peak around 300–400 bp including adapters, with no adapter-dimer peak near 120–130 bp. Repeat a 0.8× bead cleanup if dimers are present [2]. (15 min)
  18. Pool and sequence paired-end (e.g., 2 × 150) to the depth required: ≥ 20× CpG coverage is a common minimum; this is roughly 300–460 M read pairs for a human genome [4]. (Core facility)
  19. Trim adapters, map with a bisulfite-aware aligner (Bismark [3] or bwa-meth with MethylDackel [4]), and call methylation as C/(C+T) per cytosine. (1–2 days)
  20. Compute conversion efficiency as 1 − (C/(C+T)) on lambda reads. Expect > 99% [1]; one protocol flags > 0.1% unconverted lambda as a warning sign [2]. (30 min)

Pause points

  • After the overnight ligation, the library can be held at 4 °C for a few hours before cleanup.
  • Store the cleaned, ligated DNA at −20 °C before bisulfite conversion.
  • Converted DNA should be amplified promptly; if needed, store at −20 °C briefly, since converted DNA is single-stranded and fragile.
  • Final libraries can be stored at −20 °C until pooling.

Expected results

  • A smooth library trace around 300–400 bp (with adapters) and a concentration of roughly 5–25 ng/µL after 4 PCR cycles [1][2].
  • Lambda spike-in conversion above 99%; non-CpG methylation near 0–1% in mammalian cells [1][4].
  • Low duplication (under about 15% is the warning threshold in [2]) and mapping efficiency of about 70–85% [3][4].

Controls

  • Unmethylated lambda DNA spike-in to calculate the conversion rate [1]
  • Optional fully methylated control (e.g., CpG-methylated pUC19) to check that methylated cytosines are protected [4]
  • No-template control carried through PCR to detect contamination
  • Chloroplast reads as an internal unmethylated control for plant samples [1]

Troubleshooting

ProblemLikely causeSolution
Library below 3 ng/µL after ligationLow input or bead lossDo not proceed; repeat with more DNA or use a lower-input method [1]
Low yield after PCRDNA degraded by bisulfite or PCR inhibitedAdd 2–4 cycles at most; use fresh conversion reagent; extra bead cleanup [1][2]
Adapter-dimer peak near 120–130 bpExcess adapter or poor ligation cleanupRepeat a 0.8× bead cleanup; keep the second 1× cleanup after ligation [1][2]
Conversion below 99%Incomplete conversion or too much DNAReduce input; mix reagent fully; check the kit lot and incubation times [1]
High duplicationToo many PCR cycles for the inputStart with 4–6 cycles and add only if needed [2]
Biased coverage or GC biasHeavy conversion damageUse fewer PCR cycles; consider post-bisulfite methods such as SPLAT [3][4]

Safety and waste

  • Bisulfite reagents are irritants and sodium hydroxide is corrosive; wear gloves and eye protection.
  • Ethidium bromide or other gel stains: follow local rules and dispose in marked waste.
  • Follow institutional rules for human-derived DNA and for consent and data handling.
  • Collect kit waste containing guanidine salts separately; do not mix with bleach.
