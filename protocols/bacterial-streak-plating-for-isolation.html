Bacterial Streak Plating for Isolation

Estimated time: Plates 1 day ahead; streaking 15 min + 16–24 h incubation | Protocol World

To obtain well-separated single colonies, each derived from one cell, from a mixed or concentrated E. coli culture by quadrant streaking on LB agar.

Dragging an inoculating loop across agar dilutes the bacteria with each sweep. Sterilizing the loop between quadrants removes the cells carried over, so that in the last streaks individual cells land far enough apart to grow into discrete colonies [7][8][9]. Antibiotic in the agar selects for plasmid-carrying strains [1][5][10]. Pouring, drying and incubating the plates carefully prevents merged colonies and spreading growth [5][8].

ItemDetails
LB agar (Miller), per liter10 g tryptone, 5 g yeast extract, 10 g NaCl, 15 g agar, water to 1 L; adjust to pH 7.0 [3][6][10]. Some recipes use 5 g NaCl (Lennox) [1]
Antibiotic stock (1000×)For example ampicillin or carbenicillin 100 mg/mL; kanamycin 50 mg/mL. Filter through 0.2 µm, aliquot, store at −20 °C [5][10]
Final antibiotic concentrationsAmpicillin or carbenicillin 100 µg/mL; kanamycin 50 µg/mL [2][5][10]
Bacterial culture or colonyLiquid culture or an isolated colony on a plate
Petri dishes100 × 15 mm sterile plastic, about 20–25 mL agar each [1][2]
Inoculating loopsMetal loop (flame sterilized) or sterile disposable plastic loops, 1–10 µL [7][9]
70% ethanolFor wiping the bench and spills

  • 50–55 °C water bath
  • Bunsen burner or loop sterilizer

  • Combine tryptone, yeast extract, NaCl and agar in a bottle larger than twice the liquid volume. Add water, adjust pH to 7.0 and swirl to mix [3][6][10]. (10 min)
  • Autoclave on a liquid cycle at 121 °C (15 psi) for 20–30 min, with the cap loose [1][5]. (1 h)
  • Cool the agar to 50–55 °C in a water bath. Antibiotic goes in only below about 50 °C [2][3][4][10]. (30 min)
  • Add antibiotic (1 mL of 1000× stock per liter) and swirl gently to avoid bubbles [5][10]. (2 min)
  • Pour about 20–25 mL into each 100 mm dish. Pop bubbles with a brief pass of a flame, and let plates set for about 30 min [1][2][5]. (20 min)
  • Dry plates with lids ajar in a hood for 1–2 h or at room temperature overnight until no condensation remains. Store inverted at 4 °C in a bag [1][3][5]. (Overnight)
  • Warm plates to room temperature. Label the plate bottom with strain, date and your initials [7][8]. (5 min)
  • Sterilize the loop in the flame until red hot and let it cool for 10–15 s, or touch it to clean agar [7][9]. (1 min)
  • Pick a small amount from an isolated colony or a loopful of culture. Streak the first quadrant with closely spaced back-and-forth strokes [7][8][9]. (1 min)
  • Flame the loop and cool it, rotate the plate about a quarter turn, then drag the loop through the edge of the first quadrant two to four times and streak the second quadrant [7][8]. (1 min)
  • Repeat for the third quadrant and again for the fourth, which trails off toward the center, each time crossing only the edge of the previous area [8][9]. (2 min)
  • Flame the loop one more time. Invert the plate and incubate at 37 °C for 16–24 h [8][9]. (16–24 h)
  • Inspect for single, well-separated colonies. Store the plate inverted at 4 °C [5]. (5 min)
  • After step 2, autoclaved agar can be kept molten at 50–55 °C for a short time or cooled and remelted.
  • After step 6, poured plates keep at 4 °C: up to about 1 month with antibiotic, and longer without [1][3][5][10].
  • After step 13, colonies stay usable for up to about 1 week at 4 °C; restreak if needed.
  • Heavy growth in the first quadrant, fewer colonies in the second and third, and separate single colonies in the third or fourth quadrant [8][9].
  • Uniform colony morphology suggests a pure culture; mixed colony types suggest contamination [8].
  • Colonies on antibiotic plates only if the strain carries the matching resistance.
  • Uninoculated plate from the same batch to confirm sterility; discard the batch if growth appears [8]
  • Resistant strain on antibiotic plate (growth) and non-resistant strain (no growth) to test the antibiotic [5]
  • Known reference strain for colony morphology

  • Flames and hot loops cause burns: keep hair and loose clothing away, and let loops cool before putting them down [8].
  • Work with the organism at the biosafety level set by your institution, usually BSL-1 for laboratory E. coli K-12 strains.
  • Wipe the bench with 70% ethanol before and after work [5][9].
  • Discard used plates, plastic loops and sharps in biohazard containers for autoclaving or disinfection according to local rules [7].
