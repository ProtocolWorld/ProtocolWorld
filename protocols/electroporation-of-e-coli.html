Electroporation of E. coli

Estimated time: About 6 h + overnight | Protocol World

To prepare electrocompetent E. coli and introduce plasmid DNA or purified ligation and assembly products by a short high-voltage pulse, giving far more transformants than chemical methods.

A brief electric field transiently permeabilizes the bacterial envelope so that DNA can enter. Cells must be harvested at mid-log phase and washed extensively in ice-cold 10% glycerol to remove salts, because residual ions raise conductivity and cause arcing [1][2][3]. Fast addition of recovery medium after the pulse improves survival [1].

ItemDetails
E. coli strainCloning strain such as DH10B, TOP10 or DH5α; fresh overnight stationary culture in LB
LB medium10 g/L tryptone, 5 g/L yeast extract, 10 g/L NaCl; about 10 mL (small batch) or 100 mL (large batch) per strain [1]
10% glycerol10% (v/v) in deionized water, sterile, chilled to 4 °C; at least 20× the pellet volume per wash [1]
SOC or LB recovery medium500–1000 µL per transformation, at room temperature [1][2]
Plasmid DNASalt-free, ideally 1–3 µL containing under 100 ng total (100 pg is enough for a miniprep plasmid) [1][2]
Selective LB agar platesWith the correct antibiotic, pre-warmed to 37 °C
Electroporation cuvettesSterile, 0.1 cm (1 mm) gap, chilled on ice [1][2]
Ice-water bathFor chilling culture and cuvettes
  • Electroporator (for example Bio-Rad MicroPulser or Gene Pulser)
  • Refrigerated centrifuge (4 °C) with 50 mL and 15 mL tube rotors
  • Shaking incubator at 37 °C
  • Spectrophotometer for OD600
  • −80 °C freezer
  • Pre-chilled 1.5 mL tubes and metal block
  • Inoculate 100 mL LB in a 500 mL flask with overnight culture to a starting OD600 of about 0.05 (10 mL in a 50 mL flask for a small batch) [1]. (5 min)
  • Shake at 37 °C for 2–3 h until OD600 is 0.4–0.6. Do not exceed mid-log, because efficiency falls sharply beyond exponential growth [1]. (2–3 h)
  • Chill the flask in ice water for more than 10 min. Cool the centrifuge to 4 °C and keep tubes and 10% glycerol on ice from here on [1][3]. (15 min)
  • Split the culture into two 50 mL tubes and spin 6,000 rpm for 5 min at 4 °C. Discard the supernatant [1]. (10 min)
  • Resuspend each pellet in 15 mL ice-cold 10% glycerol by pipetting or brief vortexing, move to a 15 mL tube, and spin 5 min at 4 °C [1]. (10 min)
  • Repeat the 10% glycerol wash until four washes are done in total. This removes the ions that cause arcing [1][3]. (30 min)
  • Resuspend each final pellet in about 500 µL ice-cold 10% glycerol (roughly 100× concentration). Adjust for any liquid left in the tube [1]. (5 min)
  • Aliquot 30–50 µL into pre-chilled tubes and use at once, or snap-freeze and store at −80 °C; frozen cells keep for up to 6 months [1][3]. (10 min)
  • Thaw an aliquot on ice, add 1–3 µL DNA (under 100 ng), flick to mix, and keep on ice for 1–10 min. Include a no-DNA control [1][2]. (5 min)
  • Pipette the mixture into a chilled 0.1 cm cuvette, tap it to the bottom without bubbles, and wipe condensation from the outside [1][2]. (2 min)
  • Pulse at 1.8 kV (200 Ω, 25 µF; field about 18 kV/cm); for a 0.2 cm cuvette use 2.5 kV [1][2]. Record the time constant; 4–5 ms is good. (1 min)
  • Immediately add 500–1000 µL room-temperature SOC or LB to the cuvette, mix to clear the gap, and transfer to a 1.5 mL tube [1][2]. (1 min)
  • Incubate at 37 °C with shaking for 30–60 min, with the tube on its side [1][2]. (30–60 min)
  • Plate about 50–200 µL on pre-warmed selective LB agar and incubate overnight at 37 °C [1][2]. (15 min + overnight)
  • After step 8: competent cell aliquots can be stored at −80 °C for up to 6 months [3].
  • After step 13: the recovered mixture can stay at 4 °C overnight and be plated the next morning if no colonies appear [2].
  • Healthy preparations give several hundred colonies or more from about 100 pg of miniprep plasmid [2]; the sources give no CFU/µg figure, so determine your own with a control plasmid.
  • The time constant is 4–5 ms; a much shorter value signals arcing or salt carryover [2].
  • No colonies on the no-DNA control plate.
  • No-DNA control to check cell sterility and antibiotic selection.
  • Control plasmid (for example 10 pg pUC19) to calculate transformation efficiency in CFU/µg.
  • Cut vector alone (no insert) when cloning, to judge background [2].

  • Never autoclave plastic cuvettes and do not soak them in bleach, because rust raises the risk of arcing [2].
  • The pulse delivers high voltage: keep the chamber closed and never touch the electrodes while charged.
  • Treat transformed E. coli as a biosafety level 1 organism; decontaminate cuvettes, tubes and plates by autoclaving or bleach.
  • Dispose of ampicillin- or kanamycin-containing media as chemical or biohazard waste per local rules.
