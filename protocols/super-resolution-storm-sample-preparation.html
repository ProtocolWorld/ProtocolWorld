Super-Resolution (STORM) Sample Preparation

Estimated time: 2 days (about 8 h hands-on) | Protocol World

To prepare fixed, immunolabeled adherent cells on #1.5 glass for direct STORM (dSTORM) with Alexa Fluor 647, so that single-molecule blinking is sparse enough to reconstruct images with a localization precision of roughly 20 nm.

In dSTORM, a cyanine dye such as Alexa Fluor 647 is driven into a long-lived dark state by intense red light in an oxygen-depleted buffer containing a thiol (2-mercaptoethanol or cysteamine). A small random subset of molecules returns to the fluorescent state in each frame, is localized, and thousands of frames are summed into one image [1][3]. Image quality is limited by labeling: a fixative that preserves structure, a specific antibody at the right density, a fresh imaging buffer and fiducial beads for drift correction [1][2][3].

Blocking buffer3% BSA + 0.05% Triton X-100 in PBS [1]
Washing buffer0.2% BSA + 0.05% Triton X-100 in PBS [1]
Primary antibodyValidated for immunofluorescence; titrate (1:600 in blocking buffer was used for a histone antibody) [1][3]
Secondary antibody or dye conjugateAlexa Fluor 647 conjugate (for two colors pair with Cy3B or Alexa Fluor 568) [1][3]
Post-fixation solution4% PFA in PBS [1]
Fiducial beads0.1 µm carboxylate FluoSpheres diluted 1:500,000 in PBS [1]
Buffer A10 mM Tris-HCl pH 8.0, 50 mM NaCl [3]
Buffer B50 mM Tris-HCl pH 8.0, 10 mM NaCl, 10% (w/v) glucose [3]
GLOX stock (250 µL)14 mg glucose oxidase + 50 µL catalase (17 mg/mL) + 200 µL Buffer A; vortex, spin at 14,000 rpm, keep supernatant; 4 °C up to 2 weeks [3]
1 M MEA77 mg cysteamine (MEA) in 1.0 mL 0.25 N HCl; 4 °C up to 1 month [3]
BME imaging buffer (per ~700 µL)7 µL GLOX + 7 µL 2-mercaptoethanol + 690 µL Buffer B; mix on ice just before imaging; best for Alexa Fluor 647 or Cy3B [3]
MEA imaging buffer (per ~700 µL)7 µL GLOX + 70 µL 1 M MEA + 620 µL Buffer B; for dual-color with Alexa Fluor 647 [3]
  • Biosafety cabinet and CO₂ incubator
  • Fume hood (PFA, glutaraldehyde, 2-mercaptoethanol)
  • Rocker or orbital shaker
  • STORM microscope: 100× oil objective (NA ≥ 1.4), 642 nm laser, EMCCD or sCMOS camera, drift correction [1]
  • Optional 561 nm and 405 nm lasers for second color and reactivation
  • STORM or dSTORM microscope: 100× oil objective (NA ≥ 1.4), 642 nm laser (and 561 nm for second color), optional 405 nm, EMCCD or sCMOS camera, focus lock or drift correction [1]
  • Analysis software such as ThunderSTORM (ImageJ/Fiji) [1]
  • Seed cells on #1.5 glass-bottom dishes so they are 50–70% confluent and well spread after overnight growth at 37 °C, 5% CO₂ [1][2]. (Day 1, 15 min)
  • Rinse once with warm PBS. Fix with the fixative suited to your target (see Materials) at room temperature, in a fume hood. Then wash 3 × with PBS [1][2][3]. (Day 2, 15–25 min)
  • If glutaraldehyde was used, quench free aldehydes (for example, with a glycine or sodium borohydride quench per your lab’s standard) to reduce autofluorescence. Skip after alcohol fixation. (10 min)
  • Permeabilize in 0.2% Triton X-100 in PBS for 10 min, then wash 3 × with PBS. Methanol-fixed cells need no extra permeabilization [1]. (15 min)
  • Block in blocking buffer for 1 h at room temperature (use heat-treated, filtered serum or BSA) [1][3]. (1 h)
  • Incubate with 150 µL of titrated primary antibody in blocking buffer at 4 °C overnight in a humid, closed chamber [1]. (Overnight)
  • Wash 3 × 5 min in washing buffer, then incubate with Alexa Fluor 647 secondary for 2 h at room temperature in the dark [1]. (2.5 h)
  • Wash 3 × 5 min in washing buffer, then 3 × in PBS. Post-fix in 4% PFA for 10 min and wash in PBS to lock antibodies in place [1][3]. (30 min)
  • Apply 200 µL of bead suspension (1:500,000) for 30 min, then rinse with PBS. Aim for at least 5 beads per field; skip if the microscope has focus lock [1]. (40 min)
  • Make GLOX, and (if needed) 1 M MEA. Keep Buffer B at room temperature and the thiol and GLOX on ice. Mix the imaging buffer immediately before use [3]. (15 min)
  • Replace PBS with about 700 µL of imaging buffer per well; seal with a clean coverslip or lid to limit oxygen re-entry. Image soon, since the buffer depletes [3]. (5 min)
  • Find a cell at low 642 nm power (about 1 mW). Raise to about 2–10 kW/cm² to switch most dyes off; pre-bleach 30 s to 3 min if blinking is too dense [1]. (10 min)
  • Acquire 20 ms frames, typically 10,000–30,000 per channel. Add 405 nm light (1–100 µW) later to reactivate dyes. In two colors, image Alexa Fluor 647 first [1]. (10–30 min)
  • Reconstruct with ThunderSTORM (ImageJ/Fiji), apply drift correction with the beads, and filter localizations by precision and intensity [1]. (30–60 min)
  • After post-fixation (step 8) cells can be stored in PBS at 4 °C, protected from light, for a few days before imaging.
  • Primary antibody incubation (step 6) is the planned overnight pause.
  • Sparse single-molecule blinking in every frame, with a clear structure (for example, a continuous microtubule or nuclear pore ring) emerging as localizations accumulate.
  • Typical localization precision of about 10–30 nm for Alexa Fluor 647 under optimal buffer conditions.
  • Beads stay in focus and report drift of a few tens of nanometers over the acquisition.
  • Secondary-only sample to measure nonspecific labeling and background blinking
  • Unlabeled, fixed cells to check autofluorescence in the 642 nm channel
  • Validated primary antibody (ChIP-grade or knockout-validated where possible) [1]
  • A well-characterized structure such as microtubules as a labeling and resolution reference
  • Fiducial beads in every field for drift correction

  • PFA and glutaraldehyde are toxic and sensitizing: use in a fume hood with gloves, goggles and a lab coat.
  • 2-Mercaptoethanol is toxic and malodorous; prepare buffers in a fume hood.
  • High-power lasers (Class 3B/4): follow laser safety training and interlocks; never look into the beam.
  • Dispose of aldehyde, thiol and bead waste as hazardous chemical waste according to institutional rules.
