Dendritic Cell Generation from Bone Marrow

Estimated time: 8–9 days | Protocol World

To differentiate large numbers of dendritic cells (DCs) from mouse bone marrow progenitors with GM-CSF, for antigen presentation, T-cell activation and phenotyping assays.

Bone marrow progenitors cultured with GM-CSF, alone or with IL-4, give loosely adherent, CD11c-positive DCs over about a week; the cytokine is replenished by partial feeding [1][2][3]. Red cells are removed by ammonium chloride lysis [1], and purity is checked by flow cytometry, with optional sorting [1][3]. Sources differ in cytokine dose and feeding schedule, so the lab should fix one scheme and keep it constant [1][2][3].

ItemDetails
MiceC57BL/6 wild type is used in [1]; age is not stated in the sources (young adults, 6–12 weeks, are common practice). Euthanize by an approved method.
R10 mediumRPMI 1640 with 10% heat-inactivated FBS and 1× penicillin–streptomycin [1][3]. Optional: 2 mM L-glutamine and 50 µM 2-mercaptoethanol [2].
GM-CSFRecombinant mouse GM-CSF at 20 ng/mL in R10 [3]. Use fresh stock; old stock gives inconsistent differentiation [3]. Alternatives: 5 ng/mL GM-CSF with 5 ng/mL IL-4 [1], or 10% B16-GM-CSF supernatant [2].
RBC lysis buffer (pH 7.3)155 mM NH₄Cl, 12 mM NaHCO₃, 0.1 mM EDTA (per 750 mL: 6.218 g, 0.756 g, 27.9 mg) [1]. Keep ice cold.
Wash and flush bufferIce-cold PBS or RPMI [1][2][3]. FACS buffer (PBS with serum or BSA) for sorting [1].
70% ethanol, sterile gauzeTo surface-sterilize bones.
Culture dishes100 × 20 mm non-tissue-culture-treated (bacteriological) Petri dishes [2][3].
Antibodies for flow cytometryAnti-CD11c (e.g., N418) and anti-CD11b; optionally anti-F4/80 to exclude macrophages [1][3]. Dilutions used in [1]: CD11b 1:400, CD11c 1:300, 45 min on ice.
Disposables25-gauge needles and 2 mL syringes, 70 µm strainers, 15 and 50 mL tubes, trypan blue.
  • Biosafety cabinet
  • CO₂ incubator (37 °C, 5% CO₂)
  • Refrigerated centrifuge (300 × g)
  • Dissection tools
  • Hemocytometer or cell counter
  • Inverted microscope
  • Flow cytometer (and sorter if purifying)
  • Euthanize the mouse, spray with 70% ethanol, and remove both hind legs above the hip. Strip off muscle with sterile gauze, keeping bones intact [1][3]. (20 min)
  • Dip the bones in 70% ethanol for 5–10 s and keep them in cold sterile medium on ice. Use only intact bones [3]. (5 min)
  • In the hood, cut both ends of each femur and tibia. Flush with ice-cold medium through a 25-gauge needle until the bones turn white (2–3 flushes) [1][3]. (20 min)
  • Pipette the marrow to break up clumps, pass through a 70 µm strainer, and spin 300 × g for 5 min at 4 °C [1][2][3]. (10 min)
  • Resuspend the pellet in 3 mL ice-cold RBC lysis buffer, 3–4 min on ice. Add 3 mL RPMI and spin 300 × g, 5 min. Repeat if the pellet is still red [1]. (15 min)
  • Resuspend in R10 and count viable cells by trypan blue. One mouse (femurs and tibias) yields roughly 6–7 × 10⁷ marrow cells [1]. (10 min)
  • Seed 2 × 10⁶ viable cells in 10 mL R10 + 20 ng/mL GM-CSF per 100 mm bacteriological dish [3]. Option [1]: 10–15 × 10⁶ cells in 15 mL with 5 ng/mL GM-CSF and IL-4. (10 min)
  • Incubate at 37 °C, 5% CO₂. Day 0 is the seeding day. (3 days)
  • Day 3: add 10 mL fresh R10 + 20 ng/mL GM-CSF to each dish without removing medium [3]. (In [1] the feed is on day 2, replacing 5 mL.) (10 min)
  • Day 6: remove 10 mL of medium, spin briefly (300 × g, 5 min) to keep floating cells, resuspend in 10 mL fresh R10 + GM-CSF and return to the dish [3]. (20 min)
  • Day 8: collect floating and loosely adherent cells by gentle PBS washes and spin briefly [3]. Avoid EDTA if macrophage carry-over matters [3]. Option: harvest on day 9–10 [3]. (30 min)
  • Count cells and stain an aliquot for CD11c and CD11b; above 90% CD11c⁺ is reported for the conditioned-medium method [2]. Sort CD11c⁺ cells if purer cells are needed [1][3]. (1–2 h)
  • Cells can be left undisturbed between feedings (days 0–3 and 3–6).
  • After harvest, use cells the same day; do not freeze immature DCs for later assays.
  • By day 6, cells show dendritic projections and form loosely adherent clusters [1].
  • Harvested cells are mostly CD11c⁺; above 90% with the conditioned-medium variant [2]. Report your own purity because no source gives a firm figure for every variant.
  • Typical use afterwards: antigen pulse and co-culture with T cells [1].
  • Unstained and single-stained (CD11b only, CD11c only) flow controls [1]
  • Isotype or fluorescence-minus-one control for gating
  • A parallel culture without GM-CSF to show cytokine dependence
  • Keep the same cytokine lot, dose and schedule across experiments [1]

  • Follow your institution's animal-use approval (IACUC) and euthanasia rules.
  • Sharps (needles, scissors) go in sharps containers.
  • Treat mouse tissue and cultures as biohazard waste (BSL-1/2 as set by your institution); bleach liquid waste.
  • Recombinant cytokines are low risk but avoid aerosols; wear gloves and a lab coat.
