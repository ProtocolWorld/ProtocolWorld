Intracellular Cytokine Staining

Estimated time: 2 days (overnight rest, 4–8 h stimulation, about 4 h staining and acquisition) | Protocol World

To identify and quantify cytokine-producing cells, such as IFN-γ⁺ or TNF⁺ T cells, at the single-cell level after polyclonal or antigen-specific stimulation.

Stimulated lymphocytes make cytokines that are normally secreted. Brefeldin A or monensin blocks Golgi transport, so cytokine builds up inside the cell [1][2][3]. Cells are stained for surface markers and viability, fixed to lock the proteins in place, permeabilized with saponin so antibodies can enter, and stained for cytokines. Flow cytometry then reports the fraction of each subset that produces each cytokine [1][2][4].

  • Biosafety cabinet
  • CO₂ incubator (37 °C, 5% CO₂)
  • Refrigerated centrifuge with plate rotor
  • 96-well round- or U-bottom plates or FACS tubes
  • Multichannel pipette
  • Cell counter or hemocytometer
  • Flow cytometer with suitable lasers and filters
  • Ice bucket
  • Prepare complete RPMI-1640 with 10% FBS, warmed to 37 °C. Use azide-free buffers for every step before fixation because the cells stay alive [1][3]. (10 min)
  • Isolate PBMCs by Ficoll-Paque density centrifugation (400 × g, 20 min, room temperature, brake off) or make a splenocyte suspension through a 70 µm strainer [3]. (60 min)
  • If using cryopreserved PBMCs, thaw quickly at 37 °C and wash twice in warm medium (473 × g, 8 min, room temperature); benzonase at 25 U/mL limits clumping [1]. (30 min)
  • Count cells and check viability. Adjust to 5–10 × 10⁶ cells/mL in warm complete RPMI [1]. (10 min)
  • Plate 200 µL (at least 1 × 10⁶ cells) per well in a 96-well plate, or 3 × 10⁶ cells/mL in flasks, and rest overnight (6–18 h) at 37 °C, 5% CO₂ [1][3]. (6–18 h)
  • Prepare stimulants. Use PMA at 10 ng/mL plus ionomycin at 1 µg/mL, or peptide mix at 1 µg/mL per peptide, or SEB at 1 µg/mL [1][2]. (10 min)
  • Prepare the transport inhibitor: brefeldin A at 10 µg/mL for most cytokines, or brefeldin A plus monensin at 5 µg/mL each if also staining CD107a or CD154 [1][2]. (5 min)
  • Keep total DMSO plus ethanol from all additions at or below 0.5% to avoid solvent toxicity [1].
  • Set up wells: unstimulated (inhibitor only), stimulated, and a positive control, plus extra unstimulated wells for FMO and isotype controls [1][3]. (10 min)
  • Add stimulant and inhibitor to the stimulated wells; add only inhibitor to the unstimulated wells. Add anti-CD107a now if needed [1][2]. (5 min)
  • Incubate at 37 °C, 5% CO₂: 4–5 h for PMA/ionomycin, or 6–8 h for peptide, SEB or anti-CD3/CD28. Cytokine accumulation is best in the last hours [1][2][3]. (4–8 h)
  • Add EDTA to 2 mM and incubate 15 min at room temperature to detach adherent cells and reduce aggregates [1][2]. (15 min)
  • Wash cells twice with 250 µL cold staining buffer (PBS, 0.1–0.5% BSA, 2 mM EDTA; no azide if staining a live-dead dye) at 470 × g for 5–10 min [1][4]. (15 min)
  • Stain with a fixable viability dye in protein-free PBS (per the manufacturer; typically 1:1,000), 15–30 min at 4 °C in the dark, then wash with staining buffer. (30 min)
  • Add surface antibodies (for example CD3, CD4, CD8) in 100 µL staining buffer at titrated amounts. Incubate 20–45 min on ice in the dark [1][2][4]. (20–45 min)
  • Wash twice with cold staining buffer (470 × g, 5 min, 4 °C) [1]. (10 min)
  • Fix with 2% formaldehyde or paraformaldehyde in PBS (or 100 µL of a commercial Cytofix/Cytoperm-type solution) for 20 min at room temperature or on ice [4]. (20 min)
  • Wash twice with 1× saponin-based permeabilization buffer (for example 1% BSA, 0.5% saponin in PBS) at 470–800 × g, 5–10 min, 4 °C [2][4]. (15 min)
  • Resuspend in 50–100 µL permeabilization buffer containing titrated anti-cytokine antibodies (for example IFN-γ, TNF, IL-2). Incubate 30–45 min on ice or at 4 °C in the dark [2][4]. (30–45 min)
  • Wash three times with permeabilization buffer, then once with staining buffer without saponin [2][4]. (20 min)
  • Resuspend in 200–300 µL staining buffer or 1% formaldehyde in PBS. Keep at 4 °C in the dark and acquire within 24 h [4]. (5 min)
  • Run single-color compensation beads, then acquire at least 100,000–500,000 live lymphocyte events per sample at low to medium flow rate. (30–60 min)
  • Gate singlets, then live cells, lymphocytes and CD3⁺ then CD4⁺ or CD8⁺ T cells. Set cytokine-positive gates using FMO and unstimulated controls [1][3]. (30 min)
  • Overnight rest of PBMCs or splenocytes at 37 °C before stimulation (about 16–18 h).
  • After fixation, cells can be held in staining buffer at 4 °C in the dark and acquired within 24 h.
  • Do not pause during the stimulation with transport inhibitor; keep it to the stated 4–8 h.
  • Unstimulated cells show very low background (typically below 0.05–0.1% cytokine⁺ events).
  • PMA/ionomycin gives clear IFN-γ⁺ and TNF⁺ populations in CD4⁺ and CD8⁺ T cells; antigen-specific responses are usually much smaller (often 0.01–1%).
  • PMA/ionomycin can reduce surface CD4 staining, so use CD4 gating carefully [5].
  • Unstimulated sample with transport inhibitor only, to set the background
  • Positive control (PMA/ionomycin or SEB) to prove the assay works
  • Fluorescence-minus-one (FMO) controls for each cytokine channel
  • Isotype or biological control (unstimulated cells) to judge nonspecific binding [5]
  • Unstained and single-color compensation controls
  • Viability dye to exclude dead cells
  • Optional blocking control: pre-incubate with unlabeled antibody or use a known cytokine-negative subset

Weak or no cytokine signalInhibitor not added, stimulation too short, or antibody not titratedAdd brefeldin A with the stimulus, extend to 6 h, check the positive control and re-titrate antibody
High background in unstimulated cellsDead cells, sticky antibodies, or too little washingUse a viability dye, add an Fc block, increase permeabilization-buffer washes
Low viability after stimulationOverlong exposure to brefeldin A, monensin or PMA/ionomycin, or high solventLimit to 4–8 h and keep DMSO plus ethanol at or below 0.5% [1]
Surface marker lost (for example CD4)PMA-induced downregulationUse CD3 and CD8 gating, or stain surface markers with antibody clones that are less sensitive
Cell loss after fixationSmall pellets, low centrifuge speedSpin at higher speed, use BSA-containing buffer, and avoid aspirating close to the pellet

  • Formaldehyde is toxic and a suspected carcinogen; handle in a fume hood with gloves.
  • PMA is a tumor promoter and brefeldin A and ionomycin are toxic; wear gloves and dispose of waste per local rules.
  • Human and mouse cells may carry pathogens; use biosafety cabinets and follow your institution's biosafety rules.
  • Sodium azide is toxic and can form explosive compounds with metals; do not pour it down drains.
