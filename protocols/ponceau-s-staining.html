Ponceau S Staining

Estimated time: 15–30 min | Protocol World

To visualize total protein on a nitrocellulose or PVDF membrane after electrotransfer, so that transfer quality can be checked and lanes can be compared or used for total-protein normalization before immunodetection.

Ponceau S is an anionic red azo dye that binds reversibly to positively charged amino groups and to non-polar regions of proteins in acidic solution. Because the interaction is non-covalent, the pink-red bands wash away with water or buffer and do not interfere with later antibody detection [1][2][3]. The stain is less sensitive than Coomassie or amido black (roughly 200 ng per band, versus about 50 ng), so it is a quick check of transfer and loading, not a way to find faint proteins [1][4].

ItemDetails
Ponceau S staining solution0.1% (w/v) Ponceau S in 5% (v/v) acetic acid: dissolve 100 mg Ponceau S in 95 mL distilled water, then add 5 mL glacial acetic acid; commercial ready-to-use solution has the same composition [1][3][4]
Alternative stain0.2% (w/v) Ponceau S in 3% (v/v) acetic acid; 0.01% in 1% acetic acid is also reported to work [1][4]
Membrane after transferNitrocellulose or PVDF carrying transferred proteins; handle with clean forceps [1][2][3]
Distilled or deionized waterFor rinsing and destaining
TBSTTris-buffered saline with 0.1% Tween-20, for the final washes before blocking [1]
0.1% NaOH (optional)For rapid, complete removal of the stain, in less than 5 min [3]
Soft pencilTo mark the marker bands and lanes on the membrane [2]
  • Orbital shaker or rocker
  • Clean staining tray
  • Flatbed scanner or camera
  • Forceps
  • Gel-doc or imager (optional)
  • After transfer, lift the membrane with forceps and rinse it briefly in distilled water. Keep it wet at all times; a dry membrane stains unevenly [1]. (2 min)
  • Place the membrane, protein side up, in a tray and add enough Ponceau S solution to cover it by about 1 cm [1][4]. (1 min)
  • Incubate with gentle shaking at room temperature for 5 min. Staining for up to 1 h is also acceptable [1][4]. (5 min)
  • Pour the stain back into its bottle; it can be reused several times and stored at 4 °C [4]. (1 min)
  • Rinse the membrane in distilled water for 1–2 min, changing the water until the bands are clear against a pale pink or white background [1][4]. (2–5 min)
  • Image at once on a scanner or camera while the membrane is wet; the stain fades within about 10 min [1][3]. (3 min)
  • Mark the molecular-weight marker bands and lane edges with a soft pencil, and cut the membrane if you plan to probe strips separately [2]. (3 min)
  • Destain with 3 washes of 10 min each in TBST or deionized water on a shaker. Repeat if any pink color remains [1]. (30 min)
  • For rapid removal, rinse for under 5 min in 0.1% NaOH, then wash with water [3]. Continue with blocking and immunodetection as usual. (5 min)
  • After imaging, the membrane can be dried between filter paper and stored at room temperature or 4 °C, then rewetted (PVDF in methanol first) and destained before blocking.
  • Pink-red bands of all transferred proteins appear within minutes, with sharp lanes and an even background.
  • The stain reports transfer efficiency and loading: even lane intensity means an even load, while blank patches mean air bubbles or poor contact [1].
  • After destaining, no color remains and antibody detection proceeds normally [2][3].
  • Stain the post-transfer gel with Coomassie to see what protein was left behind
  • Include a molecular-weight marker lane and a known lysate on each blot
  • Keep an unstained membrane for background comparison

Weak or no bandsToo little protein, over-transfer of small proteins, old stain, or wrong transfer directionLoad more protein, shorten transfer, use fresh stain, check the sandwich orientation [1]
Blank patchesAir bubbles, insufficient pre-wetting, or particles under the membraneRoll out bubbles, pre-wet the membrane about 5 min, filter transfer buffer [1]
High backgroundToo little washing or a dried membraneWash longer with water, keep the membrane wet [1]
Stain faded before imagingDelay, or membrane left to dryImage immediately; re-stain for 1 min if needed [1]
Red stain persists on PVDFDye is retained strongly by PVDFWash again in TBST or use brief 0.1% NaOH [1][3]
  • Acetic acid is corrosive and has strong fumes: wear gloves and eye protection, and work in a ventilated area.
  • Ponceau S powder is an irritant; avoid inhaling dust.
  • Collect used stain and NaOH rinses as chemical waste according to local rules; do not pour concentrated acid down the drain.
