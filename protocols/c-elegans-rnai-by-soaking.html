C. elegans RNAi by Soaking

Estimated time: 3–4 days (about 4 h hands-on) | Protocol World

To knock down a target gene in Caenorhabditis elegans by soaking L4 or L1 worms in a small volume of double-stranded RNA (dsRNA) and scoring the phenotype in the treated animals or their progeny.

Injected or ingested dsRNA triggers sequence-specific gene silencing in C. elegans [2][4]. In the soaking method, worms take up in vitro-synthesized dsRNA from a few microliters of buffer containing spermidine and gelatin over about 24 h, then recover on food, and the knockdown phenotype is scored in the soaked animals or their F1 progeny [1][2][3]. Soaking needs only a PCR product as template and small volumes, which makes it practical for screening many genes [1][3], but it requires several micrograms of dsRNA per sample.

ItemDetails
cDNA clone or genomic DNA templateInsert of about 0.5–1.5 kb in a vector flanked by T7 and T3 promoters, or PCR primers carrying T7 promoter sequences [1][2]
T3 and T7 primers, Taq polymerase, 10× PCR buffer, dNTPsPCR mix (50 µL): primers 0.8 µM each; 94 °C 5 min; 35 cycles of 94 °C 1 min, 64 °C 30 s, 72 °C 2.5 min; final 72 °C 10 min [1]. Use the T7 and T3 primers matching your vector
In vitro transcription reagents5× transcription buffer, 1 M DTT, ribonucleotides (2.5 mM each), T7 and T3 RNA polymerases (40 U) [1]. A commercial T7 high-yield kit also works
Phenol:chloroform, chloroform, 3 M sodium acetate (pH 5.2), isopropanol, 70% ethanolFor dsRNA cleanup and precipitation [1]
Soaking buffer (1×)0.25× Mg-free M9 with 3 mM spermidine and 0.05% gelatin. Autoclave the gelatin and filter the buffer; gelatin can be omitted when siliconized tubes are used [1]
5× Mg-free M9 salts (per 100 mL)3.4 g Na₂HPO₄, 1.5 g KH₂PO₄, 0.25 g NaCl, 0.5 g NH₄Cl, as listed in [1]
NGM plates with and without OP50Standard 60 mm plates; a bacteria-free plate is used for worm preparation [1]
WormsWild-type N2, or a sensitized strain such as rrf-3 or eri-1 for neuronal targets [5][6]. Healthy L4 hermaphrodites or synchronized L1 larvae
Other0.2 mL PCR tubes, M9 buffer, platinum worm pick, dissecting microscope

  • 37 °C heat block or incubator
  • Microcentrifuge
  • Agarose gel system or Bioanalyzer, and NanoDrop

  • Amplify the template by PCR with T7 and T3 primers (35 cycles; 72 °C extension for 2.5 min for a 1–2 kb insert). Check 2 µL on an agarose gel [1]. (3 h)
  • Use the PCR product without purification for transcription. For each strand, set up 100 µL: 20 µL 5× buffer, 3 µL 1 M DTT, 20 µL NTPs (2.5 mM each), 30 µL PCR product, 40 U T7 or T3 polymerase [1]. (15 min)
  • Incubate sense (T7) and antisense (T3) reactions at 37 °C for 2–3 h, then combine them into one tube. DNase treatment is not required [1]. (2–3 h)
  • Extract once with phenol:chloroform and once with chloroform. Precipitate with 0.1 volume 3 M sodium acetate (pH 5.2) and 0.6 volume isopropanol [1]. (45 min)
  • Pellet, wash with 70% ethanol, air-dry briefly and dissolve the RNA in 16 µL of 1× soaking buffer. Run 0.5 µL on a denaturing gel to confirm a single band [1]. (30 min)
  • Anneal if desired: 68 °C for 10 min, then 37 °C for 30 min [1]. Adjust to 1–5 mg/mL; soaking typically uses 0.5–5 mg/mL [1][2]. Store at −80 °C. (45 min)
  • For L4 soaking: wash young L4 hermaphrodites in M9 and transfer them to a bacteria-free NGM plate for a few minutes to remove bacteria [1]. (10 min)
  • Pipet 4 µL of dsRNA in soaking buffer into a 0.2 mL PCR tube. Add 4–8 L4 worms (or 15–20 L1 larvae for postembryonic phenotypes) [1][2]. (15 min)
  • For L1 soaking: cut open 10–20 gravid adults in M9 with 0.05% gelatin, let the embryos hatch overnight in M9, and use the hatched L1s [1]. (overnight)
  • Soak at 20 °C for 24 h with the cap closed and no food [1][2]. Worms get no food during soaking, so do not extend it beyond about 24 h. (24 h)
  • Recover the worms onto seeded NGM plates with OP50. Move each P0 worm to a fresh plate every 24 h [1]. (15 min)
  • Score phenotypes in P0 and F1 progeny under a dissecting microscope, for example embryonic lethality, sterility or morphology. The first F1 progeny laid after recovery are often fully affected [1]. (2–4 days)
  • Repeat in triplicate with controls on the same day. Rear worms at 25 °C if you need to reveal temperature-sensitive phenotypes [1]. (3–4 days)
  • After step 6: store dsRNA at −80 °C; avoid repeated freeze–thaw cycles [1].
  • After step 11: worms can be scored the next days.
  • Target-specific phenotype (for example embryonic lethality or paralysis) in a fraction of soaked P0 animals and in the F1 progeny, as defined by the gene [1][3][4].
  • Silencing is often incomplete: many genes show lower penetrance than injection, and neurons, pharynx, vulva and sperm can be resistant [2][4][6].
  • No phenotype in the buffer-only and unrelated dsRNA controls.
  • Buffer-only soak (no dsRNA) in the same tubes
  • Unrelated dsRNA, for example GFP dsRNA in a non-GFP strain
  • A positive control gene with a visible phenotype, such as unc-22 (twitching) or lin-26, as recommended for RNAi experiments [2]
  • Wild-type versus sensitized strain (rrf-3 or eri-1) for neuronal targets [5][6]
ProblemLikely causeSolution
No phenotypeLow dsRNA concentration or poor quality, or a refractory tissue such as neuronsRe-check dsRNA on a denaturing gel, raise concentration within 1–5 mg/mL and try rrf-3 or eri-1 strains [1][5][6]
Worms die during soakingOver-long soak, contaminated tubes or too many worms per volumeKeep soaking to about 24 h, use fewer worms per tube and clean buffer [1][2]
Worms stick to the plasticWorms and RNA adhere to the tube wallInclude 0.05% gelatin in the soaking buffer or use siliconized tubes [1]
Weak penetrance in F1Progeny laid long after soakingCollect the first F1 progeny after recovery and score early [1]
Variable results between repeatsDifferent dsRNA batches or worm stagesStandardize dsRNA concentration, worm stage and temperature, and repeat in triplicate [2][5]
  • Phenol and chloroform are toxic and volatile: use a fume hood, gloves and eye protection, and dispose of organic waste as hazardous waste [1].
  • Isopropanol and ethanol are flammable; keep away from heat.
  • C. elegans and OP50 are low-risk, but wash hands and bleach or autoclave used plates. Use RNase-free technique and wear gloves.
