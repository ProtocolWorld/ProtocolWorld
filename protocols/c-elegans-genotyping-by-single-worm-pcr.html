C. elegans Genotyping by Single-Worm PCR

About 3 h · 2026-11-20

Lyse a single C. elegans in proteinase K buffer and genotype it by direct PCR, with full recipes, cycling conditions, allele-specific SNP primer design, controls and troubleshooting.

⬇ Word (.docx)⬇ PowerPoint (.pptx)

Purpose

To determine the genotype of one individual Caenorhabditis elegans (deletion, insertion or point mutation) by lysing the animal in a PCR tube and amplifying the target locus directly from the crude lysate, without DNA purification.

Principle

A single worm placed in a small volume of buffer containing proteinase K is digested at 60–65 °C, and the protease is then destroyed at 95 °C so that it cannot degrade the DNA polymerase. The crude lysate holds enough genomic DNA (a worm has about 100 Mb of genome per haploid set and hundreds to thousands of cells) to template a PCR directly [1][2][3]. Genotype is read by amplicon size (deletions and insertions) or, for single-nucleotide changes, by allele-specific primers whose 3′ end sits on the polymorphism and carries an extra deliberate mismatch at the −2 position [4].

Materials and reagents

ItemDetails
Worm lysis buffer, 0.5× (Chen and Schedl)100 µL of 5× colorless GoTaq reaction buffer plus 900 µL Milli-Q water. Make fresh in small batches [1].
Proteinase K, 20 mg/mL stockAdd 5 µL to every 100 µL of diluted buffer (about 0.95 mg/mL final) immediately before use; keep on ice [1].
Alternative detergent lysis buffer (WLB)50 mM KCl, 10 mM Tris-HCl pH 8.3, 2.5 mM MgCl₂, 0.45% NP-40 (IGEPAL), 0.45% Tween-20, 0.01% gelatin. Add proteinase K to 0.1 mg/mL (Schedl lab) up to about 0.6 mg/mL (Caltech) [2][3].
Taq-type DNA polymerase with 5× bufferFor example GoTaq (Promega M3008) or an equivalent; 2.5 mM each dNTP [1][4].
Primers, 10 µM each20–27 nt, melting temperature about 58–61 °C, designed for a 200–600 bp product (wild-type and mutant products should differ clearly in size) [1][4].
Milli-Q or nuclease-free waterFor dilution of the lysate and for the master mix.
Agarose, 1× TBE, DNA stain, loading dye, DNA ladder1% agarose in 1× TBE with SYBR Safe (5 µL per 100 mL) [1]; use 2% agarose for products smaller than 300 bp.
Worm strain platesNGM plates seeded with E. coli OP50 carrying L4 or young adult animals; wild-type N2 and a known mutant as controls.
0.2 mL PCR tubes or strips, filtered pipette tipsFiltered tips are advised at every step to prevent amplicon carry-over [1].

Equipment

  • Dissecting stereomicroscope
  • Worm pick (flattened platinum wire in a glass pipette or a pipette tip)
  • Thermal cycler with heated lid
  • Microcentrifuge or plate spinner
  • Gel electrophoresis system and imager
  • Multichannel pipette (for large screens)
  • Micropipettes with filtered tips

Procedure

  1. Label PCR tubes, one per worm. Prepare lysis mix by adding 5 µL of 20 mg/mL proteinase K to 100 µL of 0.5× GoTaq buffer; keep on ice [1]. (10 min)
  2. Pipette 5 µL of lysis mix into the bottom of each PCR tube. Spin briefly so the drop sits at the bottom [1]. (5 min)
  3. Under the stereomicroscope, pick one worm (an L4 or young adult is ideal) with a clean pick and transfer it into the drop. Avoid carrying bacteria or agar [1][3]. (1 min per tube)
  4. Confirm under the microscope that the animal is submerged in the drop. Include one tube with a pick touched to the plate but no worm (no-template control) [1]. (2 min)
  5. Optional: freeze the tubes at −80 °C. Freeze–thaw can aid lysis and tubes keep indefinitely in the freezer, but this is not required [3]. (15 min or longer)
  6. Run in the cycler: 65 °C for 45 min (lysis), 95 °C for 15 min (proteinase K inactivation), hold at 4 °C [1]. With detergent WLB buffer use 60 °C for 60 min instead [2][3]. (about 1 h)
  7. Spin the tubes briefly, add 15 µL of Milli-Q water to each (20 µL total) and mix well. This template can be stored at −20 °C and serves about 20 reactions [1]. (5 min)
  8. Make a PCR master mix for 50 µL: 10 µL 5× buffer, 2 µL 2.5 mM dNTPs, 2 µL forward primer, 2 µL reverse primer, 0.25 µL polymerase, 34 µL water. Scale to the number of reactions [1]. (10 min)
  9. Dispense 24 µL of master mix per tube and add 1 µL of diluted lysate (25 µL reaction). Include wild-type, mutant, no-template and water controls [1]. (10 min)
  10. Cycle: 95 °C 2 min; 35 cycles of 95 °C 20 s, 55 °C 20 s, 72 °C 30 s; final 72 °C 2 min; hold at 4 °C. Lengthen extension about 1 min per kb for products over 600 bp [1][4]. (about 50 min)
  11. Load 15 µL on a 1% agarose, 1× TBE gel with SYBR Safe. Run at 100 V for about 20 min with a ladder and image [1]. (40 min)
  12. Score each worm: one wild-type band = wild type, one mutant band = homozygous mutant, both = heterozygote. For point mutations see the allele-specific scheme below [1][4]. (10 min)

Pause points

  • After step 5 the tubes can sit at −80 °C indefinitely.
  • After step 7 the diluted lysate can be stored at −20 °C and reused for about 20 reactions.
  • PCR products can wait at 4 °C overnight before gel loading.

Expected results

  • A single clean band of the expected size in each lane for homozygotes, two bands for heterozygotes, and no band in the no-template control.
  • Allele-specific SNP genotyping: split each lysate into two reactions, one with the mutant-specific forward primer and one with the wild-type-specific forward primer. Homozygotes amplify in only one reaction and heterozygotes in both [4].
  • For allele-specific primers, put the SNP base at the 3′ end and add a second deliberate mismatch two bases upstream (position −2, usually changed to A or T); the matched allele then has one mismatch and the other allele has two, which suppresses mispriming [4].

Controls

  • Wild-type (N2) worm lysate as a size reference
  • Known homozygous mutant lysate, and a known heterozygote if available
  • No-template control (pick without worm) to detect contamination
  • Water control in the master mix
  • Both allele-specific reactions on the same lysate for SNP assays [1][4]

Troubleshooting

ProblemLikely causeSolution
No product in any laneWorm missed the buffer, inactive proteinase K, or old dNTPsCheck under the scope that the worm is in the drop, use fresh enzyme and thaw a new dNTP aliquot for each experiment [1]
Faint band or smearToo much lysate or too many bacteria carried overUse 1 µL of the diluted lysate, pick cleanly and optionally raise cycles or use nested primers [1][3]
Band in the no-template controlAmplicon carry-overUse filtered tips, separate pre- and post-PCR areas and fresh aliquots [1]
Heterozygote shows only one bandPreferential amplification of the shorter productRedesign primers so the two products are close in size; shorten extension; use allele-specific primers
Weak product from the detergent bufferResidual NP-40 or Tween interfering, or proteinase K left activeConfirm the 95 °C step, dilute the lysate in water or use nested primers, which gave the most reproducible results in the Schedl lab protocol [3]

Safety and waste

  • Wear gloves and a lab coat; C. elegans and E. coli OP50 are Biosafety Level 1 organisms.
  • SYBR Safe and similar stains are less hazardous than ethidium bromide, but still follow your institution's rules for gel waste.
  • Treat used plates, worm picks and tubes as biological waste: autoclave plates; dispose of tips and tubes in the biohazard bin.
  • Handle thermal cycler blocks and heated lids carefully; they reach 95–105 °C.
