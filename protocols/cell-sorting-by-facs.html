Cell Sorting by FACS

5–8 hours · 2026-11-24

Sort a defined live mammalian cell population by FACS: prepare a filtered single-cell suspension in sort buffer with viability dye, set nozzle and drop delay, gate out doublets, sort into serum-coated tubes and check purity.

⬇ Word (.docx)⬇ PowerPoint (.pptx)

Purpose

To isolate a defined, viable population of mammalian cells by fluorescence-activated cell sorting (FACS) at high purity, and to check purity and recover the cells for culture or RNA extraction.

Principle

A sorter forms droplets from a vibrating nozzle, measures fluorescence and scatter on each cell, and charges the droplet that holds a target cell so electrostatic plates deflect it into a collection tube [3]. Accurate drop delay, a nozzle at least 5× the cell diameter, and a single-cell, dead-cell-free sample determine purity and yield [1][3]. Wider sort masks raise purity but lower recovery [3]. Aerosol containment and decontamination are required for human-sourced cells [4].

Equipment

  • Cell sorter with aerosol management system (e.g., BD FACSAria, Sony, Beckman or similar)
  • Biological safety cabinet
  • Refrigerated centrifuge
  • 35–40 µm strainer-cap FACS tubes
  • 5 mL round-bottom polypropylene and polystyrene tubes
  • Hemocytometer or automated cell counter
  • Microscope
  • Ice or cold block (4 °C)

Procedure

  1. Pre-coat collection tubes: add 1 mL of FBS or PBS + 20% FBS (or 2% BSA) to each 5 mL polystyrene tube, coat 2 h at room temperature or overnight at 4 °C, then pour off [2][5]. (2 h)
  2. Prepare sort buffer: Ca²⁺/Mg²⁺-free PBS or HBSS (no phenol red) with 1–2% BSA or FBS, 1–2 mM EDTA and 25 mM HEPES; filter-sterilize and chill to 4 °C [1][2][3].
  3. Prepare collection medium: complete culture medium with 20–50% FBS, 25 mM HEPES and antibiotics (e.g., penicillin–streptomycin). Add 0.5–1 mL to each coated tube and keep at 4 °C [1][2][5].
  4. Make a single-cell suspension (tissue digestion, EDTA or enzyme detachment). Check under the microscope; do not stop dissociation while clumps remain [2][3]. (30–60 min)
  5. Pellet cells (300–400 × g, 5 min, 4 °C) in round-bottom tubes, discard supernatant and flick gently to loosen the pellet [3]. (10 min)
  6. Stain as in the companion surface-marker staining protocol (titrated antibodies, Fc block, 30 min at 4 °C, dark); wash twice in cold sort buffer. Do not stain in culture medium [1][2]. (45 min)
  7. If many dead cells are present, add DNase I at 25–50 µg/mL (omit EDTA, which inhibits DNase I). Add 10% Pluronic F-68 to 1% only if cells clump persistently [2][5].
  8. Count cells and resuspend at the concentration for your nozzle: about 20–50 × 10⁶/mL (70 µm), 15–30 × 10⁶/mL (85 µm) or 5–10 × 10⁶/mL (100 µm); use 0.5–3 mL per tube [1][2][5].
  9. Filter through a 35–40 µm strainer-cap tube within 5 min of loading, then add a viability dye (e.g., DAPI 0.1 µg/mL or PI 1 µg/mL) just before sorting [1][3][5]. (5 min)
  10. Prepare controls in the same buffer: unstained, viability dye alone, one single-stain per fluorochrome for compensation, FMO controls and an isotype or biological negative [2][5].
  11. Keep samples at 4 °C and protected from light; transport in a leak-proof container with a solid cap to the sorter, with extra buffer and collection tubes [1][2][3].
  12. Sorter setup (by operator): choose a nozzle at least 5× the cell diameter [3]: 70 µm (cells <14 µm, about 70 psi), 85 µm (<17 µm, 40–45 psi) or 100 µm (<20 µm, 20 psi) [1][2]. (20–30 min)
  13. Confirm the AMS (aerosol evacuation) is on, then verify a stable stream and satellite drop fusion. Set the sample and collection chamber temperature to 4 °C if available [3][4].
  14. Run alignment/fluorescent beads to set the drop delay; confirm the sort is accurate (≥95% of beads in a single drop) before loading cells [3].
  15. Run controls first: set voltages with the unstained sample, record single stains for compensation and set gates using FMO and viability controls [2][5]. (20 min)
  16. Gate sequentially: FSC-A/SSC-A for cells, FSC-H/FSC-A and SSC-H/SSC-A to exclude doublets, viability dye-negative live cells, then the marker-defined population [2][5].
  17. Pick the sort mode: purity for clean sorts, yield for rare cells, single-cell only for cloning. Keep events at about 18,000/s (70 µm) or 6,000/s (100 µm) [2][3].
  18. Test-sort about 100–500 cells. If abort rate is high, dilute the sample or lower the flow rate; check the stream regularly [2][3].
  19. Run the sort with the sample kept at 4 °C. Mix collection tubes periodically, since sheath fluid layers on top, and swap tubes as they fill [3][5]. (1–4 h)
  20. Re-analyze 5–10% of the sorted cells on the same sorter (or an analyzer) with the same gates; purity is the percent in the target gate [3]. (10 min)
  21. Pellet the sorted cells (300–400 × g, 5–10 min, 4 °C) and discard the supernatant, leaving about 100 µL; resuspend in fresh medium [2]. (10 min)
  22. For culture, plate in warm complete medium (add 50 µg/mL gentamicin for the first week) and rest cells before assays; for RNA, pellet and lyse in lysis buffer [1][3].
  23. Decontaminate: flush sample lines with fresh 10% bleach, rinse with sterile water, wipe collection holders and surfaces, and dispose of waste as biohazard [4]. (20 min)

Pause points

  • After staining and washing, keep cells at 4 °C in the dark for up to a few hours.
  • Pre-coated collection tubes can be coated overnight at 4 °C.
  • After sorting, sorted pellets can be lysed and frozen at −80 °C (RNA workflows) or cultured.

Expected results

  • Sorted populations at ≥90–95% purity on re-analysis with the original gates [3].
  • Post-sort viability high enough for culture; yields depend on the starting frequency and sort mask [3].
  • Single-stain, FMO and viability controls give clear, reproducible gates.

Controls

  • Unstained cells to set autofluorescence and voltages
  • Viability dye alone and a dead-cell (heat-killed) control for gating
  • Single-stain compensation controls with the same fluorochromes as the sample
  • FMO controls for dim or spread-prone markers
  • Pre-sort sample re-analyzed to compare with the sorted fraction

Troubleshooting

ProblemLikely causeSolution
Nozzle clogs or frequent abortsClumps, debris or too high a concentrationRe-filter at 35–40 µm, add DNase I or EDTA, dilute the sample [1][2][5]
Low purity on re-analysisWrong drop delay, wide gates or high event rateRe-check drop delay with beads, use a purity mask, lower the rate [3]
Low post-sort viabilityHigh pressure, small nozzle or dry collection tubesUse a larger nozzle at lower pressure and serum-coated tubes with rich medium [1][2][3]
Low recoveryCell loss on tube walls or tight sort maskPre-coat tubes, use yield mask, mix tubes during the sort [2][3]
Stream unstablePartial clog or bubbleStop the sort, wait for the aerosol system to clear, then clean or replace the nozzle [4]
Bacterial contamination of cultureSorting is aseptic, not sterileAdd antibiotics to the collection medium and culture medium [2][3][5]

Safety and waste

  • Treat human and primate cells as potentially infectious; sort at BSL-2 with enhanced precautions: solid-front gown, gloves, eye protection and an N95 or better respirator [4].
  • Run the aerosol management system during every sort; do not open the sort chamber until it has run for at least 2 minutes after a stream failure [4].
  • Do not sort radioactive samples or known pathogens without prior facility approval [1][5].
  • Decontaminate lines, chambers and surfaces after sorting with freshly made 10% bleach, then rinse with water [4].
  • Disclose the origin and any viral vector of samples to the core before scheduling [1].
