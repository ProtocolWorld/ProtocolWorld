Preparation of NGM Plates and OP50 Lawns

2.5 h bench work + 2–3 days drying + overnight lawn · 2026-11-17

Pour NGM agar, dry it, and seed a thin OP50 lawn for maintaining C. elegans, with recipes, volumes and troubleshooting.

⬇ Word (.docx)⬇ PowerPoint (.pptx)

Purpose

To pour nematode growth medium (NGM) agar plates and seed them with a thin lawn of E. coli OP50, the standard food source for maintaining Caenorhabditis elegans.

Principle

NGM is a low-peptone agar that lets the uracil-requiring OP50 strain grow only a thin lawn, so worms stay visible and easy to pick and mate [1]. Heat-labile cholesterol and the salts are added after autoclaving, once the agar has cooled to about 55 °C [1][3]. Plates are dried for a few days, which exposes contaminants and removes excess moisture, then seeded from a fresh OP50 liquid culture [1][2].

LB broth10 g tryptone, 5 g yeast extract, 5 g NaCl per liter, pH 7.0; 100 mL per 250 mL bottle, autoclaved
LB agar streak plate with OP50LB plus 15 g/L agar, pH 7.5
60 mm Petri dishesSterile, about 10 mL NGM each

Equipment

  • Autoclave
  • 2 L Erlenmeyer flask with foil cap
  • 55 °C water bath
  • Peristaltic pump or sterile 25 mL pipette
  • Laminar-flow hood or clean bench
  • 37 °C shaking incubator
  • Airtight plate container
  • Parafilm

Procedure

  1. Weigh 3 g NaCl, 17 g agar and 2.5 g peptone into a 2 L flask, add 975 mL water, cover the mouth with foil, and autoclave for 50 min [1]. (1 h 15 min)
  2. Move the flask to a 55 °C water bath for 15 min so the agar cools without setting [1][3]. (15 min)
  3. Add 1 mL of 1 M CaCl₂, 1 mL of 5 mg/mL cholesterol in ethanol, 1 mL of 1 M MgSO₄ and 25 mL of 1 M potassium phosphate buffer (pH 6.0). Swirl to mix without making bubbles [1][3].
  4. Dispense about 10 mL per 60 mm dish (roughly two-thirds full) using a pump or sterile pipette [1][2]. Do not move the dishes until the agar has gelled. (20 min)
  5. Leave the plates at room temperature for 2–3 days with lids on. Discard any plate showing growth. This also lets excess moisture evaporate [1]. (2–3 days)
  6. Streak OP50 on an LB agar plate, pick one colony into 100 mL of LB broth, and shake at 37 °C overnight [1][2]. (overnight)
  7. Using sterile technique, pipette 50–120 µL of OP50 culture onto the center of each 60 mm plate (50 µL in [1], 100–120 µL in [2]). Keep the lawn off the edge [1][2].
  8. Incubate seeded plates overnight at room temperature, or about 8 h at 37 °C. Cool to room temperature before adding worms [1][2]. (8–16 h)
  9. Store seeded plates in an airtight container; they stay usable for about 2–3 weeks [1][2]. Unseeded plates keep for several weeks in a sealed container [1].

Pause points

  • After pouring: dry plates for 2–3 days, or seal them and keep them for several weeks before seeding [1][2].
  • After the OP50 overnight culture: store at 4 °C for several months if kept sterile [1][2].
  • After seeding: store in an airtight container for 2–3 weeks [1].

Expected results

  • Clear, level agar with no cracks, bubbles or visible contamination after drying.
  • A thin, even lawn that does not reach the plate edge; OP50 stays thin because it needs uracil that NGM barely supplies [1].
  • Worms placed on the lawn move normally and lay eggs.

Controls

  • Keep 1–2 unseeded plates from each batch to check agar sterility.
  • Streak OP50 from the culture onto LB agar to confirm a single colony type.
  • Record batch date on each sleeve of plates.

Troubleshooting

ProblemLikely causeSolution
Mold or bacterial colonies on unseeded platesNon-sterile additions or open lidsDiscard the batch; sterilize stocks and work in a clean hood
Lawn reaches the plate edgeCulture spread too farSpot in the center and do not spread; worms that climb the wall dry out [1]
Cracked or very dry agarOver-drying or storage without a containerSeal plates in an airtight box and use within the stated time
Precipitate or cloudy agarPhosphate or salts added while too hotCool to 55 °C before additions and swirl well
Worms crawl between platesCondensation under lidsDry plates and wrap stocks with Parafilm [1]

Safety and waste

  • Hot agar and autoclaved flasks cause burns: use heat-resistant gloves.
  • Cholesterol stock in ethanol is flammable; keep away from flames.
  • OP50 is a standard laboratory E. coli (biosafety level 1); autoclave used plates and cultures before disposal.
