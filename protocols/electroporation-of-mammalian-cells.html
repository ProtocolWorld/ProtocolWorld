Electroporation of Mammalian Cells

2 h + 24–60 h recovery · 2026-11-27

Electroporate mammalian cells in 0.4 cm cuvettes: log-phase cells, ice-cold buffer, 10–40 µg DNA, cell-type starting pulse settings, recovery, and viability and efficiency checks.

⬇ Word (.docx)⬇ PowerPoint (.pptx)

Purpose

To introduce plasmid DNA (or siRNA) into cultured mammalian cells, including suspension and hard-to-transfect lines, with brief electrical pulses.

Principle

A short high-voltage pulse transiently permeabilizes the cell membrane so nucleic acid in the surrounding buffer can enter. Success depends on matching waveform, field strength, buffer, and temperature to the cell type: too little energy gives low uptake, too much kills the cells [1][2]. Square-wave pulses deliver a set voltage for a set duration, while exponential-decay pulses are set by voltage and capacitance [2]. Healthy, log-phase cells and clean, endotoxin-free DNA are as important as the pulse settings [3].

Materials and reagents

ItemDetails
CellsLog-phase, healthy, mycoplasma-free, split or fed the day before; about 5 × 10⁶ cells per stable transfection or 1–4 × 10⁷ per transient transfection in a 0.4 cm cuvette [1][3]
Plasmid DNAEndotoxin-free; 10–40 µg for transient expression, 1–10 µg for stable lines (linearized); 1 µg selectable marker plus 10 µg gene of interest for co-transfection [1][3]
Electroporation bufferIce-cold PBS without Ca²⁺/Mg²⁺, HeBS, serum-free medium, or a commercial low-conductivity buffer; calcium and magnesium lower efficiency [1][2]
Complete growth mediumPre-warmed to 37 °C, without antibiotics until after recovery [3]
Electroporation cuvettes0.4 cm gap (sterile); keep on ice before use [1]
Trypan blue or viability stainFor viability counts
Culture plates6-well or 10 cm dishes, pre-filled with medium

Equipment

  • Electroporator
  • Biosafety cabinet
  • Refrigerated centrifuge
  • Ice bucket
  • CO₂ incubator
  • Cell counter
  • Flow cytometer or microscope

Procedure

  1. Passage cells the day before so they are in log-phase growth; check morphology and viability, and avoid trypsinizing for longer than needed [3]. (Day before)
  2. Harvest cells, count, and pellet at about 640 × g for 5 min at 4 °C [1]. (15 min)
  3. Wash once in half the original volume of ice-cold electroporation buffer and pellet again [1]. (10 min)
  4. Resuspend cells in ice-cold buffer at 1 × 10⁷ cells/mL for stable work, or up to 8 × 10⁷/mL for transient work [1]. (5 min)
  5. Mix DNA with 0.5 mL of cell suspension in a 0.4 cm cuvette (use less if your device requires it). Flick to mix and hold on ice for 5 min [1]. (5 min)
  6. Wipe the cuvette dry and pulse at room temperature. Starting points: exponential 250 V/200–350 µF (HeLa), 300 V/250 µF (K562), or square wave 250 V, 20 ms (CHO) [1][2]. (1 min)
  7. For PBS or other low-resistance buffers in a 0.4 cm cuvette, try 1200 V/25 µF first, then tune voltage until viability is about 40–70% [1].
  8. Return the cuvette to ice for 10 min [1]. (10 min)
  9. Gently add pre-warmed complete medium (for example, dilute 20-fold), rinse the cuvette, and plate immediately [1]. (5 min)
  10. Incubate at 37 °C, 5% CO₂. Check viability and attachment after a few hours, and replace medium if debris is heavy [3].
  11. For transient assays, analyze at 24–60 h (for example, GFP by microscopy or flow cytometry); for stable lines, grow 48 h before adding selection [1][2]. (24–60 h)

Pause points

  • After resuspending cells in buffer: keep on ice while preparing cuvettes.
  • After pulsing: the 10 min recovery on ice is part of the protocol, not a pause.
  • After plating: incubate for 24–60 h before analysis.

Expected results

  • Viability of about 40–70% after the pulse for optimized conditions, with efficient expression of a reporter at 24–48 h [1][2].
  • Stable lines: colonies appear after selection (about 1 in 10³–10⁴ viable fibroblasts) [1].

Controls

  • No-pulse control with DNA to separate DNA toxicity from pulse toxicity
  • Pulse without DNA to measure pulse-induced death
  • Reporter plasmid (for example, GFP or luciferase) to measure efficiency
  • Vendor control plasmid if efficiency is low [3]
  • Negative control siRNA when delivering siRNA [2]

Troubleshooting

ProblemLikely causeSolution
Low viabilityVoltage or capacitance too high, unhealthy cells, endotoxinLower voltage or capacitance, use log-phase cells, use endotoxin-free DNA [1][3]
Low expression with good viabilityPulse too mild, too little DNA, poor DNARaise voltage in steps, increase DNA within limits, check DNA quality and promoter [1][3]
Arcing or a popping soundSalts, bubbles, wet cuvette exterior, too much DNAUse low-salt buffer, avoid bubbles, dry cuvette, purify DNA and reduce volume or salt
Large changes in pH or heatElectrolysis in the bufferKeep cells on ice and consider adding 20 mM HEPES pH 7.5 [1]
Batch variability in primary cellsDonor differences, passage driftUse consistent cell stocks and repeat optimization [3]

Safety and waste

  • High-voltage equipment: use only intact cuvettes and follow the manufacturer's safety guidance.
  • Do not touch the chamber during a pulse.
  • Handle cells under the biosafety level required for the line; dispose of cuvettes as biohazardous waste.
  • Wear gloves and a lab coat; dispose of DNA-containing liquid waste according to institutional rules.
