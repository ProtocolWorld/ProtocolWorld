Stripping and Reprobing Western Blot Membranes

Estimated time: 2–4 h plus re-blocking | Protocol World

To remove bound primary and secondary antibodies and detection substrate from a western blot membrane so the same blot can be probed again for a second protein (for example, a loading control or a phospho-protein and its total form) without running another gel.

Antibodies are released from the membrane-bound antigen either by low pH with SDS (mild stripping, which disrupts the antibody–antigen interaction) or by heat, SDS and a reducing agent (stringent stripping, which denatures the antibodies) [1][2][3]. PVDF binds more protein and tolerates stripping better than nitrocellulose, and chemiluminescent or fluorescent blots can be stripped, whereas chromogenic precipitates cannot [2][3][4]. Each round removes some antigen, so signals should not be compared quantitatively between rounds [1][4].

ItemDetails
Blotted PVDF membraneAlready probed and imaged by ECL or fluorescence; kept moist at all times (nitrocellulose is acceptable for 1–2 mild cycles)
Mild stripping buffer (1 L)15 g glycine, 1 g SDS, 10 mL Tween 20 in 800 mL water; adjust to pH 2.2 with HCl; bring to 1 L [1][3]
Stringent stripping buffer (100 mL)20 mL 10% SDS, 12.5 mL 0.5 M Tris-HCl pH 6.8, 67.5 mL water; add 0.8 mL 2-mercaptoethanol in a fume hood immediately before use [1][3]
PBS and TBST1× PBS; TBS with 0.1% Tween 20 (use phosphate-free TBST for phospho-specific antibodies)
Blocking buffer5% nonfat dry milk or 5% BSA in TBST (BSA for phospho-antibodies)
Detection reagentsECL substrate and HRP-conjugated secondary antibody (or fluorescent secondary)
Optional: Ponceau S0.1% (w/v) in 5% acetic acid, to confirm protein is still on the membrane
Deionized waterFor rinses before and after stripping
  • Chemical fume hood
  • Shaking platform or rocker
  • Heated incubator or water bath at 50 °C
  • Shallow tray or box with a tight-fitting lid
  • Chemiluminescence or fluorescence imager
  • Forceps and clean gloves
  • Image the blot completely, then rinse the membrane twice in deionized water for 1 min each to remove substrate. Never let the membrane dry [2][4]. (5 min)
  • Start with the mild method. Submerge the membrane, protein side up, in mild stripping buffer in a tray, covering it completely, and agitate at room temperature [1][3]. (10 min)
  • Discard the buffer, add fresh mild buffer and agitate again at room temperature. Add 5–10 min at 37 °C only for high-affinity antibodies [1][3]. (10 min)
  • Wash the membrane 2 × 10 min in PBS, then 2 × 5 min in TBST with agitation to remove all acid and SDS [1]. (30 min)
  • Check the strip: add ECL substrate and image with the same exposure as before. Any remaining signal means antibody is still bound [3][4]. (10 min)
  • If signal remains, move to the stringent method. In a fume hood, warm the stringent buffer with fresh 2-mercaptoethanol to 50 °C and cover the membrane [1][3]. (5 min)
  • Incubate at 50 °C in a closed container with gentle agitation for 30 min (up to 45 min for stubborn antibodies) [1][3]. (30–45 min)
  • Rinse under running water for 1–2 min, then wash 6 × 5 min in TBST. Residual mercaptoethanol damages the next antibody, so wash extensively [1][3]. (35 min)
  • Re-block the membrane in blocking buffer for 1 h at room temperature with agitation [3][5]. (1 h)
  • Confirm complete stripping: incubate with secondary antibody alone for 1 h, wash 3 × 5 min in TBST, add ECL and image. No bands should appear [4][5]. (1.5 h)
  • Optional: stain with Ponceau S for 1 min and destain in water to confirm that transferred protein remains before reprobing [5]. (5 min)
  • Probe with the next primary antibody (overnight at 4 °C or 1 h at room temperature), then repeat the usual washes, secondary antibody and detection.
  • After the final wash of step 4 or step 8, the membrane can be stored in PBS or TBST at 4 °C, wrapped to stay moist, for a few days [2].
  • After re-blocking (step 9), the membrane can stay in blocking buffer at 4 °C overnight.
  • Stripping check shows no band at the original molecular weight when only secondary antibody and ECL are applied.
  • The next primary antibody gives its own bands; background should be similar to a fresh blot.
  • Band intensity of the retained antigen is lower than in the first round, usually modestly with the mild method and more with the stringent method.
  • Strip-check lane: ECL on the stripped membrane before reblocking, and secondary-only after reblocking
  • Ponceau S stain after stripping to confirm protein retention
  • Probe a loading control (for example, β-actin) last, or first if its antibody is weak
  • Keep a duplicate membrane unstripped when absolute quantification is needed
ProblemLikely causeSolution
Bands still visible after strippingHigh-affinity antibody or very strong signalExtend mild stripping, add 5–10 min at 37 °C, or switch to stringent buffer at 50 °C for 30–45 min
Weak or absent signal on reprobeAntigen lost during stripping or too many cyclesUse mild conditions first, limit the number of cycles, probe low-abundance targets first, and check Ponceau S
High background after reprobingResidual SDS or mercaptoethanol, or poor re-blockingWash more extensively in TBST and re-block for the full hour
Membrane dried or became brittleDried between rounds or nitrocellulose usedKeep the membrane wet; re-wet dried PVDF in methanol before use; use PVDF for repeated stripping
Phospho-signal lostHarsh stripping or phosphate-based buffersUse mild stripping and phosphate-free TBST; probe the phospho-protein first and the total protein second
  • 2-Mercaptoethanol is toxic, volatile and malodorous: handle only in a fume hood with gloves and eye protection.
  • The mild buffer is acidic (pH 2.2) and contains SDS: avoid skin and eye contact.
  • Collect stringent buffer containing mercaptoethanol as hazardous chemical waste according to institutional rules; do not pour it down the drain.
