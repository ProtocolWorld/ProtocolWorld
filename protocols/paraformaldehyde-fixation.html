Paraformaldehyde Fixation

About 1.5 hours (plus 2 h to prepare fresh fixative) · 2026-11-17

Make 4% paraformaldehyde from powder and use it to fix cultured cells for immunofluorescence, including quenching with NH₄Cl, Triton X-100 permeabilization, suspension-cell and methanol alternatives, and safety notes.

⬇ Word (.docx)⬇ PowerPoint (.pptx)

Purpose

To prepare 4% paraformaldehyde (PFA) fixative and use it to fix cultured adherent cells on coverslips for immunofluorescence, preserving cell structure and antigen location, with notes for suspension cells and for methanol as an alternative.

Principle

Formaldehyde, released when PFA powder is depolymerized by gentle heat in buffer, forms cross-links between proteins that hold cellular structures in place. Fixation that is too short or too long can damage cell structure or mask epitopes, so time and concentration are kept within a narrow range [4]. Leftover aldehyde groups can add background, so they are quenched (here with NH₄Cl), and a mild detergent such as Triton X-100 then opens membranes for antibodies [5][6]. Formaldehyde cross-linking can denature some epitopes, so a few antibodies work only with milder fixation or methanol/acetone instead [4][5].

Equipment

  • Chemical fume hood
  • Magnetic stirrer hot plate and water bath (60 °C)
  • pH meter
  • Filter or 0.22 µm syringe filter
  • Cultured cells on coverslips in a multiwell plate
  • Fine forceps, pipettes and waste containers for aldehyde waste
  • Fluorescence microscope

Procedure

  1. Prepare 4% PFA in a fume hood: weigh 4 g PFA and add 80 mL PBS (about 100 mL final volume) in a flask with a stir bar [1][6]. (10 min)
  2. Heat to about 60 °C while stirring until the powder dissolves; do not exceed 70 °C and do not boil, because formaldehyde vaporizes [1][2][3]. (30–60 min)
  3. If the solution stays cloudy, add 1–2 drops of 5 M NaOH; it should clear. Cool to room temperature [3].
  4. Adjust to pH 7.4 with dilute HCl, then bring to 100 mL with PBS and filter [2][3]. (10 min)
  5. Use fresh (best results) or freeze aliquots at −20 °C; use a thawed aliquot once and discard the rest. Date every aliquot and keep diluted fixative for only about a week [1][2][3].
  6. Wash adherent cells on coverslips once with PBS, gently adding and removing liquid at the wall of the well [6]. (2 min)
  7. Slowly add 4% PFA and fix at room temperature for 15–20 min (10–15 min is also used). Do not leave cells in PFA overnight, which raises autofluorescence [3][4][5][6]. (15–20 min)
  8. Remove the PFA into a waste container for aldehyde waste. Wash 3 times with PBS, 5 min each [4][6]. (15 min)
  9. Quench free aldehydes with 50 mM NH₄Cl for 15 min, then wash 3 times with PBS [6]. (30 min)
  10. Permeabilize with 0.1% Triton X-100 in PBS for 5 min at room temperature, then wash 3 times with PBS [5][6]. (20 min)
  11. Block for 30 min in blocking buffer before adding the primary antibody; proceed with your immunostaining protocol [4]. (30 min)
  12. Alternative one-step fix and permeabilization: 4% PFA with 0.5% Triton X-100 for 20–30 min, then wash and quench as above [6]. (20–30 min)
  13. Suspension cells: dilute stock to 2–4% PFA, add to cells for a final concentration of 0.5–2% PFA and fix on ice for 15–30 min, then wash twice with PBS [3].
  14. Methanol alternative: add ice-cold methanol (−20 °C) slowly for at least 10 min at −20 °C, then rehydrate by replacing half of the liquid with PBS several times [6].
  15. Mount or stain immediately, or store fixed cells in PBS at 4 °C protected from light [4].

Pause points

  • PFA can be prepared ahead and frozen in single-use aliquots at −20 °C [1][2].
  • After the PBS washes following fixation, coverslips can be held in PBS at 4 °C for a short time before staining.
  • Do not pause in PFA itself; overnight storage in PFA increases autofluorescence [3].

Expected results

  • Cells keep their shape and attached morphology, with intact membranes and organelles when viewed by phase contrast.
  • Specific immunofluorescence signal with low background and low autofluorescence in the unstained control [3].
  • Reproducible staining across wells when fixation time and concentration are kept constant.

Controls

  • Unstained, fixed cells to check autofluorescence from the fixative [3]
  • Secondary-antibody-only control to check non-specific binding
  • Compare PFA with methanol fixation if an antibody gives no signal, since cross-linking can mask epitopes [4][5]
  • A non-permeabilized sample for membrane-bound epitopes, which can work better without permeabilization [5]

Troubleshooting

ProblemLikely causeSolution
PFA stays cloudyPowder not fully depolymerizedAdd 1–2 drops of 5 M NaOH, keep at about 60 °C and stir longer [3]
High autofluorescenceFixed too long or PFA too oldShorten fixation, never fix overnight, make fresh PFA or use methanol-free ampoules [3][4]
Weak or no antibody signalEpitope masked by cross-linking or over-fixationShorten fixation, try milder fixation, or use methanol or acetone [4][5]
Cells detach or look damagedRough pipetting or fixative at wrong temperatureAdd and remove liquid slowly at the well wall; keep fixation times within range [4][6]
Membrane protein not detectedPermeabilization damages the epitopeOmit Triton X-100 and stain unpermeabilized cells [5]

Safety and waste

  • PFA and formaldehyde are toxic, irritating to eyes, skin and airways, can cause sensitization, and are classified as carcinogenic to humans. Weigh powder and heat solutions only in a fume hood [1][2][3].
  • Wear nitrile gloves, eye protection and a lab coat. Do not let the water bath exceed 70 °C, because formaldehyde vaporizes [3].
  • Collect all PFA solution and rinse waste in a labeled aldehyde waste container; dispose of it according to local rules and never pour it down the drain [1].
  • Treat fixed human cell lines according to your institution's biosafety rules.
