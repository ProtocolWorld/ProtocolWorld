C. elegans Strain Freezing and Thawing

Estimated time: 2 h + overnight at −80 °C; test thaw next day | Protocol World

To cryopreserve Caenorhabditis elegans strains so they can be stored for many years and revived on demand, without the genetic drift and contamination risk of continuous culture.

Freshly starved L1–L2 larvae tolerate freezing far better than well-fed adults, eggs or dauers [1][3]. The larvae are suspended in a phosphate-buffered solution with glycerol as a cryoprotectant, cooled slowly at about 1 °C per minute in an insulated box at −80 °C, and then kept at −80 °C or in liquid nitrogen [1][2][4]. A test vial is always thawed to prove that the stock recovers before the original plates are discarded [1]. Glycerol suspensions let worms settle, so a vial normally gives one recovery [1]. A 35–45% recovery of frozen animals is typical in liquid nitrogen [1].

ItemDetails
S buffer (0.1 M NaCl, 0.05 M potassium phosphate, about pH 6.0)Mix 129 mL 0.05 M K₂HPO₄ with 871 mL 0.05 M KH₂PO₄, add 5.85 g NaCl, autoclave [1]
2× freezing solutionMix 70 mL S buffer with 30 mL glycerol (30% v/v) and autoclave, so the 1:1 mixture ends at 15% glycerol [1]
Starved worm plates2–3 medium (6 cm) NGM plates just cleared of OP50, rich in L1–L2 larvae, uncontaminated [1][3]
NGM plates seeded with OP50One large (10 cm) plate per vial thawed
Cryovials1.8 mL screw-cap tubes (O-ring caps for liquid nitrogen), labeled with strain, date and initials [1][2]
Sterile 15 mL tubesFor collecting the washed worms
Insulated boxStyrofoam with walls at least about 2 cm (3/4 in) thick, or an isopropanol freezing container [1][2]
  • −80 °C freezer
  • Liquid nitrogen storage dewar (long-term master stock)
  • Dissecting stereomicroscope
  • Pipettes and sterile glass or plastic Pasteur pipettes
  • 20 °C incubator
  • Cryo gloves and face shield
  • Chunk or transfer about 20 young adults onto each of 2–3 plates and grow at 20 °C for 4–5 days until the OP50 is gone and most animals are L1–L2 [2][3]. (4–5 days)
  • Check under the microscope: plenty of L1–L2, correct phenotype, no mold or bacterial contamination. Do not freeze plates with mostly adults or dauers [1][3].
  • Plan 0.6 mL of S buffer per vial (for example 3.6 mL for six vials). Wash worms off each plate with S buffer and pool the suspension in a sterile 15 mL tube [1].
  • Add an equal volume of 2× freezing solution to the worm suspension, so the final glycerol is 15%, and mix gently by inversion [1].
  • Aliquot 1 mL per labeled 1.8 mL cryovial, mixing between vials so every one holds similar numbers of worms; screw the caps tight [1][3].
  • Place the vials in the insulated box (about 1 °C/min cooling) and put the box at −80 °C for at least 12 h [1][2][4]. (12 h)
  • The next day, thaw one test vial at room temperature until no ice remains and pour all of it onto a large OP50-seeded NGM plate [1].
  • Look for moving worms within minutes. After 2–3 days at 20 °C, pick 10–15 animals singly and check that their progeny show the correct phenotype and no contamination [1].
  • If the test succeeds, keep at least one vial in liquid nitrogen as the master stock and the rest at −80 °C; record the location in a freezer log [1][2].
  • To recover a stock, thaw a vial at room temperature, pour it onto an OP50 plate and incubate at 20 °C; do not centrifuge, because the glycerol prevents a firm pellet [1][2].
  • Check for crawling worms the next day and confirm that the line is healthy after 3–5 days. Never refreeze a thawed vial; make a fresh stock from the recovered worms [3].
  • After step 5 vials can wait briefly at room temperature before going into the insulated box, but do not leave them for more than about an hour.
  • After step 6 vials can stay at −80 °C overnight; the test thaw in step 7 is done the next day.
  • After step 9 stocks are stable for years at −80 °C or in liquid nitrogen.
  • Moving L1–L2 larvae within minutes of thawing and many crawling animals within 1–2 days.
  • A recovery of about 35–45% of frozen animals from liquid nitrogen [1].
  • Recovered progeny with the correct phenotype and no contamination.
  • Always thaw one test vial before discarding the working plates [1]
  • Verify phenotype and genotype of progeny after revival
  • Keep the master stock in liquid nitrogen and working stocks at −80 °C [1]
  • Record the strain, date and number of vials in a freezer log

  • Liquid nitrogen can cause severe cold burns and sealed vials may burst: wear cryo gloves, a face shield and a lab coat, and use O-ring cryovials for liquid nitrogen storage.
  • Let the isopropanol freezing container warm for a few minutes before opening, and keep isopropanol to the fill line [3].
  • Autoclave or bleach contaminated plates and buffers before disposal; follow your institutional rules for cultures of E. coli OP50.
