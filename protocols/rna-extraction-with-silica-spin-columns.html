RNA Extraction with Silica Spin Columns

About 45 min · 2026-11-20

Purify total RNA from cultured cells with guanidine lysis and a silica spin column, with optional on-column DNase, quality checks and troubleshooting.

⬇ Word (.docx)⬇ PowerPoint (.pptx)

Purpose

To purify total RNA from cultured mammalian cells (with notes for tissue) using a guanidine-based lysis buffer and a silica-membrane spin column, giving RNA suitable for RT-qPCR, microarrays and RNA sequencing.

Principle

Cells are disrupted in a buffer containing a chaotropic guanidine salt that denatures proteins and inactivates RNases at once [1][2]. Ethanol is added so that RNA binds to the silica membrane in the high-salt environment while proteins, most DNA fragments and other contaminants pass through or are washed away [1][2]. Salt and ethanol are removed with wash buffers, a dry spin clears the membrane, and the RNA is eluted in a small volume of RNase-free water [1][2][3]. An optional on-column DNase I digestion removes genomic DNA [3]. The approach replaces the phenol-based single-step method but keeps its guanidinium chemistry [4].

Materials and reagents

ItemDetails

Equipment

  • Microcentrifuge (≥8,000 × g)
  • Fume hood
  • RNase-free filter tips and tubes
  • Vortex mixer
  • Spectrophotometer or fluorometer
  • Bioanalyzer (optional)
  • −80 °C freezer

Procedure

  1. Wear gloves, clean the bench and pipettes with an RNase decontaminant, and use RNase-free tips and tubes. Add β-ME to the lysis buffer (10 µL per mL) in the fume hood [1][2]. (10 min)
  2. Harvest cells: pellet suspension cells at 300 × g for 5 min; for adherent cells, rinse with cold PBS and either trypsinize and pellet, or lyse directly in the dish [1]. (10 min)
  3. Count the cells. Do not exceed 1 × 10⁷ cells per spin column; lyse fewer cells for high-RNA-content lines to avoid clogging [1][2].
  4. Remove all supernatant, then add 350 µL lysis buffer (fewer than 5 × 10⁶ cells) or 600 µL (up to 1 × 10⁷ cells). Flick to loosen the pellet; vortex 1 min if under 1 × 10⁵ cells [1][2].
  5. Homogenize the lysate: pass it through a QIAshredder-type column (2 min, full speed) or through a 20-gauge needle at least 5 times. Incomplete homogenization lowers yield [1].
  6. Optional pause: lysate can be frozen at −70 °C to −80 °C for several months; thaw and warm to room temperature before continuing [1].
  7. Add 1 volume of 70% ethanol to the lysate (350 µL or 600 µL) and mix by pipetting. Do not centrifuge; precipitate may form, which is normal [1]. (2 min)
  8. Load up to 700 µL (including any precipitate) onto the spin column in a collection tube. Close the lid and centrifuge ≥8,000 × g for 15 s. Discard the flow-through [1].
  9. If the sample is larger than 700 µL, load the remainder and spin again, discarding the flow-through each time.
  10. Optional DNase: add 350 µL wash buffer 1 and spin 15 s; apply 80 µL DNase I mix to the membrane, incubate 15 min at 20–30 °C; add 350 µL wash buffer 1 and spin 15 s [1][3]. (20 min)
  11. Without DNase, add 700 µL wash buffer 1 (Zymo: 400 µL prewash), spin 15–30 s at ≥8,000 × g and discard the flow-through. With DNase, step 10 replaces this wash [1][3].
  12. Add 500 µL ethanol-containing wash buffer, centrifuge 15 s at ≥8,000 × g, and discard the flow-through [1].
  13. Add another 500 µL wash buffer and centrifuge for 2 min at ≥8,000 × g to dry the membrane; check that no liquid touches the column bottom [1].
  14. Place the column in a new 1.5 mL tube. If ethanol carry-over is suspected, first spin the column 1 min at full speed in a fresh collection tube [1][3].
  15. Add 30–50 µL RNase-free water directly onto the membrane, incubate 1 min, and centrifuge 1 min at ≥8,000 × g to elute. Reapply the eluate for a more concentrated RNA [1].
  16. Measure A260 and the A260/A280 and A260/A230 ratios; 1 A260 unit is about 40 µg/mL RNA. Aliquot and store at −80 °C [3][4]. (10 min)

Pause points

  • The cell lysate in lysis buffer can be stored at −70 °C to −80 °C for several months before purification [1].
  • Purified RNA can be stored at −80 °C in RNase-free water; avoid repeated freeze–thaw cycles by making small aliquots [3][4].

Expected results

  • Typical yields per 1 × 10⁶ cells are about 10 µg for NIH/3T3, 15 µg for HeLa and 35 µg for COS-7 cells; the column binds up to about 100 µg of RNA [1][3].
  • A260/A280 is about 1.9–2.1 in 10 mM Tris·Cl pH 7.5 (above 1.8 in water) and A260/A230 is above 1.8 [1][3][4].
  • On a denaturing gel or Bioanalyzer, the 28S and 18S rRNA bands appear sharp at about 2:1 with no smear and little genomic DNA contamination.

Controls

  • No-reverse-transcriptase control in qPCR to detect genomic DNA carry-over
  • Lysis-buffer-only blank for spectrophotometry
  • Reference RNA of known concentration or a previously extracted sample
  • Mock extraction without cells (checks for RNase or DNA contamination of reagents)

Troubleshooting

ProblemLikely causeSolution
Low yieldIncomplete lysis or homogenization, too few cells, or ethanol not addedHomogenize thoroughly; verify cell number; add ethanol before loading [1]
Clogged columnToo many cells or viscous genomic DNAUse fewer cells, add more lysis buffer, shear with a needle or QIAshredder [1][2]
Low A260/A280Eluting in unbuffered water or protein carry-overMeasure in 10 mM Tris·Cl pH 7.5; re-elute; repeat lysis if needed [1]
Degraded RNARNase contamination, slow lysis, or prolonged storage of the lysateUse RNase-free supplies; lyse immediately; keep lysate at −70 °C [1][4]
Genomic DNA in RNAOverloaded column or no DNaseUse on-column or in-solution DNase I digestion [1][3]
Low A260/A230Guanidine salt or ethanol carry-overDo the dry spin; avoid touching the column rim; add one more wash [3]

Safety and waste

  • Lysis and wash-1 buffers contain guanidine salts (irritants); wear gloves, goggles and a lab coat.
  • Never add bleach or acidic solutions to waste containing guanidine; reactive compounds can form [1][5].
  • β-Mercaptoethanol is toxic and foul-smelling; handle only in a fume hood and collect waste as hazardous chemical waste.
  • Phenol-containing reagents (for example QIAzol or TRIzol) are toxic and corrosive and require a fume hood [5].
  • Treat human and animal cells as potentially infectious and use appropriate biosafety conditions.
