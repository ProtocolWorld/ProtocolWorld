Aseptic Technique and Biosafety Cabinet Use

Estimated time: 30 min + working time | Protocol World

To keep mammalian cell cultures free of bacteria, fungi, mycoplasma and cross-contaminating cells, and to protect the worker, by using a Class II biosafety cabinet (BSC) and sterile handling correctly.

Aseptic technique is a set of habits that keep microorganisms away from cultures, media and tools. A Class II BSC supports this by pulling room air in through the front grille and passing the downflow air through HEPA filters (at least 99.99% efficient at about 0.3 µm), which protects the product, the worker and the environment [1]. The cabinet works only if its air curtain stays intact, so cluttering, fast arm movements, drafts and open flames defeat it [1][2]. Type A2 cabinets, with a minimum inflow of 100 linear feet per minute, are the usual choice for cell culture [1]. Clean-to-dirty work order, wiped surfaces, single-use sterile pipettes and capped containers prevent most contamination [2][3][5].

  • Check that the cabinet has a current certification label and that the airflow monitor or alarm shows normal operation. Do not use a cabinet in alarm [1]. (1 min)
  • Put on a buttoned lab coat with cuffs and gloves. Tie back long hair. Make sure the cabinet is away from doors, drafts and foot traffic [1][2]. (2 min)
  • If the blower was off, run it for at least 5 min before starting (or follow the manufacturer's instruction) [1]. Close the drain valve. (5 min)
  • Wipe the work surface, the inside of the sash and the side walls with 70% ethanol, from back to front, and let it dry [1][2]. (2 min)
  • Spray the outside of each bottle, flask, plate and pipette pack with 70% ethanol and wipe it before it enters. Spray gloves and let them dry for about 30 s [2][5]. (3 min)
  • Load only what you need. Put clean items on one side, cultures in the middle and waste on the other. Do not cover the front or rear grilles [1][6]. (2 min)
  • Work at least 10 cm (4 in.) behind the front grille. Move arms slowly and straight in and out, then wait about 1 min before starting so the airflow settles [1]. (1 min)
  • Open sterile packs only when needed. Hold pipettes at the plugged end and do not touch the barrel or the tip to bench or bottle threads [2]. (during work)
  • Use one pipette per bottle. Never put a used pipette back into medium. Place bottle and flask caps face down on a clean area or keep them in hand; cap vessels when not in use [2][6].
  • Avoid pouring from bottles. Dispense medium by pipette. Keep containers open as briefly as possible and do not talk, sing or cough over open cultures [2][5].
  • Discard pipettes into a horizontal discard tray inside the cabinet, not an upright cylinder. Do not use open flames; they disturb airflow and are a fire risk [1][2].
  • Suction liquid waste through an aspirator fitted with a HEPA filter and an overflow flask containing disinfectant [1]. Change the sterile tip for each vessel.
  • When finished, close all vessels, then wipe each item with 70% ethanol before removing it. Move cultures to the incubator [2][5]. (3 min)
  • Treat liquid waste with 10% bleach for 20–30 min before disposal, then discard it. Autoclave or dispose of solid waste as biohazard waste [1][6]. (20–30 min)
  • Clean the work surface with 70% ethanol. Let the blower run for a few minutes with no activity, then lower the sash to its designated height or switch off as the manual says [1][6]. (5 min)
  • Clean the surface below the work tray and the grilles periodically. Arrange annual certification by an accredited technician and after any move or filter change [1].
  • Cultures may be left safely in the incubator once vessels are closed and the cabinet has been cleaned.
  • Cabinet cleaning (the area under the tray) can be scheduled weekly rather than daily [1].
  • Cultures remain clear, with no sudden turbidity, no color change of the phenol red indicator and no floating filaments.
  • Routine mycoplasma tests of every line are negative.
  • The BSC passes annual certification.
  • Medium-only control well or flask in the incubator to check sterility
  • Routine mycoplasma testing by PCR, Hoechst staining, ELISA or culture [3]
  • Cell line authentication by STR profiling when cross-contamination is suspected [3]
  • Annual cabinet certification and daily check of the airflow indicator [1]

  • Treat all human and primate cells as potentially infectious; use BSL-2 practice [1][3].
  • Wear gloves and a lab coat; wash your hands before and after work [4].
  • Flammable solvents and open flames do not belong in a cabinet [1].
  • Bleach is corrosive and reacts with some chemicals: do not mix it with acids or ammonia; rinse metal surfaces with water or 70% ethanol after bleach [1][6].
  • UV lamps are not a substitute for chemical disinfection; if present, follow the manufacturer's maintenance schedule and keep them off while people are in the room [1].
  • Report all spills. Cover a small spill with disinfectant-soaked towels for 20–30 min, then wipe up [1].
