Passaging Adherent Cells

Estimated time: 45–60 min | Protocol World

To subculture an adherent mammalian cell line by detaching the monolayer with trypsin-EDTA, counting the cells and reseeding them at a defined density or split ratio, keeping the culture in healthy, exponential growth. Volumes are given for a 75 cm² flask (T-75) with scaling notes for other vessels.

Cells attach through integrins and calcium-dependent adhesion proteins. Rinsing with calcium- and magnesium-free salt solution removes serum, which inhibits trypsin; trypsin cleaves surface proteins and EDTA chelates divalent cations, so cells round up and lift [1][2][3]. Serum-containing medium then neutralizes the enzyme, and reseeding at the proper density keeps cells at 70–90% confluence, in log phase, without crowding [1][3]. Detach just long enough, because prolonged trypsin exposure damages cells [3][4].

Complete growth mediumMedium recommended for the cell line (for example DMEM with 10% FBS), pre-warmed to 37 °C [1][5]
DPBS or PBS, no Ca²⁺/Mg²⁺Pre-warmed to 37 °C [1][2]
Trypsin-EDTA0.05% trypsin with 0.53 mM EDTA is a common working solution; 0.25% trypsin/0.53 mM EDTA is also used. Pre-warm to 37 °C. Check the cell line's data sheet [1][3][4]
Trypan blue0.4% solution, mixed 1:1 with the cell suspension for counting [1]
Culture vesselsNew flasks, plates or dishes, labeled with cell line, date, your initials and passage number [5][6]
Ethanol70% ethanol for wiping the biosafety cabinet and items [5][6]
Vessel volume guideMedium per vessel: T-25 5–10 mL; T-75 15–25 mL; T-175 35–60 mL; 6-well 2–3 mL per well; 100 mm dish 10–12 mL [1]. For other vessels scale PBS and trypsin by growth area; Thermo Fisher gives up to about 2 mL PBS and 0.5 mL trypsin-EDTA per 10 cm², and smaller practical volumes (5 mL PBS per T-75) are common [2][4][5]
  • Class II biosafety cabinet
  • CO₂ incubator, 37 °C, 5% CO₂
  • Inverted phase-contrast microscope
  • Centrifuge with swing-out rotor (125–200 × g)
  • Hemocytometer or automated cell counter
  • Aspirator with vacuum trap, serological pipettes and pipette controller
  • Examine the culture under the microscope. Passage at 70–90% confluence in log phase with viability above 90%; do not let the monolayer reach 100% [1][2]. (5 min)
  • Pre-warm medium, DPBS and trypsin-EDTA to 37 °C. Wipe the cabinet and items with 70% ethanol; work with sterile technique [1][5][6]. (15 min)
  • Aspirate the spent medium, tilting the flask and drawing from the corner opposite the cells [5][6].
  • Rinse with DPBS (no Ca²⁺/Mg²⁺): about 5 mL for a T-75, 2 mL for a T-25, added to the wall opposite the cells. Rock gently and aspirate; repeat once if serum is heavy [2][3][5][6]. (2 min)
  • Add trypsin-EDTA to just cover the monolayer, 2–3 mL for a T-75, and rock to spread [1][3]. (1 min)
  • Incubate at 37 °C and watch under the microscope. Cells round up and detach in about 2–5 min for most lines, up to 15 min for stubborn ones [1][2][3]. Do not exceed 10 min where possible [4]. (2–15 min)
  • Tap the side of the flask gently if less than 90% of cells have lifted. Do not strike or shake it hard [1][2][4].
  • Neutralize with complete medium at 2–3 times the trypsin volume (6–8 mL for a T-75). Pipette over the surface to wash off remaining cells [1][2][4]. (2 min)
  • Pipette gently to a single-cell suspension, avoiding foam. Check that more than 95% of cells are single [1][4].
  • Optional spin: transfer to a 15 mL tube, centrifuge 5 min at 200 × g (ATCC uses 125 × g for sensitive cells), and resuspend the pellet in a known volume of fresh medium [1][2][3]. (10 min)
  • Mix 10 µL cells with 10 µL 0.4% trypan blue. Load a hemocytometer within 2–5 min, count the four corner squares, average them, and multiply by 2 × 10⁴ for cells/mL [1]. Aim for 50–100 cells per square. (10 min)
  • Calculate viability as unstained cells divided by total cells; proceed if above 90% [1][2].
  • Seed new vessels at 10³–10⁴ cells/cm², or split by ratio (for example 1:4–1:10 for many lines, 1:2 for primary cells) [1][6]. Add the cells to vessels already holding pre-warmed medium: 12–15 mL for a new T-75 [1]. (5 min)
  • Label vessels, rock gently in a cross pattern, and return to the incubator. Record the passage number [1][5][6].
  • Check attachment and spreading after 24 h. Change the medium or refeed 2–3 times per week, and do not passage more often than every 48 h [1][3].
  • Aspirate residual cells into the vacuum trap and discard contaminated plastic as biohazardous waste [3][6].
  • Cells can be left in the incubator after reseeding; no other pause is needed.
  • If the trypsinized suspension cannot be processed immediately, neutralize it and keep it at room temperature for a short time; do not leave cells in trypsin [2][4].
  • A suspension of more than 95% single cells with viability above 90% [1][2].
  • Cells attach within a few hours and are spread by the next day [1][6].
  • The culture returns to 70–90% confluence after the expected doubling interval; doubling time = T × ln 2 / ln(Xe/Xb), where Xb and Xe are the starting and final cell counts after time T.
  • Check morphology and medium for contamination at every passage, and test for mycoplasma periodically [1]
  • Record passage number or population doublings; diploid lines senesce after about 20–80 doublings, continuous lines do not [1]
  • Keep a low-passage frozen stock and thaw a new vial before the working culture ages
ProblemLikely causeSolution
Cells will not detachSerum carry-over, weak or old trypsin, over-confluenceWash more thoroughly, use fresh warm trypsin, extend incubation up to about 10 min, or tap gently [1][4]
Cell clumpsHarsh pipetting, lysed cells releasing DNA, over-centrifugationPipette gently; lower g-force; ask your supplier about DNase treatment [1]
Poor reattachmentToo long in trypsin, low serum, residual trypsinShorten exposure, spin out trypsin, use enough serum or attachment factor [1]
Low viabilityRough handling, wrong pH or osmolality, cells left at high densityHandle gently, check medium and CO₂, passage earlier [1]
Cells grow slowlySeeded too sparsely or split ratio too highSeed denser; use a lower split ratio, especially for sensitive or senescing cells [1][3]
Detached too early or cells lostTrypsin too strong for the lineUse 0.05% trypsin or a milder dissociation reagent and shorten exposure [2][4]
  • Work in a biosafety cabinet with gloves and a lab coat; handle human or primate cell lines at biosafety level 2 [1].
  • Disinfect liquid waste and discard contaminated plastics as biohazardous waste according to your institution's rules [3][6].
  • Ethanol is flammable: keep it away from flames and wipe cabinets with it only when the burner is off.
