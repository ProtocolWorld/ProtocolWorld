Genomic DNA Extraction from Plant Tissue

Estimated time: 3–4 h (optional overnight precipitation) | Protocol World

To isolate PCR-grade genomic DNA from fresh or frozen leaf tissue (for example Arabidopsis thaliana, tobacco or rice; 100 mg per sample) with a CTAB-based method, including adjustments for polysaccharide- and polyphenol-rich species.

Cetyltrimethylammonium bromide (CTAB) is a cationic detergent that breaks membranes and, in 1.4 M NaCl, keeps most polysaccharides in solution while DNA is released [1][2]. EDTA limits nuclease activity, and PVP with β-mercaptoethanol binds or reduces polyphenols that would otherwise oxidize and brown the DNA [3][4]. Chloroform:isoamyl alcohol (24:1) removes proteins, lipids and pigments into the organic phase and interphase, leaving DNA in the upper aqueous phase [1][2]. Isopropanol precipitates the DNA, a 70% ethanol wash removes CTAB and salt, and RNase A removes co-extracted RNA [1][3]. Volumes below are for about 100 mg of tissue and are a starting point to optimize.

β-Mercaptoethanol (β-ME)Add fresh to the warmed buffer, about 0.2–2% (v/v); use only in a fume hood [3][5]
PVP (optional, for phenolic species)Soluble PVP-40, 1–2% (w/v) in the buffer, or about 100 mg per gram of tissue added at grinding [3][4][5]
RNase ADNase-free; 50–100 µg/mL final, added to the lysis buffer or to the redissolved DNA [1][3]
Chloroform:isoamyl alcohol24:1 (v/v); keep in a glass or solvent-resistant container [1][2]
Isopropanol100%, ice cold (−20 °C) [1][2]
70% ethanolIce cold; optional wash with 10 mM ammonium acetate in 70% ethanol to remove residual CTAB [1][5]
TE buffer or nuclease-free water10 mM Tris-HCl pH 8.0 with 0.1–1 mM EDTA; low-EDTA TE suits downstream enzymes [1][2]
5 M NaCl (optional)For a second precipitation of polysaccharide-rich DNA: add 1/10 volume plus 0.4 volume isopropanol [3]
Plant tissue and liquid nitrogenYoung, healthy leaf, 100 mg fresh or frozen; keep at −80 °C if not used the same day [1][2]
Agarose gel reagents1% agarose in 1× TAE or TBE, DNA stain and a size ladder (for example 1 kb) [1][2]
  • Fume hood
  • Mortar and pestle or bead mill (steel ball bearing, 1/8 inch) for 1.5–2 mL tubes
  • Water bath or heat block, 60–65 °C
  • Refrigerated microcentrifuge (at least 12,000 × g; 20,000 × g where available)
  • Freezer, −20 °C
  • Vortex mixer
  • NanoDrop spectrophotometer and Qubit fluorometer (dsDNA assay)
  • Agarose gel electrophoresis system
  • Wide-bore pipette tips
  • Dewar for liquid nitrogen and cryo gloves
  • Turn on a 60–65 °C water bath and warm the CTAB buffer in it for 5–10 min. Chill the mortar, pestle and tubes with liquid nitrogen [1]. (15 min)
  • In a fume hood, add β-mercaptoethanol to the warm buffer just before use. Use 0.2% (v/v) for most leaves, up to 2% for phenolic species [3][5].
  • For phenolic or polysaccharide-rich species, also include 1–2% PVP-40 in the buffer or add PVP to the mortar at grinding [3][4][5].
  • Weigh about 100 mg young leaf tissue. Freeze it in liquid nitrogen and keep it frozen until the buffer is added [1][2]. (5 min)
  • Grind to a fine powder with the chilled mortar and pestle, topping up with liquid nitrogen as needed. Do not let the powder thaw [1][2]. (3–5 min)
  • Alternative: grind frozen leaf in a 2 mL tube with a steel ball bearing in a bead mill until no fragments remain [1].
  • Move the powder into a 2 mL tube and immediately add 300 µL of warm CTAB buffer per 100 mg tissue (about 650 µL for 40 mg in tough species) [1][2].
  • Add RNase A to 50–100 µg/mL final concentration. Vortex, then invert until all powder is suspended [1]. (2 min)
  • Incubate at 60–65 °C for 30–60 min, inverting every 10 min. Use longer incubation (up to 2 h) for old or tough leaves [1][2][3]. (30–60 min)
  • Cool the lysate to room temperature for about 10–15 min. Cooling before chloroform lowers the risk of the tube popping [1][5]. (10–15 min)
  • In the fume hood, add 1 volume (about 300 µL) of chloroform:isoamyl alcohol (24:1). Shake or vortex for 15 s to emulsify [1][2]. (1 min)
  • Centrifuge at 12,000–20,000 × g for 10 min at room temperature. Three layers form: upper aqueous, a green interphase, lower chloroform [1][2]. (10 min)
  • Transfer about 200–250 µL of the upper aqueous phase to a new tube with a wide-bore tip. Avoid the interphase [1][2].
  • Repeat the chloroform extraction (steps 11–13) when the aqueous phase is green, brown or cloudy; a second extraction usually gives cleaner DNA [1][2]. (10 min)
  • Add 1 volume of ice-cold isopropanol (or 0.7 volume at room temperature for quick mini-preps). Mix gently by inversion; fine threads may appear [1][4]. (1 min)
  • Incubate at −20 °C for at least 30–60 min. Leave overnight for low-copy or dilute samples [1][2]. (30–60 min)
  • Centrifuge at 12,000–20,000 × g for 15 min at 4 °C. A small off-white pellet should lie at the tube bottom; mark the tube side [1]. (15 min)
  • Pour off or pipette away the supernatant without touching the pellet [1].
  • Add 500 µL–1 mL of ice-cold 70% ethanol and invert to wash. Optional: use 10 mM ammonium acetate in 70% ethanol for 5 min to remove CTAB [1][2][5]. (5 min)
  • Centrifuge at 9,000–12,000 × g for 5 min at 4 °C. Remove all ethanol with a pipette; repeat the wash if the pellet is yellow or brown [1][2]. (5 min)
  • Air-dry the pellet upside down for 5–10 min. Do not over-dry, which makes DNA hard to dissolve [1][2]. (5–10 min)
  • Dissolve the pellet in 50–100 µL TE (low EDTA) or nuclease-free water. Rest at room temperature for 1 h or overnight; flick gently to mix [1][2]. (1 h or overnight)
  • If RNase was not added at lysis, add 1 µL RNase A (1 µg/µL) per 100 µL and incubate at 37 °C for 15–60 min [3][5]. (15–60 min)
  • Polysaccharide-rich samples: spin at 12,000 rpm for 5 min, keep the supernatant, add 1/10 volume 5 M NaCl and 0.4 volume isopropanol, chill at −20 °C 20 min, spin 5 min and wash [3]. (40 min)
  • Measure concentration and purity: A260/A280 about 1.8, A260/A230 above 1.8–2.0. Use Qubit (dsDNA assay) when RNA or polysaccharides may inflate NanoDrop values [1][2].
  • Run 1–5 µL (50–200 ng) on a 1% agarose gel. High-molecular-weight DNA should show a tight band above 10 kb with little smear [1][5]. (45 min)
  • Store DNA at 4 °C for short term (weeks) or at −20 °C long term. Aliquot to avoid repeated freeze–thaw [2].
  • After grinding: store the frozen powder at −80 °C in tubes for weeks.
  • After isopropanol addition: keep at −20 °C overnight, or at room temperature over a weekend for large preps [2][5].
  • After resuspension: keep at 4 °C for days or at −20 °C for months.
  • Typical yield from 100 mg of Arabidopsis or tobacco leaf is about 5–30 µg, depending on species and age; one mini-prep of 25 mg tissue yielded about 20–34 µg in a published CTAB procedure [4].
  • A260/A280 of 1.7–2.0; A260/A230 below 1.8 means residual CTAB, polysaccharide or phenol.
  • Gel shows a main high-molecular-weight band (more than 10–20 kb) with little smear; a low band at the bottom indicates RNA if RNase was omitted.
  • DNA is suitable for PCR, Sanger sequencing and cloning [1][2]; long-read or next-generation sequencing may need extra cleanup and longer spins [1].
  • Extraction blank: all reagents with no tissue, to detect contamination on the spectrophotometer and in PCR.
  • Positive control: leaf of a well-characterized species (for example Arabidopsis Col-0) processed in parallel.
  • Gel check of every prep for integrity and RNA removal.
  • Test a 1:10 dilution of DNA in PCR with a known primer pair to detect carry-over inhibitors.

  • β-Mercaptoethanol is toxic, flammable and foul smelling: open and pipette only in a fume hood, wear nitrile gloves and eye protection [3][5].
  • Chloroform is a suspected carcinogen and toxic by inhalation: work in a fume hood and collect all waste in labeled halogenated solvent bottles [1][5].
  • Liquid nitrogen causes severe cold burns and can displace oxygen: wear cryo gloves, face shield and lab coat, and use only vented containers.
  • CTAB is harmful and toxic to aquatic life: collect CTAB-containing waste as hazardous chemical waste; never pour it down the drain.
  • Phenol (if used) is corrosive: use gloves and eye protection and report skin contact immediately [5].
  • Balance centrifuge tubes and use tubes rated for chloroform to avoid breakage.
