Freezing Cells for Cryostorage

1 h hands-on + overnight at −80 °C · 2026-11-15

Freeze mammalian cells for long-term storage: harvest in log phase, suspend in DMSO freezing medium, cool at about −1 °C per minute and keep in liquid nitrogen vapor.

⬇ Word (.docx)⬇ PowerPoint (.pptx)

Purpose

To preserve mammalian cell lines for long-term storage with high post-thaw viability, using controlled-rate cooling and storage in liquid nitrogen vapor.

Principle

Cells survive freezing when a cryoprotectant such as dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO) limits intracellular ice formation, and when they are cooled slowly, at about −1 °C per minute, so water leaves the cells before it freezes [1][2][5]. Healthy cells in log phase, a suitable cell density and a rapid, cold workflow give the best recovery. Vials must finally be stored below the glass transition of the medium, in practice below −130 °C, because storage at −80 °C is only a short-term holding step [1][2][6].

Materials and reagents

ItemDetails

Equipment

  • Biosafety cabinet
  • Centrifuge (200–300 × g)
  • Cell counter or hemocytometer
  • −80 °C freezer
  • Liquid nitrogen storage tank (vapor phase)
  • Dry ice container or cold-transfer carrier
  • Cryo gloves, face shield, lab coat

Procedure

  1. Check cell health: confirm no contamination or mycoplasma, and change the medium about 24 h before harvest [1][2]. (24 h before)
  2. Pre-chill the freezing medium to 2–8 °C and label cryovials with cell line, passage number, date, cell number and your initials [1][3][4]. (15 min)
  3. Adherent cells: remove medium, rinse with PBS, add pre-warmed trypsin (3–5 mL per T-75) and incubate at 37 °C until cells round up, usually about 2 min [1][3]. (2–5 min)
  4. Neutralize with serum-containing medium (at least an equal volume), pipette gently and transfer to a conical tube. Suspension cells go straight to the tube [1][4]. (5 min)
  5. Take a small sample and count cells with trypan blue to confirm viability above 90% [2][4]. (5 min)
  6. Centrifuge at 200–300 × g for 5 min (lower speeds for fragile cells). Remove the supernatant completely without disturbing the pellet [2][3][4]. (5 min)
  7. Resuspend the pellet gently in cold freezing medium at 1–5 × 10⁶ viable cells/mL (typically 2–4 × 10⁶) [1][3][4]. (5 min)
  8. Dispense 1 mL into each cryovial, mixing the suspension often. Work quickly, because cells should not sit in DMSO medium at room temperature for more than 10 min [2][3]. (10 min)
  9. Place the vials in a Mr. Frosty or CoolCell container, filled and equilibrated as the maker directs, and put it in a −80 °C freezer for a cooling rate of about −1 °C/min [2][3][5]. (Overnight)
  10. After at least 12–24 h at −80 °C, move vials on dry ice into the liquid nitrogen vapor phase, below −130 °C [1][2][3]. (10 min)
  11. Record each vial's tank, rack, box and position in the inventory log [1][4]. (5 min)
  12. About 1–2 weeks later, thaw one vial in a 37 °C water bath, dilute into pre-warmed medium and check viability, growth and sterility before relying on the stock [6]. (1–7 days)

Pause points

  • After step 1, cultures can continue to grow until ready.
  • After step 9, vials can remain at −80 °C overnight, and for up to about 1 week if needed [3]; transfer to nitrogen vapor for long-term storage.

Expected results

  • Post-thaw viability is typically above 70–80% for robust lines, with attachment within 24 h.
  • The test vial grows with normal morphology and no contamination.
  • Inventory records show each vial's location.

Controls

  • Test-thaw one vial from each new freeze
  • Keep a freeze record: viability and count before freezing, cells per vial, passage, medium and date
  • Test for mycoplasma before freezing [1][6]

Troubleshooting

ProblemLikely causeSolution
Low post-thaw viabilityLow viability or wrong density before freezing, rough harvest, slow handling in DMSOFreeze only healthy log-phase cells; handle gently; work fast on cold medium [1][3]
Poor reproducibilityAged freezing container or inconsistent coolingReplace container per the maker; keep the −80 °C freezer closed [3][5]
Clumping after thawCell density too highReduce density; test several concentrations [6]
DMSO-sensitive line differentiatesCell type reacts to DMSOTry 10% glycerol or a commercial medium [1][4]
Vials warm during transferSlow transfer from −80 °C to nitrogenMove on dry ice or in a cold carrier [1][5]
Contamination after thawContaminated culture or poor techniqueTest before freezing; work aseptically [1][6]

Safety and waste

  • Liquid nitrogen causes severe cold burns and displaces oxygen; wear cryo gloves, a face shield and a lab coat.
  • Liquid nitrogen can enter a vial stored in the liquid phase and make it burst on warming; use vapor-phase storage and a face shield [1][2][4].
  • DMSO penetrates skin and carries dissolved substances with it; wear gloves and follow local disposal rules [1][2].
  • Work with cells in a biosafety cabinet under your institution's biosafety rules.
