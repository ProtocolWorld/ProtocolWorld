Genomic DNA Extraction from Bacteria

About 6 h + overnight · 2026-11-15

Purify high-molecular-weight genomic DNA from bacteria with lysozyme, SDS, proteinase K, CTAB and phenol–chloroform extraction, then RNase treatment and ethanol precipitation.

⬇ Word (.docx)⬇ PowerPoint (.pptx)

Purpose

To purify high-molecular-weight genomic DNA from a bacterial culture (E. coli and other Gram-negative or Gram-positive species) that is clean enough for PCR, restriction digestion, library preparation and sequencing.

Principle

Lysozyme weakens the peptidoglycan wall, SDS dissolves membranes, and proteinase K digests proteins. A high-salt CTAB step at 65 °C complexes polysaccharides and residual protein, and organic extractions (chloroform, then phenol:chloroform) remove them. Isopropanol precipitates the DNA, RNase removes RNA, and a second ethanol precipitation removes salts and phenol traces [1][2]. Gentle mixing protects long DNA fragments from shearing [1][3].

Equipment

  • Microcentrifuge capable of about 16,000 × g (room temperature and 4 °C)
  • 37 °C incubator or heat block
  • 56 °C heat block
  • 65 °C water bath or heat block
  • −20 °C freezer (and −80 °C if available)
  • Chemical fume hood
  • Wide-bore pipette tips (to limit shearing)
  • Fluorometer (Qubit) or spectrophotometer (NanoDrop)
  • Agarose gel electrophoresis system

Procedure

  1. Pellet 1.5 mL of culture, discard the supernatant, and resuspend the cells in TE. Adjust to OD600 of about 1.0 (not above 1.2) with TE, then transfer 740 µL to a 2 mL tube [1]. (15 min)
  2. Add 20 µL of 100 mg/mL lysozyme, mix by inversion, and incubate at 37 °C for 30 min. This step is essential for Gram-positive and some Gram-negative species [1]. (30 min)
  3. Add 40 µL of 10% SDS and mix gently, then add 8 µL of 10 mg/mL proteinase K and mix [1]. (5 min)
  4. Incubate at 56 °C for 1–3 h until the solution clears and becomes viscous; if lysis is incomplete, continue up to 16 h [1]. (1–3 h)
  5. Add 100 µL of 5 M NaCl and mix, then add 100 µL of CTAB/NaCl pre-warmed to 65 °C. Mix and incubate at 65 °C for 10 min [1]. (15 min)
  6. In a fume hood, add 0.5 mL chloroform:isoamyl alcohol (24:1), invert gently about 20 times, and spin at maximum speed (≥12,000 × g) for 10 min at room temperature. Keep the upper aqueous phase [1]. (15 min)
  7. Add an equal volume (about 0.5 mL) of phenol:chloroform:isoamyl alcohol (25:24:1), mix by inversion, spin 10 min, and move the aqueous phase to a new tube [1][2]. (15 min)
  8. Repeat with 0.5 mL chloroform:isoamyl alcohol (24:1): mix, spin 10 min, and keep the aqueous phase. Avoid the interphase in every extraction [1][2]. (15 min)
  9. Add 0.6 volume of −20 °C isopropanol (for example, 240 µL to 400 µL of aqueous phase) and invert until DNA strands appear [1]. (5 min)
  10. Incubate at −20 °C for at least 2 h or overnight, then spin at maximum speed for 15 min at 4 °C and discard the supernatant [1]. (2 h – overnight)
  11. Wash the pellet with 1 mL cold 70% ethanol, spin 5 min at 4 °C, remove all ethanol with a pipette, and air-dry for 20 min or until just translucent [1]. (30 min)
  12. Dissolve the pellet in about 170 µL DNase-free water by gentle flicking and a brief 4 °C rest; avoid vortexing [1]. (15 min)
  13. Set up RNase: 170 µL DNA, 20 µL 10× RNase buffer and 10 µL DNase-free RNase (200 µL total). Incubate 1 h at 37 °C [1]. (1 h)
  14. Run 5 µL beside an untreated aliquot on a 1% agarose gel. If much RNA remains, add 10 µL more RNase and repeat the incubation [1]. (30 min)
  15. Heat-inactivate the RNase at 70 °C for 15 min and chill on ice [1]. (15 min)
  16. Add 1/10 volume of 3 M sodium acetate and 2.5 volumes of 100% ethanol, mix, and hold at −80 °C for 30 min or −20 °C for 2 h to overnight [1]. (30 min – overnight)
  17. Spin 20 min at 4 °C, wash with cold 70% ethanol (3–5 min spin), remove all ethanol, and dry 5–15 min without heat [1]. (40 min)
  18. Resuspend in 100 µL TE. Measure concentration by Qubit or NanoDrop, and run 1 µL on a 1% agarose gel to check integrity. Store at −20 °C or −80 °C [1]. (30 min)

Pause points

  • After step 10: the isopropanol precipitate can stay at −20 °C overnight.
  • After step 11: the air-dried pellet can be stored at −20 °C for several days before resuspension.
  • After step 16: the ethanol precipitation can stay at −20 °C overnight; the purified DNA keeps for months at −20 °C or −80 °C in TE.

Expected results

  • Yield is typically tens of micrograms from 1.5 mL of culture; the exact amount depends on species and growth phase (no yield is stated in the sources, so measure it).
  • A260/280 of about 1.8 and A260/230 of 1.8–2.2 indicate clean DNA [1].
  • A single high-molecular-weight band with little smear and no low-molecular-weight RNA on a 1% agarose gel.

Controls

  • Include a mock extraction (water instead of cells) to detect reagent contamination.
  • Run an RNase-untreated aliquot on the gel to judge RNA removal.
  • Quantify by fluorometer (Qubit): residual phenol raises NanoDrop readings by absorbing near 270 nm [1].

Troubleshooting

ProblemLikely causeSolution
Low yieldPoor lysis or too few cellsExtend proteinase K digestion (up to 16 h), confirm lysozyme activity, and collect cells at late log phase [1]
Protein carryover (low A260/280)Too many cells or interphase carried overKeep OD600 at 1.2 or below and repeat the chloroform extraction [1]
Sheared, smeared DNAVortexing or hard shakingMix by gentle inversion and use wide-bore tips [1][3]
Phenol contamination (peak near 270 nm)Aqueous phase taken too close to the interphaseRe-extract with chloroform and repeat the ethanol precipitation [1][2]
Gram-positive cells do not lyseThick peptidoglycanUse fresh lysozyme, confirm the 37 °C incubation, and see the dedicated Gram-positive protocol [3]

Safety and waste

  • Phenol and chloroform are toxic and corrosive: work in a fume hood with nitrile gloves, a lab coat, and eye protection [3].
  • CTAB is harmful to aquatic life; do not pour it down the drain.
  • Collect phenol and chloroform waste in a labeled organic solvent container; dispose of tubes and tips as chemical waste per local rules.
  • Treat the culture according to its biosafety level and autoclave bacterial waste.
