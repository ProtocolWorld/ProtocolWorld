Lactate Dehydrogenase (LDH) Assay

About 1.5–3 h · 2026-11-19

Two complementary LDH methods: a kinetic NADH-based activity assay read at 340 nm, and a colorimetric LDH-release cytotoxicity assay read at 490 nm. Includes unit calculations, % cytotoxicity and controls.

⬇ Word (.docx)⬇ PowerPoint (.pptx)

Purpose

To measure LDH enzyme activity (U/mL) in a sample and to quantify cell membrane damage by the amount of LDH released into culture medium.

Principle

LDH reduces pyruvate to lactate while oxidizing NADH to NAD⁺; the fall in A340 (ε = 6.22 mM⁻¹ cm⁻¹) gives activity in units, where 1 U converts 1.0 µmol pyruvate per min at pH 7.5 and 37 °C [1]. In cell culture, LDH leaking from damaged cells oxidizes lactate with NAD⁺; diaphorase then uses the NADH to turn a tetrazolium dye (INT) into a red formazan read near 490 nm [2][3][4]. Cytotoxicity is scaled between spontaneous and maximum (detergent-lysed) release [2][4].

Materials and reagents

ItemDetails
Sodium phosphate buffer100 mM, pH 7.5 at 37 °C (about 12 mg/mL monobasic sodium phosphate adjusted with 1 M NaOH) [1]
β-NADH, disodium salt0.13 mM in cold buffer, prepared fresh; discard yellow or clumpy powder [1]
Sodium pyruvate34 mM (3.74 mg/mL) in cold buffer, freshest lot [1]
BSA diluent1% (10 mg/mL) in buffer, to stabilize dilute enzyme and for blanks [1]
LDH sample or standardDiluted to 0.15–0.50 U/mL in cold BSA diluent just before use [1]
Colorimetric LDH-release kitSubstrate mix (lactate, NAD⁺, diaphorase, INT), 10X lysis solution (Triton X-100), stop solution [2]
Culture mediumLow serum (about 1–5%) or serum-free; phenol red–free if possible [2][4]
LDH positive controlOptional; kit control diluted 1:5,000 in PBS with 1% BSA, prepared fresh [2]
Plates and cuvettes1 cm quartz or UV-transparent cuvettes; flat, clear-bottom 96-well plates

Equipment

  • UV–Vis spectrophotometer with 37 °C cuvette holder (340 nm)
  • Plate reader (490 nm; 680 nm reference)
  • CO₂ incubator (37 °C, 5% CO₂)
  • Centrifuge with plate rotor (up to 250 × g)
  • Calibrated micropipettes and multichannel pipette
  • Syringe needle for popping bubbles

Procedure

  1. Part A: Pre-warm the reader to 37 °C and mix 2.80 mL of 0.13 mM NADH with 0.10 mL of 34 mM pyruvate in each cuvette. Blank cuvette also gets 0.10 mL of 1% BSA [1]. (10 min)
  2. Equilibrate the cuvettes at 37 °C and watch A340 until it holds steady [1]. (5 min)
  3. Add 0.10 mL of diluted sample (0.15–0.50 U/mL) to the test cuvette and 0.10 mL BSA to the blank; invert to mix immediately (3.00 mL total) [1]. (1 min)
  4. Record the drop in A340 for about 5 min and take ΔA340/min from the steepest linear stretch for test and blank [1]. (5 min)
  5. Calculate U/mL = (ΔA/min test − ΔA/min blank) × 3 × dilution factor ÷ (6.22 × 0.1) [1].
  6. Part B: Seed cells in a 96-well plate and treat with compound or vehicle to a final volume of 100–150 µL per well; incubate at 37 °C for the exposure time [2]. (Per experiment)
  7. Set up triplicate controls: medium only (background), untreated cells (spontaneous release), and cells for maximum release [2][3].
  8. For maximum release, add 10 µL of 10X lysis solution per 100 µL of cells (about 0.8% Triton X-100 final) 45 min before harvest [2]. Check lysis by microscope. (45 min)
  9. Add 10 µL of 10X lysis solution to 100 µL of cell-free medium as a volume-correction control [2].
  10. Optional: centrifuge the plate at 250 × g for 4 min so floating cells do not carry over [2]. (4 min)
  11. Transfer 50 µL of supernatant from every well to a fresh flat, clear-bottom 96-well plate [2].
  12. Add 50 µL of reconstituted substrate reagent to each well, cover from light, and incubate 30 min at room temperature [2]. (30 min)
  13. Add 50 µL of stop solution to each well [2]. (1 min)
  14. Pop bubbles with a needle and read A490 within 1 h; also read a reference near 680 nm and subtract it [2][3]. (5 min)
  15. Subtract the mean medium background from all wells, and the volume-correction mean from the maximum-release wells [2].
  16. Calculate % cytotoxicity = (Experimental − Spontaneous) ÷ (Maximum − Spontaneous) × 100 [2][4].

Pause points

  • After step 5, store dilute enzyme on ice and run the next samples promptly.
  • After step 11, keep supernatant plates covered at room temperature for a short time before adding reagent.
  • After step 13, read the plate within 1 h of adding stop solution [2].

Expected results

  • Kinetic assay: a steady, linear fall in A340 for test samples, with a negligible slope in the blank; report activity as U/mL or U/mg protein.
  • Cytotoxicity assay: spontaneous release is low, maximum release is clearly higher, and % cytotoxicity rises with dose or time.
  • Absorbance stays within the linear range of the reader; if it exceeds it, dilute the supernatant or shorten development to 15–20 min [2].

Controls

  • Blank cuvette with BSA in place of enzyme (kinetic assay)
  • Medium-only background, also corrects for serum LDH and phenol red
  • Spontaneous release: untreated or vehicle-treated cells
  • Maximum release: lysed cells (Triton X-100)
  • Volume correction: lysis solution in cell-free medium
  • Optional LDH positive control and a compound-interference control (compound added to lysate) [4]

Troubleshooting

ProblemLikely causeSolution
High background in cytotoxicity assaySerum LDH or phenol red in mediumReduce serum to about 5% or less, use phenol red–free medium, and subtract the medium blank [2][4]
High spontaneous releaseOvergrown cells, rough pipetting, temperature swingsKeep density below 1.5 × 10⁶ cells/mL, pipette gently, centrifuge at 250 × g or less [2]
Low signalToo few cells, short exposure, light-degraded substrateOptimize seeding, extend exposure (LDH half-life in medium is about 9 h), and protect reagent from light [2][4]
Readings erratic or too highAir bubbles in wellsPop bubbles with a needle and re-read [2][3]
Kinetic assay is non-linear or fastEnzyme too concentrated or NADH depletedDilute the sample into 0.15–0.50 U/mL and use the initial linear rate [1]
False elevation in serum or blood samplesHemolysis releases red-cell LDHAvoid hemolyzed samples and run matched controls [3][4]

Safety and waste

  • Wear gloves, a lab coat and eye protection; Triton X-100 is an irritant.
  • Treat cell cultures and biological samples as potentially infectious and follow local biosafety rules.
  • Test compounds and dissolving solvents (such as DMSO) may be hazardous; check safety data sheets.
  • Dispose of kit reagents and biological waste according to institutional policy.
