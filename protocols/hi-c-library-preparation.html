Hi-C Library Preparation

≈ 6 days (4–5 days if condensed) · 2026-11-27

Prepare a biotin-enriched Hi-C sequencing library from about 5 × 10⁶ mammalian cells using formaldehyde plus DSG crosslinking, DdeI/DpnII digestion, in situ proximity ligation and streptavidin pulldown, with built-in gel QC.

⬇ Word (.docx)⬇ PowerPoint (.pptx)

Purpose

To build an Illumina-ready Hi-C library that captures genome-wide chromatin contacts from about 5 × 10⁶ mammalian cells, with quality checks at each stage.

Principle

Formaldehyde (and the longer-range crosslinker DSG) freezes chromatin contacts. Nuclei are permeabilized and the chromatin is cut with restriction enzymes that leave 5′ overhangs. The overhangs are filled in with a biotinylated nucleotide, and the blunt ends that were close in 3D space are ligated inside intact nuclei. After crosslink reversal, DNA is sheared, and only biotin-marked ligation junctions are captured on streptavidin beads, adapter-ligated and amplified for paired-end sequencing [1][2][3]. A tight, high-molecular-weight ligation product and low dangling-end content are the key quality signs [1][2].

Equipment

  • Dounce homogenizer with pestle A
  • Thermomixer with interval shaking (65 °C, 37 °C, 23 °C, 16 °C)
  • Refrigerated microcentrifuge (up to 18,000 × g) and swing-bucket centrifuge
  • Covaris focused ultrasonicator (M220 or E220)
  • Magnetic rack
  • Thermocycler
  • Qubit fluorometer and gel electrophoresis system
  • Tube rotator

Procedure

  1. Fix 5 × 10⁶ cells in 1% formaldehyde in HBSS for exactly 10 min at room temperature (rock monolayers every 2 min) [1][2]. (10 min)
  2. Quench with 2.5 M glycine to 128 mM for 5 min at room temperature, then at least 15 min on ice. Pellet at 1,000 × g for 10 min and wash once with DPBS [1]. (≈ 45 min)
  3. Resuspend in 9.9 mL DPBS, add 100 µL fresh 300 mM DSG (3 mM) and rotate for 40 min at room temperature [1]. (40 min)
  4. Quench with glycine to 400 mM for 5 min; pellet 2,000 × g, 15 min. Resuspend in 1 mL 0.05% BSA–DPBS, move to a 1.7 mL tube, spin again (4 °C) and remove all liquid [1]. (≈ 45 min)
  5. Pause point: snap-freeze the pellet in liquid nitrogen and store at −80 °C, or continue directly [1][2].
  6. Lyse in 1 mL ice-cold lysis buffer with protease inhibitors for 15 min on ice. Dounce 30 strokes, rest 1 min on ice, then 30 more strokes [1]. (30 min)
  7. Pellet nuclei at 2,500 × g for 5 min at room temperature, then wash twice with 500 µL ice-cold 1× NEBuffer 3.1 [1]. (15 min)
  8. Resuspend in 1× NEBuffer 3.1 to 360 µL and save 18 µL at 4 °C as the chromatin integrity (CI) control [1].
  9. Add 38 µL 1% SDS and incubate at 65 °C for 10 min without shaking. Cool on ice, then add 43 µL 10% Triton X-100 to quench the SDS [1]. (15 min)
  10. Add 40 µL DdeI (10 U/µL), 8 µL DpnII (50 U/µL) and 4 µL 1× NEBuffer 3.1. Digest about 16 h at 37 °C, 900 rpm, 30 s on/4 min off [1]. (≈ 16 h)
  11. Heat-inactivate at 65 °C for 20 min, then place on ice. Keep 10 µL as the digestion control (DC) at 4 °C [1]. (20 min)
  12. Add 70 µL biotin fill-in mix (535 µL total). Incubate 4 h at 23 °C with interval shaking; the low temperature is important [1][2]. (4 h)
  13. Add 665 µL ligation mix (1,200 µL total). Ligate for 4 h at 16 °C with interval shaking [1][2]. (4 h)
  14. Pause point: ligated samples are stable at 4 °C for a few days [1].
  15. Reverse crosslinks: add 50 µL proteinase K (10 mg/mL) and incubate 2 h at 65 °C, then add another 50 µL and continue overnight at 65 °C [1]. (≈ 18 h)
  16. For CI and DC controls, bring to 50 µL with TLE, add 10 µL proteinase K and incubate overnight at 65 °C [1].
  17. Extract with 2 volumes (2.6 mL) phenol:chloroform:isoamyl alcohol in a phase-lock tube; vortex 1 min, spin 5 min at 1,500–3,500 × g [1]. (20 min)
  18. Move the aqueous phase to a 35 mL tube and add water to 1,250 µL, 125 µL 3 M sodium acetate pH 5.2 and 3.4 mL ice-cold ethanol. Chill 15 min on dry ice [1]. (30 min)
  19. Spin at 18,000 × g for 30 min at 4 °C, discard the supernatant and air-dry the pellet for about 10 min [1]. (40 min)
  20. Dissolve in 450 µL TLE, load on an Amicon Ultra-0.5 filter and spin 10 min at 14,000 × g. Wash twice with 450 µL TLE, then recover in about 100 µL [1]. (≈ 1 h)
  21. Add 1 µL RNase A (1 mg/mL) and incubate at 37 °C for at least 20 min [1]. (20 min)
  22. QC gel: run CI, DC and Hi-C DNA on a 0.8% agarose gel with a mass ladder. The Hi-C lane must run higher than DC, and CI should show a thick band above 10 kb [1][2]. (1.5 h)
  23. Remove unligated biotin ends: 5–15 µg DNA, 1× NEBuffer 2.1, 0.025 mM each dATP and dGTP, 39 U T4 DNA polymerase in 130 µL. 4 h at 20 °C, then 20 min at 75 °C [1][3]. (4.5 h)
  24. Shear to below 500 bp on a Covaris (M220: 50 W peak, 20% duty, 200 cycles/burst, 180 s) [1]. (1 h)
  25. Size-select with AMPure XP: add 0.8× beads, keep the supernatant, then add fresh beads to 1.1× and keep the bound fraction (about 100–300 bp). Elute in 50 µL TLE [1]. (1.5 h)
  26. Check both fractions on a 2% agarose gel and quantify by Qubit [1].
  27. End-repair the 100–300 bp fraction (T4 DNA polymerase, T4 PNK and Klenow with dNTPs and ligation buffer), 30 min at 20 °C, then 20 min at 75 °C [1][3]. (1 h)
  28. Pull down biotinylated junctions: bind to streptavidin beads (2 µL per µg DNA, at least 10 µL) in 1× binding buffer for 15 min at room temperature [1][2]. (30 min)
  29. Wash the beads with TWB, then 1× binding buffer, then TLE, and resuspend in 41 µL TLE [1][2].
  30. A-tail on beads with Klenow exo− and dATP for 30 min at 37 °C, then 20 min at 65 °C [1][3]. (1 h)
  31. Ligate annealed Illumina adapters to the beads with T4 DNA ligase for 2 h at room temperature. Wash twice in TWB, then 1× binding buffer and 1× NEBuffer 2.1 [1][3]. (2.5 h)
  32. Run a titration PCR with 5, 7, 9 and 11 cycles on 1.5 µL beads. Choose the cycle number one below the lowest that gives a visible smear on a 2% gel [1][3]. (2 h)
  33. Run the production PCR at that cycle number, clean up with 1.0× AMPure XP, elute in 30 µL water and check 1 µL on a 2% gel [1]. (2 h)
  34. Quantify, store at 4 °C or −20 °C, and sequence paired-end (50–100 bp reads) [1][2].

Pause points

  • The protocol can be paused after sterilization and labeling; store solutions as described.

Expected results

  • The Hi-C library runs as a clear upward shift versus the digestion control, with most DNA above 3 kb; typical yield before shearing is above 20 µg, and below 2 µg is probably insufficient [1].
  • The final library is a smear of about 300–600 bp on a 2% gel.
  • Good sequencing quality: more than 50% uniquely mapped reads, a high fraction of cis (intrachromosomal) pairs (for example above 70% for many cell types), low dangling ends and self-circles and low PCR duplicates [1][2].
  • Compartments are visible with about 20–25 million valid pairs; loop calling needs roughly 1 billion reads [1].

Controls

  • CI (chromatin integrity) sample taken after lysis, to confirm intact high-molecular-weight chromatin
  • DC (digestion control) taken after fill-in, to confirm digestion
  • A 3C-style control without the biotin fill-in step can be kept for troubleshooting [3]
  • Include a control cell line of known Hi-C quality when setting up the method

Troubleshooting

ProblemLikely causeSolution
No upward shift after ligationPoor digestion, inactive ligase or wrong buffer (potassium acetate buffers inhibit DpnII/DdeI)Check the DC band, use fresh ligase and NEBuffer 3.1, and repeat with fresh cells
Low DNA yield (< 2 µg)Too few cells or over-fixationCount cells carefully, keep fixation to 10 min and increase starting cells
Smear instead of tight ligation bandIncomplete SDS quench, over-ligation or degraded chromatinConfirm 1% Triton X-100 after SDS, limit ligation to 4 h and check the CI band
High dangling ends in sequencingIncomplete biotin removal from unligated endsRepeat the T4 DNA polymerase step and keep the 20 °C incubation
Too many PCR duplicatesLow complexity library with many PCR cyclesUse fewer cycles from the titration and start with more cells
PCR fails on beadsBead carryover inhibits the polymeraseUse a bead lot that tolerates PCR, or elute DNA from the beads at 98 °C first

Safety and waste

  • Formaldehyde and phenol:chloroform are toxic and volatile: work in a fume hood with nitrile or butyl gloves and collect as hazardous waste.
  • DSG is dissolved in DMSO, which carries chemicals through skin; wear gloves.
  • Dispose of ethanol, phenol and biological waste according to institutional rules.
  • Handle human or animal cell lines at the biosafety level your institution requires.
