BCA Protein Assay

About 1 hour · 2026-11-17

Measure total protein in lysates with the bicinchoninic acid (BCA) assay in a 96-well plate: prepare BSA standards (25–2,000 µg/mL), mix the working reagent, incubate, read at 562 nm and calculate concentrations from the standard curve.

⬇ Word (.docx)⬇ PowerPoint (.pptx)

Purpose

To measure the total protein concentration of cell or tissue lysates (for example RIPA lysates of cultured mammalian cells) with the bicinchoninic acid (BCA) colorimetric assay in a 96-well plate, so that equal amounts of protein can be loaded for Western blotting or other assays.

Principle

In alkaline solution, protein reduces Cu²⁺ to Cu⁺ (the biuret reaction); the peptide backbone and the side chains of cysteine, tyrosine and tryptophan all contribute [1]. Two BCA molecules chelate each Cu⁺ ion, producing a water-soluble purple complex that absorbs strongly at 562 nm, and the color deepens in proportion to protein concentration [2][3][4]. Unknowns are read against a standard curve of bovine serum albumin (BSA) [1][2].

Materials and reagents

ItemDetails
BCA kitBCA Reagent A (alkaline carbonate/BCA solution) and Reagent B (copper(II) sulfate); for example Pierce BCA kit [1][3] or Abcam ab287853, where the reagents are named Reagent 1 and Reagent B [2]
BSA standard2 mg/mL (2,000 µg/mL) BSA in water or in the same buffer as your samples [1][2]
Working reagent (WR)Mix Reagent A and Reagent B 50:1 (v/v); 200 µL per well. It turns green and is briefly turbid, then clears [1][2][3]
Sample diluentThe lysis buffer used for the samples (for example RIPA), or water if the sample is diluted enough [2]
WaterDeionized, for the blank and for dilutions
Lysate samplesDiluted to fall within 25–2,000 µg/mL; run at least duplicates, ideally three dilutions per sample [2]
Compatibility reagent (optional)A kit-specific reagent that reduces interference from reducing agents, chelators and detergents [4]

Equipment

  • 96-well clear flat-bottom microplate
  • Plate reader or spectrophotometer reading at 562 nm
  • Incubator or heat block set to 37 °C
  • Plate shaker (optional)
  • Multichannel pipette and sterile tips
  • Microcentrifuge tubes for standard dilutions

Procedure

  1. Allow the kit reagents and samples to reach room temperature. Plan wells: 8 standards (including a blank) plus each sample, all at least in duplicate [2]. (10 min)
  2. Prepare the standards from 2,000 µg/mL BSA: 300 µL stock (2,000), then 300 µL stock + 100 µL diluent (1,500), 300 µL stock + 300 µL diluent (1,000) [2]. (10 min)
  3. Continue the series: 300 µL of the 1,000 standard + 300 µL diluent (500); then 300 µL of the 500 standard + 300 µL diluent (250); then 300 µL of the 250 standard + 300 µL (125) [2].
  4. Make the 25 µg/mL standard from 100 µL of the 125 standard + 400 µL diluent. The blank (0 µg/mL) is 400 µL of diluent only [2].
  5. Dilute samples so that they fall within the 25–2,000 µg/mL range; lysates at 1–5 mg/mL usually need a 1:5 to 1:20 dilution. Use the same diluent as the standards [2].
  6. Mix the working reagent: 50 parts Reagent A to 1 part Reagent B. Calculate 200 µL per well plus about 10% extra, and mix until the green color is uniform [1][2][3]. (5 min)
  7. Pipette 25 µL of each standard, blank and diluted sample into separate wells of the plate (about 10 µL is acceptable when sample is scarce) [1][2][3].
  8. Add 200 µL working reagent to each well. Mix on a plate shaker for 30 s [2][3]. (2 min)
  9. Cover the plate and incubate at 37 °C for 30 min (or at room temperature for 2 h) [1][2][3]. (30 min)
  10. Cool the plate to room temperature (about 10 min) so that all wells are at the same temperature before reading [1][2]. (10 min)
  11. Read absorbance at 562 nm (a filter close to 562 nm is acceptable) [2][3]. (2 min)
  12. Subtract the mean blank (0 µg/mL) OD from every reading. Plot OD562 against BSA concentration and fit a line, Y = aX + b [2].
  13. Calculate sample concentration C = D × (Y − b) / a, where D is the dilution factor. A four-parameter curve fit is also used and may be more accurate than a purely linear fit [2][3].
  14. Test-tube alternative: 100 µL sample + 2 mL working reagent, then 37 °C for 30 min or room temperature for 2 h (25–2,000 µg/mL), or 60 °C for 30 min for the 5–250 µg/mL range [2].

Pause points

  • Standards diluted in water or buffer are stable for 1 week at 4 °C [2].
  • Working reagent is best prepared fresh but can be kept in a closed container at room temperature for several days [2].
  • Lysates can be frozen at −20 °C or −80 °C and assayed later.

Expected results

  • A smooth standard curve with OD562 rising with concentration and a high coefficient of determination (R² of about 0.99 for a good linear fit) across 25–2,000 µg/mL [2].
  • Sample replicates that agree closely, with OD values lying inside the range of the standards.
  • Blank wells that stay pale green, clearly lighter than the purple standards.

Controls

  • Blank (0 µg/mL) in the same diluent as the standards and samples, subtracted from all readings [2]
  • Standards prepared in the same buffer as the samples, for example RIPA, so that detergent effects cancel out
  • Replicates (duplicate or triplicate) for every standard and sample [2]
  • A second dilution of each sample to confirm that the reading falls inside the linear range [2]

Troubleshooting

ProblemLikely causeSolution
Sample OD higher than the top standardProtein too concentratedDilute the sample and repeat; keep readings inside the standard curve [2]
Blank is purple or very darkReducing agent (DTT, β-mercaptoethanol) or chelator (EDTA, EGTA) in the bufferDilute the sample, remove the interfering agent, or use the kit's compatibility reagent [1][4]
Unexpected color in detergent buffersDetergent or buffer interference; vendor claims differ for SDSCheck the kit's compatibility table and prepare standards in the same buffer; one source lists RIPA as compatible [4], another notes SDS as incompatible [3]
Uneven color across the plateTemperature gradients or poorly mixed wellsMix 30 s on a shaker, equilibrate to room temperature before reading [2][3]
Standard curve is not linearStandards outside the linear range or a pipetting errorRemake the dilution series; try a four-parameter fit [2][3]

Safety and waste

  • BCA reagent A is alkaline and reagent B contains copper(II) sulfate: wear gloves, eye protection and a lab coat, and check the kit's safety data sheet.
  • Treat lysates from human or primate cells as potentially infectious at the biosafety level your institution requires.
  • Collect used working reagent and kit waste as chemical waste according to local rules; do not pour copper-containing solutions down the drain.
