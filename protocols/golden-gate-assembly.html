Golden Gate Assembly

About 2 h + overnight · 2026-11-20

One-pot Type IIS assembly of multiple DNA fragments with BsaI-HFv2 and T4 ligase, including thermocycling programs by fragment number, E. coli transformation and screening.

⬇ Word (.docx)⬇ PowerPoint (.pptx)

Purpose

To assemble one or several DNA fragments into a destination plasmid in a single tube using BsaI-HFv2 and T4 DNA ligase, then transform E. coli and screen the colonies for correct constructs.

Principle

BsaI is a Type IIS restriction enzyme: it recognizes GGTCTC but cuts one base downstream on the top strand and five bases downstream on the bottom strand, leaving a four-base overhang that is not part of the recognition site [1]. Fragments are designed so that adjacent pieces carry matching, unique overhangs and the BsaI sites sit at the outer ends, facing away from the junctions. During repeated cycling, BsaI removes the sites and T4 DNA ligase joins compatible overhangs; correct products lack BsaI sites and therefore stay ligated, while incorrect or unassembled pieces are cut again [1][4]. A final 60 °C step favors cutting of remaining sites without ligation [1]. Fidelity depends on the weakest overhang pair, so overhangs are designed with ligase-fidelity data [4].

Equipment

  • Thermocycler
  • Heat block or 42 °C water bath
  • Shaking incubator (37 °C)
  • Static incubator (37 °C)
  • Microcentrifuge
  • DNA quantification device
  • Gel electrophoresis system
  • Ice bucket

Procedure

  1. Design fragments: confirm no internal BsaI (GGTCTC or GAGACC) sites exist in inserts or backbone; if so, domesticate them or switch to another Type IIS enzyme [1][4]. (variable)
  2. Choose unique, four-base overhangs for each junction using the NEBridge Golden Gate Assembly Tool and Ligase Fidelity data; avoid palindromic overhangs [1][4]. (variable)
  3. For amplicon inserts, add six flanking bases at the 5′ end of each primer, then the BsaI site facing the insert. Amplify with a proofreading polymerase and spin-column purify [1][4][3].
  4. Quantify each fragment and calculate 0.05 pmol of vector and of each insert (about 65 ng for 2 kb). NEB suggests 2:1 insert:vector for PCR-product inserts [1][3].
  5. On ice, combine in a PCR tube: 2 µL 10× T4 DNA Ligase Buffer, destination vector, each insert, 1 µL enzyme mix (2 µL for more than 10 inserts), and water to 20 µL [1]. (10 min)
  6. Mix gently and spin briefly. Place in the thermocycler with the heated lid on [1].
  7. Choose the program: one insert, 37 °C for 5 min; 2–10 inserts, 30 cycles of 37 °C for 1 min then 16 °C for 1 min; 11–20 inserts, 30 cycles of 37 °C for 5 min then 16 °C for 5 min [1]. (1–3 h)
  8. Finish every program with 60 °C for 5 min, then hold at 4 °C. For complex assemblies, raise the cycle number to 45–65 [1][4].
  9. Optional pause: store the assembly at −20 °C overnight; thaw on ice before transforming.
  10. Thaw 50 µL of competent E. coli on ice for 10 min, add 2 µL of the assembly reaction, flick gently, and incubate on ice for 30 min [1]. (40 min)
  11. Heat shock at 42 °C for 30 s, return to ice for 5 min, then add 950 µL room-temperature outgrowth medium [1].
  12. Shake at 250 rpm at 37 °C for 60 min [1]. (60 min)
  13. Plate 50 µL of a 1:5 dilution for a single insert, or 50–100 µL undiluted for multiple inserts, on selective plates. Incubate at 37 °C overnight [1]. (overnight)
  14. Count colonies on the assembly and control plates. Pick 4–8 isolated colonies (white if using lacZ screening) for colony PCR with primers flanking the junction [1].
  15. Grow positive colonies in selective LB overnight, prepare minipreps, and confirm by restriction digest and by sequencing across every four-base junction [1][4]. (overnight)

Pause points

  • The completed assembly reaction can be stored at −20 °C before transformation.
  • Plates can be stored at 4 °C for several days once colonies appear; grow cultures for plasmid preparation the same day when possible.

Expected results

  • Expected colony numbers are in the hundreds for a simple one- to five-fragment assembly with high-efficiency cells; NEB's five-insert lacZ test gives more than 250 colonies with more than 80% blue [1].
  • Colony PCR amplicons match the expected size, and restriction digests of minipreps give the predicted banding pattern.
  • Sequencing across the junctions shows correct, seamless four-base joins and no frameshifts.

Controls

  • No-insert reaction (vector, buffer and enzyme mix only) to estimate background from uncut vector [1]
  • Undigested vector transformed alone to check cell competence and antibiotic selection
  • Cell-only plate with antibiotic to confirm there is no growth
  • Optional positive control using a validated assembly kit insert set

Troubleshooting

ProblemLikely causeSolution
Few or no coloniesPoor overhang design, too little DNA, inactive ligase buffer or low-efficiency cellsCheck overhang fidelity, quantify DNA, use fresh buffer, add 45–65 cycles [1][4]
Many colonies, wrong or empty vectorUncut vector, incomplete BsaI digestion or internal BsaI sitesRe-check sequences for internal sites and use more enzyme mix or cycles [1][4]
Mis-assemblies with deleted regionsPrimer dimers or PCR-introduced errorsUse a proofreading polymerase and avoid over-cycling PCR; purify inserts [1][4]
Insert cut at an internal siteHidden recognition sequence or Dcm overlapDomesticate site; avoid CC(A/T)GG overlap upstream of GGTCTC [1][4]
Frameshifts in sequenceSingle-base run (for example AAAA) in a precloned partRe-sequence precloned inserts and replace the mutated part [4]
Low efficiency with many fragmentsAssembly efficiency falls as fragment number increasesUse 50 ng per precloned insert, more cycles, and 2 µL enzyme mix for more than 10 inserts [1][4]

Safety and waste

  • Ethidium bromide and other gel stains are mutagenic; wear gloves and use a safer stain where possible.
  • Heated blocks and 42 °C baths can burn; use caution when removing tubes.
  • Treat E. coli cultures as biohazardous waste: autoclave or treat with 10% bleach before disposal.
  • Antibiotic-containing media and X-gal are chemical waste; follow your institution's rules.
