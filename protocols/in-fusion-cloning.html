In-Fusion Cloning

2 days (about 4 h hands-on) · 2026-11-26

Seamless cloning with In-Fusion Snap Assembly: design 15 bp overlap primers, linearize the vector, mix at a 2:1 insert:vector ratio, incubate at 50 °C for 15 min, transform Stellar cells and verify clones.

⬇ Word (.docx)⬇ PowerPoint (.pptx)

Purpose

To insert one or more PCR-amplified DNA fragments into a linearized plasmid vector in a precise, seamless and directional manner, without ligase or restriction sites in the final construct, and to recover the clones in E. coli.

Principle

Primers add about 15 bp of sequence identical to the vector end (or to the neighboring fragment) to each fragment. The In-Fusion enzyme, a vaccinia virus DNA polymerase with 3′→5′ exonuclease activity, chews back 3′ ends to expose complementary single-stranded tails that anneal; nicks are repaired inside E. coli after transformation [1][2][3][4][5]. Efficiency is higher than 95% correct clones for single inserts [2].

Equipment

  • Thermocycler or 50 °C heat block
  • Water bath or heat block at 42 °C
  • 37 °C shaking incubator and plate incubator
  • Gel electrophoresis system and spectrophotometer
  • Ice bucket
  • Primer design tool at takarabio.com (optional)

Procedure

  1. Design primers: 15 bp 5′ overlap with the vector end (20 bp for 3+ fragments) plus an 18–25 nt gene-specific part with 40–60% GC and a Tm of 58–65 °C; forward and reverse within 4 °C [1][3]. (45 min)
  2. Linearize the vector by restriction digest (two non-overlapping sites at least 5 bases apart) or inverse PCR. Avoid over-digestion; follow the enzyme's recommended time [1][2][3]. (2 h)
  3. Run the digest on a gel, excise the band and purify. Use gel purification where possible. Measure concentration and keep purified DNA to 5 µL or less in the final reaction [1][3]. (1 h)
  4. Amplify each insert in a 25 µL PCR with a proofreading polymerase: 10 pg–1 ng plasmid or 100 pg–100 ng genomic DNA template; use the polymerase manufacturer's cycling conditions [1][3]. (1.5 h)
  5. Confirm one clean band on a gel. If the template was a plasmid, treat the product with DpnI (37 °C, 15–30 min) or gel-purify the insert to remove template carry-over [2][3]. (1 h)
  6. Calculate volumes for a 2:1 insert to vector molar ratio, with about 200 ng total DNA (50–200 ng vector). Use 2:2:1 for two inserts. Use a 3–5:1 ratio for fragments of 150–350 bp [2][3]. (10 min)
  7. On ice, assemble a 10 µL reaction: 2 µL 5× Snap Master Mix, vector, insert(s), nuclease-free water to 10 µL. If DNA exceeds 7 µL, double everything and make 20 µL [1][3]. (10 min)
  8. Set up controls: positive (1 µL pUC19 vector, 2 µL control insert, 2 µL master mix, water to 10 µL) and negative (linearized vector alone, no insert) [3]. (5 min)
  9. Mix gently by pipetting, spin briefly, and incubate at 50 °C for 15 min. Place the tubes on ice. Do not extend the incubation; longer times do not improve efficiency [1][2][3]. (20 min)
  10. Thaw Stellar cells on ice. Add 2.5 µL of each reaction (never more than 5 µL) to 50 µL of cells in a 14 mL tube. Mix by flicking; do not vortex [3]. (10 min)
  11. Keep on ice for 30 min, heat-shock at 42 °C for exactly 45 s, then return to ice for 1–2 min [3]. (35 min)
  12. Add 450 µL of pre-warmed SOC (total 500 µL) and shake at 160–225 rpm at 37 °C for 1 h [3]. (1 h)
  13. Plate 20 µL and 100 µL of the culture (each in 100 µL SOC) on selective LB plates; plate 1/5–1/3 for multi-fragment assemblies. Incubate at 37 °C overnight [1][3]. (overnight)
  14. Next day, count colonies on test, positive and negative plates. Pick 4–8 colonies per construct from the test plate and grow each overnight in LB with antibiotic at 37 °C. (30 min + overnight)
  15. Prepare plasmid DNA (miniprep) and verify by diagnostic restriction digest or PCR, then sequence across the insert junctions to confirm sequence and reading frame [1][3]. (3 h)

Pause points

  • Linearized, purified vector and insert fragments can be stored at −20 °C.
  • Completed assembly reactions can be stored at −20 °C until transformation [2].
  • Pick colonies after overnight growth; transformation plates can be kept briefly at 4 °C.

Expected results

  • The positive control gives several hundred colonies; the negative control gives few colonies, with fewer than 5% blue [2][3].
  • Single-insert cloning yields at least 95% correct clones with junction errors below 2% [2]; multi-insert assemblies are less efficient (about 4–10 of 10 clones correct from 2 to 5 inserts) [2].

Controls

  • Positive: pUC19 vector + 2 kb control insert
  • Negative: linearized vector without insert (hundreds of colonies indicate incomplete linearization)
  • Competent-cell check: transform a known plasmid to confirm efficiency above 10⁸ cfu/µg

Troubleshooting

ProblemLikely causeSolution
Few or no coloniesToo much reaction volume, low cell competency, low DNA, contaminants from gel purificationAdd 5 µL or less to cells, use ≥10⁸ cfu/µg cells, use 100–200 ng total DNA, re-purify [2][3]
Many colonies, no insertIncomplete vector linearization or template plasmid carry-overRe-digest with extra enzyme and gel purify; treat PCR product with DpnI [3]
Wrong insertsNon-specific PCR productGel purify the correct band or redesign primers [3]
Low efficiency for 3+ fragmentsOverlap too short or fragments poorly balancedUse 20 bp overlaps and more of the smallest fragment (at least 20 ng) [2][3]
Few white colonies after blue-white screeningOld plates or antibiotic, wrong strainUse fresh plates and a recA1 endA1 strain [2][3]

Safety and waste

  • E. coli K-12 strains are BSL-1: wear gloves and a lab coat and disinfect surfaces with 70% ethanol or 10% bleach.
  • Autoclave or bleach-treat cultures and plates before disposal.
  • Handle gel stains (for example ethidium bromide) as mutagens and use UV shielding.
  • Dispose of antibiotic-containing media and plates according to institutional rules.
