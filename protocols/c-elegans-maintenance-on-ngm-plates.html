C. elegans Maintenance on NGM Plates

Media 2 h + 2–3 days drying; routine transfer 15 min · 2026-11-15

Pour and seed NGM plates with OP50, keep C. elegans stocks by chunking or picking, and rescue contaminated cultures with a hypochlorite egg prep.

⬇ Word (.docx)⬇ PowerPoint (.pptx)

Purpose

To prepare nematode growth medium (NGM) plates seeded with E. coli OP50 and to maintain, transfer and rescue Caenorhabditis elegans stocks so that healthy, uncontaminated animals are available for experiments.

Principle

C. elegans is grown on a thin lawn of E. coli OP50 on NGM agar, a low-nutrient medium that keeps the bacterial lawn thin and the worms easy to see [1]. Cholesterol is added because worms cannot synthesize it [1]. Development is temperature dependent (16–25 °C), so plates can be held cool to slow growth or warm to speed it up [1]. Stocks are propagated by moving an agar chunk or by picking individual animals, and contaminated stocks are rescued by exploiting the resistance of embryos to hypochlorite [1][2].

Equipment

  • Autoclave
  • 55 °C water bath
  • Dissecting stereomicroscope
  • Bunsen burner or pick sterilizer
  • Incubator (15–25 °C)
  • 37 °C shaking incubator
  • Sterile spatula or scalpel
  • Airtight plate container

Procedure

  1. In a 2 L flask, mix 3 g NaCl, 2.5 g peptone, 17 g agar and 975 mL water. Cover the mouth with foil [1][3]. (15 min)
  2. Autoclave on a liquid cycle until the agar is fully molten; 121 °C for 20 min is typical, longer for large volumes [1][2]. (1 h)
  3. Cool the flask in a 55 °C water bath for about 15 min [1][2]. (15 min)
  4. Add, swirling after each: 1 mL cholesterol stock, 1 mL 1 M CaCl₂, 1 mL 1 M MgSO₄ and 25 mL 1 M potassium phosphate (pH 6.0) [1][2].
  5. Pour about two-thirds of each dish (roughly 7 mL for 60 mm), keeping the volume constant. Add optional drugs just before pouring [1][3]. (30 min)
  6. Leave plates lid-on at room temperature for 2–3 days to dry and reveal contamination. Discard any plate with growth [1]. (2–3 days)
  7. Streak OP50 on LB agar, inoculate LB broth from one colony and grow overnight at 37 °C. Store at 4 °C for later use [1][4]. (Overnight)
  8. Seed each 60 mm plate with about 50 µL of OP50, keeping the spot in the center, away from the edge [1].
  9. Let the lawn grow overnight at room temperature, or about 8 h at 37 °C. Cool to room temperature before adding worms [1]. (Overnight)
  10. To transfer by chunking, flame a spatula, cut a block of agar bearing worms and place it, worm side down, on a fresh seeded plate [1]. (5 min)
  11. To transfer by picking, flame the pick, touch a bacterial lawn, lift single worms and touch them to the new agar until they crawl off [1].
  12. Label the plate bottom with strain and date. Incubate at 20 °C (16 °C slows growth, 25 °C speeds it) [1]. (2–3 days)
  13. Re-transfer before the food runs out. Pick heterozygous stocks every 1–3 generations; chunk homozygous stocks [1].
  14. To rescue a contaminated stock, wash gravid adults off in water, add fresh hypochlorite–NaOH, vortex until bodies dissolve, then wash the embryos in M9 [1][2][4]. (20 min)
  15. Spread the embryos on the edge of a clean seeded plate. Next day, move hatched larvae to another clean seeded plate [1][2].

Pause points

  • After pouring: plates dry for 2–3 days at room temperature [1].
  • OP50 overnight culture can be stored at 4 °C for months [1].
  • Seeded plates keep for 2–3 weeks in an airtight container [1].
  • Embryos in M9 can arrest overnight as L1 larvae [2].

Expected results

  • A thin, even OP50 lawn with no mold or foreign colonies.
  • Worms move within minutes of transfer and produce progeny of all stages within about 2–3 days at 20 °C [2][4].
  • Embryos from a hypochlorite prep hatch overnight in M9 and arrest as L1 larvae [2].

Controls

  • Keep one unseeded NGM plate per batch as a sterility check
  • Keep a plate seeded with OP50 only (no worms) to confirm the lawn is clean
  • Maintain a wild-type N2 stock for comparison with mutant strains

Safety and waste

  • Hypochlorite and NaOH are corrosive: wear gloves and eye protection and mix the egg-prep solution fresh.
  • Cholesterol stock is in ethanol, which is flammable. Keep it away from the flame used to sterilize picks.
  • C. elegans and OP50 are generally handled at biosafety level 1; follow your institution's rules.
  • Discard used plates in a biohazard bag and autoclave them before disposal [1].
