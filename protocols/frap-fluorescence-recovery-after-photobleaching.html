FRAP (Fluorescence Recovery After Photobleaching)

≈ 3–4 h + analysis · 2026-11-26

Measure protein mobility in live mammalian cells: bleach an EGFP-fusion ROI on a confocal microscope, image recovery, correct and normalize, and fit mobile fraction and half-time.

⬇ Word (.docx)⬇ PowerPoint (.pptx)

Purpose

To measure how mobile a fluorescently tagged protein is in living mammalian cells: its mobile fraction, recovery half-time and, when the model fits, an apparent diffusion coefficient.

Principle

A small region of interest (ROI) in a cell expressing an EGFP fusion is bleached with a brief, intense laser pulse. Recovery of fluorescence reflects unbleached molecules moving in by diffusion, binding exchange or transport, and is followed with low-intensity imaging [1][2]. The mobile fraction is Mf = (F∞ − F₀)/(Fᵢ − F₀), where Fᵢ is the pre-bleach value, F₀ the first post-bleach value and F∞ the plateau; the immobile fraction is 1 − Mf. The half-time t½ is the time to reach half of the recovery; for a single exponential, t½ = ln 2/b [3]. For pure diffusion in a circular ROI, D ≈ (rₙ² + rₑ²)/(8 t½), with rₙ the nominal and rₑ the effective radius of the bleached spot [1]. Simple formulas can be off by 50% or more, so t½ and Mf are the safest primary readouts [2].

Materials and reagents

ItemDetails
Mammalian cellsFlat adherent cells such as COS-7, HeLa or HEK293 on 35 mm glass-bottom dishes, 50–70% confluent at transfection [1][2]
EGFP fusion plasmidProtein of interest tagged with EGFP (preferred over wild-type GFP or YFP); check that the tag does not change localization [1][2]
Transfection reagentLipofectamine 2000 or FuGENE HD, used per the supplier's manual about 24 h after plating [1]
Imaging mediumPhenol-red-free DMEM or RPMI with 10% FBS, 2 mM glutamine and 25 mM HEPES, pH 7.4; serum ≤ 20% to limit autofluorescence [1][2]
Fixed-cell control dishSame transfected cells fixed in 4% formaldehyde for 15 min, kept in PBS [2]
Immersion oilType matched to the objective, as supplied by the microscope maker

Equipment

  • Confocal microscope with 488 nm laser and bleach module
  • 40×/1.3 NA oil objective
  • Stage incubator, 37 °C and 5% CO₂
  • ImageJ/Fiji and easyFRAP [4]
  • CO₂ incubator and biosafety cabinet

Procedure

  1. Seed cells on glass-bottom dishes. About 24 h later transfect with the EGFP construct; image 16–48 h after transfection [1][2]. (day 1–3)
  2. Replace medium with pre-warmed phenol-red-free HEPES medium. Switch on the stage heater and lasers at least 5 min before imaging so the stage settles [1][2]. (15 min)
  3. Find a flat, healthy cell with low to moderate expression. Avoid very bright cells, which may aggregate or mislocalize [2]. (5 min)
  4. Set 512 × 512 pixels at about 4× zoom, imaging laser low (0.1–1% on an argon laser), and gain/pinhole so the brightest pixels are unsaturated. Keep settings fixed for the series [1][2]. (10 min)
  5. Draw a circular bleach ROI of about 2–3 µm for cytoplasm or membrane, plus an ROI over the whole cell and one over background outside the cell [1][2].
  6. Program the time series: at least 5 pre-bleach frames (about 1 frame/s), then bleach, then post-bleach frames until the curve reaches a plateau [1][2].
  7. Bleach with the 488 nm line at 100% transmission, starting with about 5 iterations. Test shorter or longer bleaching until the ROI loses 80–90% or more of its intensity [1][2].
  8. Image recovery at about 1 frame/s: 20–120 s for soluble or membrane proteins, longer (up to 5 min) for slow, nuclear or transported proteins [1][2]. (1–5 min)
  9. Do not refocus during a run. Discard series with drift, cell movement or structures crossing the ROI [2].
  10. Collect at least 12 cells per condition on at least 3 separate days; include a no-bleach series and the fixed-cell control run with identical settings [1][2]. (2–3 h)
  11. Export mean intensities of the bleach, whole-cell and background ROIs. Subtract background and correct for acquisition photobleaching with the whole-cell signal [2]. (30 min)
  12. Normalize (double normalization), fit a single exponential or, if needed, a double exponential, and report Mf and t½ as mean ± SD per condition [3][4]. (30–60 min)

Pause points

  • Cells can be kept in the incubator between sessions; imaging is best done within 48 h of transfection.
  • Exported intensity tables and raw images can be stored and analyzed later.

Expected results

  • A step-like loss of fluorescence in the ROI at bleaching, then exponential recovery to a plateau.
  • Soluble cytoplasmic proteins typically recover within seconds to about 20 s; membrane proteins plateau after about 1–2 min; nucleocytoplasmic transport can take about 5 min [1][2].
  • The fixed-cell control shows no recovery.

Controls

  • Whole-cell (unbleached) ROI in every frame to correct acquisition photobleaching and drift
  • Background ROI outside the cell for subtraction
  • No-bleach time series to check photofading
  • Fixed-cell control to confirm bleaching and separate photophysics from biology
  • Repeat bleach of the same ROI: D should be unchanged and Mf near 100%, otherwise suspect damage [2]

Troubleshooting

ProblemLikely causeSolution
Bleach too shallow (< 50%)Too few iterations or low laser powerAdd iterations, use 100% transmission, shrink the ROI or change the laser line [1][2]
Recovery too fast to resolveSmall ROI or slow frame rateCrop the frame to a strip and use bidirectional scanning; enlarge the ROI [1]
Curve rises then fallsFocal drift or cell movementEquilibrate the stage; do not refocus; discard the series [1][2]
Strong photofading in the reference cellImaging laser too highLower laser power, raise gain, adjust pinhole [1][2]
Cells round upPhototoxicity or poor environmentReduce exposure; refresh medium; check 37 °C and CO₂
Mobile fraction too low or fits poorBinding, several populations or transportUse a double exponential or a reaction model and report only t½ and Mf [2][3]

Safety and waste

  • Class 3B/4 lasers: never look into the beam and keep reflective objects off the stage; follow the facility's laser-safety training.
  • Formaldehyde is toxic and a suspected carcinogen: fix in a hood and collect waste separately.
  • Treat transfected cultures, plastics and media as biohazardous waste (bleach or autoclave) per institutional rules.
  • Clean oil objectives with lens tissue only.
