Magnetic Bead Cell Separation (MACS)

2–3 h · 2026-11-25

Purify mouse splenic B cells by CD43 depletion with MicroBeads on an LS column: MACS buffer, spleen prep, labeling, column loading, flow-through collection, positive-selection elution and purity checks.

⬇ Word (.docx)⬇ PowerPoint (.pptx)

Purpose

To purify a cell population from a mixed single-cell suspension, here mouse splenic B cells, using antibody-coupled superparamagnetic MicroBeads and a column held in a magnet.

Principle

Cells are incubated with antibodies conjugated to nanometer-sized magnetic beads. When the suspension is passed through a column of ferromagnetic matrix inside a magnet, bead-labeled cells are retained and unlabeled cells flow through. In negative selection (for example, depleting CD43⁺ cells to enrich resting B cells) the flow-through is the product, which leaves the target cells untouched. In positive selection the column is removed from the magnet and the retained, labeled cells are flushed out with a plunger [1][2][4]. Cold, EDTA-containing buffer without clumps and air bubbles keeps columns from clogging and prevents antibody capping [1][2]. Purity is checked by flow cytometry [1][3].

Equipment

  • Refrigerated centrifuge (4 °C, 400 × g)
  • Hemocytometer or automated cell counter
  • Magnetic separator with stand for LS columns
  • Flow cytometer
  • Ice bucket

Procedure

  1. Prepare MACS buffer: PBS with 0.5% BSA and 2 mM EDTA, pH 7.2–7.4. Filter at 0.22 µm, degas, and chill to 4 °C [1]. (20 min)
  2. Collect the spleen into cold PBS and press it between two frosted slides to release cells. Pass the suspension through a 70 µm mesh [1]. (15 min)
  3. Pellet at 400 × g for 3 min at 4 °C. Resuspend in 3 mL ACK lysis buffer and incubate 3 min at room temperature (never more than 4 min) [1]. (10 min)
  4. Quench with 7 mL cold PBS, pellet (400 × g, 3 min, 4 °C), resuspend in 10 mL cold PBS and count. Keep a small aliquot as the pre-sort sample for flow cytometry [1]. (10 min)
  5. Pellet the cells again. Resuspend 1 × 10⁷ cells in 100 µL MACS buffer, and scale up proportionally for more cells [1]. (5 min)
  6. Add 10 µL anti-CD43 MicroBeads per 1 × 10⁷ cells. Mix gently and incubate on ice for 10–15 min [1]. (15 min)
  7. Wash with 1 mL MACS buffer per 1 × 10⁷ cells and pellet at 400 × g for 3 min at 4 °C. Remove all supernatant [1]. (10 min)
  8. While the cells spin, place an LS column in the magnet with an uncapped 15 mL tube beneath. Equilibrate with 3 mL MACS buffer and let it drain completely [1]. (5 min)
  9. Resuspend the pellet in 500 µL MACS buffer, with no clumps. Load up to 1 × 10⁸ cells onto the column without introducing air [1]. (5 min)
  10. When the cells have entered the matrix, add 6 mL MACS buffer, then 3 mL more once the reservoir is empty. Collect all flow-through (about 9.5 mL): the unlabeled B cells [1]. (15 min)
  11. Pellet the flow-through at 400 × g for 3–5 min at 4 °C, resuspend in cold PBS or medium, and count [1]. (15 min)
  12. Check purity: stain the pre-sort and post-sort aliquots for B220 and CD43 with Fc block, with a viability dye, and acquire at least 20,000 events [1][3]. (45 min)
  13. For positive selection, wash the column with buffer, remove it from the magnet over a collection tube, add 5 mL buffer and push it through with the plunger [2][4]. (10 min)
  14. For higher purity, pass the eluted cells over a freshly equilibrated second column [1]. (20 min)

Pause points

  • MACS buffer can be prepared ahead and stored at 4 °C for up to 3 months [1].
  • After the final count, keep the purified cells on ice or in culture medium until the downstream assay.

Expected results

  • Negative selection of B cells (CD43 depletion) from a mouse spleen: purity about 95–98% B220⁺ cells and a yield of about 15–35 × 10⁶ cells per spleen [1].
  • Directly from whole blood on a MACS instrument, typical median purities were about 97.5% for T cells, 99% for granulocytes and 89% for B cells, with recoveries of 58–78% [3].
  • A column-based negative-selection mouse CD4⁺ T cell kit reached 95.7% purity with 85% yield [2].

Controls

  • Unseparated (pre-sort) aliquot stained identically to measure the starting frequency [1][3]
  • Unstained and isotype or fluorescence-minus-one controls for flow cytometry
  • Viability dye to exclude dead cells from the purity calculation [3]
  • A fraction of the retained cells (column eluate in a negative selection) to confirm that depletion removed the unwanted cells [1]

Troubleshooting

ProblemLikely causeSolution
Column clogs or flows slowlyClumps, DNA from dead cells or air bubblesFilter cells through 30 µm mesh; degas buffer; avoid bubbles; keep everything cold [1][2]
Low purityInsufficient depletion or overloaded columnDo not exceed 1 × 10⁸ labeled cells per LS column; check CD43 depletion; run a second column [1]
Low yield or viabilityLong lysis or rough handlingKeep ACK lysis to 4 min or less; dissociate gently; work on ice [1]
Weak labelingToo few beads or cells at high densityTitrate beads (suppliers state lot-specific amounts); keep 1 × 10⁷ cells per 100 µL [1][2]
Target epitope blockedThe MicroBead antibody blocks the antibody used in flowUse a non-competing antibody for purity checks; for example CD20 when CD19 beads are used [3]

Safety and waste

  • Keep pacemakers, implants and magnetically sensitive items at least 20 cm from the magnet [5].
  • Use animal tissue in accordance with approved protocols and treat cell waste as biohazardous: inactivate with 10% bleach or autoclave.
  • Sodium azide in staining buffer is toxic and can form explosive metal azides in drains; dispose of it as chemical waste.
