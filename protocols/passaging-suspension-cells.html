Passaging Suspension Cells

30–45 min per passage · 2026-11-18

Subculture non-adherent mammalian cells such as Jurkat in log phase: count with trypan blue, calculate the carry-over volume, seed at a defined density by dilution or centrifugation, and feed or split every 2–3 days.

⬇ Word (.docx)⬇ PowerPoint (.pptx)

Purpose

To subculture non-adherent mammalian cells (for example Jurkat T cells) so they stay in exponential growth with high viability, using a count-based seeding density instead of a fixed guess.

Principle

Suspension cells have no monolayer to detach, so they are passaged by diluting the culture, or by pelleting and resuspending it, to a defined seeding density. Cells must be subcultured in log phase: at high density they clump, the medium turns acidic and turbid, and viability falls [1][4]. Counting before every split lets you calculate the exact volume to carry over and keeps growth reproducible. Typical Jurkat cultures are kept between about 1 × 10⁵ and 3 × 10⁶ cells/mL and fed or split every 2–3 days [3][4]. The values below are a starting point; always follow the datasheet for your specific line.

Equipment

  • Class II biosafety cabinet
  • CO₂ incubator (37 °C, 5% CO₂, humidified)
  • Inverted phase-contrast microscope
  • Centrifuge with swinging-bucket rotor
  • 37 °C water bath or bead bath
  • Tally counter
  • Pipette controller and micropipettes

Procedure

  1. Pre-warm complete medium to 37 °C and wipe the hood, flask and tube racks with 70% ethanol. Label the new flask with line, passage number, date and your initials. (10 min)
  2. Check the culture on the inverted microscope. Cells should be round, bright and mostly single; note clumps, debris, or a cloudy or yellow medium [1][4].
  3. Resuspend the culture by gently pipetting up and down 3–5 times, or swirl the flask, so clumps break up and the sample is representative.
  4. Remove 100 µL, mix 1:1 with 0.4% trypan blue, load a hemocytometer and count cells in the four corner squares and the center square [4]. (5 min)
  5. Calculate density: cells/mL = mean cells per square × dilution factor (2 for a 1:1 mix) × 10⁴. Viability = unstained cells ÷ total cells × 100 [4].
  6. Calculate the transfer volume: V(transfer) = (target density × final volume) ÷ current density. Example: 3 × 10⁵ cells/mL in 10 mL from 1.5 × 10⁶ cells/mL needs 2 mL.
  7. Dilution method: for a healthy culture, add the calculated volume to a new flask and top up with pre-warmed medium to the final volume. This keeps the cells undisturbed [1][4].
  8. Pellet method: transfer the cells to a 15 mL tube and spin at 200–300 × g for 5 min. The Jurkat SOP uses 500 × g; use the speed that suits your line [2][3][4]. (5–10 min)
  9. Remove the supernatant carefully, leaving the pellet intact, and resuspend gently in fresh warm medium; avoid foaming or bubbles [2].
  10. Seed the new flask at the target density (for example 2–3 × 10⁵ cells/mL for Jurkat), using about 5–10 mL in a T-25 or 15–30 mL in a T-75 [3][4].
  11. Return flasks to the incubator at 37 °C with 5% CO₂ and loosened or vented caps; lay flasks flat so the liquid forms a shallow layer.
  12. Re-count and feed or split every 2–3 days, and record counts, viability and passage number in a culture log [3][4]. (2–3 days)

Pause points

  • After step 6 the calculated cultures can wait in the incubator for a few minutes while you prepare flasks.
  • After step 12 the cells can be left in the incubator until the next feed or split in 2–3 days.

Expected results

  • Viability above 90% at each passage for a healthy, log-phase line.
  • Steady growth with a consistent doubling time over several passages.
  • A round, single-cell morphology with little debris and a clear, pink medium.

Controls

  • Keep a low-passage frozen stock of the line so cultures can be replaced when they drift
  • Include a flask of medium only (no cells) in the incubator to confirm sterility
  • Count the same sample twice, or with two counters, if a result looks inconsistent
  • Test routinely for mycoplasma, which cannot be seen by eye

Troubleshooting

ProblemLikely causeSolution
Cells clumpOvergrowth, dead-cell DNA release or rough pipettingSplit earlier, pipette gently, and remove debris with a low-speed spin
Low viability after a splitSeeding too sparsely, harsh centrifugation, old mediumSeed at a higher density, lower the spin speed, use fresh medium
Slow growthWrong serum or medium lot, high passage number, mycoplasmaTest the medium lot, thaw a new vial, and test for mycoplasma
Medium turns yellow quicklyDensity too high or acid buildupSplit more often and keep cultures below the recommended density
Turbid medium overnightBacterial or fungal contaminationDiscard the flask, disinfect the incubator and hood, and check reagents

Safety and waste

  • Treat all human and primate cell lines as potentially infectious; work in a biosafety cabinet and follow your biosafety committee rules.
  • Disinfect liquid waste with a suitable agent (for example 10% bleach for 20–30 min) before disposal, and place tips and flasks in biohazard waste.
  • Trypan blue is a suspected carcinogen: wear gloves and discard it as chemical waste according to local rules.
