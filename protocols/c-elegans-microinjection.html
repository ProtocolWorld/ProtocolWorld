C. elegans Microinjection

About 3 h (plus 3–5 days for F1 screening) · 2026-11-21

Introduce plasmid DNA into the C. elegans distal gonad by microinjection to generate extrachromosomal-array transgenic lines, with a rol-6 or fluorescent co-injection marker, F1 screening and line maintenance.

⬇ Word (.docx)⬇ PowerPoint (.pptx)

Purpose

To introduce DNA into the C. elegans germline by gonad microinjection so that transgenic lines carrying extrachromosomal arrays can be recovered, screened with a visible co-injection marker and maintained.

Principle

The adult hermaphrodite gonad is a syncytium, so DNA delivered into the cytoplasmic core of the distal gonad is packaged into maturing oocytes. Injected plasmids recombine into large extrachromosomal arrays that are transmitted to a fraction of the progeny and can be followed with a dominant marker such as rol-6(su1006) (rolling) or a pharyngeal fluorescent protein [1][2]. Success depends on clean DNA, a clear needle, young adults that are well mounted under oil, and fast injection before the animals desiccate [1][4].

Materials and reagents

Equipment

  • Inverted or upright microscope with DIC (Nomarski) optics and 10× and 40× objectives
  • Micromanipulator and pressure injector (for example FemtoJet or Tritech MINJ-1)
  • Needle puller (optional if using pre-pulled needles)
  • Microcentrifuge capable of 12,000 × g at 4 °C
  • Dissecting stereomicroscope
  • Heat plate (80–100 °C) or oven for drying pads
  • 20 °C and 15 °C incubators

Procedure

  1. One day ahead, pick about 40 L4 hermaphrodites onto fresh OP50 plates and grow them overnight at 20 °C so that they become young gravid adults [1]. (Day −1)
  2. Purify plasmids with a column-based kit, elute in water or 1× TE and measure the concentration; avoid boiling-lysis and phenol–chloroform preparations [1]. (30 min)
  3. Prepare the mix: marker plus gene-of-interest plasmid, topped up with carrier DNA to 100–150 ng/µL total. Above 200 ng/µL blocks needles; below 100 ng/µL gives unstable arrays [1][4]. (15 min)
  4. Spin the mix at maximum speed (about 12,000 × g) for 15–30 min at 4 °C, then use only the top 5–15 µL to avoid particles. Freeze the remainder at −20 °C [1][4]. (15–30 min)
  5. Prepare 2% agarose pads: place about 50–100 µL of molten agarose between a slide and a coverslip, then dry on a 100 °C plate for about 1 min (or 15 min at 80 °C) [1][4]. (15 min)
  6. Load 1 µL of cleared mix into a needle with a microloader tip, check for air bubbles and mount the needle on the micromanipulator [1][4]. (10 min)
  7. Move the worm plate to 15 °C for 1 h. Meanwhile, set the injector to deliver about 10 pL per pulse [1]. (1 h)
  8. Put the needle tip in a drop of oil on the pad. Break it open by touching the pad edge or a thin glass fragment at 20×, then confirm that pressing Inject expels a visible droplet [1][4]. (5 min)
  9. Put about 50 µL of halocarbon oil on a pad edge, move 10–20 adults into it for 5–10 min, then pick one onto the dry pad and press it gently flat with a hairpin pick [1]. (10 min)
  10. Locate the worm at 10×, orient it at 20–45° to the needle, then switch to 40× DIC and focus on the cytoplasmic core of the distal gonad arm [1][4]. (2 min)
  11. Move the worm onto the needle, not the needle into the worm. Press Inject until the gonad visibly swells and fluid flows. Injecting each arm gives the best yield [1][2]. (1–2 min per worm)
  12. Within 10–15 min of mounting, add 10–20 µL M9 to the worm, wait about 2 min until it moves, and transfer it to a seeded plate. Inject 20–25 animals per session [1][4]. (3 min)
  13. Incubate the plates at 20 °C for 3–5 days. Score the F1 progeny for the co-injection marker (rolling or pharyngeal fluorescence) [1][4]. (3–5 days)
  14. Pick single marker-positive F1 animals to individual plates and check their F2 progeny for stable transmission of the array [1][4]. (Days 5–9)
  15. Keep at least three independent lines per construct. Pass 10–30 transgenic animals every 3–5 days; transmission of arrays varies widely (about 5–80%) [1][2]. (Ongoing)

Pause points

  • The injection mix can be spun and stored at −20 °C for later use [1].
  • Dried agarose pads can be prepared in advance and stored at room temperature in a closed box [1][4].
  • Pre-pulled or home-pulled needles can be stored in a closed dust-free box; use within about 2 weeks [4].
  • After injection, plates can be left at 20 °C for 3–5 days before screening [1].

Expected results

  • A successful injection swells the gonad and releases a clear wave of liquid along the gonad arm.
  • With a rol-6(su1006) co-marker, F1 rollers typically appear 3–5 days after injection; 20–50 roller progeny per injected animal were reported for optimized pRF4 mixes [2].
  • Only a minority of transgenic F1 animals (about 5–10%) pass the array stably; arrays are transmitted non-Mendelian and mosaic, from about 5% to 80% of progeny [1].
  • Injection efficiency is usually in the range of 1–10% or higher of injected animals giving transgenic F1 progeny [1].

Controls

  • Co-injection marker alone (for example pRF4) to show that injection and screening work
  • Injection of a mix without the gene of interest, to distinguish marker effects from transgene effects
  • Wild-type uninjected siblings kept under the same conditions for phenotype comparison
  • At least three independent lines per construct to control for position-independent array variability

Troubleshooting

ProblemLikely causeSolution
Needle clogged or no liquid expelledParticles in the mix or a closed tipRe-spin the mix for 15–30 min at 4 °C, break the needle again on the pad edge, or use a fresh needle [1][4]
Worms die after injectionNeedle too large, too much pressure or worms dried outUse a smaller tip, lower the pressure, inject within 10–15 min and recover in M9 promptly [1]
No transgenic F1 progenyDNA too dilute, poor injection or low-quality DNAUse 100–150 ng/µL total DNA from column-purified plasmids and target the distal gonad core [1][4]
Arrays lost within a few generationsNormal array instability or toxic transgenePass 10–30 animals every 3–5 days and lower the plasmid concentration if the transgene is toxic [1]
Gonad does not swellTip too closed or the worm movedReopen the tip, reposition the worm on a dry area of the pad and re-inject [1][4]

Safety and waste

  • Hydrofluoric acid used to etch or open needles causes severe burns that may be delayed. Use it only with goggles, lab coat, gloves, small volumes and an approved spill procedure, or use pre-pulled needles instead [5].
  • Broken glass needles are sharp: dispose of them in a sharps container.
  • Halocarbon oil and agarose waste go into your institution's chemical or solid waste streams as directed.
  • Handle transgenic worms according to your institutional biosafety rules and autoclave or bleach used plates before disposal.
