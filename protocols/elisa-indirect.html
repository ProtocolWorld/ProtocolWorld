ELISA (Indirect)

Estimated time: Overnight coat + 5–6 h | Protocol World

To detect and quantify an antigen, or an antibody against a coated antigen, in mammalian-derived samples such as serum, cell lysate or culture medium using an indirect enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay.

The antigen is adsorbed to a high-protein-binding microplate, remaining sites are blocked, and a primary antibody binds the target. An enzyme-labeled secondary antibody (here HRP) binds the primary antibody, so several enzyme molecules amplify the signal [1][2][4]. HRP converts the substrate TMB to a blue product that turns yellow when acid is added, and absorbance at 450 nm reflects the amount of bound antibody [1][2]. Washing between steps removes unbound reagents and keeps background low [5][6].

ItemDetails
Coating bufferCarbonate–bicarbonate, pH 9.6: 1.5 g Na₂CO₃ and 2.93 g NaHCO₃ per liter [1]; or antigen in PBS [3]
AntigenPurified, 1–10 µg/mL in coating buffer, 100 µL per well; empirically optimized for lysates [1][2]
96-well plateHigh protein-binding ELISA plate (not tissue culture treated) [2][5]
Wash buffer (PBS-T)PBS with 0.05% Tween-20 [1][3]
Blocking buffer1% BSA in PBS (150–200 µL per well) [1][2]
Primary antibodyDiluted in blocking or wash buffer; titrate; 100 µL per well [1][2][4]
HRP-conjugated secondary antibodyAnti-species, titrated per the datasheet; 100 µL per well [1][2][4]
TMB substrateReady-to-use, 100 µL per well, protected from light [1][2]
Stop solutionSulfuric acid, 50 µL per well of 0.2 M [1]; adjust the acid to the kit volume
Samples and standardsSerum clotted 20 min, spun, aliquoted and stored at −80 °C; lysate spun at 18,000 × g for 20 min at 4 °C [4]
  • Microplate reader (450 nm)
  • Multichannel pipette
  • Plate washer or wash bottle
  • 37 °C incubator or room-temperature bench
  • Plate sealer or lid
  • Refrigerator (4 °C)
  • Microcentrifuge
  • Dilute antigen to 1–10 µg/mL in carbonate coating buffer (pH 9.6) and add 100 µL to each well [1][2]. (15 min)
  • Cover the plate and incubate overnight at 4 °C; a 1–2 h incubation at room temperature also works [1][4]. (Overnight)
  • Empty the wells and wash 3 times with PBS-T, filling each well fully; tap the plate dry on paper [1][3][5]. (10 min)
  • Add 150–200 µL of blocking buffer (1% BSA in PBS) per well and incubate 1 h at 37 °C or 1–2 h at room temperature [1][2][4]. (1–2 h)
  • Wash 3 times with PBS-T [1][3].
  • Add 100 µL of primary antibody or diluted serum in duplicate or triplicate, with blanks and controls. Incubate 1 h at 37 °C or 2 h at room temperature [1][2][4]. (1–2 h)
  • Wash 3 times with PBS-T, using a short soak of about 30 s per wash to lower background [3][5].
  • Add 100 µL of HRP-conjugated secondary antibody at its titrated dilution; incubate 1–2 h at room temperature [1][2][4]. (1–2 h)
  • Wash 3–5 times with PBS-T. Do not use sodium azide in any buffer, because it inhibits HRP [2][6]. (10 min)
  • Add 100 µL of TMB to each well and develop in the dark for about 15–30 min, until positive wells are clearly blue [1][2]. (15–30 min)
  • Add 50 µL of stop solution (0.2 M H₂SO₄) per well. Wells turn yellow [1].
  • Read absorbance at 450 nm within 30 min of stopping [1]. Subtract the blank [4]. (5 min)
  • Plot the standard curve (concentration against blank-corrected signal) and calculate sample values [4].
  • Coated plates can wait overnight at 4 °C [1][4].
  • Primary antibody incubation can be done overnight at 4 °C [4].
  • Do not pause after adding TMB; read within 30 min of the stop [1].
  • Positive wells develop blue color, which turns yellow after the acid stop.
  • Blank and negative-control wells stay near background.
  • Duplicate wells should agree closely; intra-assay CV below about 10% and standard-curve R² above 0.99 are common targets [4].
  • Blank wells (no antigen or sample) to subtract background [4]
  • Negative control: species- and isotype-matched non-specific immunoglobulin as the primary [3]
  • Secondary-antibody-only wells to detect non-specific binding
  • Positive control: a known-positive serum or antibody
  • Standards and samples in duplicate or triplicate [2]
ProblemLikely causeSolution
High backgroundPoor washing, weak blocking, antibody too concentrated, plate left after stopWash longer, strengthen the block, dilute reagents, read promptly [5][6]
Low or no signalAntigen not bound, antibody too dilute, azide present, wrong plateUse a binding plate, titrate antibodies, wash out azide [5][6]
Edge effect or driftUneven temperature, evaporation, stacked platesSeal plates, bring reagents to room temperature, do not stack [5]
Poor duplicatesBubbles, uneven coating or washing, pipetting errorMix reagents, remove bubbles, calibrate pipettes [5]
Run-to-run variationDegraded reagents, freeze–thaw of samplesMake buffers fresh; avoid repeated freeze–thaw [5]
  • Sulfuric acid stop solution is corrosive: wear gloves, a lab coat and eye protection, and add acid to water when diluting.
  • Treat serum and lysates as potentially infectious and handle them under your institution's biosafety rules.
  • Sodium azide is toxic; keep it out of HRP buffers.
  • Collect acid, biohazardous and sharps waste separately and dispose of it as local rules require.
