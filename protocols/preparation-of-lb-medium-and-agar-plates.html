Preparation of LB Medium and Agar Plates

2 hours plus overnight drying · 2026-11-14

Mixing, autoclaving and pouring Lysogeny Broth (Lennox) and LB agar plates, with antibiotic addition at about 50 °C.

⬇ Word (.docx)⬇ PowerPoint (.pptx)

Purpose

To prepare sterile LB liquid medium and LB agar plates (with or without antibiotic) for routine culture of E. coli and plasmid selection.

Principle

LB supplies peptides and amino acids (tryptone), vitamins and trace nutrients (yeast extract) and NaCl for osmotic balance. Autoclaving sterilizes the medium; agar solidifies it. Heat-labile antibiotics are added only after the medium has cooled to about 50 °C [1][3].

Materials and Reagents

Reagent / itemConcentration / details
Tryptone10 g/L [3]
Yeast extract5 g/L [3]
NaCl5 g/L for LB Lennox (the Miller formulation uses 10 g/L; one vendor powder at 25 g/L is used in a CSH recipe) [2][3]
Agar (Bacto)15 g/L for plates [1][2][3]
pHAbout 6.7 adjusted with NaOH in one recipe [3]; many labs aim for 7.0
Ampicillin stock100 mg/mL in water; 0.5 mL per liter gives 50 µg/mL [1]
WaterDeionized or ultrapure

Equipment

  • Autoclave
  • Autoclavable bottle or flask (fill to 50–60% volume)
  • Magnetic stirrer
  • pH meter
  • 50 °C water bath
  • Sterile 10 cm Petri dishes (20–25 mL per plate)
  • Bunsen burner or laminar flow hood

Procedure

A. LB liquid medium (1 L, Lennox)

  • Dissolve 10 g tryptone, 5 g yeast extract and 5 g NaCl in about 800 mL water; stir until clear [3].
  • Adjust pH (about 6.7–7.0) with NaOH and bring to 1 L [3].
  • Autoclave at 121 °C, 15 psi for 15 min on a liquid cycle [2][3].
  • Cool to about 50 °C or room temperature; add antibiotic only after cooling [1][3]. Store at room temperature for general use or 4 °C if antibiotic-containing.

B. LB agar (1 L)

  • Prepare as above and add 15 g agar per liter before autoclaving [1][3]. (A powdered LB mix at 25 g/L plus 15 g/L agar is an accepted alternative [2].)
  • Autoclave 15 min at 121 °C, 15 psi [2].
  • Cool in a 50 °C water bath, with gentle swirling to avoid bubbles [1][2].

C. Add antibiotic and pour

  • At about 50 °C add antibiotic, for example 0.5 mL of 100 mg/mL ampicillin per liter for 50 µg/mL final; mix by swirling [1].
  • Pour 20–25 mL per 10 cm dish in a clean hood or near a flame [2].
  • Flame the surface briefly to pop bubbles, then let plates solidify at room temperature [2].

D. Dry, label and store

  • Dry plates (lids ajar in a hood or overnight at room temperature) to remove surface moisture.
  • Label with medium, antibiotic and date. Invert and store at 4 °C; plates without antibiotic keep for months (up to 3 months at 4 °C in one recipe) [2].

Expected Results

  • Clear, pale-yellow broth and firm, smooth, bubble-free plates.
  • Sterile after incubating one plate overnight at 37 °C (no growth).
  • Plates with antibiotic select for resistant transformants; a plate without antibiotic supports dense E. coli lawns.

Troubleshooting

ProblemPossible causeSolution
Agar solidifies in the bottle before pouringCooled too longRe-melt briefly, then cool again to 50 °C [3]
Antibiotic ineffectiveAdded to hot agar or old stockAdd only at about 50 °C; use fresh stock [1]
Bubbles on platesVigorous swirling or pouringSwirl gently; flame the surface [2]
ContaminationNon-sterile technique or short autoclaveAutoclave 15 min at 121 °C; pour near a flame or in a hood; discard contaminated plates [2]
Soft or wet platesToo little agar or insufficient dryingCheck agar at 15 g/L and dry plates before storage [1][2]

Safety Notes

  • Hot media and autoclave steam cause burns; use heat-resistant gloves and loosen caps before autoclaving.
  • Agar can boil over when re-melted; heat in short bursts.
  • Handle antibiotic powders in a hood and dispose of used plates as biohazard waste (standard laboratory practice).
