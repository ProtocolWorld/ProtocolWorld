Verification of Clones by Restriction Digest

≈ 2.5 h (plus overnight cultures) · 2026-11-27

Confirm cloning success with an analytical restriction digest: predict fragment sizes, set up 20 µL digests with an uncut control, run a 1% agarose gel, interpret supercoiled versus digested lanes, check orientation, and follow with sequencing.

⬇ Word (.docx)⬇ PowerPoint (.pptx)

Purpose

To confirm that a plasmid recovered from E. coli colonies contains the intended insert, with the right size and orientation, before sequencing or downstream use.

Principle

Restriction enzymes cut DNA at defined sequences, so a correct plasmid gives a predictable set of fragments on an agarose gel [3][4]. Enzymes that flank the insert release it from the backbone; an enzyme that cuts asymmetrically inside the insert and once in the backbone gives different fragment sizes for the two orientations [3]. Comparing digested lanes with an uncut lane and a ladder shows whether the enzymes worked and whether the pattern matches the map [4]. A clean digest is strong evidence but not proof, so keep a record and confirm key constructs by Sanger sequencing [7].

Materials and reagents

ItemDetails
Plasmid miniprepsFrom 3–6 colonies per construct, grown 12–16 h at 37 °C in 5 mL LB with the correct antibiotic [7]; keep a patch plate or glycerol stock of each colony

Equipment

  • 37 °C heat block or water bath
  • Microcentrifuge
  • Gel electrophoresis system and power supply
  • Gel imager
  • Spectrophotometer or Qubit for DNA concentration
  • Computer with sequence-analysis software (e.g., SnapGene, Benchling, NEBcutter, or Addgene Sequence Analyzer)

Procedure

  1. Predict fragment sizes first: digest the construct map in silico with your chosen enzyme(s) and also the empty vector. Check the sites are unique and distinct [3][4]. (15 min)
  2. Check buffer, temperature and methylation sensitivity with NEBcloner or Double Digest Finder; avoid dam/dcm-blocked sites unless the DNA is from a dam–/dcm– strain [4][6]. (10 min)
  3. Quantify the miniprep DNA. Plan about 200–500 ng per digest; too much DNA smears bands [4][6]. (10 min)
  4. Make a master mix per set of digests: for one 20 µL reaction, 2 µL 10× buffer, 0.5 µL of each enzyme (≈ 5 U each for 10 U/µL stocks) and water; keep enzyme volume ≤ 10% of the total [1][2]. (10 min)
  5. Add about 300 ng plasmid to each tube, then the master mix; add enzyme last. Mix by flicking, give a brief spin; do not vortex [1][2]. (10 min)
  6. Prepare an uncut control: the same DNA and buffer in the same volume without enzyme [4]. (2 min)
  7. Incubate at the enzyme's temperature, usually 37 °C, for 1 h (5–15 min with Time-Saver enzymes) [1][5]. (1 h)
  8. Add 4 µL 6× loading dye to each 20 µL reaction (or 10 µL stop solution per 50 µL) [1]. (2 min)
  9. Cast a 1% agarose gel in 1× TAE with stain. Load 5 µL of the ladder and the whole digest (about 24 µL) with the uncut lane alongside [4][5]. (20 min)
  10. Run at about 100 V for 45 min, or at 80–150 V until the dye is 75–80% down the gel [4][5]. (45 min)
  11. Image the gel and compare each lane with the predicted sizes. Supercoiled uncut plasmid runs fastest and also shows relaxed and nicked forms [4]. (10 min)
  12. Pick a clone whose fragments match the prediction. For orientation, check the asymmetric enzyme pattern against both orientations [3]. (10 min)
  13. Confirm the chosen clone by Sanger sequencing, covering the full insert and junctions, and store a glycerol stock [7]. (1–2 days)

Pause points

  • Overnight cultures and glycerol stocks can be kept before screening.
  • Digests can be stored at 4 °C overnight after adding loading dye, then run the next day.

Expected results

  • Digested lanes show the predicted fragments, for example a 7.3 kb plasmid with a 1.2 kb insert cut with two enzymes gives ≈ 6 kb and 1.2 kb [4].
  • The uncut lane shows supercoiled, relaxed and nicked forms, and differs clearly from the digested lanes [4].
  • The empty-vector digest gives a smaller band than clones that contain the insert.

Controls

  • Uncut plasmid (confirms the enzymes cut and gives migration reference) [4]
  • Empty vector digest in parallel
  • Single-enzyme lanes to verify each site
  • DNA ladder for sizing
  • Optional: lambda DNA with several known sites to test enzyme activity if all digests fail [2]

Troubleshooting

ProblemLikely causeSolution
Digest lane looks like the uncut laneEnzyme inactive, wrong buffer or inhibitor in the DNACheck enzyme and buffer; test with control DNA; clean up the miniprep with a column [2][4]
Extra or unexpected bandsStar activity (enzyme too high or too much glycerol)Keep enzyme ≤ 10% of the volume; use fewer units or High-Fidelity enzymes [2][4]
Partial digestToo little enzyme, short incubation or methylation blockExtend to 1–2 h; use more units per µg; use dam–/dcm– DNA if the site is blocked [2][4]
Smeared lanesToo much DNA or RNA carry-overLoad less DNA; add RNase A; quantify the miniprep [4]
Fragments too small to seeFragments below about 500 bp are faintChoose enzymes that give larger fragments or use a higher-percentage gel [5]

Safety and waste

  • Ethidium bromide and other DNA stains are hazardous: wear gloves and dispose of gels and buffer per local rules.
  • Wear UV protection when using a transilluminator.
  • Restriction enzymes are stored at −20 °C; keep on ice and minimize time out of the freezer [1].
  • Dispose of bacterial cultures after bleach treatment or autoclaving.
