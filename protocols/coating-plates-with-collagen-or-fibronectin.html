Coating Plates with Collagen or Fibronectin

Estimated time: 2–3 h (collagen) or 1.5 h (fibronectin) | Protocol World

To prepare evenly coated, sterile culture surfaces that support attachment and spreading of fastidious or weakly adherent mammalian cells.

Extracellular matrix proteins adsorb to plastic or glass from dilute solution and form a thin film that cell integrins can bind. Collagen I is kept soluble in dilute acetic acid and used at about 5–10 µg/cm² [1][2][4][6]. Fibronectin is used at about 1–5 µg/cm² in sterile water or a neutral buffered salt solution [3][6]. Coating density and time affect cell spreading, so both should be optimized per cell type [3][4]. Neutral pH or warm temperatures turn collagen into a gel, and drying out damages fibronectin films [3][6].

  • Biosafety cabinet with UV lamp
  • Micropipettes and sterile low-binding tips
  • Aspirator with sterile tips
  • Refrigerator (2–8 °C) and −20 °C freezer
  • CO₂ incubator (37 °C, 5% CO₂)
  • Cell counter or hemocytometer
  • Choose the protein and a starting dose: collagen I 5–10 µg/cm² or fibronectin 1–5 µg/cm², then plan to optimize for your cells [1][3][4][6]. (5 min)
  • Calculate the volume that just covers the surface (about 1 mL per 6-well well, 250–300 µL per 24-well well, 50 µL per 96-well well, 1 mL per 35 mm dish); these are area-based estimates. (5 min)
  • Collagen: dilute stock in cold sterile 0.02 N acetic acid, not in PBS or medium, so it stays liquid; mix gently [2][4][6]. (5 min)
  • Fibronectin: thaw or dissolve per the product sheet (lyophilized powder about 30 min at 37 °C), without vortexing, then dilute in the sterile diluent the sheet names (water, HBSS or PBS) [3][6].
  • Work in the biosafety cabinet and add the solution to each vessel, tilting to cover the whole surface; avoid bubbles [1][3].
  • For coverslips, place them in a well first and flood with enough solution to cover them without floating.
  • Collagen I: incubate 1 h at room temperature, or several hours at 37 °C, or overnight at 2–8 °C [2][5][6]. (1 h–overnight)
  • Fibronectin: incubate for about 1 h at room temperature, as for ibidi labware [6]; Sigma gives no time, so optimize [3]. (45–60 min)
  • Aspirate the excess solution without touching the surface; unused sterile fibronectin solution can coat another plate and keeps up to 1 week at 2–8 °C [3].
  • Collagen: rinse 2–3 times with PBS (about 2–3× the coating volume) or once with medium to remove residual acid [2][4][6]. (5 min)
  • Fibronectin: air dry in the cabinet for at least 45 min at room temperature if drying is desired; do not over-dry [3][6]. (45 min)
  • Check sterility: stock solutions are sterile, so aseptic handling is enough; otherwise UV the dried surface or rinse with 70% ethanol and then PBS [5].
  • Optional UV step: expose the open dried plate to UV in the closed hood (up to overnight for collagen) and then rinse once with sterile water or PBS [5].
  • Do not filter-sterilize collagen; the protein is lost on membranes [5].
  • Use immediately, or seal plates and store at 2–8 °C: 2–4 weeks for fibronectin [3]; collagen plates keep at 2–8 °C but validate the period [4].
  • Add pre-warmed medium to the coated wells before cells, to avoid drying, then seed at the density normal for the cell line [3][6]. (5 min)
  • Incubate at 37 °C, 5% CO₂ and check attachment and spreading under the microscope after 1–4 h and again at 24 h. (1–24 h)
  • After step 7: collagen can stay at 2–8 °C overnight.
  • After step 14: sealed coated plates can be stored at 2–8 °C until use.
  • An even, invisible thin film; wells wet uniformly with no dried patches.
  • Cells attach and spread within hours and form a uniform monolayer compared with uncoated controls.
  • Uncoated well of the same plate type as a negative control
  • Cell-free coated well in medium to check for contamination
  • Test two or three coating densities when using a new cell type
ProblemLikely causeSolution
Uneven coatingToo little volume, tilted plate, bubbles, or surface not fully wettedUse enough volume to cover the surface, tap gently to remove bubbles, and keep plates level
Collagen forms a gel or fibersDiluted in PBS or medium, pH above 5, or warmed before dilutionDilute in cold 0.02 N acetic acid and keep it cold until use; gel forms at about pH 6.5–7.5 [2]
Poor cell attachmentDose too low, over-drying, expired coating, or acid left in wellsRaise the dose, avoid long drying, use fresher plates and rinse collagen thoroughly
ContaminationNon-sterile stock, open storage, or no UV or rinseWork aseptically, seal stored plates, and add UV or ethanol steps if the stock was handled outside the cabinet
Coating dries out or cracksPlates left open or stored unsealedKeep a thin layer of liquid or seal plates; discard plates with discoloration or cracks [3]
Cells clump or detach unevenlyCoating too thick or too concentratedLower the µg/cm² and rinse well
  • Acetic acid is an irritant; wear gloves and eye protection.
  • If using chloroform to sterilize collagen, handle in a chemical fume hood and dispose as hazardous waste.
  • UV light harms eyes and skin; keep the sash closed and the lamp off while working.
  • Human- or animal-derived proteins and cell cultures follow local biosafety rules; discard liquid waste with disinfectant and solid waste as biohazard.
