Microscale Thermophoresis

≈ 4 h (including labeling and a 16-capillary run) · 2026-11-27

Measure binding affinity (Kd) between a fluorescently labeled target and an unlabeled ligand by microscale thermophoresis: labeling, 16-point 1:1 dilution series, capillary loading, instrument settings, data checks, Kd fitting and troubleshooting.

⬇ Word (.docx)⬇ PowerPoint (.pptx)

Purpose

To measure the binding affinity (dissociation constant, Kd) between a fluorescently labeled target, usually a protein, and an unlabeled ligand in solution, using small sample volumes and without immobilization.

Principle

An infrared laser heats a tiny spot in a glass capillary and creates a temperature difference. Molecules move along this gradient (thermophoresis), and the movement depends on size, charge and hydration shell, all of which change when a ligand binds [1][2][3]. The instrument follows the fluorescence of the labeled target; the normalized fluorescence (Fnorm) is plotted against ligand concentration, and a binding model is fitted to give Kd. The initial fluorescence change at the moment the laser turns on (T-jump) can also report binding [1].

Materials and reagents

ItemDetails
Labeled targetHis-tagged protein with a Ni-NTA dye (200 nM protein mixed 1:1 with 100 nM dye in PBS, 30 min at room temperature) [1], or protein labeled with an amine- or cysteine-reactive RED dye (5–20 µM protein, about one dye per molecule) following the kit manual [3]
Assay bufferBuffer matched exactly for target and ligand, e.g., PBS (137 mM NaCl, 2.5 mM KCl, 10 mM NaH₂PO₄, 2 mM KH₂PO₄, pH 7.4) with 0.05% Tween-20 to prevent sticking to the capillary [1][3]
Unlabeled ligandStock of at least 100× the expected Kd, spun for 5–10 min at 13,000 rpm before use to remove aggregates [3]
CapillariesStandard-treated or premium-coated capillaries for the instrument, 16 per titration [3][4]
TubesSmall (PCR-type) tubes, at least 20 µL of sample per tube [2][3]
Optional additives0.5–1 mg/mL BSA or up to 10% glycerol if adsorption occurs; avoid opaque buffers [3]

Equipment

  • Monolith-type MST instrument with the matching RED or BLUE detector
  • Capillary tray
  • Pipettes for 5–20 µL volumes
  • Microcentrifuge
  • Analysis software (M.O. Affinity Analysis) and a curve-fitting program [1][3]

Procedure

  1. Label the target. For a His-tagged protein, mix 200 nM protein and 100 nM Ni-NTA dye 1:1 and incubate 30 min at room temperature in the dark [1]. (30 min)
  2. Spin the labeled target for 10 min at 11,000 × g and keep it on ice, protected from light [1]. (10 min)
  3. Dilute the labeled target to 2× the final concentration in assay buffer: 10 nM for the RED detector (final 5 nM) [3]. Check that the final concentration is above the detector minimum. (10 min)
  4. Prepare the ligand at 2× the highest final concentration in the same buffer, at least 50× the expected Kd (at least 10× per facility guidance) [3][4]. (15 min)
  5. Pipette 10 µL of assay buffer into tubes 2–16 and 20 µL of ligand into tube 1 [2][3]. (5 min)
  6. Make a 16-point 1:1 series: move 10 µL from tube 1 to tube 2, mix by pipetting, and continue to tube 16. Discard 10 µL from tube 16 so each tube holds 10 µL. Do not vortex [2]. (10 min)
  7. Add 10 µL of 2× labeled target to each tube (20 µL total, same target concentration in every tube). Mix gently by pipetting and incubate 10 min at room temperature [1][2]. (15 min)
  8. Touch each capillary to the liquid and let it fill without touching the middle. Do not fill the ends flush with the opening. Load from highest to lowest ligand concentration into the tray [1][3]. (10 min)
  9. Run a pre-test or capillary scan to set the LED power so that the fluorescence lies in the instrument's recommended range (for RED, about 3,000–15,000 counts) [3]. (5 min)
  10. Set MST power (medium, 40%, is the usual start; low 20%, high 60%) and the laser timing, for example 5 s laser off, 30 s on, 5 s off. Set the temperature (22 °C is common) [1][2][3]. (5 min)
  11. Start the 16-capillary run. Repeat with fresh dilutions for at least three independent titrations [3][4]. (≈ 30 min per run)
  12. Inspect the data: capillary scans should be symmetrical, with initial fluorescence within ±10% of the average, and thermographs should not be bumpy. Exclude outliers (thumbs-down) only with a reason [1][2][3]. (20 min)
  13. Plot Fnorm against log ligand concentration, merge replicates measured with the same LED and MST power, and fit a Kd (1:1 binding) model or a four-parameter dose–response curve [1][3]. (30 min)

Pause points

  • Labeled target can be kept on ice in the dark for a short time before the experiment [1].
  • Do not store diluted ligand or mixed samples for long; evaporation and adsorption change the concentration. Load the capillaries soon after incubation [2].

Expected results

  • A sigmoidal dose–response curve with a clear plateau at low and high ligand concentrations; the midpoint gives the Kd.
  • Example: the PD-1/PD-L1 interaction measured by MST gave a Kd of about 7 µM [2].
  • Replicate titrations give similar Kd values, and non-binding controls give a flat curve.

Controls

  • Target alone in buffer (and a pre-test with target alone versus target plus saturating ligand) to confirm a signal change [3]
  • A non-binding or mutant ligand, which should give a flat curve [2]
  • Identical buffer in all 16 capillaries
  • At least three replicate titrations [4]

Troubleshooting

ProblemLikely causeSolution
Bumpy thermograph or irregular capillary scanAggregation or adsorption to the glassSpin or filter samples, add 0.05% Tween-20 or 0.5–1 mg/mL BSA, or purify aggregates away by size-exclusion chromatography [3]
Fluorescence differs between capillariesLigand-induced quenching or stickingCheck capillary scans and use initial-fluorescence analysis if the change is real; test for adsorption with an SD test [1][3]
Signal too low or saturatedLED power or target concentration not suitableAdjust LED power or the labeled target concentration; the target should be about the Kd or lower [3]
Noisy points or outliersBubbles, pipetting error or evaporationPrepare at least 20 µL per tube, use small tubes, and load carefully [2]
No binding curveTop ligand concentration too lowRaise the highest ligand concentration; use a concentration-finder tool if available [4]

Safety and waste

  • Wear gloves and a lab coat. Fluorescent dyes in DMSO can pass through skin.
  • Handle the instrument only after facility training; follow the facility rules for the IR laser and booking [3][4].
  • Dispose of used capillaries in a sharps container [3].
  • Dispose of protein, dye and buffer waste according to your institution's chemical and biological waste rules.
