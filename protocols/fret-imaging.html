FRET Imaging

Estimated time: 3 days (≈ 4 h imaging) | Protocol World

To measure Förster resonance energy transfer (FRET) between a donor and an acceptor fluorescent protein in live mammalian cells, so that molecular proximity (about 2–8 nm) or biosensor activation can be quantified with proper corrections and controls.

When an excited donor (CFP, Cerulean or mTurquoise2) lies within about 10 nm of an acceptor (YFP or Venus), energy passes non-radiatively and the acceptor emits. In sensitized-emission FRET, the donor is excited and the acceptor channel is read. Three images are needed: donor excitation/donor channel (IDD), donor excitation/FRET channel (IDA) and acceptor excitation/acceptor channel (IAA). The FRET channel also contains donor bleed-through and direct acceptor excitation, which are measured in single-labeled cells and subtracted [1][2][3]. Tandem constructs with known efficiencies (about 30–45%) calibrate the system [4]. FRET indicates proximity, not proven binding; confirm with an independent method.

Cell lineHEK293 or HT-1080, grown in DMEM + 10% FBS at 37 °C, 5% CO₂ [1][4]
Plasmids (same backbone and promoter)FRET sensor or interaction pair; donor-only; acceptor-only; CFP-linker-Venus tandem (positive control); unlinked CFP + Venus (negative control); non-responsive sensor mutant (biological control)
Transfection reagentLipofectamine 2000, 2.5 µL per µg DNA; DNA and lipid each diluted in 250 µL Opti-MEM per well [1]
Culture vessels6-well plates (4.5 × 10⁵ cells/well); 18 mm no. 1.5 coverslips or 4-well chambered coverglass (2–4 × 10⁴ cells/well) [1][4]
Fibronectin2.5 µg/mL in PBS; coat 60 min at 37 °C, wash 3 × with PBS (for spreading cells) [1]
Trypsin-EDTA0.25%, 0.5 mL per well
Imaging mediumPhenol red–free medium or HBSS + 20 mM HEPES, pH 7.4, pre-warmed to 37 °C (general practice; lowers background)
PBSWarm (37 °C), for 3 washes about 30 min before imaging [4]
Immersion oilLow-autofluorescence type
  • Confocal or widefield microscope with 37 °C stage and objective heater
  • 63× / 1.4 NA oil objective
  • Donor excitation 405–458 nm; acceptor excitation 500–514 nm
  • Emission windows: donor 470–500 nm; FRET and acceptor 520–620 nm
  • Cooled CCD or PMT detector, 12-bit or better
  • ImageJ/Fiji or vendor FRET software
  • CO₂ incubator, biosafety cabinet
  • Lasers warmed up ≥ 1 h before use
  • Day 1: seed 4.5 × 10⁵ cells per well in 6-well plates, one well per construct plus one untransfected well, so they reach ≤ 60% confluence [1][4]. (30 min)
  • Day 2: dilute 1.5 µg DNA and 3.75 µL Lipofectamine 2000 each in 250 µL Opti-MEM; wait 5 min, combine and incubate ≥ 20 min at room temperature [1]. (30 min)
  • Replace medium with 2 mL antibiotic-free DMEM + 10% FBS, add the complexes dropwise, and incubate at 37 °C, 5% CO₂ for 24 h [1]. (24 h)
  • Day 3: trypsinize (0.5 mL), stop with 1.5 mL medium, and replate 100–150 µL onto coated coverslips or chambers; let cells adhere ≥ 60 min, or 24 h for good spreading [1][4]. (1–24 h)
  • About 30 min before imaging, wash 3 × with 1 mL warm PBS and add warm imaging medium; keep the stage at 37 °C [4]. (30 min)
  • Warm lasers and stage for 1–2 h. Select the 63× objective, check Köhler illumination and set 12-bit acquisition with sequential channels [1][4]. (1–2 h)
  • Image a cell-free field in all three channels to record background; subtract it later from every pixel [3][4].
  • On the brightest tandem-control cell, set laser/exposure so no pixel nears 4095 and none reads 0 (offset > 0). Then lock all settings for the whole session [1][4].
  • Acquire IDD, IDA and IAA for each cell in rapid sequence, keeping excitation low to limit bleaching [1][3].
  • Image ≥ 5 donor-only cells (gives donor bleed-through, DER) and ≥ 5 acceptor-only cells (gives acceptor cross-excitation, AER) with identical settings [1][3].
  • Image the tandem, unlinked CFP + Venus and untransfected cells, then ≥ 25 cells of the sensor or interaction pair and its mutant [1][4].
  • Optional cross-check: bleach the acceptor in an ROI at 514 nm (1–5 scans) in ≥ 5 cells and confirm donor gain; E = 1 − Dpre/Dpost [1][2].
  • Subtract background; calculate DER = IDA/IDD (donor-only) and AER = IDA/IAA (acceptor-only) from ROI means [1][3].
  • Compute corrected FRET: Fc = IDA − DER × IDD − AER × IAA. Exclude saturated or near-background pixels and perinuclear autofluorescent regions [1][2][3].
  • Normalize: Fc/donor (dFRET) and Fc/acceptor (aFRET), calibrated with the tandem; for biosensors report the FRET/CFP ratio after a 3 × 3 median filter [1][3].
  • Compare conditions per cell (≥ 25 cells) and keep donor:acceptor ratios within about 0.1–10 [2].
  • Cells can be left after transfection (step 3) for 24–48 h.
  • Collect images and analyze later; do not change settings between controls and samples.
  • Tandem controls give clear FRET (reported 43%, 38% and 31% for 5-, 17- and 32-amino-acid linkers) [4].
  • Unlinked CFP + Venus gives corrected FRET indistinguishable from zero [3].
  • A functional Akt sensor showed about 3-fold higher FRET/CFP than a non-activatable mutant [1].
  • Donor-only and acceptor-only cells give stable DER and AER that do not depend on expression level.
  • Donor-only and acceptor-only cells for DER and AER
  • Untransfected cells for autofluorescence; cell-free field for background
  • Tandem positive control and unlinked CFP + Venus negative control
  • Non-responsive sensor mutant as biological control
  • Identical settings for all samples in one session

  • Class 3B/4 lasers: never look into the beam path; keep covers closed during scanning.
  • HEK293 and recombinant DNA: use BSL-1/2 practice per your institution; treat culture waste with 10% bleach or autoclave.
  • If cells are fixed, 4% paraformaldehyde is toxic: use in a fume hood and dispose as hazardous chemical waste.
  • Wear gloves, lab coat and eye protection.
