Blue-White Screening

Estimated time: About 1 h hands-on plus 16–24 h incubation | Protocol World

To distinguish E. coli colonies carrying recombinant plasmids (inserts in the lacZα cloning site) from colonies carrying empty vector, using X-gal and IPTG indicator plates.

Vectors such as pUC18/19, pBluescript and pGEM encode the N-terminal α-peptide of β-galactosidase and a multiple cloning site within it. Hosts such as DH5α, XL1-Blue and JM109 carry lacZΔM15, which encodes the C-terminal portion of the enzyme. Neither fragment is active alone, but together they restore activity (α-complementation) [1][3]. IPTG induces the lac promoter, and active enzyme cleaves X-gal to a blue indigo-type precipitate, so empty-vector colonies are blue. An insert in the cloning site disrupts the α-peptide, so recombinant colonies remain white [1][2][3].

ItemDetails
Competent E. coli with lacZΔM15DH5α, XL1-Blue, JM109 or TOP10; strains with F′ lacIq also work
Ligation or transformation reactionVector with lacZα, for example pUC19, pBluescript or pGEM-T
LB agar with antibioticExample: ampicillin 100 µg/mL; autoclaved, cooled to 50 °C before additions
X-gal stock20 mg/mL in N,N-dimethylformamide (DMF) or DMSO; protect from light (foil); store at −20 °C in glass or polypropylene; stable 6–12 months [1][2]
IPTG stock100 mM in water, filter-sterilized; store in aliquots at −20 °C [1]
SOC mediumFor outgrowth after heat shock
Sterile glass beads or spreader
Positive control plasmidIntact vector (for example pUC19) for an all-blue control plate
  • 37 °C incubator
  • 4 °C refrigerator
  • Water bath at 42 °C (for heat-shock transformation)
  • Biosafety hood or clean bench
  • Plate-pouring supplies
  • Option A (in-agar): melt LB agar, cool to 50 °C, then add antibiotic, X-gal to 40–60 µg/mL (2–3 µL of 20 mg/mL per mL) and IPTG to 0.1–1 mM. Pour plates [1][2]. (30 min)
  • Option B (surface): to a dry antibiotic plate add 40 µL of 100 mM IPTG and 120 µL of 20 mg/mL X-gal, spread over the whole surface, and dry 30 min in a hood [1]. (40 min)
  • Prepare a control plate set: transformation with intact vector (all blue expected), and with no DNA (no colonies expected) [2]. (5 min)
  • Transform competent cells with the ligation reaction by heat shock (about 42 °C for 30–45 s) and recover in SOC at 37 °C for 1 h [2]. (1.5 h)
  • Plate 50–200 µL on pre-warmed X-gal/IPTG plates and spread evenly with sterile beads. Let the liquid absorb, then invert the plates [1][2]. (15 min)
  • Incubate inverted at 37 °C for 16–24 h until colonies are visible and blue color has developed [1][2]. (16–24 h)
  • Optional: move plates to 4 °C for about 1 h to a few hours to deepen the blue color and make faint colonies easier to call [2]. (1–4 h)
  • Score colonies. Blue means intact lacZα (no insert). White means a disrupted α-peptide, so a likely recombinant. Pick well-separated white colonies from the plate center [1][2]. (10 min)
  • Patch or restreak 4–8 white colonies on a new X-gal/IPTG plate, then confirm by colony PCR, miniprep and restriction digest, or sequencing [2]. (1–2 days)
  • After scoring, plates can be wrapped and kept at 4 °C for a few days, though colonies stop growing and may smear.
  • Prepared in-agar plates keep about 1 week at 4 °C in clear sleeves and up to 1 month in the dark [1].
  • Control with intact vector gives all blue colonies; no-DNA control gives none.
  • A good ligation gives a mix of white (insert) and some blue (re-ligated vector) colonies; the white fraction depends on vector dephosphorylation and insert ratio.
  • Most picked white colonies should contain the expected insert, but this must be confirmed [2].
  • Intact vector transformation (all blue)
  • Cut vector without ligase (very few colonies)
  • No-DNA plate (no colonies; checks antibiotic and contamination)
  • Uncut X-gal plate with untransformed cells to check the plate formulation

  • DMF is a reproductive toxin and skin irritant; handle in a fume hood with gloves.
  • X-gal and IPTG powders: avoid dust; wear gloves and eye protection.
  • Treat all E. coli plates as biohazardous waste: autoclave before disposal.
