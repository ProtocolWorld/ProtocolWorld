TOPO Cloning

2 h hands-on + overnight · 2026-11-24

Insert a Taq-amplified PCR product into a TOPO TA vector in 5 minutes without ligase, transform One Shot TOP10 E. coli, and screen blue/white colonies by colony PCR or EcoRI digestion.

⬇ Word (.docx)⬇ PowerPoint (.pptx)

Purpose

To insert a Taq-amplified PCR product (with 3′ A-overhangs) into a linearized TOPO TA vector in about 5 minutes, without ligase, and to recover recombinant clones in chemically competent E. coli TOP10.

Principle

The linear TOPO TA vector carries single 3′ thymidine overhangs and vaccinia virus topoisomerase I covalently attached to its 3′ ends. A PCR product with a complementary 3′ A-overhang, made by Taq polymerase, attacks the topoisomerase–DNA bond; the enzyme is released and the strands are joined, so the vector closes around the insert [1][2]. Proofreading polymerases leave blunt ends and must be A-tailed first, and 5′-phosphorylated products cannot be TOPO cloned [1][2][3]. Insertion disrupts the lacZα fragment, so recombinant colonies are white or pale blue on X-gal plates [1].

Materials and reagents

ItemDetails
PCR productFresh, made with Taq (or Taq in ≥10:1 excess over a proofreading enzyme), with a 7–30 min final extension at 72 °C; primers without 5′ phosphates [1][2]
TOPO TA vectorLinearized vector with topoisomerase I (pCR2.1-TOPO or equivalent), 1 µL per reaction; keep on ice [1][2]
Salt Solution1.2 M NaCl, 0.06 M MgCl₂ (6× stock); 1 µL gives about 200 mM NaCl and 10 mM MgCl₂ in the 6 µL reaction [1][2]
One Shot TOP10 chemically competent E. coli50 µL per transformation; stored at −80 °C and thawed on ice [1][2]
S.O.C. medium250 µL per transformation, at room temperature [1][3]
LB agar plates with antibiotic50 µg/mL kanamycin or 100 µg/mL ampicillin, less than 1 month old, pre-warmed to 37 °C [1][2]
X-gal40 µL of 40 mg/mL in dimethylformamide spread per plate; TOP10F′ cells also need 40 µL of 100 mM IPTG [1][2]
Sterile water, ice, sterile spreaderNuclease-free water for the reaction
Colony PCR or miniprep reagentsM13 forward and reverse primers; EcoRI (cuts on each side of the insert in pCR2.1-TOPO) [1]

Equipment

  • Thermal cycler
  • Heat block or water bath at 42 °C
  • 37 °C incubator and shaking incubator (200 rpm)
  • Ice bucket
  • Microcentrifuge tubes and pipettes
  • Agarose gel system

Procedure

  1. Check the PCR on a gel: one clean band of the expected size. Gel-purify if there are extra bands, primer dimers or a product above 1.5 kb [2]. (1 h)
  2. If the PCR used a proofreading enzyme, add A-tails: 0.7–1 U Taq per tube, 72 °C for 8–10 min with no cycling, then place on ice [1]. (15 min)
  3. Thaw the TOPO vector and Salt Solution on ice; label tubes; pre-warm selective plates at 37 °C, and spread X-gal if the cells need it [1][2]. (15 min)
  4. Combine 0.5–4 µL fresh PCR product (about 5–10 ng of a 2 kb fragment; insert:vector near 1:1) with 1 µL Salt Solution and water to 5 µL [1][2]. (2 min)
  5. Add 1 µL TOPO vector (6 µL total). Mix gently by flicking; do not pipette up and down [1]. (1 min)
  6. Incubate 5 min at room temperature (22–23 °C). For inserts over 1 kb, up to 30 min gives more colonies [1][3]. Then place on ice. (5–30 min)
  7. Add 2 µL of the TOPO reaction to a thawed vial of One Shot TOP10 cells. Mix gently and keep on ice for 5–30 min [1][3]. (5–30 min)
  8. Heat-shock the cells for exactly 30 s at 42 °C without shaking, then return the tube to ice at once [1][3]. (1 min)
  9. Add 250 µL room-temperature S.O.C. and shake horizontally at 200 rpm and 37 °C for 1 h [1][3]. (1 h)
  10. Spread 10–50 µL on one pre-warmed selective plate and a second, different volume on another; add 20 µL S.O.C. to small volumes for even spreading [1][3]. (10 min)
  11. Incubate the plates upside down at 37 °C overnight (about 16 h) [1]. (16 h)
  12. Pick about 10 white or light-blue colonies; avoid dark blue ones. Screen by colony PCR with M13 primers or grow cultures for miniprep and EcoRI digestion [1][3]. (1 h + overnight)
  13. Confirm the insert by Sanger sequencing, then make a glycerol stock (0.85 mL culture + 0.15 mL glycerol) at −80 °C [1][3]. (30 min)

Pause points

  • The TOPO reaction can be stored at −20 °C overnight before transformation [1].
  • Plates with colonies can be sealed and kept at 4 °C for a few days before screening.
  • Purified PCR product can be kept on ice or at −20 °C for up to 2 weeks [3].

Expected results

  • An efficient reaction gives several hundred colonies per plate, and most of them (typically over 90%) are white or pale blue with the correct insert [1][3].
  • Colony PCR with M13 primers shows a band larger than the empty vector product by the insert length; EcoRI digestion releases the insert [1].

Controls

  • Vector-only reaction (water instead of PCR product): few colonies, mostly dark blue [1]
  • Positive control with the control PCR insert supplied with the kit [1]
  • Plate an untransformed aliquot of cells on selective medium to confirm the antibiotic works

Troubleshooting

ProblemLikely causeSolution
Few or no coloniesOld plates, PCR product too dilute or pH above 9, cells thawed too long, or product with 5′ phosphatesUse fresh plates, concentrate or clean up the PCR, adjust pH with 1 M Tris-HCl pH 8, keep cells at −80 °C until use [2]
Many colonies but no insertBlunt PCR product (no A-overhangs) or small artifacts under 100 bp that clone efficientlyAdd A-tails with Taq; gel-purify the band [1][2]
All colonies dark blueEmpty vector re-closed, or too little PCR productUse fresh product at about 1:1 insert:vector; check the PCR on a gel [1][2]
Poor blue/white contrastX-gal or IPTG missing, or glucose in the mediumSpread X-gal (and IPTG for TOP10F′) and avoid glucose [2]
Wrong insert sizePrimer dimers or non-specific products clonedGel-purify the band before the reaction [2]

Safety and waste

  • X-gal is dissolved in dimethylformamide, which is toxic and a reproductive hazard: wear nitrile gloves and work in a fume hood.
  • Kanamycin and ampicillin are sensitizers; avoid skin contact and do not pour antibiotic waste down the drain.
  • Handle E. coli K-12 derivatives at the biosafety level required by your institution; autoclave or bleach plates and cultures before disposal.
  • Ethidium bromide and other gel stains need gloves and designated waste; use a safer stain where possible.
