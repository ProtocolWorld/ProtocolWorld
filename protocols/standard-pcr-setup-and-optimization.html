Standard PCR Setup and Optimization

Estimated time: 2.5–3 h | Protocol World

To amplify a defined DNA fragment of up to about 3 kb from plasmid or genomic DNA using Taq DNA polymerase, and to optimize the reaction when it gives no product or the wrong product.

Repeated cycles of template denaturation, primer annealing and polymerase extension double the target region each cycle. Specificity depends mainly on primer design, the annealing temperature, and the Mg²⁺ concentration; yield depends on template quality, enzyme amount and cycle number [1][2][3]. Changing one variable at a time (annealing temperature, Mg²⁺, additives, hot start) converts a failed reaction into a clean single band [3][4].

DMSO (optional)Molecular-biology grade; 1–2% final for GC-rich templates [2][3]

Sterile thin-walled 0.2 mL PCR tubes or plateNuclease-free
  • Thermocycler with heated lid and gradient function
  • Micropipettes with aerosol-barrier tips
  • Ice bucket or cold block
  • Gel electrophoresis system and power supply
  • Gel imager (blue light or UV)
  • NanoDrop or similar spectrophotometer for template concentration
  • Design primers 20–30 nt long, 40–60% GC, Tm within 5 °C of each other, a G or C near the 3′ end, and no runs of more than 4 identical bases. Check with Primer-BLAST [2][3]. (30 min)
  • Dissolve primers to 100 µM (add 10 µL water per nmol), then dilute to 10 µM working stocks. Quantify template and dilute to 1–10 ng/µL (plasmid: 0.1–1 ng/µL) [2][3]. (15 min)
  • Wear gloves, clean the bench and pipettes, and set up on ice. Prepare a master mix for N reactions + 10%: 2.5 µL 10× buffer, 1.5 µL 25 mM MgCl₂, 0.5 µL 10 mM dNTPs per reaction [1][2]. (10 min)
  • To the master mix add 0.5 µL of each 10 µM primer, 0.125 µL (0.625 U) Taq and water to 24 µL per reaction (before template). Mix by gentle pipetting; do not vortex the enzyme [1][3]. (5 min)
  • Aliquot 24 µL of master mix into each PCR tube, then add 1 µL of template. Prepare a no-template control (1 µL water) and, if possible, a positive control template [3]. (10 min)
  • Close the tubes, spin briefly, and place them in a thermocycler block preheated to 95 °C with the lid at 105 °C (hot-block start) [1]. (2 min)
  • Run: 95 °C for 30 s (2–4 min for GC-rich or genomic templates); then 30 cycles of 95 °C for 15–30 s, annealing at Tm − 5 °C for 30 s, and 68 °C for 1 min per kb [1][2]. (1–2 h)
  • Finish with a final extension at 68 °C for 5 min and hold at 4 °C. Some labs extend at 72 °C; either works with Taq if time per kb is kept [1][2][3]. (5 min)
  • Mix 5 µL of product with 1 µL of 6× loading dye. Load alongside a DNA ladder on a 1% agarose gel in 1× TAE and run at 5–8 V/cm for 30–45 min [2]. (45 min)
  • Image the gel and compare band sizes with the ladder and controls. The no-template lane must be clean; primer dimers appear as a faint band below 100 bp [3]. (10 min)
  • To optimize, run an annealing gradient from 5 °C below to 5 °C above the calculated Tm in 2 °C steps with one master mix; pick the highest temperature giving a strong single band [1][2][4]. (2 h)
  • If bands are still faint, titrate MgCl₂ from 1.5 to 3.0 mM in 0.5 mM steps (add 1 µL of 25 mM MgCl₂ to a 25 µL reaction for +1 mM), or add DMSO to 1–2% for GC-rich targets [2][3][4]. (2 h)
  • If non-specific bands persist, use hot-start Taq, lower primers toward 0.1 µM, cut cycles to 25–28, or use touchdown PCR (start 10 °C above Tm, drop 1 °C per cycle) [3][4]. (2 h)
  • Store products at −20 °C. Purify with a spin column or excise the correct band from a gel before cloning or sequencing. (15 min)
  • After the cycling program, products can be held at 4 °C overnight before running the gel.
  • Purified products and primer stocks can be stored at −20 °C.
  • A single, sharp band at the expected size in the sample lane, and no band in the no-template lane.
  • With 25–30 cycles, typical yields are enough for gel analysis and cloning; Taq adds a single 3′ A overhang to products, which suits TA cloning [3].
  • No-template control (water in place of DNA) to detect contamination
  • Positive control template with known primers to confirm that reagents and cycler work
  • Ladder on every gel for size calibration

Product in no-template laneContaminated reagents or aerosolsReplace all stocks, clean bench with 10% bleach, use barrier tips, separate pre- and post-PCR areas
  • Wear gloves and a lab coat. Handle ethidium bromide, if used, as a mutagen; SYBR Safe, GelRed or similar are safer options.
  • UV light burns skin and eyes: use a shield or blue-light imager.
  • Dispose of gels and stain-contaminated waste according to institutional rules; never pour stain solutions down the drain.
  • Keep post-PCR products out of the pre-PCR area to prevent contamination.
