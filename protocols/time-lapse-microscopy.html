Time-Lapse Microscopy

General Lab Methods · 2–3 h setup + 12–48 h imaging · 2026-11-26

Live-cell time-lapse imaging of adherent mammalian cells on an inverted widefield or confocal microscope with a stage-top incubator (37 °C, 5% CO₂, humidified). Covers imaging medium, glass-bottom dishes, hardware autofocus, light-dose control, multi-position acquisition and basic drift correction.

Time-Lapse Microscopy

Estimated time: 2–3 h setup + 12–48 h imaging | Protocol World

To record the dynamics of adherent mammalian cells over many hours while keeping them healthy, in focus and minimally damaged by illumination.

Cells imaged through the bottom of a vessel need stable temperature, CO₂ and humidity, low-background medium and a thin glass bottom [1][3]. Excitation light generates reactive oxygen species, so the lowest dose giving usable signal, sparse sampling and few channels protect cell health [2][3][4]. Thermal settling causes axial drift, which hardware autofocus counters [1][3][4]. Controls such as unlabeled cells and mitotic timing reveal imaging stress [1][3].

  • Inverted widefield or confocal microscope
  • Stage-top or enclosure incubator (37 °C, 5% CO₂, humidity)
  • Hardware autofocus (e.g., Perfect Focus or equivalent)
  • Motorized XY stage with multi-position software
  • Sensitive camera and high-NA objective
  • Shuttered LED or laser light source
  • Anti-vibration table
  • Biosafety cabinet and CO₂ culture incubator
  • Image analysis software (e.g., Fiji/ImageJ)
  • Seed cells on the glass-bottom dish 24–48 h earlier so they reach about 40–60% confluence at imaging; allow cells to attach in the incubator [1][3]. (24–48 h)
  • Switch on the incubator, set 37 °C, 5% CO₂ and humidity, and fill the humidity reservoir with sterile water [1][4]. (30 min)
  • Let the whole stage, objective and enclosure warm up for 45–60 min before use so thermal expansion settles and focus drift is reduced [1][4]. (45–60 min)
  • Replace growth medium with pre-warmed, phenol red-free imaging medium; use L-15 or HEPES only if CO₂ is not available [1][4].
  • Add or confirm any dye at the lowest effective concentration and keep dishes shielded from room light to limit pre-acquisition exposure [3][4]. (15–30 min)
  • Wipe the dish bottom, mount it in the holder, and seal or lid it so evaporation is limited; keep the lid warm to reduce condensation [1][3].
  • Apply pre-warmed immersion oil if needed and allow the objective to equilibrate with the sample for a few minutes [1]. (5–10 min)
  • Find cells in transmitted light or at low excitation, then activate hardware autofocus and test that it holds focus [3][4].
  • Set the lowest excitation power giving adequate signal-to-noise, with the shortest exposure and a sensitive camera; avoid UV and unneeded channels [2][3][4].
  • Choose the interval to match the biology (e.g., minutes for mitosis, up to hours for wound healing) and avoid oversampling [2][4].
  • Define several XY positions per dish, space them over the dish, and confirm that autofocus works at each one [3][4].
  • Add a Z-stack only if needed; use the fewest slices and limit autofocus light use, which can add several-fold exposure [4].
  • Run a short test series and check for blebbing, rounding, stalled division or fading; reduce light dose if any appear [1][2][3].
  • Start the full acquisition and check the first frames, then check the stage, medium level and gas supply periodically [1][3]. (12–48 h)
  • Verify focus and cell health at the end; compare mitotic timing and morphology of imaged versus unimaged cells [1][3].
  • Correct XY drift by registering frames (e.g., Fiji registration plugins) and apply the same correction to all channels [3].
  • Correct photobleaching and background, then segment or track cells and record the metadata (exposure, interval, power, objective) [2][3].
  • Remove the dish, decontaminate it, and clean the objective and stage according to the facility rules.
  • After the cell seeding in step 1 (cells can remain in the culture incubator for 24–48 h)
  • After the incubator warm-up in step 3 if imaging will start later (leave the stage on)
  • After the test series in step 13 while you review the light dose
  • After acquisition, imaging data can be stored and analyzed later
  • Cells remain attached, motile and healthy over the whole run, and divide at normal rates.
  • Cells stay in focus at every position without manual correction.
  • Signal stays above background with minimal photobleaching across frames.
  • Unlabeled or untreated cells imaged with identical settings to detect autofluorescence and light effects
  • Unimaged cells in the same incubator to compare growth and mitotic timing with imaged cells [1][3]
  • Vehicle-only dish for any drug treatment
  • Mitotic timing as a read-out of phototoxicity [1]
  • Fixed or dead cells for bleaching and drift checks

  • Lasers and bright light sources can injure eyes: follow the facility interlock and eyewear rules.
  • Handle compressed CO₂ cylinders securely and check for leaks; ensure ventilation in small rooms.
  • Treat cell lines and any viral reporters under the appropriate biosafety level, and discard dishes as biohazard waste.
  • Dispose of immersion oil, dyes and sharps according to institutional waste rules.
