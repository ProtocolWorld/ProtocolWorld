Time-Lapse Microscopy

2–3 h setup + 12–48 h imaging · 2026-11-26

Live-cell time-lapse imaging of adherent mammalian cells on an inverted widefield or confocal microscope with a stage-top incubator (37 °C, 5% CO₂, humidified). Covers imaging medium, glass-bottom dishes, hardware autofocus, light-dose control, multi-position acquisition and basic drift correction.

⬇ Word (.docx)⬇ PowerPoint (.pptx)

Purpose

To record the dynamics of adherent mammalian cells over many hours while keeping them healthy, in focus and minimally damaged by illumination.

Principle

Cells imaged through the bottom of a vessel need stable temperature, CO₂ and humidity, low-background medium and a thin glass bottom [1][3]. Excitation light generates reactive oxygen species, so the lowest dose giving usable signal, sparse sampling and few channels protect cell health [2][3][4]. Thermal settling causes axial drift, which hardware autofocus counters [1][3][4]. Controls such as unlabeled cells and mitotic timing reveal imaging stress [1][3].

Equipment

  • Inverted widefield or confocal microscope
  • Stage-top or enclosure incubator (37 °C, 5% CO₂, humidity)
  • Hardware autofocus (e.g., Perfect Focus or equivalent)
  • Motorized XY stage with multi-position software
  • Sensitive camera and high-NA objective
  • Shuttered LED or laser light source
  • Anti-vibration table
  • Biosafety cabinet and CO₂ culture incubator
  • Image analysis software (e.g., Fiji/ImageJ)

Procedure

  1. Seed cells on the glass-bottom dish 24–48 h earlier so they reach about 40–60% confluence at imaging; allow cells to attach in the incubator [1][3]. (24–48 h)
  2. Switch on the incubator, set 37 °C, 5% CO₂ and humidity, and fill the humidity reservoir with sterile water [1][4]. (30 min)
  3. Let the whole stage, objective and enclosure warm up for 45–60 min before use so thermal expansion settles and focus drift is reduced [1][4]. (45–60 min)
  4. Replace growth medium with pre-warmed, phenol red-free imaging medium; use L-15 or HEPES only if CO₂ is not available [1][4].
  5. Add or confirm any dye at the lowest effective concentration and keep dishes shielded from room light to limit pre-acquisition exposure [3][4]. (15–30 min)
  6. Wipe the dish bottom, mount it in the holder, and seal or lid it so evaporation is limited; keep the lid warm to reduce condensation [1][3].
  7. Apply pre-warmed immersion oil if needed and allow the objective to equilibrate with the sample for a few minutes [1]. (5–10 min)
  8. Find cells in transmitted light or at low excitation, then activate hardware autofocus and test that it holds focus [3][4].
  9. Set the lowest excitation power giving adequate signal-to-noise, with the shortest exposure and a sensitive camera; avoid UV and unneeded channels [2][3][4].
  10. Choose the interval to match the biology (e.g., minutes for mitosis, up to hours for wound healing) and avoid oversampling [2][4].
  11. Define several XY positions per dish, space them over the dish, and confirm that autofocus works at each one [3][4].
  12. Add a Z-stack only if needed; use the fewest slices and limit autofocus light use, which can add several-fold exposure [4].
  13. Run a short test series and check for blebbing, rounding, stalled division or fading; reduce light dose if any appear [1][2][3].
  14. Start the full acquisition and check the first frames, then check the stage, medium level and gas supply periodically [1][3]. (12–48 h)
  15. Verify focus and cell health at the end; compare mitotic timing and morphology of imaged versus unimaged cells [1][3].
  16. Correct XY drift by registering frames (e.g., Fiji registration plugins) and apply the same correction to all channels [3].
  17. Correct photobleaching and background, then segment or track cells and record the metadata (exposure, interval, power, objective) [2][3].
  18. Remove the dish, decontaminate it, and clean the objective and stage according to the facility rules.

Pause points

  • After the cell seeding in step 1 (cells can remain in the culture incubator for 24–48 h)
  • After the incubator warm-up in step 3 if imaging will start later (leave the stage on)
  • After the test series in step 13 while you review the light dose
  • After acquisition, imaging data can be stored and analyzed later

Expected results

  • Cells remain attached, motile and healthy over the whole run, and divide at normal rates.
  • Cells stay in focus at every position without manual correction.
  • Signal stays above background with minimal photobleaching across frames.

Controls

  • Unlabeled or untreated cells imaged with identical settings to detect autofluorescence and light effects
  • Unimaged cells in the same incubator to compare growth and mitotic timing with imaged cells [1][3]
  • Vehicle-only dish for any drug treatment
  • Mitotic timing as a read-out of phototoxicity [1]
  • Fixed or dead cells for bleaching and drift checks

Troubleshooting

ProblemLikely causeSolution
Focus driftStage or objective still warming; vibration; autofocus offExtend warm-up to 60 min, use hardware autofocus, use an anti-vibration table [1][3][4]
Phototoxicity or blebbingExcess light dose or too-frequent framesReduce power and exposure, lengthen interval, use longer wavelengths and fewer channels [2][3][4]
Condensation on the lid or opticsHumid air meeting a cooler surfaceWarm the lid or heated cover and let the enclosure equilibrate before imaging (general practice)
PhotobleachingHigh excitation intensity or long exposureLower intensity, use a sensitive camera, use brighter or more stable probes [2][4]
EvaporationLow humidity or open dishFill the humidity reservoir, lid the dish and check medium level; evaporation changes osmolality [1][3]
Medium turns alkalineLow CO₂ with bicarbonate mediumConfirm CO₂ supply or switch to HEPES or L-15 for short runs [1][4]
High backgroundPhenol red or serum fluorescenceUse low-fluorescence medium such as FluoroBrite [4]

Safety and waste

  • Lasers and bright light sources can injure eyes: follow the facility interlock and eyewear rules.
  • Handle compressed CO₂ cylinders securely and check for leaks; ensure ventilation in small rooms.
  • Treat cell lines and any viral reporters under the appropriate biosafety level, and discard dishes as biohazard waste.
  • Dispose of immersion oil, dyes and sharps according to institutional waste rules.
