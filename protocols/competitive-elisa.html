Competitive ELISA

Mammalian Cells · 7–8 h + overnight coating · 2026-11-17

Quantify a small antigen in cell culture supernatant by indirect competitive ELISA: coat antigen, pre-incubate sample with limiting primary antibody, detect with HRP and TMB, and fit an inverse four-parameter curve.

Competitive ELISA

Estimated time: 7–8 h + overnight coating | Protocol World

To measure a small antigen, such as a peptide or secreted factor in mammalian cell culture supernatant, by an indirect competitive ELISA in which free antigen in the sample competes with plate-bound antigen for a limited amount of primary antibody.

The plate is coated with purified antigen. Sample or standard is pre-incubated with a fixed, limiting amount of primary antibody, and the mixture is added to the plate. Antigen in the sample occupies antibody binding sites, so less antibody binds to the plate. An enzyme-labeled secondary antibody and substrate then give a signal that is inversely related to the amount of antigen in the sample, producing a falling (inverse) standard curve [1][2][3]. Coating density, primary antibody and conjugate dilutions must be optimized by checkerboard titration before the first quantitative run [1][3].

PBS, pH 7.48 g NaCl, 0.2 g KCl, 1.44 g Na₂HPO₄, 0.24 g KH₂PO₄ per 1 L water [3]

Purified antigenFor coating; start at 1 µg/mL with a checkerboard range of 0.2–10 µg/mL [2][3]
Primary antibodySpecific for the antigen; fixed, pre-optimized dilution [3]
HRP-conjugated secondary antibodyAnti-species; start with 20–200 ng/mL in assay diluent and optimize [3]
TMB substrate solutionReady-to-use TMB for HRP; protect from light [3]
Stop solution2 M H₂SO₄ [3]
Antigen standardSame antigen as the coating; diluted from a stock of 10,000 pg/mL [3]
SamplesCell culture supernatant clarified at 1,500 rpm for 10 min at 4 °C; aliquot and store at −80 °C [3]

  • Optimize first: run a checkerboard of coating antigen versus primary antibody dilution to find a combination that gives a strong but not saturated signal with no analyte [1][3]. (1 day)
  • Coat each well with 100 µL antigen in coating buffer. Seal and incubate overnight at 4 °C (or 30 min at 37 °C). Do not let wells dry at any stage [3]. (overnight)
  • Flick out the coating solution and wash 3 times with 200 µL PBST per well. Tap the plate on clean paper towels [3]. (10 min)
  • Block with 150 µL blocking buffer for 1 h at 37 °C. Wash 2 times with PBST [1][3]. (1 h 15 min)
  • Prepare standards in assay diluent: a 5,000 pg/mL top standard, six two-fold dilutions down to 78.1 pg/mL, and a zero standard (B0). Use within about 2 h [3]. (20 min)
  • Dilute samples in assay diluent if needed. Clarify supernatants first (1,500 rpm, 10 min, 4 °C) and keep samples on ice until use [3]. (15 min)
  • In tubes or a separate plate, mix 50 µL standard or sample with 50 µL primary antibody at the optimized dilution. Prepare duplicates or triplicates. Incubate 1 h at 37 °C [3]. (1 h)
  • Transfer 100 µL of each pre-incubated mixture to the coated, blocked wells. Incubate 1 h at 37 °C, then wash 3 times with PBST [3]. (1 h 15 min)
  • Add 100 µL HRP-secondary antibody in assay diluent. Incubate 30–60 min at 37 °C, then wash 3–5 times with PBST, tapping dry after the last wash [3]. (1 h)
  • Bring TMB to room temperature. Add 100 µL per well, protect from light and let color develop 15–30 min at room temperature [2][3]. (15–30 min)
  • Watch the B0 wells. Stop when they are clearly blue but not saturated by adding 50 µL 2 M H₂SO₄ per well; the color turns yellow [3]. (2 min)
  • Read absorbance at 450 nm within 30 min of stopping [4]. (5 min)
  • Average replicates and subtract the blank. Calculate %B/B0 = (A of sample − A of NSB) / (A of B0 − A of NSB) × 100 [1][3]. (15 min)
  • Plot absorbance or %B/B0 against log concentration and fit a four-parameter logistic curve. Interpolate samples within the steep part of the curve and multiply by the dilution factor [4]. (20 min)
  • Coated plates can be kept overnight at 4 °C, sealed and protected from drying [3].
  • Standards and samples are best made fresh on the day; unused samples go to −80 °C in aliquots [3].
  • The signal is highest in B0 wells (no analyte) and falls as the antigen concentration in the standard increases, giving a sigmoidal inverse curve on a log scale [1][3].
  • Samples with high antigen give low absorbance; the usable range is the steep middle part of the curve.
  • Duplicate wells agree closely, and the fitted curve is well described by the four-parameter model [4].
  • B0 (zero standard, no analyte) gives the maximum signal [2][3]
  • NSB (non-specific binding) wells with no primary antibody
  • Substrate blank wells with assay diluent only
  • Standards in the same matrix as the samples (or a matched diluent) [3]
  • Duplicate or triplicate wells for every standard and sample [3]
  • Optional spike-and-recovery of known antigen into a sample
ProblemLikely causeSolution
Low B0 signalToo little coating antigen or antibody, or inactive conjugateRaise antigen or antibody concentration; check conjugate and substrate [1][3]
High background in NSB wellsIncomplete blocking or washingIncrease blocking time and wash volume; add a soak step between washes [3][4]
Flat curve (no competition)Antibody in large excess, or antigen not recognizedUse less primary antibody; re-run the checkerboard [1][3]
Poor replicate agreementWells dried out, uneven washing or pipettingKeep wells wet, wash evenly and use fresh tips [3][4]
No color at allSodium azide in a buffer inhibits HRPMake azide-free buffers [1][3]
Signal above rangeSample too dilute in antigen, or over-developedShorten substrate time and re-test [4]
Edge effectsUneven temperature or evaporationSeal plates, avoid stacking in the incubator and use a humid chamber [1]
  • 2 M sulfuric acid is corrosive: wear gloves and eye protection and add it carefully.
  • TMB is light-sensitive; handle it with gloves and collect waste as chemical waste.
  • Sodium azide is toxic and inhibits HRP: do not use it in this assay.
  • Treat cell culture supernatants as potentially infectious (biosafety level of the cell line) and decontaminate plates before disposal.
