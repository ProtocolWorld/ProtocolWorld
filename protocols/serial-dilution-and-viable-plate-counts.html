Serial Dilution and Viable Plate Counts

1–1.5 h bench work + 24–48 h incubation · 2026-11-18

Count living bacteria by making tenfold serial dilutions, plating by spread or drop plate method, counting colonies in the countable range and calculating CFU/mL.

⬇ Word (.docx)⬇ PowerPoint (.pptx)

Purpose

To determine how many living, colony-forming bacteria are in a liquid sample by diluting it in tenfold steps, plating the dilutions and counting colonies, and to express the result as colony-forming units per milliliter (CFU/mL).

Principle

A viable cell that lands on suitable agar grows into one visible colony, so the colony count reflects the number of cells able to reproduce. Raw cultures are far too dense to count, so the sample is diluted in a tenfold series until a plate carries a countable number of colonies, commonly 30–300 per plate for spread and pour plates [1][4]. Drop plating puts several small 10 µL drops on one plate, and the drop with about 3–30 colonies is counted, which saves agar and time [2][3]. CFU/mL is then colony count ÷ (dilution × volume plated in mL) [1]. Accurate pipetting, thorough mixing and a fresh tip at every step determine how reliable the result is [1][2].

Materials and reagents

ItemDetails
Sterile diluentPhosphate-buffered saline (PBS) or isotonic saline (about 0.85–0.9% NaCl); sterile peptone water is also used. Avoid plain water, which stresses cells osmotically [4]
Bacterial sampleExample: overnight E. coli culture in LB broth, or any liquid sample of interest
Agar platesMedium suited to the organism (for example LB agar for E. coli); dried at room temperature for about 30 min or in a 37 °C incubator with lids ajar
Dilution tubesSterile 1.5 mL microcentrifuge tubes with 900 µL diluent each, or 15 mL tubes with 9 mL diluent each [2][3]
Sterile pipette tipsP1000, P200 and P20; use one fresh tip for every transfer
Sterile spreader or glass beadsFor spread plates; cooled before use if flame sterilized
Marker and labelsLabel plates on the base with sample, dilution and date
70% ethanolFor bench disinfection

Equipment

  • Micropipettes (P20, P200, P1000)
  • Vortex mixer
  • Incubator set to the organism's growth temperature (for example 37 °C)
  • Bunsen burner or ethanol lamp, if flaming spreaders
  • Colony counter or a lit surface and a tally counter
  • Biohazard waste container

Procedure

  1. Label dilution tubes 10⁻¹ to 10⁻⁸ and pipette 900 µL of sterile diluent into each. Label the bottom of each plate with the sample, dilution and replicate. (10 min)
  2. Vortex the sample for about 6–8 s so cells are evenly suspended before the first transfer [2].
  3. With a fresh tip, transfer 100 µL of the sample into the 10⁻¹ tube. Dispense slowly into the diluent and discard the tip [1].
  4. Vortex the 10⁻¹ tube for about 6–8 s, then transfer 100 µL with a new tip into the 10⁻² tube [1][2].
  5. Repeat the vortex-and-transfer step through 10⁻⁸, changing tips each time so carry-over does not distort the counts [1][2].
  6. Plate the dilutions you expect to be countable (for an overnight culture often 10⁻⁵ to 10⁻⁷). Plate the most dilute tube first and work toward the most concentrated, using a fresh tip for each [4].
  7. Spread plate: pipette 100 µL onto the center of a dry plate, spread evenly with a sterile spreader until the liquid is absorbed, and avoid pooling at the edge [5]. (10 min)
  8. Drop plate alternative: vortex, then place five 10 µL drops from the same dilution on one sector of a plate, duplicating each dilution; keep the plate flat until the drops are absorbed [2][3].
  9. Prepare a sterility control by plating 100 µL of un-inoculated diluent, and keep one un-inoculated plate beside the others.
  10. Leave plates lid-up until the liquid is absorbed (about 10–15 min), invert them and incubate at the organism's temperature, for example 37 °C for 24 h (up to 48 h for slow growers) [2][5]. (24–48 h)
  11. Count colonies on plates with 30–300 colonies (spread plates) or, for drops, those with about 3–30 colonies per drop [1][2]. Ignore plates outside the range when a better one exists.
  12. Calculate: CFU/mL = colonies ÷ (dilution × mL plated). Example: 148 colonies at 10⁻⁶ with 0.1 mL plated = 1.48 × 10⁹ CFU/mL [1]. Average duplicates and report two significant figures.

Pause points

  • After step 5 the dilution tubes should be plated promptly; do not hold them for more than about 30 min.
  • After step 10 plates can be moved to 4 °C once colonies are visible, to prevent overgrowth until counting.

Expected results

  • A clean tenfold fall in colony number from one dilution to the next, with plates in the countable range for at least one dilution.
  • Duplicate plates that agree within about 20–30%.
  • No growth on the sterility control plates.

Controls

  • Sterility control: plate sterile diluent to confirm it was not contaminated
  • Un-inoculated plates incubated with the others to check the agar
  • Duplicate or triplicate plates per dilution to estimate variability
  • A fresh tip at every step, confirmed by checking that the colony count falls as dilution rises

Troubleshooting

ProblemLikely causeSolution
Too many colonies to countDilution too lowPlate a higher dilution from the series
Fewer than 30 colonies on every plateSample was too dilute or cells did not survivePlate lower dilutions or a larger volume, and check the diluent
Counts do not fall tenfold per dilutionPoor mixing, pipetting error or tip carry-overVortex every tube, use fresh tips, and re-check pipette calibration
Drops run together or spreadWet agar or moving the plateDry plates before use and keep them still until absorbed
Spreading or swarming coloniesMotile organism such as ProteusUse a harder (higher %) agar or a different method; drop plating is not suited to swarmers [2]
Colonies on the sterility controlContaminated diluent, tips or agarRepeat with fresh sterile reagents and clean technique

Safety and waste

  • Treat cultures according to their biosafety level; for E. coli K-12 and similar strains wear a lab coat, gloves and eye protection.
  • Never pipette by mouth; disinfect the bench with 70% ethanol before and after.
  • Flame sterilization of spreaders: keep ethanol away from the flame and let spreaders cool before touching agar.
  • Autoclave or disinfect cultures, tubes, tips and used plates before disposal in biohazard waste.
