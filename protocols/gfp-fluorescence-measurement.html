GFP Fluorescence Measurement

Estimated time: 3–4 h | Protocol World

To measure GFP fluorescence from bacterial cultures in a 96-well plate reader and convert the raw signal into values that can be compared between days, plates and instruments.

Raw plate-reader counts depend on the instrument, gain, plate and volume, so they cannot be compared directly. The method therefore (1) reads blanks and a non-fluorescent strain to remove medium and cell autofluorescence, (2) reads optical density so fluorescence can be divided by cell density, and (3) reads a fluorescein dilution series and a silica microsphere dilution series on the same plate type, volume and settings, which convert counts into standard units of fluorescence and cell number [1][2][4]. The excitation and emission filters must match the GFP variant: reading EGFP with a wild-type GFP filter set lost about 75% of the signal in one test [5].

ItemDetails
GFP-expressing strainFor example E. coli carrying a constitutive GFP construct
Non-fluorescent control strainSame host and growth conditions; used to subtract autofluorescence [3][4]
Positive control strainA strain with known, stable GFP expression; flag the run if it is more than 3-fold from its usual value [4]
Low-fluorescence mediumM9 salts with glycerol or glucose (for example M9TG: 1× M9 salts, 10 g/L tryptone, 5 g/L glycerol [3]). LB fluoresces, so wash LB-grown cells in PBS before reading [3]
1× PBS137 mM NaCl, 2.7 mM KCl, 10 mM Na₂HPO₄, 1.8 mM KH₂PO₄ (8 g NaCl, 0.2 g KCl, 1.44 g Na₂HPO₄, 0.24 g KH₂PO₄ per liter) [3]
Fluorescein100 µM stock: dissolve the powder in 1 mL 1× PBS (check no particles remain in the tip); working solution 10 µM = 100 µL stock + 900 µL PBS [2]
Silica microspheres0.89 µm monodisperse silica; vortex 300 µL for 30 s, mix 100 µL with 900 µL water as the stock. Do not store at or below 0 °C [2][1]
96-well platesBlack walls with clear flat bottom, preferably; use the same plate type for calibration and samples [2][3]
WaterWater or buffer for blank wells
  • Plate reader with fluorescence (485 nm excitation) and absorbance (600 nm) modes
  • Filters or monochromator: 485/20 excitation and 530/30 emission (or 535/20)
  • Plate shaker or incubator for growing cultures (37 °C, 250 rpm)
  • Centrifuge for 15 mL or 50 mL tubes (4,700 × g)
  • Multichannel pipette
  • Grow overnight cultures of the GFP strain, the non-fluorescent control and the positive control in low-fluorescence medium at 37 °C and 250 rpm, in triplicate [1][3]. (overnight)
  • If cells were grown in LB, pellet 10 mL at 4,700 × g for 5 min, discard the supernatant and resuspend in 10 mL PBS; repeat once. Cells grown in M9 medium need no wash [3]. (20 min)
  • Set up the plate map first: sample wells in triplicate, non-fluorescent control wells, PBS or medium blank wells, positive control wells. Leave the outer wells empty or filled with PBS to limit edge effects. (10 min)
  • Calibration plate A, fluorescein: add 100 µL PBS to wells 2–12 of rows A–D. Add 200 µL of 10 µM fluorescein to column 1. Transfer 100 µL from column 1 to 2, mix 3 times, and continue to column 11, discarding the last 100 µL [2]. (20 min)
  • Leave column 12 as PBS only (blank). Repeat the dilution series in rows B, C and D so that each dilution has four replicates [2]. (10 min)
  • Calibration plate B, microspheres: add 100 µL water to columns 2–12, then 200 µL of the microsphere stock (vortexed 10 s) to column 1 and serially dilute 100 µL as above. Column 12 is water only [2]. (20 min)
  • Sample plate: add 160 µL PBS and 40 µL washed culture to each well (200 µL total) [3]. Alternatively, load about 125 µL of culture directly if you use the same volume for calibration [1]. (15 min)
  • Set the reader: temperature 37 °C or room temperature; GFP excitation 485 nm with 20 nm bandwidth; emission 530/30 nm (or 535/20); automatic gain off; record the gain used [1][2][3]. (5 min)
  • Choose gains for the GFP read. One published setup reads GFP at three gains (50, 75, 100 on a BioTek Synergy Mx) so a saturated well can be replaced by a lower-gain read [3]. (5 min)
  • Read the microsphere plate at absorbance 600 nm with path-length correction off. Re-mix each row just before reading because beads settle within about 10 min [2]. (10 min)
  • Read the fluorescein plate with the same gain and filters you will use for cells [2]. (10 min)
  • Read the sample plate: shake briefly (for example 30 s, medium), read OD600, then GFP fluorescence [3]. (10 min)
  • Subtract the blank (PBS or medium) from every OD600 and fluorescence value. Then subtract the mean fluorescence of the non-fluorescent control, scaled to its OD, from each GFP sample [1][3][4]. (15 min)
  • Divide blank-corrected fluorescence by blank-corrected OD600 to get fluorescence per cell density. Use the fluorescein curve to convert counts to µM fluorescein and the microsphere curve to convert OD to particle number [2][4]. (30 min)
  • Overnight cultures can sit at 4 °C for a few hours before washing.
  • Fluorescein and microsphere stocks can be prepared ahead, but dilute the fluorescein standard fresh in PBS [2].
  • Fluorescein standards give a straight line of counts against concentration with a clean blank; flag points that are within 2 blank standard deviations of the blank or that are saturated [4].
  • The GFP strain shows fluorescence clearly above the non-fluorescent control; the positive control is within about 3-fold of its usual value [4].
  • With calibrated units, plate-reader results from different labs agreed within roughly 1.5-fold of flow cytometry in the iGEM InterLab studies [4].
  • Medium or PBS blank, for background of the plate and medium [1][3]
  • Non-fluorescent strain of the same host, for cell autofluorescence [1][3][4]
  • Positive control strain, to check the whole workflow [4]
  • Fluorescein and microsphere calibration plates using the same plate type, volume and settings as the sample plate [2]
ProblemLikely causeSolution
Signal saturates the detectorGain too high or cells too brightLower the gain or dilute the culture; use the lowest-gain read [3]
Weak signal with the wrong filtersFilter set does not match the GFP variantUse 485/20 and 530/25 for EGFP; use about 400/30 and 508/20 for wild-type GFP [5]
High background in blanksFluorescent medium such as LB or yeast extractUse M9-based medium or wash the cells in PBS [3]
Calibration does not match cell readingsDifferent plate, volume or gainRepeat calibration under exactly the same conditions as the experiment [2]
Microsphere OD driftsBeads settleMix the plate just before reading and read quickly [2]
Odd OD values for a red-absorbing proteinThe protein absorbs at 600 nmRead OD at 660 nm and calibrate that wavelength separately [2]
  • Fluorescein and GFP strains are low hazard, but handle bacteria under the biosafety level of your institution and disinfect plates before disposal.
  • Decontaminate bacterial cultures with bleach or autoclave before disposal; collect fluorescein waste according to local rules.
