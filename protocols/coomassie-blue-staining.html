Coomassie Blue Staining

Estimated time: 3–5 h or overnight | Protocol World

To visualize and roughly quantify proteins in SDS-polyacrylamide gels by dye binding, using either classic R-250 staining or the lower-background colloidal G-250 method.

Coomassie Brilliant Blue binds proteins mainly through ionic interactions with basic residues (Arg, Lys, His) plus hydrophobic contacts, turning a faint dye color into blue bands. Classic R-250 stain uses methanol and acetic acid, which fix proteins in the gel and keep the dye dissolved, but the dye also soaks the gel matrix, so an acid/methanol destain is needed [1][4]. Colloidal G-250 formulations keep most dye in particles that cannot enter the gel, so bands develop against a nearly clear background and water alone is enough to destain [2][3][4]. Classic staining is commonly quoted as detecting roughly 0.1–0.5 µg of protein per band, while colloidal formulations commonly reach about 10–30 ng; actual limits depend on the protein and formulation, and one source reports 5–10 ng for some proteins and 25–30 ng for others [5]. Both approaches stain proteins reversibly and are compatible with mass spectrometry if heating and cross-linking fixatives are avoided [3].

ItemDetails
SDS-PAGE gel after electrophoresisMini gel, 1.0 mm thick, run with a prestained or unstained protein marker
Classic R-250 staining solution0.1% (w/v) Coomassie Brilliant Blue R-250, 40% (v/v) methanol, 10% (v/v) acetic acid in deionized water. Stir 2 h or more, then filter through filter paper if any precipitate remains [1][5]
Classic destain solution40% (v/v) methanol and 10% (v/v) acetic acid in deionized water (a lighter 15% methanol/10% acetic acid destain is also used) [1][5]
Fixing solution50% (v/v) methanol and 10% (v/v) acetic acid in deionized water, or 25% (v/v) isopropanol and 10% (v/v) acetic acid [1][5]
Colloidal G-250 stain (Blue Silver type)0.12% (w/v) Coomassie G-250, 10% (w/v) ammonium sulfate, 10% (v/v) phosphoric acid, 20% (v/v) methanol. Add reagents in the order acid, salt, dye, water, then methanol; store in a brown bottle at room temperature for up to about 6 months [2]
Dye-free colloidal option60–80 mg G-250 per 1 L water, stirred 2–4 h, then 3 mL concentrated HCl added (final pH about 2); store in the dark [3]
Deionized waterAt least 500 mL per gel for rinses and water destain
Storage solution5–7% (v/v) acetic acid in water, or water with 20% ammonium sulfate at 4 °C for commercial colloidal stains [1][4][5]
Staining boxes with lidsClean, lidded plastic trays that fit the gel with room for about 10 gel volumes of solution (about 50–100 mL for a mini gel)
  • Orbital or platform shaker
  • Fume hood
  • Gel imager or flatbed scanner (white light)
  • Microwave (optional, for the rapid method)
  • Magnetic stirrer and filter paper
  • Nitrile gloves, lab coat and safety glasses
  • Polyethylene bags or sealable containers for storage
  • Wear gloves and handle the gel only by its edges. Pry the cassette open and release the gel into a clean lidded box [1]. (3 min)
  • For the classic method, add 50 mL of fixing solution (about 10 gel volumes) and shake gently at room temperature [1][5]. (30–60 min)
  • Pour off the fixing solution. For colloidal G-250, rinse the gel three times with 100 mL deionized water, 5 min each, to remove SDS and salts [3][4]. (15 min)
  • Classic staining: cover the gel with 50–100 mL of 0.1% R-250 stain and shake slowly with the lid on [1][5]. (30–60 min)
  • For thicker (1.5 mm) or high-percentage gels, extend R-250 staining to 1–3 h; overnight staining is acceptable [1]. (1–3 h)
  • Pour off the R-250 stain into a waste bottle and rinse the gel briefly with deionized water. Do not reuse the stain [1]. (1 min)
  • Add 50–100 mL of destain solution (40% methanol, 10% acetic acid) and shake gently until bands are clear and the background is pale [1][5]. (1–2 h)
  • Change the destain once or twice as it turns blue. A crumpled lab wipe in the corner of the box can absorb free dye; do not exceed about 24 h in destain [1].
  • Colloidal staining: after the rinses, add 50–100 mL of colloidal G-250 stain (enough to float the gel) and shake gently [2][4]. (3 h to overnight)
  • Bands appear within minutes to an hour; heavily loaded bands show early, but maximum sensitivity needs several hours or an overnight stain [2][4].
  • Pour the stain into a labeled waste container. Destain with at least 100 mL of deionized water, shaking gently [2][3]. (30 min)
  • Replace the water two to three times, 30 min each, until the background is clear. A final water wash can last several hours without loss of bands [2][4]. (1–2 h)
  • Optional rapid method: microwave the gel in water for 30 s, shake 3–5 min, repeat once, then microwave 10 s in G-250 stain and shake 15–30 min. Never let the liquid boil [3]. (30–45 min)
  • Skip all microwave steps if the gel is going to mass spectrometry. Use two to three 10 min water washes instead and stain longer [3].
  • Place the gel on a white light box or flatbed scanner, remove air bubbles and bits of debris, and image it at 300 dpi or higher in color or grayscale. (5 min)
  • Save images in an uncompressed format (TIFF). Include the marker lane and a scale bar, and record exposure settings. (3 min)
  • For short-term storage, seal the gel in a bag with 1–2 mL of 5–7% acetic acid or water; it keeps for weeks at room temperature and months at 4 °C [1][4]. (5 min)
  • For longer storage of colloidally stained gels, use 20% ammonium sulfate at 4 °C; gels left in plain water for more than a few days lose sensitivity [4].
  • To excise bands for mass spectrometry, use a clean scalpel, wear gloves and cut as little blank gel as possible. Place each piece in a low-binding 1.5 mL tube with water [3].
  • Discard used stains and destains into a labeled flammable-solvent waste container. Wash trays with water and let them dry [1].
  • After fixing, the gel can sit in fixing solution overnight at room temperature in a sealed box.
  • Stained gels can be stored for days to months as described in the storage steps [1][4].
  • Excised gel bands in water can be kept at −20 °C until digestion.
  • Blue protein bands against a pale or clear background; marker bands should be sharp and match expected sizes.
  • Classic R-250 typically shows about 0.1–0.5 µg per band; colloidal G-250 can reveal about 10–30 ng per band (formulation and protein dependent) [2][5].
  • Band intensity roughly follows protein amount but only over a limited range, so calibrate with standards before attempting quantification [5].
  • Load a protein marker or a known amount of BSA (for example 0.05, 0.1 and 0.5 µg) to judge sensitivity and linearity.
  • Include a blank lane (sample buffer only) to check for stain artifacts.
  • Process an unstained gel strip in parallel if verifying that destaining does not remove bands.
ProblemLikely causeSolution
High blue backgroundInsufficient destaining, old or dye-saturated destain, or R-250 gel left in stain overnightReplace the destain more often, add absorbent paper to the box, and extend destaining; for colloidal stains wash longer in water [1][2].
Faint or missing bandsToo little protein, residual SDS, or stain time too shortLoad more sample, wash the gel in water before colloidal staining, and extend staining time [3][4].
Bands vanish after destainingOver-destaining in acid/methanol or poor fixationKeep destain time under about 24 h and stop once the background is pale; fix small proteins for the full recommended time [1].
Blue precipitate or speckles on the gelDye aggregates in old or unfiltered stainFilter the R-250 stain or prepare fresh colloidal stain, and keep the lid closed [1].
Uneven or patchy stainingGel stuck to the tray or too little solutionUse at least 10 gel volumes, shake continuously, and make sure the gel floats freely [1][4].
Gel shrinks or cracks during destainingHigh methanol or acid content on thin or low-percentage gelsHandle the gel gently and rehydrate in water or 5% acetic acid before imaging.
Weak peptide recovery in mass spectrometryHeating or cross-linking during stainingAvoid microwaving, use neutral solutions, and wash thoroughly with water [3].
  • Methanol is flammable and toxic if swallowed, inhaled or absorbed; work in a fume hood and keep away from ignition sources.
  • Glacial acetic acid is corrosive and its vapor irritates eyes and lungs; wear gloves, goggles and a lab coat.
  • Concentrated phosphoric acid and hydrochloric acid cause burns; add acid to water slowly and handle in a fume hood.
  • Do not boil flammable solvents in a microwave; heat only dye in water or aqueous solutions and use loosely covered boxes [3][4].
  • Collect used stain and destain as hazardous flammable waste according to institutional rules; do not pour down the drain.
  • Acrylamide residues in gels are neurotoxic; wear gloves and dispose of gels as chemical waste.
