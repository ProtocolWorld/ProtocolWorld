Plasmid Midiprep and Maxiprep

About 4–5 h after an overnight culture · 2026-11-18

Alkaline lysis and anion-exchange purification of 100 µg to about 1 mg of plasmid DNA from 25–500 mL of E. coli culture, with culture volumes, buffer volumes, centrifugation settings and troubleshooting for midi and maxi scale.

⬇ Word (.docx)⬇ PowerPoint (.pptx)

Purpose

To purify 100 µg to about 1 mg of endotoxin-reduced, transfection- and sequencing-grade plasmid DNA from 25–500 mL of E. coli culture using alkaline lysis and an anion-exchange resin.

Principle

Cells from a recA⁻ endA⁻ E. coli host such as DH5α or XL1-Blue are lysed with NaOH and SDS, which denatures chromosomal DNA and proteins, while the covalently closed plasmid stays renaturable. Neutralization with potassium acetate precipitates SDS, protein and chromosomal DNA, leaving plasmid in the cleared lysate [1][2]. Plasmid DNA binds the positively charged resin at moderate salt, RNA and protein are washed away at higher salt, and DNA is eluted at high salt and concentrated by isopropanol precipitation [1][2]. Plasmid copy number sets the culture volume: high-copy plasmids (pUC, pcDNA) need less than low-copy plasmids (pBR322 derivatives, cosmids) [1][3].

Equipment

  • Shaking incubator (37 °C, 225–300 rpm)
  • High-speed refrigerated centrifuge with rotor for 50 mL polypropylene tubes (up to 20,000 × g)
  • Column rack or stand
  • Polypropylene 15 mL and 50 mL tubes (not polycarbonate)
  • Spectrophotometer or NanoDrop
  • Agarose gel electrophoresis system

Procedure

  1. Pick a single colony into 2–5 mL of LB plus antibiotic in a tube at least 4 × the volume. Shake at 37 °C and 225–300 rpm for about 8 h [1][3]. (8 h)
  2. Dilute the starter 1:500 to 1:1000 into selective LB. High-copy: 25 mL (midi) or 100 mL (maxi). Low-copy: 100 mL (midi) or 500 mL (maxi) [1][3]. (5 min)
  3. Grow at 37 °C with vigorous shaking for 12–16 h (low-copy plasmids about 20 h). Aim for an A600 of 1–1.5, before deep stationary phase [1][2]. (12–16 h)
  4. Harvest cells at 4,000–6,000 × g for 10–15 min at 4 °C. Pour off all the medium. Pellets can be frozen at −20 °C at this point [1][3]. (20 min)
  5. Resuspend the pellet completely in resuspension buffer with RNase A: 4 mL (midi) or 10 mL (maxi) of P1; the HiPure kit uses 10 mL R3 for both. No clumps should remain [1][3]. (5 min)
  6. Add an equal volume of lysis buffer and invert 4–6 times. Do not vortex. Incubate at room temperature for 5 min; never exceed 5 min. The lysate becomes clear and viscous [1][3]. (5 min)
  7. Add an equal volume of neutralization buffer and mix at once by inverting 4–6 times. With P3, incubate on ice for 15 min (midi) or 20 min (maxi) [3]. (15–20 min)
  8. Clear the lysate: spin at 20,000 × g for 30 min at 4 °C, transfer the supernatant, and spin again for 15 min. Alternatively filter through a cartridge or filter module [1][3]. (45 min)
  9. Equilibrate the column with equilibration buffer by gravity: 4 mL for a midi column or 10 mL for a maxi column (HiPure: 15 mL or 30 mL EQ1) [1][3]. (5 min)
  10. Apply the cleared lysate to the column and let it drain by gravity. Do not let the resin run dry before loading. Flow can take 10–15 min [1][3]. (15 min)
  11. Wash with wash buffer: 2 × 10 mL (midi) or 2 × 30 mL (maxi) of QC. HiPure uses 20 mL or 50 mL of W8. This removes RNA, protein and carbohydrates [1][3]. (15 min)
  12. Elute into a fresh polypropylene tube with 5 mL (midi) or 15 mL (maxi) of elution buffer. Do not use polycarbonate tubes, which dissolve in isopropanol [1][3]. (5 min)
  13. Add 0.7 volumes of room-temperature isopropanol (3.5 mL midi, 10.5 mL maxi). Mix and spin at 12,000–15,000 × g for 30 min at 4 °C. Mark the tube; the pellet is glassy [1][3]. (35 min)
  14. Decant carefully. Wash the pellet with room-temperature 70% ethanol (2–3 mL midi, 5 mL maxi) and spin at 12,000–15,000 × g for 5–10 min at 4 °C [1][3]. (15 min)
  15. Remove the ethanol and air-dry for 5–10 min. Do not over-dry. Dissolve in 100–200 µL (midi) or 200–500 µL (maxi) of TE pH 8.0 or 10 mM Tris-HCl pH 8.5 [1][3]. (15 min)
  16. Measure A260/A280 (target 1.8–1.9) and concentration, check 100–200 ng on a 0.8–1% agarose gel, and store at −20 °C in aliquots [1]. (20 min)

Pause points

  • The pellet from step 4 can be frozen at −20 °C for several days or weeks.
  • The eluate from step 12 can be kept at 4 °C overnight at most before precipitation.
  • The finished DNA is stable at −20 °C for years; avoid repeated freeze–thaw cycles.

Expected results

  • High-copy plasmid typically yields 2–6 µg per mL of overnight culture, so about 100–350 µg from a midi and 500–850 µg from a maxi [1].
  • A260/A280 of 1.8–1.9 and a mainly supercoiled band on an agarose gel with little RNA or chromosomal DNA.
  • Low-endotoxin DNA suitable for sequencing, transfection and in vitro transcription when pyrogen-free plastics are used [1].

Controls

  • Run uncut, cut and a known-concentration standard on an agarose gel to confirm size and supercoiled form
  • Keep a sample of the cleared lysate and flow-through to check binding efficiency if yield is low
  • Always include a blank (elution buffer) for spectrophotometer readings

Troubleshooting

ProblemLikely causeSolution
Low yieldCulture too small for a low-copy plasmid, old antibiotic, incomplete lysis, or cells over-grownScale up the culture and double buffer volumes above 200 mL; use fresh antibiotic and 12–16 h growth [1]
Genomic DNA contaminationVortexing or lysis longer than 5 minMix by gentle inversion and stop lysis at 5 min [1][3]
RNA in the sampleRNase A missing or degraded in resuspension bufferAdd fresh RNase A to the resuspension buffer; store at 4 °C [1]
Column clogs or flows slowlyPrecipitate carried overRe-centrifuge the lysate or use a filter cartridge [1]
DNA will not dissolve or loses qualityPellet over-dried or pipetted vigorouslyAir-dry only briefly; rinse tube walls and avoid repeated pipetting [3]

Safety and waste

  • Lysis buffer (NaOH/SDS) is caustic; wear gloves, lab coat and eye protection.
  • Isopropanol and ethanol are flammable; keep away from ignition sources.
  • Dispose of bacterial culture, used columns and tubes as biohazard waste after autoclaving or bleach inactivation; collect liquid column waste (salt and isopropanol) per institutional rules, and never mix bleach with acidic or alcohol-containing waste.
