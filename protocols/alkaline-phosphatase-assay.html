Alkaline Phosphatase Assay

Estimated time: About 2 h | Protocol World

To measure alkaline phosphatase (ALP) activity colorimetrically with p-nitrophenyl phosphate (pNPP), either for a purified enzyme in a cuvette (Part A) or for cell lysates, tissue extracts, serum and culture medium in a 96-well plate (Part B).

ALP hydrolyzes colorless pNPP at alkaline pH to inorganic phosphate and p-nitrophenol (pNP). At alkaline pH pNP is a yellow phenolate ion that absorbs strongly at 405 nm, so the rate of absorbance increase, or the end-point absorbance after a fixed time, is proportional to enzyme activity [1][2][3]. One unit is defined as the hydrolysis of 1 µmol of pNPP per minute under stated conditions (pH 9.8 and 37 °C for diethanolamine units) [1][2]. Activity is calculated either from the extinction coefficient of pNP (about 18.5 mM⁻¹ cm⁻¹ at 405 nm) [1] or from a pNP standard curve [3].

ItemDetails
Part A buffer: 1.0 M diethanolamine (DEA), 0.5 mM MgCl₂, pH 9.8 at 37 °CDissolve DEA (105 mg/mL final; about 12.4 g of 85% DEA per 100 mL), adjust to pH 9.8 with 5 M HCl and add 0.5 mL of 1 M MgCl₂ per liter. Make fresh and protect from light [1][2].
pNPP substrate, 0.67 MDissolve about 247 mg of pNPP disodium salt in 1 mL water. Make fresh and protect from light [1][2].
Enzyme solutionDilute ALP in cold DEA buffer to about 0.15 unit/mL immediately before use; start from a stock of 2000 units/mL or more for liquid samples [1].
Part B kit-style reagents (end-point)ALP assay buffer, 5 mM pNPP (2 tablets in 5.4 mL assay buffer, good for 12 h on ice), enzyme mix and stop solution, for example the Abcam ab83369 kit [3].
p-Nitrophenol (pNP) standard, 1 mMPrepare from the kit pNPP standard: 40 µL of 5 mM standard plus 160 µL assay buffer. Use within 4 h [3].
Cold PBS pH 7.4For washing cells and tissue.
SamplesPart B: 1 × 10⁵ cells or 10 mg of tissue; serum or plasma (without EDTA or citrate); culture medium (phenol red-free preferred) [3][4].
96-well clear flat-bottom plateNot tissue-culture treated; one well per standard, sample and background control [3][4].
  • Spectrophotometer with 405 nm and a 37 °C cuvette holder (Part A)
  • Microplate reader with 405 nm filter and a plate shaker (Part B)
  • Dounce homogenizer for tissue
  • Refrigerated microcentrifuge
  • Calibrated micropipettes and a multichannel pipette
  • Water bath or incubator at 37 °C
  • Ice bucket
  • Part A. Prepare fresh DEA buffer, 0.67 M pNPP and enzyme diluted to about 0.15 unit/mL in cold buffer. Pre-warm buffer and spectrophotometer to 37 °C [1]. (20 min)
  • Pipette into 3 mL cuvettes: Test = 2.91 mL buffer + 0.05 mL pNPP. Blank = 2.95 mL buffer + 0.05 mL pNPP. Mix by inversion, equilibrate to 37 °C and follow A405 until constant [1]. (5 min)
  • Add 0.04 mL of enzyme dilution to the Test cuvette (3.00 mL total; final about 11 mM pNPP and about 0.006 unit). Mix by inversion and record A405 for about 5 min [1]. (5 min)
  • Take the maximum linear rate (ΔA405/min) over at least four points in a 1-minute window for Test and Blank. Run triplicates with 0.04, 0.045 and 0.05 mL of enzyme [1]. (10 min)
  • Part A: units/mL = (ΔA405/min Test − Blank) × 3.00 × df ÷ (18.5 × 0.04); 3.00 mL = assay volume, df = dilution factor, 0.04 mL = enzyme volume [1]. (5 min)
  • Part B. Cells: wash about 1 × 10⁵ cells in cold PBS, resuspend in 50 µL assay buffer and spin at top speed for 15 min at 4 °C. Keep the supernatant on ice [3]. (30 min)
  • Tissue: wash 10 mg in cold PBS, homogenize in 4–6 volumes of assay buffer on ice (10–50 strokes), spin 15 min at 4 °C. Dilute serum or plasma 10-fold in assay buffer [3]. (30 min)
  • Standards (duplicate): 0, 4, 8, 12, 16 and 20 nmol pNP per well in 120 µL, then add 10 µL enzyme mix per standard well; no pNPP is added to standards [3]. (15 min)
  • Samples: add 2–80 µL sample per well, adjusted to 80 µL with assay buffer. For colored samples, add background wells with 20 µL stop solution before the pNPP [3]. (10 min)
  • Add 50 µL of 5 mM pNPP to the sample wells (130 µL), mix gently and incubate for 60 min at 25 °C protected from light [3]. (60 min)
  • Add 20 µL stop solution to every sample well (final 150 µL), shake gently and read OD 405 nm immediately [3]. (5 min)
  • Subtract the blank (standard 0), plot the standard curve and read nmol pNP (B). Activity = B ÷ (60 min × sample mL) × dilution factor, in nmol/min/mL (= mU/mL) [3]. (15 min)
  • Kinetic alternative: mix 150–195 µL working reagent (200 µL buffer pH 10.5, 5 µL 0.2 M Mg acetate, 2 µL 1 M pNPP) with 5–50 µL sample; read A405 at 0 and 4 min [4]. (10 min)
  • Cell and tissue extracts can be frozen in aliquots at −80 °C; fresh samples are preferred [3].
  • ALP in serum is stable for 48 h at 4 °C and 2 months at −20 °C [4].
  • Do not pause after pNPP is added or after the stop solution.
  • Part A: a linear increase in A405 for the first few minutes in the Test cuvette and a flat Blank; typical diluted enzyme gives ΔA405 of a few hundredths per minute [1].
  • Part B: a linear standard curve of OD 405 against nmol pNP, with sample readings inside the 0–20 nmol range; the kit detects about 10–250 µU per well [3].
  • Kinetic plate assay is linear up to about 800 U/L; dilute samples above that and multiply by the dilution factor [4].
  • Blank without enzyme (Part A) or standard 0 (Part B) to correct for spontaneous pNPP hydrolysis [1][3]
  • Sample background control for colored or turbid samples (stop solution added before substrate) [3]
  • Positive control: a known amount of purified ALP
  • Heat-treated sample (for example 95 °C for 10 min) or an inhibitor such as levamisole as a specificity check (not specified in the cited manuals)
  • Duplicate or triplicate wells for every standard and sample
ProblemLikely causeSolution
No signalInactive enzyme, wrong pH, or inhibitors in the sampleUse fresh buffer; avoid EDTA, oxalate, fluoride and citrate, which inhibit ALP [3][4]
Reading above the standard curveToo much sample or incubation too longRepeat with less sample or a higher dilution rather than changing the time much [3]
High blankOld pNPP, light exposure, or colored samplesMake pNPP fresh, protect from light, use phenol red-free medium and a sample background control [1][3]
Non-linear rate (Part A)Enzyme too concentrated or substrate depletedDilute the enzyme further and use the first linear minute [1]
Poor replicatesPipetting error or delayed reading after stoppingUse a multichannel pipette, add reagents quickly and read within minutes [4]
  • Diethanolamine is an irritant and harmful if absorbed: wear gloves and eye protection.
  • Concentrated HCl and NaOH-type stop solutions are corrosive; handle them in a fume hood where needed and wear splash protection.
  • pNPP and p-nitrophenol are harmful if swallowed or absorbed; do not touch pNPP tablets with bare hands [3].
  • Treat serum, plasma and human cell samples as potentially infectious and follow your institution's biosafety rules.
  • Collect assay waste containing pNP and DEA as chemical waste; do not pour it down the drain.
  • Refer to the safety data sheets for every reagent [1].
