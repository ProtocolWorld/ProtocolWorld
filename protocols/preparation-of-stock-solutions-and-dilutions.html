Preparation of Stock Solutions and Dilutions

Estimated time: 1–2 h | Protocol World

To calculate, prepare, label and store accurate stock solutions, and to make working dilutions and serial dilutions from them.

A solution's molarity is moles of solute per liter of final solution, so the solid is dissolved in less than the final volume and then brought to volume (QS) [3]. Dilutions follow C₁V₁ = C₂V₂, and the total of a serial dilution is the product of each step factor, not their sum [1][3]. Errors usually come from unit mismatches, wrong molecular weights such as hydrate forms, and pipetting error at small volumes [1][3].

  • Write the target: compound, final concentration, volume, solvent. Look up MW on the bottle, using the hydrated MW if it is a hydrate [1][3]. (5 min)
  • Calculate mass = molarity (mol/L) × MW (g/mol) × volume (L). Example: 0.1 M × 58.44 × 0.5 L = 2.922 g [3].
  • Weigh the solid on a balance into a clean beaker and record the actual mass [3]. (5 min)
  • Dissolve in about 70–80% of the final volume of water with stirring. Adding all the solvent at once ignores the volume the solid displaces [3]. (10 min)
  • If a buffer is needed, adjust pH with a calibrated meter, adding acid or base slowly. Confirm the target pH against the recipe source [3][4].
  • Transfer to a volumetric flask or cylinder and add water to the final volume (QS). Invert to mix [3]. (5 min)
  • If sterility is needed, filter through 0.22 µm or autoclave stable solutions. Make heat- or light-labile compounds fresh or aliquot them and store at −20 °C [1].
  • Label the container (name, concentration, solvent, date, initials) and store appropriately; aliquot expensive or labile stocks [1].
  • To dilute, use V₁ = C₂V₂ / C₁. Example: 1 mM in 100 mL from a 100 mM stock needs 1 mL stock plus 99 mL diluent [1]. Units must match on both sides [3].
  • For a 1:10 serial dilution, put 9 mL diluent in each tube, move 1 mL stock into tube 1, mix well, then move 1 mL on to tube 2, changing tips each time [1][2]. (15 min)
  • Check the math: five 1:10 steps give 1:100,000; do not add the factors [1][2]. For viable counts, multiply colonies by the dilution factor and divide by the volume plated [2].
  • After weighing: close the container and continue within the same session.
  • After QS and labeling: store the stock as described; dilutions of labile compounds should be made fresh [1].
  • A clear solution at the stated concentration and pH, labeled and dated.
  • Working dilutions that reproduce expected readings in downstream assays.
  • A recorded calculation that another person can follow and check.
  • Check final pH of buffers with a calibrated meter [4].
  • Measure a known standard (e.g., absorbance) to confirm a dilution series is linear.
  • Write the dilution convention (1:10 = 1 part plus 9 parts) on the sheet, since authors use it differently [2].
