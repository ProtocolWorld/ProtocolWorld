Poly(A) Selection for RNA-seq

General Lab Methods · About 1.5–2 h · 2026-11-17

Oligo(dT) magnetic beads capture polyadenylated mRNA from eukaryotic total RNA (mammalian cells as the example) so that rRNA is removed before RNA-seq library prep. This generic workflow uses two binding rounds for high purity; kit-specific values are labeled.

Poly(A) Selection for RNA-seq

Estimated time: About 1.5–2 h | Protocol World

To enrich polyadenylated mRNA from high-quality eukaryotic total RNA (for example 1–5 µg with the NEB module or 25–1000 ng with the Illumina workflow, from mammalian cells) before fragmentation and cDNA library construction for RNA-seq.

Oligo(dT) on magnetic beads base-pairs with the poly(A) tail of mature mRNA in a high-salt binding buffer after a brief heat denaturation. rRNA and other non-polyadenylated RNA stay in the supernatant and are washed away. A low-salt Tris buffer with heat releases the mRNA, and a second bind–wash–elute round raises purity [1][2][3]. Because capture relies on the tail, the method suits intact eukaryotic RNA only: bacterial RNA and non-polyadenylated transcripts (such as replication-dependent histone mRNAs and many lncRNAs) are lost, and degraded RNA shows 3′ bias. Choose rRNA depletion for those samples.

ItemDetails
High-quality total RNADNase-treated, intact (high RIN, commonly ≥ 7–8 in lab practice); 25–1000 ng with the Illumina workflow, 1–5 µg with the NEB module [1][3]
Oligo(dT)25 magnetic beadsKit-specific: NEB uses 20 µL beads per sample [1]; Dynabeads: 1 mg (200 µL) binds up to about 2 µg mRNA [2]
Binding bufferHigh salt. Example (Thermo lysis/binding buffer): 100 mM Tris-HCl pH 7.5, 500 mM LiCl, 10 mM EDTA, 1% LiDS, 5 mM DTT [2]
Wash bufferExample (Thermo Washing Buffer B): 10 mM Tris-HCl pH 7.5, 0.15 M LiCl, 1 mM EDTA [2]
Elution buffer10 mM Tris-HCl pH 7.5 [2]; kit Tris buffers in [1]
Nuclease-free waterTo adjust RNA volume to 50 µL [1]
QC reagentsRNA Pico chip or equivalent fragment analyzer; fluorometric RNA assay [1][3]
RNase decontamination solutionFor benches, pipettes and racks
  • Magnetic rack for 0.2 mL tubes or plates
  • Thermal cycler with programmable hold (65 °C, 80 °C, 4 °C, 25 °C)
  • Low-bind, nuclease-free 0.2 mL tubes
  • Filter tips and calibrated pipettes (2–200 µL)
  • Ice bucket
  • Bioanalyzer, TapeStation or similar
  • Mini centrifuge
  • Work RNase-free: wear gloves, clean the bench and pipettes, use filter tips. Keep reagents (not beads) on ice and bring binding and wash buffers to room temperature [2]. (10 min)
  • Dilute 1–5 µg total RNA to 50 µL with nuclease-free water in a 0.2 mL tube (NEB E7490 values); for Illumina Stranded mRNA Prep use 25–1000 ng in 25 µL [1][3].
  • Resuspend the oligo(dT) beads fully. Move 20 µL per sample (NEB value) to a new tube, add 100 µL binding buffer and mix by pipetting [1].
  • Place on the magnet at room temperature until clear (about 2 min) and discard the supernatant. Repeat the wash once, for two washes in total [1]. (5 min)
  • Resuspend washed beads in 50 µL binding buffer, add the 50 µL RNA, and mix thoroughly [1].
  • Denature and anneal: 65 °C for 5 min, then hold at 4 °C. Remove tubes when the block reaches 4 °C [1][3]. (6 min)
  • Resuspend by gentle pipetting 6 times and incubate 5 min at room temperature; pipette 6 times again and incubate another 5 min [1]. (10 min)
  • Magnet for about 2 min until clear; discard the supernatant (it holds rRNA) without touching the beads [1].
  • Off the magnet, add 200 µL wash buffer and pipette 6 times. Return to the magnet for 2 min, discard the supernatant, and repeat once more for two washes [1]. (8 min)
  • First elution: add 50 µL Tris elution buffer, pipette gently 6 times, then 80 °C for 2 min and hold at 25 °C [1][3]. (5 min)
  • Second round: add 50 µL binding buffer, pipette gently 6 times, and incubate 5 min at room temperature. Pipette again and incubate 5 more min [1]. (10 min)
  • Magnet for about 2 min until clear and discard all supernatant. Wash once with 200 µL wash buffer, magnet 2 min, then discard the supernatant [1].
  • Spin tubes briefly, return to the magnet and remove every trace of wash buffer with a 10 µL tip; residual buffer can inhibit downstream enzymes [1][2].
  • Final elution: add 17 µL Tris buffer (NEB value), pipette 6 times, 80 °C for 2 min, hold at 25 °C. Place on the magnet right away for 2 min [1][3]. (5 min)
  • Transfer the mRNA-containing supernatant to a clean nuclease-free tube on ice. Pause point: store at −80 °C, or proceed at once to fragmentation and first-strand synthesis [1].
  • Optional QC: run 1 µL on an RNA Pico chip. Expect a broad mRNA smear with little 18S/28S rRNA signal [1].
  • Library prep: with Illumina Stranded mRNA Prep, elute in 25 µL, fragment on-bead with 94 °C for 8 min, and recover 17 µL supernatant for first-strand synthesis [3].
  • Pause points in the full library workflow: after second-strand cleanup, adapter-ligation cleanup or PCR, store at −25 to −15 °C for up to 7 days; final library for up to 30 days [3].
  • The protocol can be paused after sterilization and labeling; store solutions as described.
  • Eluted RNA shows a broad mRNA-size distribution with 18S and 28S rRNA peaks strongly reduced or absent on a Bioanalyzer RNA Pico trace [1].
  • Yield is typically only a small fraction of input because mRNA is about 1–5% of total RNA [2].
  • Poly(A)-selected libraries are rich in exonic mRNA reads and suitable for gene expression profiling in intact eukaryotic samples.
  • Run a high-quality reference total RNA (for example Universal Human Reference RNA) alongside samples [3]
  • Include a no-RNA (water) control through capture to detect contamination
  • Keep an aliquot of the unselected total RNA to compare rRNA peaks before and after
  • Check RNA integrity and DNase treatment before starting [3]
ProblemLikely causeSolution
Low mRNA yieldBeads overloaded, incomplete mixing or poor bindingUse more beads or less RNA, mix fully, extend binding time; elute longer or twice [2]
Residual rRNA in the eluateOnly one binding round or incomplete washingPerform the second binding round and remove all supernatant between washes [1]
Downstream enzyme reactions failCarryover of wash buffer or detergentRemove residual wash with a fine tip; some binding buffers contain LiDS, a strong enzyme inhibitor [1][2]
Strong 3′ bias in coveragePartially degraded RNA; truncated molecules keep the poly(A) tail but lose 5′ sequenceUse only intact RNA; for low-RIN or FFPE RNA choose rRNA depletion
Bead clumping or stickingBeads dried or frozen, or genomic DNA in the sampleKeep beads in liquid, never freeze; DNase-treat RNA [2][3]
Expected transcripts missingNon-polyadenylated RNA (histone mRNAs, many lncRNAs, bacterial RNA)Use rRNA depletion for these targets and for prokaryotes
  • Wear gloves and a lab coat; RNase contamination from skin ruins the sample.
  • Some binding buffers contain LiDS, DTT or EDTA, and bead storage buffers contain sodium azide: check the SDS, avoid skin contact, and flush azide waste with plenty of water [2].
  • Use only as research reagents; follow institutional rules for waste.
  • Hot thermal cycler blocks (65–94 °C) can cause burns.
