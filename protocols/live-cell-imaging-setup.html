Live-Cell Imaging Setup

Estimated time: 2–3 h setup + imaging run | Protocol World

To prepare cultured cells and a microscope so that time-lapse fluorescence imaging reports normal cell behavior rather than stress caused by temperature, pH, evaporation or excess light.

Cells imaged outside a CO₂ incubator quickly drift away from physiological conditions: bicarbonate-buffered medium turns alkaline, small volumes evaporate, and the objective can pull the sample several degrees below the set temperature [1][2][4]. Excitation light adds a second stress through photobleaching and reactive oxygen species, so the aim is the lowest light dose that still gives a usable signal [1][3]. A warm, humidified, stable system plus a phototoxicity check, such as mitotic timing, shows whether the cells behave normally [1].

Imaging mediumPhenol red–free medium with low autofluorescence (for example FluoroBrite DMEM) plus serum and supplements as for growth [3]

Immersion oilType matched to the objective and warmed with the system
  • Inverted fluorescence microscope (widefield, spinning-disk or confocal)
  • Stage-top incubator or heated enclosure set to 37 °C
  • Objective heater if using an immersion objective with a stage-top incubator
  • Premixed 5% CO₂/air supply with humidifier, or CO₂ mixer
  • LED or other low-heat light source with a fast shutter
  • Hardware autofocus or fixed focus beacons
  • Thermocouple probe or non-contact thermometer to read the temperature at the cells
  • Sensitive camera and acquisition software
  • Seed cells onto No. 1.5 glass-bottom dishes 24–48 h ahead so they are 50–70% confluent and healthy at the start of imaging [2]. (24–48 h before)
  • Turn on the heater, objective heater and gas supply 1–2 h early; set 37 °C and 5% CO₂ for mammalian cells. A heated enclosure can need longer to settle [1][2]. (1–2 h)
  • Place a mock dish of medium on the stage and focus the immersion objective on it so the whole system reaches thermal equilibrium (about 45–60 min) [1]. (45–60 min)
  • Check the temperature at the sample with a thermocouple taped to a spare dish or a non-contact thermometer; adjust the setpoints until the cells see 37 °C, not just the chamber [1]. (10 min)
  • Humidify the chamber with wetted wipes or a water reservoir and keep the gas premixed and warm, or seal the dish to prevent evaporation and osmolarity drift [1][2][4]. (5 min)
  • Replace the growth medium with pre-warmed imaging medium that lacks phenol red. Sensitive lines may recover better if you mix conditioned and fresh medium about 1:3 [1][3]. (5 min)
  • Add probes at the lowest workable concentration and incubate as the dye requires; wash with imaging medium if the dye is not fluorogenic [3]. (15–60 min)
  • Carry the dish to the microscope and seat it firmly in the holder; close the enclosure and let it settle for 15–30 min before the run to limit focus drift [1][2]. (15–30 min)
  • Find fields with transmitted light or the lowest fluorescence intensity; keep room lights off and minimize time under excitation during setup [2][3]. (10 min)
  • Set exposure, power and gain so the signal-to-noise is just adequate (not cover-quality), use the fewest channels and Z-planes, and avoid oversampling in time [1][3]. (10–20 min)
  • Set focus control: use hardware autofocus or fixed beacons rather than autofocus in a fluorescence channel on every frame, which can multiply light dose up to ~10-fold [2][3]. (5 min)
  • Run a short test (5–10 frames) and compare the first and last frames for bleaching, rounding, blebbing or drift; lower the dose if they differ [1][3]. (10 min)
  • Start the time-lapse. Check the first hour closely, then at intervals for focus, medium level and cell health [1]. (hours to days)
  • After the run, record the settings (power, exposure, interval, objective, temperature) so the experiment can be repeated [2].
  • Cells can wait in the incubator after seeding or labeling, but not after the medium has been swapped to imaging medium on the stage.
  • The microscope warm-up can be left running for several hours; do not interrupt a time-lapse once started.
  • Cells stay attached with normal morphology and motility throughout the run.
  • Mitotic timing (nuclear envelope breakdown to anaphase) matches incubator-grown cells; lengthened mitosis signals imaging stress [1].
  • Focus is stable within the depth of field; fluorescence decays slowly and evenly.
  • Unlabeled cells imaged with the same settings to check autofluorescence and light damage
  • Unilluminated dish kept in the same chamber to compare growth and morphology
  • Mitotic timing or division rate as a phototoxicity readout [1]
  • Fresh-versus-old frame comparison for bleaching, normalized to the first frame and to background [2]

  • Do not look into the light path or use damaged light guides; UV and laser light can injure eyes.
  • Heated stages and gas cylinders need securing; follow your facility's rules for compressed gas.
  • Handle cell lines, viruses and transfected cells under the biosafety level approved for your work.
  • Dispose of dyes and contaminated media as chemical or biohazardous waste according to your institution's rules.
