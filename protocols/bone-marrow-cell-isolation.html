Bone Marrow Cell Isolation

Estimated time: ≈ 1.5–2 h per 4–6 mice | Protocol World

To obtain a viable single-cell suspension of mouse bone marrow cells (about 4–8 × 10⁷ nucleated cells from femurs and tibias of one adult mouse) for flow cytometry, cell culture or cell sorting.

Long bones contain bone marrow in the medullary cavity. Cutting the bone ends and flushing the cavity with cold buffer through a needle expels intact marrow plugs, which are dispersed through a cell strainer. Red blood cells are removed by hypotonic ammonium chloride (ACK) lysis, which spares nucleated cells, followed by washing and counting [1][3]. Alternatively, spinning the bones in a punctured microcentrifuge tube releases the marrow without flushing [2]. Keeping cells cold and working quickly preserves viability [1].

ItemDetails
MiceAdult (6–12 weeks) mice, for example C57BL/6; follow your approved animal protocol [1][2]
Flush bufferIce-cold HBSS or PBS without Ca²⁺/Mg²⁺ (optionally with 2% FBS) [1][4]
Culture mediumDMEM or RPMI 1640 with 10% FBS for resuspension [1][3]
ACK lysing buffer0.16 M NH₄Cl, 10 mM KHCO₃, 0.13 mM EDTA in sterile water. For 500 mL: 4.28 g NH₄Cl, 0.5 g KHCO₃, 0.024 g disodium EDTA; filter and store at 4 °C [1]
70% ethanolTo disinfect fur and instruments
Syringes and needles10 mL syringes with 23–26 G needles [1][3]
Cell strainers70 µm nylon, with 5 mL syringe plunger
TubesSterile 50 mL and 15 mL conical tubes; 0.5 mL and 1.5 mL tubes for the optional centrifugation method
Counting reagentsTrypan blue or a fluorescent viability dye such as 7-AAD (1 µg/mL) [4]
Dissection toolsSterile scissors, forceps, scalpel and gauze; dissecting board or pins
  • Biosafety cabinet
  • Refrigerated centrifuge with swing-bucket rotor (300–600 × g) and microcentrifuge (≥ 10,000 × g)
  • Hemocytometer or automated cell counter
  • Ice bucket
  • CO₂ euthanasia chamber (or other IACUC-approved method)
  • 37 °C water bath (optional, for lysis)
  • Prepare ice-cold flush buffer and ACK buffer, label tubes and put everything on ice. Fill 10 mL syringes with 23–26 G needles [1][3]. (15 min)
  • Euthanize the mouse by an IACUC-approved method (for example CO₂ followed by a secondary method) and spray the abdomen and hind limbs with 70% ethanol [1][2]. (5 min)
  • Cut the skin and dislodge each hind limb at the hip joint. Remove muscle and tissue with scissors and gauze; cut below the knee to separate the tibia [1][2]. (10 min)
  • Clean the femur and tibia of all muscle, trim away the epiphyses with scissors, and place the bones in ice-cold buffer in a Petri dish on ice [1][2]. (10 min)
  • Hold each bone with forceps over a 50 mL tube topped with a 70 µm strainer. Insert the needle into the cavity and flush with 5–10 mL cold buffer until the bone turns white [1][3]. (10 min)
  • Flush from both ends. If the yield is expected to be below 10⁷ cells, scrape the inner surface with the needle and flush again with about 5 mL [1].
  • Disperse the marrow by pressing it through the strainer with a 5 mL plunger and rinse the strainer with 5 mL buffer [1][3]. (5 min)
  • Pellet cells at 300–600 × g for 5 min at 4 °C (for example 350 × g) and discard the supernatant [1][3][4]. (5 min)
  • Lyse red cells: resuspend the pellet in 1 mL ACK buffer per mouse and incubate 5 min at room temperature (or 2 min at 37 °C) [1]. (5 min)
  • Stop lysis by adding 10 mL cold buffer or medium containing serum, then pellet at 350 × g for 5 min at 4 °C [1][3]. (10 min)
  • Resuspend in 5–10 mL cold buffer or medium and pass again through a fresh 70 µm strainer to remove clumps [1][4]. (5 min)
  • Count cells with a hemocytometer or automated counter and assess viability with trypan blue or 7-AAD [3][4]. (10 min)
  • Adjust to the density needed (for example 1 × 10⁷ cells/mL for staining) and keep on ice; use within about 5 h for best viability [1][4].
  • Optional rapid method: cut off the epiphyses and place each bone, cut end down, in a 0.5 mL tube pierced with an 18 G needle, nested in a 1.5 mL tube [2]. (20 min)
  • Spin the nested tubes at ≥ 10,000 × g for 15 s, then resuspend the marrow pellet in buffer or medium [2].
  • The protocol can be paused after sterilization and labeling; store solutions as described.
  • Expected yield is about 4–5 × 10⁷ nucleated cells from the femurs and tibias of one adult mouse, and up to about 8 × 10⁷ has been reported for femur preparations [1][4].
  • Viability is usually above 90% by trypan blue.
  • The suspension is single cells with little red cell contamination; cells stay viable on ice for at least 5 h [1].
  • Unstained and viability-dye controls if the cells go to flow cytometry [4]
  • Count before and after lysis to calculate recovery
  • Compare cell yield between mice of the same strain, age and sex

  • Animal work requires approval from your Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee (IACUC) and training in humane euthanasia.
  • Wear gloves, lab coat and eye protection; sharps (needles, scalpel) must go into a sharps container.
  • Treat all mouse tissue as potentially infectious; dispose of carcasses, tissue and solutions according to institutional rules.
  • ACK and 70% ethanol are low hazard but should be handled with gloves.
