ELISpot Assay

3 days (about 6 hours hands-on) · 2026-11-18

Count cytokine-secreting cells (IFN-gamma, human PBMC) on PVDF plates: capture antibody, stimulation, detection, BCIP/NBT development and SFU calculation.

⬇ Word (.docx)⬇ PowerPoint (.pptx)

Purpose

To count individual cytokine-secreting cells, for example IFN-γ-secreting T cells in human peripheral blood mononuclear cells (PBMCs), after antigen or mitogen stimulation. The method is far more sensitive than a bulk ELISA because each secreting cell leaves its own spot.

Principle

Cells are cultured on a PVDF membrane pre-coated with a capture antibody against the cytokine. Secreted cytokine is captured next to the cell that made it. After the cells are washed away, a biotinylated detection antibody and a streptavidin–enzyme conjugate bind the captured cytokine, and a precipitating substrate such as BCIP/NBT turns each site into a visible spot [1][2][3]. One spot corresponds to one secreting cell, so results are reported as spot-forming units (SFU) per million cells. Antibody concentrations, cell numbers and incubation times are starting points and must be optimized for each kit and cytokine [2][3].

Materials and reagents

ItemDetails
96-well PVDF ELISpot platesSterile, with underdrain; pre-coated plates are supplied with some kits [1][3]
Capture antibodyAnti-IFN-γ; 15 µg/mL, 100 µL per well is a general recommendation [2], or the kit's own dilution; coat overnight at 4 °C [3]
35% ethanol3.5 mL ethanol + 6.5 mL water per plate; 25 µL per well for pre-wetting [3]
PBS1×: 8 g NaCl, 0.2 g KH₂PO₄, 1.44 g Na₂HPO₄·2H₂O per liter, pH 7.4 [3]
Blocking mediumCulture medium containing 10% fetal calf serum (1 mL FCS + 9 mL medium) [3]
Cells and mediumPBMCs with at least 90% viability [1]; complete RPMI medium; cells prepared by Ficoll density gradient [1]
StimuliPositive control: PHA at 10 µg/mL [1], or PMA 1 ng/mL + ionomycin 500 ng/mL [3]. Antigen: for example a CEF peptide pool at the supplier's recommended concentration [2]. Negative control: medium only
Biotinylated detection antibodyAnti-IFN-γ in PBS with 1% BSA, 100 µL per well, at the kit's dilution [1][3]
Streptavidin–alkaline phosphatase100 µL per well, at the kit's dilution [1][3]
SubstrateBCIP/NBT (ready to use), 100 µL per well [1][3]

Equipment

  • Biosafety cabinet and CO₂ incubator (37 °C, 5% CO₂, humidified)
  • Centrifuge with swing-out rotor and cell counter or hemocytometer
  • Multichannel pipette and sterile reagent reservoirs
  • Automated ELISpot reader (or a dissecting microscope for manual counts)
  • Plate washer or squirt bottle; paper towels for blotting
  • 4 °C refrigerator

Procedure

  1. Day 1: Add 25 µL 35% ethanol to each well for 30 s. Empty the wells and wash 3 times with 200 µL sterile PBS. Do not let the membrane dry [3]. (15 min)
  2. Add 100 µL capture antibody in PBS to every well, seal the plate and incubate overnight at 4 °C [2][3]. (Overnight)
  3. Day 2: Wash once with 200 µL PBS, then add 100 µL blocking medium (10% FCS) per well and incubate for 2 h at room temperature. Wash 3 times with PBS [3]. (2.5 h)
  4. Isolate PBMCs by Ficoll gradient (30 min, 900 × g, 18–20 °C, no brake) and wash twice in HBSS (10 min, 400 × g). Count cells and confirm viability of at least 90% [1]. (1.5 h)
  5. Adjust cells to the chosen density, for example 2 × 10⁵ cells in 100 µL per well. Published ranges are 1 × 10⁵–2.5 × 10⁵ cells per well [3]; titrate for your assay. (10 min)
  6. Remove the blocking medium. Add stimuli and cells: antigen, positive control (PHA 10 µg/mL), and medium-only wells, all in triplicate [1][3]. (20 min)
  7. Incubate at 37 °C, 5% CO₂ for 16–24 h. Do not move, shake or stack the plates during incubation [1][2]. (16–24 h)
  8. Day 3: Empty the wells, add 200 µL PBS and incubate at 4 °C for 10 min to detach cells. Wash 3 times with PBS [3]. (20 min)
  9. Add 100 µL biotinylated detection antibody per well and incubate for 1.5 h at room temperature. Wash 3 times with PBS [3]. (1.5 h)
  10. Add 100 µL streptavidin–alkaline phosphatase per well and incubate for 1 h at room temperature. Wash 3 times with PBS [3]. (1 h)
  11. Peel off the plastic underdrain. Rinse both sides of the membrane 3 times under running distilled water and blot on paper towels [3]. (10 min)
  12. Add 100 µL BCIP/NBT per well and monitor by eye. Spots usually appear within 5–15 min; kit protocols allow up to 1 h [1][3]. (5–60 min)
  13. Stop by rinsing 3 times with running distilled water. Dry the plate in the dark: 60–90 min at room temperature, or 15–30 min at 37 °C [1][3]. (1–2 h)
  14. Count spots with an automated reader within 24 h, protected from light. Calculate SFU per 10⁶ cells after subtracting the mean of the medium-only wells [1][3]. (30 min)

Pause points

  • After step 2: coated plates can be kept at 4 °C overnight [3].
  • After step 13: dried plates can be stored wrapped in foil at 4 °C for up to 2 weeks, and spots can sharpen after overnight storage at 4 °C [3][4].
  • Do not pause once the cells are in the plate or during the antibody and substrate steps.

Expected results

  • Positive-control wells give many distinct, round, dark blue to purple spots; medium-only wells give very few or no spots.
  • Spot-forming units per million cells = (mean spots in stimulated wells − mean spots in medium-only wells) × (10⁶ / cells per well). Worked example (illustrative arithmetic): 2 × 10⁵ cells per well, 48 spots with antigen and 3 spots with medium give (48 − 3) × 5 = 225 SFU per 10⁶ PBMCs.
  • Reader outputs include spot number and also spot size or intensity, which reflect the amount of cytokine per cell [1][4].

Controls

  • Negative control: unstimulated cells in medium, same number of cells per well [2][3]
  • Positive control: a polyclonal activator such as PHA, anti-CD3 or PMA/ionomycin, to prove the cells and reagents work [1][2][3]
  • Medium-only wells without cells to check for false-positive spots from reagents [4]
  • Run every condition in replicate wells (triplicate is common practice)
  • Do not mix reagent lots or vendors within a plate [3]

Troubleshooting

ProblemLikely causeSolution
High background or dark membraneToo many cells, too long a substrate step, or inadequate washingPlate fewer cells, shorten development, wash more thoroughly and dry the plate completely [4]
Few or no spotsToo few cells, weak stimulus, old substrate or low antibody concentrationRaise the cell number, check the stimulus, use fresh substrate and verify antibody concentrations [4]
Blank patches in a wellMembrane dried after pre-wetting or cells not evenly mixedKeep wells wet and mix the cell suspension before plating [4]
Merged, confluent spotsToo much capture antibody or stimulus, or too long an incubationReduce cell number or stimulus and shorten the culture time [4]
Cell-free centers or edge effectsForceful washing, or plates stacked during incubationWash gently and do not stack plates in the incubator [4]
Membrane damagedToo much ethanol or too long a pre-wetUse only the stated volume (for example 15 µL of 35% ethanol per well for some plate types) for no more than about 1 min [2]

Safety and waste

  • Human blood and PBMCs are potentially infectious. Use universal precautions, work in a biosafety cabinet and follow your institution's biosafety rules.
  • PMA is a skin irritant and tumor promoter; wear gloves and handle it as chemical waste.
  • Ethanol is flammable. BCIP/NBT substrates contain organic solvents and may be harmful if swallowed or inhaled; wear gloves and handle according to the safety data sheet.
  • Disinfect liquid waste (cells and media) with bleach before disposal and discard plates as biohazard waste.
