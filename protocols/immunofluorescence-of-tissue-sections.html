Immunofluorescence of Tissue Sections

2 days + 2–3 days prep · 2026-11-15

Detect a protein in fixed mouse tissue sections: fixation, cryosectioning, antigen retrieval, blocking, primary and fluorescent secondary antibodies, DAPI and antifade mounting.

⬇ Word (.docx)⬇ PowerPoint (.pptx)

Purpose

To detect and localize a protein in fixed mouse tissue sections with fluorophore-labeled antibodies, and to record its distribution relative to cell nuclei by fluorescence microscopy.

Principle

Aldehyde fixation preserves tissue structure and antigens, and sucrose cryoprotection prevents ice-crystal damage during freezing [1][2]. Detergent and serum permeabilize the section and block nonspecific binding sites. A primary antibody then binds the target, and a fluorophore-conjugated secondary antibody raised against the primary's host species makes the signal visible [1][2][3]. DAPI marks nuclei, an antifade mountant slows photobleaching, and no-primary controls show whether the signal is specific [3][4]. Paraffin sections follow the same staining logic but need deparaffinization and almost always antigen retrieval [3][4].

Equipment

  • Cryostat (−20 °C chamber)
  • Humidified slide chamber
  • Fume hood
  • Microwave or decloaking chamber for retrieval
  • Hydrophobic barrier pen
  • Fluorescence or confocal microscope
  • −80 °C freezer, 4 °C refrigerator

Procedure

  1. Perfuse the anesthetized mouse through the heart with PBS and then 4% PFA, following your approved animal protocol [1]. (20 min)
  2. Dissect the tissue and post-fix in 4% PFA at 4 °C with gentle agitation: 2 h for brain [1], up to overnight for larger tissue [6]. (2 h–overnight)
  3. Wash 3 × 10 min in cold PBS. Tissue can stay at 4 °C for a few days; add 0.05% sodium azide for longer storage [1]. (30 min)
  4. Cryoprotect in 10%, then 20%, then 30% sucrose in PBS at 4 °C, 8–12 h each or until the tissue sinks [1]. A single overnight step in 30% sucrose also works [6]. (1–2 days)
  5. Rinse off surface sucrose, embed in OCT, and freeze on dry ice or in dry-ice-chilled isopentane. Store blocks at −80 °C [1][5]. (20 min)
  6. Equilibrate the block in the cryostat at −20 °C for 30 min, then cut 10–20 µm sections onto charged slides [1][6]. (1 h)
  7. Air-dry slides at room temperature for 15 min to overnight. Store at −20 °C or −80 °C if staining later [1][5].
  8. Warm frozen slides to room temperature, then rinse 3 × 5 min in PBS. Draw a hydrophobic barrier around each section. (20 min)
  9. Optional antigen retrieval: boil sections in 0.1 M citrate (pH 6.0) for 15 s [1], or microwave in 1× citrate solution and hold near 70 °C for 20 min [2]; cool, wash. (30–50 min)
  10. Permeabilize and block 1 h at room temperature in humidified chamber: 5–10% normal serum + 0.3% Triton X-100 in PBS [1][2][3]. (1 h)
  11. Drain the block and add 100–300 µL primary antibody in diluent per slide. Incubate overnight at 4 °C in a humidified chamber [1][2][3]. (Overnight)
  12. Wash 3 × 10 min in PBS at room temperature, keeping sections wet [1]. (30 min)
  13. Add secondary antibody (about 1:300) for 1–2 h at room temperature in the dark [1][2]. (1–2 h)
  14. Wash 3 × 10 min in PBS in the dark. (30 min)
  15. Optional: quench lipofuscin autofluorescence with TrueBlack for 30 s, then wash 3 × 5 min in PBS. Do this after all detergent steps [3][4]. (10 min)
  16. Counterstain with DAPI (about 300 nM) for 2–5 min, then rinse 3 × in PBS [7]. (10 min)
  17. Mount with antifade medium and a No. 1.5 coverslip. Cure overnight at room temperature in the dark, then seal the edges [2][4]. (Overnight)
  18. Image soon, using identical exposure settings for all samples. Keep slides flat at 4 °C in the dark, or at −20 °C for longer [1][2]. (1–2 h)

Pause points

  • After step 3, fixed tissue can wait several days at 4 °C.
  • After step 5, OCT blocks keep at −80 °C for months.
  • After step 7, unstained slides can be stored at −20 °C or −80 °C.
  • After mounting (step 17), slides can be stored in the dark at 4 °C until imaging.

Expected results

  • Specific signal appears at the expected cellular location (for example membrane, cytoplasm or nucleus) with DAPI-labeled nuclei in the same field.
  • Staining is absent or very faint in the no-primary control and in negative tissue.
  • Tissue morphology is intact, without freezing holes, folds or lifting.

Controls

  • No-primary control: block, then secondary only, to reveal nonspecific secondary binding [3][4]
  • Unstained section to assess tissue autofluorescence
  • Isotype control for monoclonal primaries [4]
  • Positive tissue known to express the target, and knockout or known-negative tissue when available
  • Littermate controls processed on the same slide set; titrate a new primary across a dilution series [4]

Troubleshooting

ProblemLikely causeSolution
Weak or no signalAntibody not validated for IF, epitope masked, or sections dried outUse a validated antibody, add retrieval, keep sections wet throughout [2]
High backgroundInsufficient blocking, antibody too concentrated, or short washesLengthen block, titrate antibodies, wash longer [1][2]
Poor tissue morphologyPoor fixation or ice-crystal damageUse fresh PFA and graded sucrose [1]
Sections fall off slidesExpired slides or over-vigorous washingUse fresh charged slides, wash gently [1]
Bright speckles or green hazeLipofuscin or aldehyde autofluorescenceUse TrueBlack or far-red fluorophores [3][4]
Spotty background in paraffin sectionsIncomplete deparaffinizationRepeat xylene steps with fresh solvent [3]

Safety and waste

  • PFA is toxic and a suspected carcinogen: prepare and handle it in a fume hood and dispose of it as hazardous chemical waste [1].
  • DAPI is a potential mutagen: wear gloves and dispose of it according to local rules [7].
  • Xylene (paraffin work) is flammable and toxic: use in a fume hood and collect as solvent waste.
  • Dry ice and isopentane cause cold burns and are flammable; wear cold-protective gloves.
  • Carry out all animal work under an approved institutional protocol.
