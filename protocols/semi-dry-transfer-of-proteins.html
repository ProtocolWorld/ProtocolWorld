Semi-Dry Transfer of Proteins

Estimated time: About 1.5 h | Protocol World

To move proteins from an SDS-polyacrylamide gel onto a PVDF or nitrocellulose membrane in a compact horizontal stack that uses little buffer and takes under an hour, so the proteins can be detected by immunoblotting.

In a semi-dry (plate-electrode) blotter, the gel and membrane sit between buffer-soaked filter paper, and the paper itself acts as the buffer reservoir between two flat electrodes. SDS-coated proteins carry a net negative charge, so they migrate from the gel toward the anode and are captured by the membrane, which must therefore lie on the anode side of the gel [1][3]. Methanol strips SDS from proteins and improves binding to the membrane, whereas a low amount of SDS in the buffer helps large proteins leave the gel [1][3]. Because the buffer volume is small and the unit cannot be actively cooled, the current and time must be limited [1][6]. Cassette systems with pre-wetted ion-reservoir stacks use shorter programs (about 3–30 min depending on protein size) instead of loose filter paper [4].

ItemDetails
Gel after SDS-PAGERun until the dye front reaches the bottom; note the gel size (cm²) and acrylamide percentage
MembranePVDF (binding capacity about 160 µg/cm²) or nitrocellulose (about 80–100 µg/cm²); use 0.2 µm pores for proteins below about 20 kDa [1]
Filter paperThick blotting paper, cut to the gel size: 2 sheets of 2.45 mm paper or 6 sheets of 0.84 mm paper per side [1]
Transfer buffer (Bjerrum–Schafer-Nielsen)48 mM Tris, 39 mM glycine, 20% (v/v) methanol; per 1 L: 5.82 g Tris base, 2.93 g glycine, 200 mL methanol, water to 1 L. Final pH is about 9.2 (acceptable 9.0–9.4). Do not adjust the pH [1]
Optional SDSAdd 3.75 mL of 10% SDS per 1 L (0.0375%, 1.3 mM) for proteins above about 80 kDa; use PVDF and expect more current and heating [1][5]
Towbin buffer (alternative)25 mM Tris, 192 mM glycine, 20% methanol, pH about 8.3 (3.03 g Tris, 14.4 g glycine, 200 mL methanol per 1 L) [1]
100% methanolTo activate PVDF
Ponceau S stain0.1% (w/v) in 5% (v/v) acetic acid; for 100 mL use 100 mg Ponceau S, 95 mL water, 5 mL glacial acetic acid [2]
TBSTTris-buffered saline with 0.1% Tween 20, for washing
Roller or 10 mL serological pipetteTo remove air bubbles
  • Semi-dry blotter with plate electrodes (platinum-coated anode, stainless-steel cathode) or a cassette system
  • Power supply with constant-voltage and current-limit settings
  • Forceps and powder-free gloves
  • Shallow trays for soaking
  • Rocking platform
  • Scissors and a ruler
  • Imaging device or scanner for the stained membrane
  • Make 1 L of transfer buffer (Bjerrum–Schafer-Nielsen: 48 mM Tris, 39 mM glycine, 20% methanol, pH about 9.2) and chill it to room temperature or 4 °C [1]. (10 min)
  • Cut the membrane and filter paper to the gel size; handle membranes only with forceps and gloves. A membrane slightly larger than the gel is acceptable [1][3]. (5 min)
  • Activate PVDF in 100% methanol for 15–30 s until translucent, then move it into transfer buffer. Wet nitrocellulose directly in buffer from one edge to avoid trapped air [1][3]. (2 min)
  • Soak the membrane and the filter paper in transfer buffer for 5–20 min so that they are fully wet [1][3]. (10 min)
  • Remove the stacking gel, rinse the gel briefly in transfer buffer, and equilibrate it for about 15 min (10–20 min) so that it stops shrinking in methanol [1]. (15 min)
  • Place the soaked paper on the anode (+) plate, centered, then lay the membrane on it. Roll out air bubbles with a roller or a pipette [1][2]. (3 min)
  • Lay the equilibrated gel on the membrane. Do not move it after contact because proteins start to transfer at once. Roll gently again to remove bubbles at the gel–membrane interface [1]. (2 min)
  • Add the top filter paper stack, roll out bubbles, and wipe away any buffer that has spilled onto the plate. Close the cathode (−) lid [1]. (2 min)
  • Connect the red lead to the anode (+) and the black lead to the cathode (−). Reversed polarity corrodes the stainless-steel cathode [1]. (1 min)
  • Run a mini gel at constant 10–15 V for 15–30 min, a large gel at 15–25 V for 30–60 min (max 25 V, 2 h) [1]; or about 1.5 mA/cm² for 15–60 min [3]. (15–60 min)
  • Switch off the power supply and disconnect the leads. Remove the top paper and mark the membrane corner that matches lane 1 with a pencil [1]. (2 min)
  • Rinse the membrane in water for 1 min, then stain with 0.1% Ponceau S for 5 min with gentle agitation [2]. (7 min)
  • Rinse in water until bands are clear and photograph the membrane at once because the stain fades in about 10 min [2]. (5 min)
  • Destain with TBST or water for 3 × 10 min until the red color is gone, then proceed to blocking [2]. (30 min)
  • Optional: stain the used gel with Coomassie to see which proteins remain after transfer.
  • After the transfer, the dry membrane can be stored between filter paper at 4 °C for several days, or at −20 °C for longer. Wet PVDF again in methanol before blocking.
  • The Ponceau-stained membrane can be photographed and then destained at any time before blocking.
  • The Ponceau S stain shows evenly distributed red bands across all lanes, with the prestained marker visible on the membrane (sensitivity about 200 ng per band) [2].
  • The gel keeps little protein after transfer when stained with Coomassie, except for very large proteins.
  • Bands in the final immunoblot are sharp, with no white spots and no signal on the back of the membrane.
  • Prestained molecular-weight marker to confirm transfer and direction
  • Ponceau S total-protein stain to check even loading and transfer, and for normalization [2]
  • A blank (unloaded) lane to judge background
  • A second membrane placed behind the first to test for blow-through of small proteins (optional)

  • Methanol is toxic and flammable: handle it in a ventilated area and collect used buffer as flammable solvent waste according to your institution's rules.
  • Acetic acid in Ponceau S stain is corrosive: wear gloves and eye protection; collect used stain as chemical waste.
  • Use a grounded power supply, keep the voltage at or below 25 V and switch off the power before touching the unit [1].
  • Acrylamide gels contain unpolymerized acrylamide, a neurotoxin: wear gloves and dispose of gels as chemical waste.
  • Do not use chloroform, toluene, benzene, or acetone on the blotter, and do not immerse it [1].
