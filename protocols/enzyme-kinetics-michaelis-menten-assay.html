Enzyme Kinetics Michaelis-Menten Assay

Estimated time: 3–4 h | Protocol World

To determine the Michaelis constant (Km) and maximum velocity (Vmax) of an enzyme by measuring initial reaction rates over a range of substrate concentrations in a 96-well plate reader. The worked example uses E. coli β-galactosidase and the chromogenic substrate ONPG.

For many single-substrate enzymes the initial velocity follows v = Vmax[S]/(Km + [S]). Km is the substrate concentration giving half of Vmax. Velocities must be measured while little substrate has been consumed, so each progress curve is read continuously and only its early linear segment is used [1][3]. Linear conditions are generally accepted when less than about 5% of the substrate has been converted (up to roughly 20% is sometimes tolerated) and the rate scales with enzyme amount [5]. Product formation is followed as absorbance, converted to concentration with a product standard curve measured under the same conditions [2][4]. Km and Vmax are then obtained by nonlinear regression and cross-checked with linearized plots and residuals [1][3][4].

ItemDetails
2× assay buffer200 mM sodium phosphate pH 7.0, 2 mM MgCl₂, 100 mM β-mercaptoethanol (add fresh). Halving it gives the 1× buffer of 100 mM sodium phosphate, 1 mM MgCl₂, 50 mM β-mercaptoethanol [1]. The 2× buffer without β-mercaptoethanol can be stored at −20 °C.
ONPG (o-nitrophenyl-β-D-galactopyranoside)10 mM in 2× assay buffer, prepared fresh; protect from light. This gives 5 mM in the well. Make a two-fold dilution series in 2× buffer (see Procedure).
β-Galactosidase (E. coli)Reconstitute to 500 U/mL in cold distilled water; dilute fresh each day in distilled water to 0.2 U/mL and keep on ice (0.1 U/mL after mixing) [1].
o-Nitrophenol (ONP) standard1 mM stock in 1× assay buffer; dilute to 0, 25, 50, 100, 200 and 400 µM in 1× buffer to calibrate absorbance against product concentration [2][4].
Distilled waterFor enzyme dilutions and blanks
Clear, flat-bottom 96-well plateUse the same plate type and volume (200 µL) for standards and reactions so the path length is identical [4].
  • Microplate reader with kinetic mode and a 420 nm filter or monochromator, preferably with temperature control
  • Multichannel pipette (20–200 µL) and reagent reservoirs
  • Ice bucket
  • Analytical balance and pH meter
  • Software for nonlinear regression (GraphPad Prism, R, Python or similar)
  • Prepare 2× assay buffer and adjust to pH 7.0. Add β-mercaptoethanol just before use and keep the buffer at the assay temperature [1]. (20 min)
  • Make 10 mM ONPG in 2× buffer. Dilute it two-fold seven times in 2× buffer (10, 5, 2.5, 1.25, 0.625, 0.31, 0.16 mM), and keep a 0 mM tube of buffer only [1]. (15 min)
  • Dilute β-galactosidase on ice to 0.2 U/mL in distilled water just before the run and mix gently without foaming [1]. (5 min)
  • Plate the ONP standards (200 µL each, in triplicate) and read A420 to build the calibration curve. Calculate the slope in absorbance per µM [2][4]. (15 min)
  • Pipette 100 µL of each 2× ONPG dilution into plate rows in triplicate. Add a no-enzyme blank row for every substrate concentration [2]. (10 min)
  • Set the reader to 420 nm, kinetic mode, one read every 15–30 s for 10 min, at a fixed temperature (25 °C or 37 °C; record it). Preheat the reader [2]. (5 min)
  • Start all reactions together: add 100 µL of 0.2 U/mL enzyme to the enzyme wells and 100 µL water to the blank wells using a multichannel pipette. Final volume is 200 µL. (2 min)
  • Place the plate in the reader at once and start reading. Note the dead time between mixing and the first read, and avoid bubbles, which distort absorbance [5]. (10 min)
  • For each well, choose the steepest linear window in the early part of the curve (for example, a 3-point window) and record the slope in absorbance units per min [4]. (30 min)
  • Subtract the matching no-enzyme blank slope, then divide by the ONP standard slope to give velocity in µM per min [2][5]. (10 min)
  • Check initial-rate conditions: product formed in the fitting window should stay under about 5% of the starting substrate. If not, shorten the window or use less enzyme [5]. (10 min)
  • Check enzyme proportionality in a separate set: halving the enzyme should halve the rate. If it does not, the assay is not valid [5]. (30 min)
  • Plot v against [S] and fit v = Vmax[S]/(Km + [S]) by nonlinear regression. Report Km and Vmax with standard errors [1][3][4]. (20 min)
  • Cross-check with Lineweaver–Burk and Eadie–Hofstee plots (omit the 0 mM point) and inspect residuals. Treat the nonlinear fit as primary [2][3][4]. (20 min)
  • If the enzyme concentration is known, calculate kcat = Vmax/[E]total and kcat/Km. Record all assay conditions with the results [4]. (10 min)
  • Reagent preparation: the 2× buffer without β-mercaptoethanol can be stored at −20 °C. Enzyme dilutions and ONPG solutions must be made fresh on the day [1].
  • Data analysis can be paused after the raw kinetic files are exported.
  • Progress curves at each substrate concentration are linear at the start and then flatten at the highest enzyme and lowest substrate levels.
  • Initial velocity rises hyperbolically with substrate and approaches a plateau. For E. coli β-galactosidase with ONPG, a published example plateaued near 2 mM and gave Km of about 0.15–0.35 mM, with differences between fitting methods [1].
  • Nonlinear regression and linearized plots give similar Km and Vmax; large disagreement points to a problem with the data or the model [1][3].
  • No-enzyme blank at every substrate concentration to correct for spontaneous hydrolysis (autohydrolysis) [2]
  • No-substrate enzyme control to detect background from the enzyme preparation [2]
  • o-Nitrophenol standard curve in the same buffer, plate and volume [2][4]
  • Enzyme-proportionality check (rate versus enzyme amount) [5]
  • Triplicate wells for every condition [4]
ProblemLikely causeSolution
Progress curves bend within the first minuteToo much enzyme, causing substrate depletionDilute the enzyme; use only the earliest linear points and recheck conversion [1][5]
Rate does not drop when enzyme is halvedInhibitor, instability or detection limitCheck enzyme storage and buffer, and remake reagents [5]
High signal in no-enzyme wellsSpontaneous substrate hydrolysisUse fresh ONPG, protect it from light and subtract the blank slope [2]
Very low signal at low substrateRate near the noise levelLengthen the read, use slightly more enzyme or a more sensitive filter, and keep conversion low
Noisy or erratic readsBubbles or uneven mixingPipette gently, spin the plate briefly and remove bubbles [5]
Km differs between fitting methodsLinearized plots distort errorsUse nonlinear regression as the main result and examine residuals [3][4]
Yellow color is weak at neutral pHThe nitrophenol product is only partly ionized near its pKaKeep pH constant between samples and standards, or stop the reaction with alkali at set times [3]
  • β-Mercaptoethanol is toxic, malodorous and harmful by skin contact. Handle it in a fume hood with gloves and eye protection, and collect waste as chemical waste.
  • ONPG and o-nitrophenol are chemical irritants; wear gloves and a lab coat and consult the safety data sheets.
  • Dispose of buffer and substrate waste according to institutional chemical waste rules. Do not pour solutions containing β-mercaptoethanol down the drain.
