Sandwich ELISA

2 days (overnight coating; 7–8 h assay) · 2026-11-17

Quantify a secreted cytokine such as IL-6 or TNF-α in cell-culture supernatant with a 96-well sandwich ELISA, TMB/HRP detection and a 4PL standard curve.

⬇ Word (.docx)⬇ PowerPoint (.pptx)

Purpose

To measure a secreted cytokine (example: human IL-6 or TNF-α) in cell-culture supernatant from stimulated mammalian cells, in a 96-well plate with TMB/HRP detection, across roughly 15–1,000 pg/mL.

Principle

A capture antibody bound to the plate traps the cytokine; a second, biotinylated antibody against a different epitope forms the 'sandwich' [1][2]. Streptavidin-HRP binds the biotin and converts colorless TMB to blue product, and sulfuric acid turns it yellow for reading at 450 nm [2][4]. Signal rises with cytokine amount; unknowns are interpolated from a 4-parameter logistic (4PL) standard curve [2][5]. Use a matched antibody pair and standard from one supplier, and follow the lot datasheet for working concentrations; values here are typical starting points to optimize.

Materials and reagents

Equipment

  • Microplate reader with 450 nm filter and 540 or 570 nm correction
  • Automated plate washer or multichannel pipette with squirt bottle
  • Refrigerated centrifuge
  • Orbital plate shaker (about 500 rpm), optional [2]
  • 4 °C refrigerator and −80 °C freezer
  • Calibrated single- and multichannel pipettes
  • Biosafety cabinet for handling supernatants
  • Curve-fitting software (4PL), e.g., GraphPad Prism or Excel add-in

Procedure

  1. Dilute capture antibody to about 2 µg/mL (range 1–4 µg/mL) in coating buffer. Prepare enough for 100 µL per well [1][4]. (10 min)
  2. Add 100 µL per well to every well to be used, seal the plate and incubate overnight at 4 °C (or overnight at room temperature) [1][3]. (Overnight (12–18 h))
  3. Next day bring the plate to room temperature. Flick out the coating solution and wash 3 times with 300–400 µL PBST per well, blotting the inverted plate on paper [1][3]. (10 min)
  4. Add 200–300 µL blocking buffer per well, seal, and incubate 1–2 h at room temperature [1][2][4]. (1–2 h)
  5. During blocking, centrifuge supernatants at 1,000–2,000 × g for 10 min at 4 °C to remove cells and debris, then keep on ice; avoid repeated freeze–thaw [5]. (15 min)
  6. Dilute the stock standard in assay diluent to the top standard, e.g., 1,000 pg/mL for IL-6, and let it sit 10–15 min with gentle mixing; do not vortex hard [3][5]. (20 min)
  7. Make 6 two-fold serial dilutions (500, 250, 125, 62.5, 31.25, 15.6 pg/mL) with fresh tips, giving 7 points. Use plain assay diluent as the blank (0 pg/mL) [5]. (15 min)
  8. Prepare test supernatants in assay diluent. If unknown, try a dilution series (e.g., neat, 1:2, 1:10, 1:100) and record every dilution factor [2][5]. (10 min)
  9. Wash the blocked plate 3 times with PBST (300–400 µL per well), then blot well on absorbent paper; do not let wells dry out [1][3]. (5 min)
  10. Add 100 µL per well of standards, blank and samples, all in duplicate. Record the plate map; keep outer wells for blank or standards if edge effects occur [2][5]. (10 min)
  11. Seal and incubate 2 h at room temperature, with gentle shaking (about 500 rpm) if available. Overnight at 4 °C raises sensitivity [1][2][3]. (2 h (or overnight pause))
  12. Wash 4–5 times with PBST (300–400 µL per well, 30–60 s soak each time for low background), and blot dry [1][2][4]. (8 min)
  13. Dilute biotinylated detection antibody to about 0.5 µg/mL (range 0.5–2 µg/mL) in assay diluent. Add 100 µL per well [1][4]. (5 min)
  14. Seal and incubate 1–2 h at room temperature (R&D and PeproTech protocols use 2 h) [1][3][4]. (1–2 h)
  15. Wash 4–5 times with PBST as before and blot dry [1][4]. (8 min)
  16. Dilute streptavidin-HRP in assay diluent to its titrated working concentration (e.g., 0.1 µg/mL). Add 100 µL per well and protect from light [2][4]. (5 min)
  17. Seal and incubate 20–30 min at room temperature, in the dark [1][3][4]. (20–30 min)
  18. Wash 5 times with PBST (a 30 s soak each time), and blot the plate thoroughly. This wash is critical for low background [1][5]. (10 min)
  19. Bring TMB to room temperature. Add 100 µL TMB per well with a multichannel pipette, keeping the order and speed the same for all wells [3][4]. (2 min)
  20. Incubate in the dark at room temperature for 15–30 min, until the highest standard is clearly blue and the blank is nearly colorless [2][3][4]. (15–30 min)
  21. Add 50 µL 2 N H₂SO₄ per well in the same order as TMB; tap gently to mix. Wells turn from blue to yellow [3][5]. (2 min)
  22. Read at 450 nm within 5–30 min of stopping; also read 570 nm (or 540 nm) for plate-optics correction, then subtract 570 from 450 nm [2][3][5]. (10 min)
  23. Check the layout, then average duplicates and subtract the mean blank OD. Reject pairs with CV above 15% and repeat [5]. (10 min)
  24. Fit a 4PL curve of mean OD (y) against concentration (x). Check the fit (R² of at least 0.99) and back-calculate standards [2][5]. (15 min)
  25. Interpolate sample concentrations from the curve and multiply by the dilution factor. Accept only ODs within the standard range [5]. (10 min)
  26. If a sample exceeds the top standard, dilute further and repeat; if below the lowest standard, report it as below the limit of detection [5]. (as needed)
  27. Record lot numbers and settings. Store leftover supernatant at −80 °C and discard plates and tips as biohazardous waste.

Pause points

  • Capture-antibody coating can run overnight at 4 °C (12–18 h); coated, sealed plates keep several days at 4 °C if so validated [1][3].
  • After blocking, remove the buffer and store the plate sealed at 4 °C for a few hours if needed.
  • Sample or standard incubation can be done overnight at 4 °C instead of 2 h at room temperature, for higher sensitivity [1].
  • Do not pause after adding streptavidin-HRP or TMB; read soon after stopping (within 5–30 min) [2][5].

Expected results

  • The standard curve is sigmoidal on a log scale. A typical blank OD450 is below 0.15–0.2 and the top standard is about 1.2–2.5 [1][4][5].
  • Duplicate CV should be below 15% [5]. R² of the 4PL fit should be near 0.99 or higher.
  • Report concentrations in pg/mL after correcting for the dilution factor. Stimulated cells typically give tens to thousands of pg/mL of IL-6 or TNF-α; unstimulated cells give little or none (depends on cell type and dose).
  • Normalizing to cell number or total protein is optional [5].

Controls

  • Blank (assay diluent only) to set background [1][5]
  • Standard curve of 7 points in duplicate on every plate [5]
  • Unstimulated-cell supernatant (negative biological control)
  • Stimulated-cell supernatant (positive control, e.g., LPS)
  • Culture medium alone, to check matrix interference; make standards in diluent that matches the sample medium where possible
  • Spike-and-recovery or serial dilution of one sample to confirm linearity [1][5]
  • No-capture-antibody well, or a well without detection antibody, to check nonspecific binding

Troubleshooting

ProblemLikely causeSolution
High background (blank OD above 0.15–0.2)Insufficient washing, too much detection antibody or streptavidin-HRP, over-long TMB incubation, contaminated bufferAdd washes and a 30–60 s soak, titrate reagents down, shorten TMB time, use fresh buffer [1][4][5]
Weak or no signal in all wellsReagent omitted or added in the wrong order, inactive HRP (azide), degraded standard or antibody, expired TMBRe-check the order, remove azide, use fresh standard and TMB; test HRP with biotinylated antibody directly [1][5]
Flat or poor standard curveDilution errors, bad reconstitution, wrong curve modelPrepare fresh standards with new tips, mix well, use 4PL rather than linear fit [5]
High duplicate CV (above 15%)Pipetting error, bubbles, uneven washing, edge effect, dried wellsCalibrate pipettes, use a multichannel, remove bubbles, seal plate, wash evenly, avoid stacking plates [5]
Sample OD above the top standardCytokine concentration too highDilute the sample further (e.g., 1:10, 1:100) in assay diluent and repeat; multiply by the dilution factor [2][5]
Sample OD below the lowest standardLow cytokine level or sample degradationTest neat, concentrate supernatant, use overnight sample incubation, or a high-sensitivity kit [1]
Signal varies across the plate (edge effect)Temperature gradient or evaporationEquilibrate the plate to room temperature, seal every incubation, avoid stacking, leave edge wells as blanks [5]
Serum-containing medium gives odd readingsMatrix interference, heterophilic antibodiesDilute samples in matched medium, use diluent with irrelevant immunoglobulin, and run spike-recovery [1]
Blue color fades or is not stableNo stop solution added, or delayed readingAdd the acid in the TMB order and read within 5–30 min [2][5]

Safety and waste

  • TMB is a possible irritant and is light-sensitive; wear gloves, avoid skin and eye contact, and dispose of it per local rules [2][3].
  • 2 N sulfuric acid (stop solution) is corrosive: wear gloves, goggles and a lab coat. Rinse spills with plenty of water and add acid to water, never water to acid.
  • Sodium azide is toxic and inactivates HRP, so keep it out of every buffer that touches HRP. If present in stocks, handle with care and avoid acid contact or drain disposal [1].
  • Cell-culture supernatants and primary or human cell lines can carry pathogens: work at the biosafety level your institution requires, use a biosafety cabinet for supernatants, and treat all liquid and plastic waste as biohazardous (e.g., 10% bleach or autoclave).
  • LPS and other stimulants may be hazardous; follow the safety data sheet.
  • Wear gloves and eye protection; label chemical waste and follow institutional disposal rules.
