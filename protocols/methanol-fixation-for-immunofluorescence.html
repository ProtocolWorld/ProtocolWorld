Methanol Fixation for Immunofluorescence

About 3–4 h (or overnight primary incubation) · 2026-11-19

Cold-methanol fixation and permeabilization of adherent cells for indirect immunofluorescence: timing, blocking, antibody incubations, DAPI staining, mounting, controls and when to choose PFA instead.

⬇ Word (.docx)⬇ PowerPoint (.pptx)

Purpose

To fix cultured adherent cells with ice-cold methanol so that intracellular antigens can be labeled by indirect immunofluorescence in a single combined fixation and permeabilization step.

Principle

Cold methanol is a precipitating (denaturing) fixative: it dehydrates cells, extracts lipids and precipitates proteins in place, so no separate permeabilization step is needed [1][2][3]. This suits many cytoskeletal, nuclear and cytoplasmic antigens whose epitopes are masked by cross-linking, but it can denature conformation-dependent epitopes, extracts soluble and membrane lipid components and is incompatible with some probes. Cross-linking fixatives such as 3–4% paraformaldehyde (PFA) preserve morphology differently and need a detergent to permeabilize [1][2]. Always test both fixatives for a new antibody.

Materials and reagents

Equipment

  • −20 °C freezer (spark-free preferred for solvents)
  • Fine forceps and 1 mL pipettes
  • Humidified incubation chamber
  • Glass slides
  • Fluorescence microscope with DAPI and secondary-antibody filter sets
  • Chemical fume hood

Procedure

  1. Chill enough 100% methanol to cover all coverslips at −20 °C for at least 1 h. Also warm PBS to room temperature [2]. (1 h)
  2. Aspirate the culture medium and rinse the cells gently with PBS, 3 times for 5 min each, at room temperature [3]. (15 min)
  3. Remove the PBS completely, then add enough ice-cold methanol to cover the cells (for example, 500 µL per well of a 12-well plate) [1][2]. (1 min)
  4. Place the plate at −20 °C for 10 min. Shorter times (5 min) are also used; optimize for your antigen [1][2][3]. (10 min)
  5. Aspirate the methanol in a fume hood and wash 3 times with PBS for 5 min each. Never let the cells dry out [2][3]. (15 min)
  6. No detergent permeabilization is required after methanol [1][3]. Block with blocking buffer for 30–60 min at room temperature [1][3]. (30–60 min)
  7. Apply primary antibody in blocking buffer. Incubate 2 h at room temperature or overnight at 4 °C in a humidified chamber [3]. (2 h or overnight)
  8. Wash 3 times with PBS (optionally with 0.1% Tween-20) for 5 min each [3]. (15 min)
  9. Add the fluorophore-conjugated secondary antibody and incubate 1 h at room temperature in the dark [3]. (1 h)
  10. Wash 3 times with PBS for 5 min each in the dark. Stain nuclei with 1 µg/mL DAPI for 5 min and wash once more [3]. (25 min)
  11. Lift each coverslip with forceps, drain the edge, and invert it onto a drop of antifade mounting medium on a glass slide [2][3].
  12. Seal the edges with nail polish if the medium is not hardening, let it cure as directed, and image within days. Store slides at 4 °C in the dark [1][3].

Pause points

  • After step 5, coverslips can be stored in PBS at 4 °C for 1–2 weeks (a datasheet suggests adding 0.1% sodium azide) [1].
  • After the primary antibody step, the overnight 4 °C incubation is a natural stopping point.
  • Mounted slides can be stored at 4 °C in the dark until imaging.

Expected results

  • Clear, specific labeling of the target structure with low background, and DAPI-stained nuclei with intact morphology.
  • Cytoskeletal and many nuclear or cytoplasmic proteins usually label well; some membrane proteins and conformational epitopes may give weaker signals, so compare with PFA.

Controls

  • Secondary-antibody-only coverslip (no primary) to measure nonspecific binding and autofluorescence
  • Positive-control antibody against an abundant, methanol-compatible antigen such as α-tubulin
  • Knockout, knockdown or isotype control for a new primary antibody
  • Parallel coverslip fixed with 4% PFA and permeabilized, to compare epitope behavior [1][2]

Troubleshooting

ProblemLikely causeSolution
Weak or no signalEpitope denatured by methanol, or antibody too diluteTest PFA fixation; raise antibody concentration or extend the primary incubation
High backgroundInsufficient blocking or washing, or secondary antibody too concentratedLengthen blocking, add washes and titrate the secondary antibody
Cells detach or look shrunkenDelivery of methanol too forcefully, drying, or over-confluent cultureAdd and remove solutions along the well wall; keep cells wet; use 60–80% confluence
Lost fluorescent protein or phalloidin signalMethanol denatures fluorescent proteins and F-actin binding sitesUse PFA-based fixation for GFP-type fusions and phalloidin staining
Poor membrane protein labelingLipid extraction by solventTry short PFA fixation with a mild detergent (saponin or digitonin) [1]

Safety and waste

  • Methanol is flammable and toxic by inhalation, skin contact and ingestion. Use it in a fume hood with gloves and eye protection, and keep it away from ignition sources.
  • Store methanol-containing plates only in a freezer approved for flammable solvents; do not use a standard food-grade freezer.
  • Collect methanol and used solvents in a labeled organic solvent waste container. DAPI and sodium azide waste should be collected as hazardous chemical waste according to institutional rules.
