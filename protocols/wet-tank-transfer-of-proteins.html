Wet Tank Transfer of Proteins

About 2.5 h (1 h transfer) or overnight at low voltage · 2026-11-22

Electroblot SDS-PAGE gels onto PVDF or nitrocellulose in a submerged tank, with buffer, methanol/SDS, voltage and cooling adjusted to protein size, and confirm transfer with Ponceau S.

⬇ Word (.docx)⬇ PowerPoint (.pptx)

Purpose

To move proteins separated by SDS-PAGE out of a polyacrylamide gel and onto a PVDF or nitrocellulose membrane in a fully submerged (wet tank) apparatus, so they can be detected by Ponceau S staining, antibodies or sequencing.

Principle

In a tank blotter the gel–membrane sandwich is held between two electrodes in a large volume of buffer. An electric field drives the SDS-coated, negatively charged proteins out of the gel toward the anode, where they bind the membrane by hydrophobic interactions (PVDF) or a mix of hydrophobic and polar interactions (nitrocellulose) [1][2][3]. The large buffer volume gives a stable pH and, with cooling, lets the transfer run longer and at higher field strength than semi-dry systems, which helps with large or hard-to-elute proteins. Methanol strips SDS from proteins and improves binding to the membrane but shrinks the gel pores and slows transfer of large proteins, whereas a small amount of SDS does the opposite, so both are adjusted to protein size [1][3].

Materials and reagents

ItemDetails
Towbin transfer buffer (1×)25 mM Tris base, 192 mM glycine, 10–20% (v/v) methanol; do not adjust the pH (about 8.3). For 1 L: 3.03 g Tris base, 14.4 g glycine, 100–200 mL methanol, water to 1 L. Make fresh and chill to 4 °C [2][3]
Optional SDSAdd 0.01–0.05% (w/v) SDS from a 20% stock for proteins above about 80 kDa (up to 0.1% with nitrocellulose). Excess SDS lowers binding to PVDF [1][3]
PVDF membrane0.45 µm for most proteins, 0.2 µm for proteins below about 20 kDa; cut about 0.5 cm larger than the gel on each side [1][3]
Nitrocellulose membrane (alternative)0.45 µm (0.2 µm for small proteins). Never soak in 100% methanol; up to 20% methanol in buffer is fine [1]
100% methanolOnly to wet PVDF (a few seconds)
Filter paper and fiber padsFour sheets of thick filter paper per side cut slightly larger than the membrane, plus two fiber pads that fit the cassette [2]
SDS-PAGE gelFreshly run; 0.75–1.0 mm thick gels transfer best (about 1.5 mm is the practical limit) [1]
Ponceau S solution0.5% (w/v) Ponceau S in 1% (v/v) acetic acid [2]; a 0.01% solution (10 mg per 100 mL in 1% acetic acid) is also sensitive [3]
Tris-buffered saline with Tween 20 (TBST) or PBSTFor washing and for the blocking step after transfer
Ice pack and magnetic stir barPlaced in the tank to keep the buffer at 4 °C and mixed [3]

Equipment

  • Wet (tank) electroblotting unit with gel holder cassettes, electrodes and cooling core or ice pack
  • Power supply capable of constant voltage and constant current
  • Magnetic stir plate (in a cold room or on ice if possible)
  • Shallow tray for assembling the sandwich under buffer
  • Blunt forceps (membrane only)
  • Roller, test tube or serological pipette for removing bubbles
  • Orbital shaker
  • Pencil (never a pen) and clear plastic sheet or imager

Procedure

  1. Make 1 L of Towbin buffer with the methanol and SDS suited to your target protein size (10% methanol, 0.05% SDS is a good start for 20–80 kDa on PVDF). Chill to 4 °C [1][2][3]. (15 min)
  2. Run the SDS-PAGE gel. Afterward, remove the stacking gel and notch one corner (for example the corner next to lane 1) to mark orientation [1]. (1–1.5 h)
  3. Soak the gel in 250 mL cold transfer buffer on a shaker: 1 min for a 0.5 mm gel, 5 min for 0.75–1 mm, 15 min for 1.5 mm. Longer soaking removes SDS and lowers transfer [1]. (1–15 min)
  4. Cut the membrane and four filter papers to size. For PVDF, immerse in 100% methanol for about 5–10 s until translucent, then move it into transfer buffer. Do not let it dry [1][3]. (5 min)
  5. For nitrocellulose, wet it by slowly lowering it onto buffer and equilibrate for 5–15 min, until it sinks [1][3]. (5–15 min)
  6. Soak the fiber pads and filter papers in cold buffer until saturated. Work with all parts submerged in a tray of buffer [2][3]. (5 min)
  7. Open the cassette with the gray or black (cathode) side down. Stack a fiber pad, then four sheets of filter paper (cathode side) [2][3]. (2 min)
  8. Lay the gel on the filter paper. Wet the gel surface with a few mL of buffer, then lower the membrane onto it from the center outward so that no air is trapped [1][2]. (2 min)
  9. Add four sheets of filter paper and the second fiber pad. Roll out any bubbles with a roller or tube, working gently so the gel is not stretched [1][2]. (3 min)
  10. Close the cassette so the membrane faces the anode (red, +) and the gel the cathode (black, −). Fill the tank with cold buffer; add the ice pack and stir bar [1][3]. (3 min)
  11. Run at 100 V constant for 60 min at 4 °C with stirring (about 200–400 mA, mini gel). For large proteins or thick gels use 30–50 V for 3 h or about 30 V for 16 h [1][3]. (1 h)
  12. Check that the current does not rise steadily and that the buffer is not hot. If it is, stop, replace the ice pack and lower the voltage [1]. (ongoing)
  13. Switch off the power and disconnect the leads. Open the cassette and mark the membrane's top and orientation in pencil, removing the gel from the membrane without sliding it [1][3]. (3 min)
  14. Rinse the membrane briefly in water and stain with Ponceau S for 2 min (0.5% solution) or 2–10 min (0.01% solution) with gentle shaking [2][3]. (2–10 min)
  15. Destain with water until bands are clear. Photograph the membrane, and mark the molecular weight marker positions with pencil if needed [2][3]. (5 min)
  16. Optionally stain the gel with Coomassie to see what stayed behind and judge transfer efficiency [1]. (30 min)
  17. Wash the membrane with water or TBST (3 × 5 min) to remove the Ponceau S, then proceed to blocking. Do not let the membrane dry before blocking [1]. (15 min)

Pause points

  • After Ponceau S staining and photography, the membrane can be blocked immediately or stored wet in TBST at 4 °C for up to a day.
  • A dried PVDF membrane can be stored at 4 °C and reactivated later with 100% methanol before blocking [3].
  • The transfer itself should not be paused once the current is running.

Expected results

  • Ponceau S shows a uniform pattern of pink-red bands across all lanes, including the marker, with sharp bands and no empty patches.
  • After transfer the gel retains little protein at the expected sizes when stained with Coomassie; the middle two-thirds of the gel usually transfers best [1].
  • The membrane is flat and intact, with no bubble-shaped blank spots.

Controls

  • Ponceau S staining after transfer to confirm transfer and check equal loading
  • A prestained molecular weight marker, which shows transfer efficiency by eye
  • Post-transfer Coomassie stain of the gel to estimate leftover protein
  • A loading control antibody later in the blot

Troubleshooting

ProblemLikely causeSolution
Blank spots or white patches on the membraneTrapped air bubbles or local drying of PVDFRoll out bubbles, assemble under buffer, keep PVDF wet [1][3]
Faint or absent high-molecular-weight bandsToo much methanol, no SDS, too short a runLower methanol to 5–10%, add 0.05–0.1% SDS, extend time or run low voltage overnight [1][3]
Small proteins pass through the membraneOver-transfer or pore size too largeUse 0.2 µm membrane, 20% methanol, no SDS, and shorten the run [1][3]
No transfer at allCassette reversed or wrong electrode orientationMembrane must sit between the gel and the anode (red). Check leads and cables [3]
Smeared or wavy bandsPoor contact or worn padsUse intact fiber pads, press the sandwich firmly and do not reuse compressed paper [3]
Overheating, buffer warmHigh current, no coolingAdd ice pack and stir bar, use a cold room, lower the voltage [1][3]

Safety and waste

  • Methanol is flammable and toxic (including reproductive toxicity). Handle in a ventilated area and collect methanol-containing buffer as chemical waste according to your institution's rules [3].
  • Tank blotters run at up to 100 V and hundreds of milliamps. Close the lid before connecting the power supply and switch the power off before opening the tank.
  • Acrylamide gels may contain unpolymerized acrylamide, a neurotoxin. Wear gloves and dispose of gels as chemical waste.
  • Ponceau S contains acetic acid. Dispose of it according to local rules. Do not reuse the transfer buffer [3].
