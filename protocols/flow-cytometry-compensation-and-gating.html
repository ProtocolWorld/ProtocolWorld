Flow Cytometry Compensation and Gating

About 3 h · 2026-11-20

Set PMT voltages, build a compensation matrix from single-stained controls, use FMO controls and apply a reproducible gating hierarchy for multicolor flow cytometry of mammalian cells.

⬇ Word (.docx)⬇ PowerPoint (.pptx)

Purpose

To set photomultiplier voltages, build a correct compensation matrix from single-stained controls, and apply a reproducible gating hierarchy so that multicolor data from mammalian cells (for example PBMCs, splenocytes or cell lines) can be interpreted reliably.

Principle

Every fluorochrome emits light into detectors meant for other colors; this spillover is a property of the dye and the instrument, not of the sample. Compensation subtracts the spillover measured in single-stained controls from each detector [1][3]. Controls must be at least as bright as the sample signal, must contain a clear positive and negative population, and must share the autofluorescence of the experimental cells [1][2][3]. Compensation corrects the average signal but not the added spread in the data, so fluorescence-minus-one (FMO) controls are used to place gates in difficult regions [3][5]. Gating then moves from the general to the specific: stable acquisition, intact cells, single cells, live cells, lineage and finally the population of interest.

Equipment

  • Flow cytometer with the panel's lasers and filters
  • Acquisition and analysis software
  • Refrigerated centrifuge (300–400 × g)
  • Pipettes and filter tips
  • Ice bucket
  • 40 µm cell strainers

Procedure

  1. Write down the panel: marker, fluorochrome, detector, and relative brightness. Plan one single-stained control per fluorochrome, plus unstained and FMO tubes [3][5]. (20 min)
  2. Prepare single cells in cold staining buffer at 1 × 10⁷ cells/mL and filter through a 40 µm strainer to remove clumps. (10 min)
  3. Aliquot 1 × 10⁶ cells (100 µL) per tube: unstained, one tube per fluorochrome, one tube per FMO, and one fully stained tube. Block Fc receptors for 10 min on ice.
  4. Stain each single-color control with its antibody (or viability dye) only, using a reagent at least as bright as in the panel, with clear positive and negative populations [1][2][3]. (30 min)
  5. Stain FMO tubes with all antibodies except one, and the full-panel tube with all antibodies, in the dark on ice for 30 min [5]. (30 min)
  6. Wash twice with 2 mL staining buffer (350 × g, 5 min, 4 °C), resuspend in 300 µL, add viability dye if it is not already included, and keep on ice in the dark.
  7. Optional: for beads, add the recommended antibody amount and beads, incubate 15–30 min, wash once and resuspend. Beads cannot be used for viability dyes [1][3].
  8. Run unstained cells. Set FSC and SSC so cells are on scale, then set each fluorescence voltage so negatives sit above the axis and positives stay linear [3][6]. (15 min)
  9. Run the brightest single stain for each detector. Lower the voltage if the positive peak is off scale; never change voltages after recording the controls [3][6].
  10. Record several thousand gated events per control (10,000 is a good aim). Gate on cells, not on the dye, so stained and unstained cells share autofluorescence [3][4].
  11. Calculate the matrix in the software, then inspect each channel pair: the medians of positive and negative populations should match, not the peak tops [3].
  12. Check for overcompensation (events pushed below the axis) and undercompensation (positives leaning diagonally). Tighten the positive gate if its MFI is low [2][3].
  13. Apply the matrix to the full-panel and FMO tubes; confirm the matrix is saved with the experiment template and that voltages are recorded.
  14. Gate the stable time window on time versus a fluorescence channel to remove clogs or flow disturbances.
  15. Gate intact cells on FSC-A versus SSC-A, then single cells on FSC-A versus FSC-H (or FSC-W), then live cells on the viability dye channel.
  16. Gate lineage markers and the population of interest; set the final boundary with the FMO control for each dim or smeared marker [3][5].
  17. Acquire at least 50,000 events per experimental sample where possible, record the gating tree, and export statistics together with the compensation matrix [4]. (30 min)

Pause points

  • After staining and the second wash, cells can be fixed with 1–2% paraformaldehyde and kept at 4 °C in the dark overnight; read within 24 h.
  • The compensation matrix and gates can be saved as a template and reused only if voltages, lot numbers and fluorochromes remain unchanged.

Expected results

  • Single-stained controls show a clear positive peak on the primary detector, and compensated spillover medians in all other detectors line up with the negative population.
  • The unstained sample sits in the first log decade; positives remain on scale and within the linear range.
  • FMO controls define a cutoff that excludes background in dim or continuous populations, and the gating tree gives reproducible percentages across replicates.

Controls

  • Unstained cells (autofluorescence and negative reference)
  • Single-stained control for every fluorochrome, including viability dye
  • FMO control for each marker whose positive boundary is unclear
  • Biological control (stimulated versus unstimulated, or known negative cells) where basal expression may be present
  • Fc-block control to check non-specific binding

Troubleshooting

ProblemLikely causeSolution
Overcompensation (events pushed below the axis)Positive gate too wide, or tops of peaks aligned instead of mediansTighten the positive gate to the bright region; align medians; recalculate [2][3]
UndercompensationControl brighter or dimmer than sample, or different autofluorescenceUse a control at least as bright as the sample on the same cell type and re-record [2][3]
Tandem-dye spillover changes between daysDifferent lot or degraded tandemMake fresh controls with the same lot as the experiment [3]
Few events in the positive gateMarker is rare or dimUse antibody capture beads or a high-expressing cell type with the same fluorochrome [1][3]
Voltage changed after controlsInstrument settings adjusted for a new sampleRe-run all controls whenever voltages, filters or laser alignment change [3]
Wide spread of negative population in a bright channelPhoton counting error and spreadingCompensation cannot remove it; use FMO controls and better fluorochrome choices [3][5]

Safety and waste

  • Treat primary human and animal cells as potentially infectious: work in Biosafety Level 2 conditions and wear gloves, lab coat and eye protection.
  • Sodium azide is toxic and can form explosive metal azides in drains; use dilute solutions and dispose as chemical waste.
  • Paraformaldehyde is toxic and a suspected carcinogen; handle in a fume hood and collect as hazardous waste.
  • Decontaminate the cytometer fluidics and sample waste with 10% bleach (final) for at least 10 minutes before disposal; follow your core facility's safety rules.
