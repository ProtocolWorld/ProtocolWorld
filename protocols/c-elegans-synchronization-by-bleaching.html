C. elegans Synchronization by Bleaching

1 h hands-on + 16–24 h L1 arrest · 2026-11-17

Alkaline hypochlorite dissolves gravid adult C. elegans and releases eggshell-protected embryos, which hatch in M9 without food into a synchronized, arrested L1 population. Plating these L1s onto NGM/OP50 gives a cohort of the same age for assays.

⬇ Word (.docx)⬇ PowerPoint (.pptx)

Purpose

To obtain an age-synchronized population of C. elegans by dissolving gravid hermaphrodites in alkaline hypochlorite, collecting the surviving embryos and hatching them into arrested L1 larvae for plating on NGM/OP50.

Principle

Hypochlorite in NaOH destroys adult worms and larvae, but embryos inside their eggshells survive a short exposure [1]. Washed embryos hatch in M9 without food and halt development at L1, so all larvae restart growth together when plated on food [1][2]. Over-exposure damages embryos, so the bleach step is kept short and stopped as soon as adults dissolve [2][3][4]. Starvation effects grow with time in M9, so use L1s within 24 h when possible [2].

Equipment

  • Vortex mixer or tube rocker
  • Swinging-bucket tabletop centrifuge
  • Dissecting microscope
  • Tube rocker or shaker at 20 °C
  • Micropipettes with filter tips
  • 20 °C incubator
  • Flame or laminar flow hood

Procedure

  1. Chunk worms to 3–6 fresh NGM/OP50 plates and grow at 20 °C until many gravid adults and eggs are present; young adults give the best egg yield [3]. (3–4 days)
  2. Wash worms and eggs off each plate with 1 mL sterile water and pool them in a sterile 15 mL tube (total below 8 mL) [1][3].
  3. Optional: spin 30 s at 1300 × g, discard supernatant to about 0.1 mL and rinse once with water to remove bacteria [1][2].
  4. Bring the volume to 3.5 mL with sterile water. Do not synchronize more than one strain per tube [1][3].
  5. Immediately before use, mix 0.5 mL 5 N NaOH and 1.0 mL bleach. Add all 1.5 mL to the 3.5 mL worm suspension [1]. (1 min)
  6. Vortex or shake a few seconds every 2 min. Check under the microscope; stop when adult bodies are dissolved and eggs are free, usually 4–8 min [1][3][4]. (4–8 min)
  7. Stop the reaction at once: fill the tube to 15 mL with water or M9, then spin 1 min at about 1300 × g [1][3]. (2 min)
  8. Discard supernatant to about 0.1 mL. Wash 3 times with 10–15 mL M9 (or water), spinning 30–60 s at 1300 × g each time; keep the pellet loose [1][2][3]. (10 min)
  9. Resuspend embryos in 5–10 mL M9 in a clean tube. Pause point: the embryos can be left for the hatching incubation [2][3]. (2 min)
  10. Rock or rotate gently at 20 °C so embryos hatch and L1s arrest without food. Most eggs hatch in 12–16 h; use within 24 h [1][2][3]. (16–24 h)
  11. Count: using a tip coated with M9 + 0.1% Triton, place 3–5 drops of 20 µL on a slide and count L1s under a dissecting microscope; compute L1 per µL [2][3]. (10 min)
  12. Spin L1s 1–2 min at 1150 × g, remove most M9 and resuspend in the volume that gives about 500 L1s per 20–50 µL [1][2]. (5 min)
  13. Pipette L1s next to, not on, the OP50 lawn (about 500 per 6 cm plate); let the liquid soak in before moving plates [2][3]. (5 min)
  14. Incubate at 20 °C. Expect mid-L1 at about 8 h, mid-L2 about 18 h, mid-L3 about 25 h and mid-L4 about 37 h after plating; young adults appear at about 55–60 h [1]. (2–3 days)

Pause points

  • Chunk worms to 3–6 fresh NGM/OP50 plates and grow at 20 °C until many gravid adults and eggs are present; young adults give the best egg yield [3] (3–4 days).
  • Incubate at 20 °C (2–3 days).

Expected results

  • Eggs and a few intact larvae are visible after the bleach step; adult carcasses are gone.
  • After the overnight incubation in M9, most eggs have hatched into motile, thin, arrested L1s of the same size.
  • Plated L1s develop together, reaching mid-L4 at about 37 h and adulthood at about 55–60 h at 20 °C [1].

Controls

  • Keep an unbleached sample of the same strain to compare development rate
  • Count L1s before plating to record the hatch rate and plating density
  • Include a wild-type N2 control sample in the same batch when testing a mutant

Troubleshooting

ProblemLikely causeSolution
Few or no eggs recoveredBleach too long or too concentrated, or plates had few gravid adultsWatch under the scope and stop at adult dissolution; use young gravid adults and fresh bleach [1][3][4]
Low hatching or abnormal larvaeOver-bleached embryos or old bleachShorten the exposure to under 5 min; make the NaOH/bleach mix fresh; wash promptly [1][3][4]
Worms develop unevenly or slowlyL1s starved too long (over 24–48 h) or fed on egg debrisPlate L1s within 24 h; wash well; plate L1s outside the lawn [2]
Larvae stick to tips and tubesAdhesion of L1s to plasticCoat tips with M9 + 0.1% Triton [2]
Few eggs from adults that were starved or oldStarved or old hermaphrodites carry fewer healthy embryosUse well-fed, young gravid adults, not starved plates [1][3]
Strain gives poor yieldBleach-sensitive strain or egg-laying defective (bagging) mutant with hatched larvae inside the motherShorten the bleach step or use dilute bleach and rely on the single-drop method; for bagging mutants pick gravid adults only [1][3][4]
Contaminated platesBacteria or fungus in the egg prepAdd more washes; use the single-drop egg prep on a clean plate [1]

Safety and waste

  • Bleach and NaOH are corrosive: wear gloves, goggles and a lab coat.
  • Make the NaOH/bleach mix fresh; do not mix bleach with acids or ammonia (toxic gases).
  • Dispose of bleach and worm waste according to institutional rules.
  • Hypochlorite and caustic splashes: rinse skin and eyes with water for 15 min.
