Electrophoretic Mobility Shift Assay (EMSA)

1 day (about 8 h) · 2026-11-27

Detect protein–DNA binding with a 3′-biotin probe: 5% native gel, binding buffer with poly(dI·dC), 15 + 60 min at 25 °C, 50–300× cold competitor, transfer, UV crosslink and chemiluminescent detection.

⬇ Word (.docx)⬇ PowerPoint (.pptx)

Purpose

To detect and compare the binding of a protein (purified or in a nuclear extract) to a short DNA probe, using a biotin-labeled probe and chemiluminescent detection instead of radioactivity.

Principle

A protein–DNA complex migrates more slowly than free DNA in a native polyacrylamide gel, so a bound probe appears as a shifted band [1][2][3]. Specificity is shown by competition with an excess of unlabeled probe and by a non-binding protein or no-protein lane [1][3]. Non-specific DNA such as poly(dI·dC) soaks up promiscuous binding [2][3][4]. Radioactive probes (³²P) work the same way but need film or a phosphor imager [2][4].

Materials and reagents

ItemDetails
Probe oligonucleotidesSense strand with a 3′ biotin, unlabeled antisense strand, 40 nt (a 20–35 bp binding region is typical) [1][3]. Biotin must be at the 3′ or 5′ end, not internal [3].
Probe annealingMix sense and antisense 1:1 at 100 µM, 95 °C for 5 min, then cool to room temperature (about 25 °C). Dilute labeled duplex to 50 nM; make unlabeled duplex at 2,500, 5,000, 10,000 and 15,000 nM (50×, 100×, 200×, 300× the probe) [1]. Store at −20 °C in single-use aliquots.
ProteinRecombinant protein at 1 µg/µL [1], or nuclear extract: 0.25 µg for an abundant factor, 10 µg or more for a rare one [3]. Keep on ice; extract in a buffer without strong chelators if your protein needs metals [4].
Binding buffer (final, 20 µL reaction)25 mM Tris-HCl pH 7.5, 80 mM NaCl, 35 mM KCl, 1 mM DTT, 10% glycerol, 5 mM MgCl₂, 10 ng/µL poly(dI·dC) [1]. Add NP-40 to 0.05% final [3]. Note: the source prints 2% NP-40 [1], which looks high; confirm before use.
5% native polyacrylamide gel (15 mL)0.75 mL 10× TBE, 2.5 mL 30% acrylamide/bis (29:1), 150 µL 10% APS, 15 µL TEMED, water to 15 mL [1]. A 19:1 ratio is also used [2].
0.5× TBERunning and transfer buffer. 10× TBE: 106 g Tris base, 55 g boric acid, 40 mL 0.5 M EDTA pH 8.0 per liter [2].
6× DNA loading dyeGlycerol-based; test without bromophenol blue if it disturbs binding [4].
Membrane and detectionPositively charged nylon membrane (e.g., Hybond-N+) [1][3]; chemiluminescent nucleic acid detection kit with streptavidin-HRP (e.g., Thermo 89880 [1] or Active Motif Gelshift kit [3]).

Equipment

  • Mini vertical gel apparatus and power supply
  • Wet or semi-dry transfer unit (cooled to about 10 °C if possible [3])
  • UV crosslinker (254 nm)
  • Heat block (95 °C)
  • Chemiluminescence imager or X-ray film
  • Rocker for membrane washes

Procedure

  1. Anneal probes: mix sense and antisense oligos 1:1, 95 °C for 5 min, cool to room temperature. Dilute to 50 nM (labeled) and prepare the 50–300× unlabeled series [1]. (30 min)
  2. Cast the 5% native gel with a 10-well comb. Let it polymerize about 30 min, then pre-run in 0.5× TBE at 120 V for 60 min [1][3]. (1.5 h)
  3. Set up seven 1.5 mL tubes: no protein; non-binding protein; protein with no competitor; protein with 50×, 100×, 200× and 300× unlabeled probe [1]. (10 min)
  4. To each tube add 17 µL binding buffer and 1 µL protein (1 µg; water in tube 1; the non-binding protein in tube 2) [1]. (5 min)
  5. Add 1 µL unlabeled probe (tubes 4–7) or 1 µL water (tubes 1–3). Incubate 15 min at 25 °C [1]. (15 min)
  6. Add 1 µL of 50 nM biotin-labeled probe to every tube (2.5 nM final). Incubate 60 min at 25 °C [1]. Shorter times of 20 min also work for many proteins [3]. (60 min)
  7. Add 3.5 µL 6× loading dye (or 5 µL of 5× buffer for 20 µL [3]) and mix by gentle pipetting, not vortexing [3]. (5 min)
  8. Flush the wells, load 20 µL per lane, and run at 120 V in 0.5× TBE at room temperature until the dye is about two-thirds down (about 30 min) [1]. Free probe must stay on the gel [4]. (30–45 min)
  9. Soak the nylon membrane in 0.5× TBE for 10 min. Transfer at 100 V for 30 min in 0.5× TBE; cool the buffer if the apparatus heats [1][3]. (45 min)
  10. Crosslink the DNA to the damp membrane at 254 nm, 120 mJ/cm² (about 1 min), DNA side up [1][3]. (5 min)
  11. Block 15 min, incubate with streptavidin-HRP (1:300 in blocking buffer [3], or per kit) for 15 min, then rinse and wash 4 × 5 min [3]. (1 h)
  12. Equilibrate 5 min, add the working chemiluminescent substrate for 5 min, and image or expose film for 2–5 min [3]. (20 min)

Pause points

  • After annealing the probe, aliquots can be stored at −20 °C.
  • After crosslinking, the dried membrane can be stored at room temperature, protected from light, before detection.
  • Do not pause during binding or between the gel run and the transfer.

Expected results

  • Tube 1 (no protein) shows only a fast-moving free-probe band.
  • Tube 3 shows a slower shifted band, with a reduced amount of free probe.
  • Unlabeled competitor at 50–300× weakens or abolishes the shifted band in a dose-dependent way [1][4].
  • The non-binding protein lane shows no shift.

Controls

  • No-protein lane (free probe)
  • Non-binding protein or unrelated extract lane
  • Unlabeled specific competitor, 50–300× molar excess (about 200× is typical [3][4])
  • Mutated-site probe, which should not compete [4]
  • Optional: antibody added to the reaction for a supershift or loss of the band [4]

Troubleshooting

ProblemLikely causeSolution
No signal at allProbe not biotinylated, poor transfer, membrane not positively charged, weak crosslinkingCheck the label, use Hybond-N+, repeat the transfer and crosslink [3]
No shiftDegraded or too little protein; buffer unsuitableTitrate protein; adjust glycerol, KCl, MgCl₂, NP-40 or EDTA; avoid vortexing [3]
All probe shifted or smearToo much protein or no non-specific DNAReduce protein; add poly(dI·dC) or a similar competitor [3][4]
Complex stuck in the wellLarge complex or gel too denseLower the acrylamide percentage [4]
Spots or high backgroundBubbles, particulates, unfiltered reagentsFilter reagents (0.2 µm), clean sponges, wash thoroughly [3]
Shifted band does not competeNon-specific binding or too little competitorIncrease competitor to 200–300×; test a mutant probe [1][4]

Safety and waste

  • Acrylamide is a neurotoxin before polymerization: wear gloves and weigh powder in a hood or use solution.
  • UV crosslinkers emit UV-C: never open them while on.
  • If you use ³²P probes, work behind shielding and follow your radiation safety office [2][4].
  • Dispose of gel, buffer and unpolymerized acrylamide as chemical waste per your institution.
