Whole-Mount Immunostaining

4 days · 2026-11-17

Indirect fluorescent immunostaining of intact fixed embryos, larvae or small organs, with zebrafish as the worked example, permeabilization options, blocking, washes and glycerol clearing.

⬇ Word (.docx)⬇ PowerPoint (.pptx)

Purpose

To detect a protein in intact, fixed specimens (embryos, larvae, small organs) with an indirect fluorescent antibody method, keeping three-dimensional context. The worked example is a zebrafish embryo or larva up to about 4 days post-fertilization (dpf), with notes for other specimens.

Principle

Fixation with paraformaldehyde (PFA) preserves structure; detergent, organic solvent or enzyme treatment lets antibodies reach the interior; serum and BSA block non-specific sites; a primary antibody binds the target and a fluorophore-labeled secondary antibody reveals it [1][2][3]. Antibody penetration is the limiting step: deeper tissue becomes much harder to stain after about 32 hpf in zebrafish, so permeabilization time, detergent and wash length must be tuned to specimen size [3][4]. Glycerol then clears and holds the specimen for imaging [1][4].

Materials and reagents

ItemDetails
PBS1× phosphate-buffered saline, pH 7.4
4% PFA4% paraformaldehyde in PBS, freshly made or thawed from frozen aliquots (4 °C) [1][4]
PBST (wash buffer)PBS with 0.1% Triton X-100 (the 0.1% detergent level is common to the sources, although some add Tween-20 as well) [1][2]
Permeabilization (choose one)(a) PBS with 1% Triton X-100, 30–60 min [3]; (b) 80% acetone at −20 °C, 40 min, for larvae [1]; (c) proteinase K at 10 µg/mL in PBST, time tested empirically, followed by 4% PFA for 20 min [4]
Bleaching solution (optional, pigmented larvae)Equal volumes of 3% H₂O₂ and 100 mM KOH, giving 1.5% H₂O₂ and 50 mM KOH, about 30 min [1]
Blocking bufferPBST with 2% normal goat serum and 2 mg/mL BSA (serum from the species of the secondary antibody) [4]; for thicker specimens, 5% BSA with 1% Triton X-100 in PBS [1]
Primary antibodyStart at 1:100 for an untested antibody; 1:500 worked for an optimized anti-tyrosine hydroxylase antibody; titrate [1][2]
Secondary antibodyAlexa Fluor–conjugated anti-species IgG, 1:250–1:500 in blocking buffer [1][2]
Nuclear counterstain (optional)DAPI, added with the secondary antibody at the supplier's recommended dilution [2]
Glycerol series25%, 50% and 70% glycerol in PBS [1][4]
MountingBuffered glycerol or antifade medium, glass capillary or a vacuum-grease well on a slide with a coverslip [1][2][4]

Equipment

  • Rocker or nutator, 4 °C and room temperature
  • 1.5 mL microcentrifuge tubes (about 10 larvae per tube)
  • −20 °C freezer (flammable-safe) for methanol or acetone
  • Fume hood
  • Stereomicroscope
  • Fluorescence or confocal microscope

Procedure

  1. Day 1. Fix specimens in 500 µL 4% PFA per tube of about 10 larvae, at 4 °C for 14–18 h on a rocker [1][4]. Fixation for 2–4 h at room temperature is possible but preserves shape less well [1]. (overnight)
  2. Wash 3 × 5 min in PBS at room temperature. Optional: dehydrate into 100% methanol and store at −20 °C for at least 6 h; rehydrate before staining [1][4].
  3. Day 2. Permeabilize: wash PBST 3 × 20 min [2]. For larvae, rinse 2 × 5 min in water, then incubate in 80% acetone at −20 °C for 40 min without shaking, and rinse 2 × 5 min in water [1]. (1–2 h)
  4. Optional bleaching for pigment: incubate in 1.5% H₂O₂ / 50 mM KOH for about 30 min, stop when pigment is gone, and wash PBST 2 × 5 min. Prolonged bleaching damages tissue [1]. (30 min)
  5. If using proteinase K instead, treat with 10 µg/mL in PBST for a pre-tested time, post-fix 20 min in 4% PFA, then wash PBST 4 × 5 min [4]. (variable)
  6. Block in 500 µL blocking buffer on a rocker for 1–3 h at room temperature, or overnight at 4 °C [1][3][4].
  7. Replace with primary antibody diluted in blocking buffer (100–500 µL per tube). Incubate overnight at 4 °C on a rocker [1][2][4]. (overnight)
  8. Day 3. Remove the primary (it can be reused up to twice) and wash PBST at least 6 × 5 min; use 5 × 30 min for larger or older specimens [1][4]. (30 min–2.5 h)
  9. Re-block for 30 min to 1 h, then add the fluorescent secondary antibody in blocking buffer, with DAPI if desired. Protect from light and incubate overnight at 4 °C [1][2][4]. (overnight)
  10. Day 4. Wash PBST 3 × 10 min in the dark, then rinse in PBS [2].
  11. Clear stepwise in 25%, 50% and 70% glycerol in PBS until specimens sink; larvae may need up to 1 h at 70% [1][4]. (1–2 h)
  12. Mount in a glass capillary or in a vacuum-grease well with a coverslip. Keep dark at 4 °C until imaging [1][2].
  13. Image with identical exposure, gain and laser settings for all conditions; quantify in ImageJ or Fiji with a fixed region and background subtraction [1].

Pause points

  • After fixation and PBS washes, specimens can be held in 100% methanol at −20 °C for months [1].
  • Fixed specimens in PBST at 4 °C remain usable for at least one week; in PBS with 50 µg/mL gentamicin they keep for a few weeks [1][2].
  • After clearing in glycerol, store dark at 4 °C until imaging [1].

Expected results

  • Specific signal that matches the known expression pattern, for example labeled neuronal populations in zebrafish larvae [1].
  • Low, even background in control specimens, with intact morphology and no cell loss.
  • Surface-only or patchy signal indicates poor penetration and calls for longer permeabilization [2].

Controls

  • No-primary-antibody control (blocking buffer only, same secondary)
  • Wild-type or knockout/knockdown specimens, if available, to confirm antibody specificity
  • Image control specimens first and keep the settings unchanged [1]
  • Staining of a well-characterized antibody in parallel when testing a new one

Troubleshooting

ProblemLikely causeSolution
Weak or uneven signalInsufficient permeabilization or antibody too diluteLengthen permeabilization or raise detergent; try 1:100; check for lot-to-lot variation [1][2]
Signal only at the surfacePoor antibody penetrationLonger detergent or acetone treatment, remove skin or dissect, use longer incubations and washes [2][3]
High backgroundInadequate blocking or washingBlock longer, add serum, increase wash number and length [2][4]
Tissue damage or collapseOver-permeabilization or prolonged bleachingShorten treatment, fix longer, lower detergent [1][2]
GFP signal lostPeroxide bleaching quenches fluorescent proteinsOmit bleaching or detect GFP with an antibody [2]
No signal at allEpitope masked by fixationTest a different fixative or antigen retrieval, such as citrate buffer heated to 94–98 °C for 20 min [2][3]

Safety and waste

  • PFA is toxic and a suspected carcinogen: handle in a fume hood with gloves and eye protection [1].
  • Acetone and methanol are flammable; store in a flammable-safe freezer.
  • KOH and hydrogen peroxide are corrosive and oxidizing. If you use a formamide–peroxide bleach, add the components in the order given by the method, because the two can react explosively when mixed alone [2].
  • Dispose of PFA, solvent and antibody waste as hazardous chemical waste; check institutional animal-care rules for the age of the specimens used.
