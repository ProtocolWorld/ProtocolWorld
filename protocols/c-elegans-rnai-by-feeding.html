C. elegans RNAi by Feeding

3 days to prepare + 2–5 days of feeding and scoring · 2026-11-24

Knock down a C. elegans gene by feeding worms HT115(DE3) bacteria expressing dsRNA from L4440: IPTG-carbenicillin NGM plates, overnight induction, L1/L4 worm staging, scoring, controls (L4440, unc-22) and tissue limitations.

⬇ Word (.docx)⬇ PowerPoint (.pptx)

Purpose

To knock down a C. elegans gene by feeding worms E. coli HT115(DE3) that express double-stranded RNA (dsRNA) for that gene, and to score the resulting phenotype.

Principle

E. coli strain HT115(DE3) lacks the dsRNA-specific RNase III and carries IPTG-inducible T7 RNA polymerase, so it accumulates dsRNA from a gene fragment cloned between two T7 promoters in the L4440 vector [1][2][3]. Worms that eat these bacteria absorb the dsRNA, which triggers systemic RNA interference. Best results come from growing the bacteria without induction, spreading them on IPTG plates and letting them induce overnight at room temperature [2]. Every experiment needs an empty-vector control and a positive-control gene.

Materials and reagents

ItemDetails
RNAi cloneGene fragment (exon sequence; avoid introns) in L4440 in HT115(DE3), from the Kamath (Ahringer) or Rual (ORFeome) libraries or cloned in house [1][4]
ControlsHT115(DE3) with empty L4440 (negative); unc-22 (twitching), or lin-26 or gfp RNAi in a matching reporter strain (positive) [1][5]

NGM-RNAi plates (6 cm)Standard NGM agar containing 25 µg/mL carbenicillin (or 50 µg/mL ampicillin) and 1 mM IPTG added after autoclaving and cooling to about 55 °C [1][2]
IPTG stock1 M in water, filter-sterilized, −20 °C; carbenicillin 50 mg/mL in water, −20 °C
WormsWild-type N2 or the strain of interest, synchronized at L1 or picked at L4 [1]

Equipment

  • 37 °C shaking incubator
  • 20 °C incubator for worms (15–25 °C range)
  • Stereo dissecting microscope
  • Worm pick (platinum wire)
  • Sterile culture tubes and spreader
  • Biosafety cabinet or clean bench
  • Centrifuge and PCR thermocycler (for clone checks)

Procedure

  1. Streak the HT115(DE3) clone (and controls) from a −80 °C stock on LB agar with 50 µg/mL ampicillin. Verify the insert by colony PCR with the primers RNAiFw and RNAiRv and sequencing [1]. (1 day)
  2. Pick one colony into 2 mL LB with 50 µg/mL ampicillin. Grow overnight at 37 °C with shaking [1][2][5]. (16 h)
  3. Prepare NGM-RNAi plates: cool autoclaved NGM to about 55 °C, add carbenicillin to 25 µg/mL and IPTG to 1 mM, pour 6 cm plates and let them set [1][2]. (1 h)
  4. Dilute the overnight culture 1:100 into fresh LB with ampicillin and grow about 6 h at 37 °C [5]. Saturated and log-phase cultures gave similar results [2]. (6 h)
  5. Spread 100–200 µL of culture on each plate. Dry in the hood, then let the lawn induce overnight at room temperature. Plates keep 1–2 weeks at 4 °C [1][3][5]. (16 h)
  6. Synchronize worms: bleach gravid adults, hatch eggs overnight in M9 to obtain L1s, or pick L4 larvae from a healthy plate [1].
  7. Transfer 20 L1s, or 3–5 L4 hermaphrodites, onto each RNAi plate (3 plates per clone). Keep at 20 °C; 15–25 °C also works [1][2][5]. (1 min/plate)
  8. Move picked adults to fresh RNAi plates after 24 h and score the progeny (lethality, sterility, morphology). The second plate is often stronger [1][5]. (24 h)
  9. Score daily under the dissecting microscope. Many phenotypes appear after 2–4 days; feeding for 36–40 h at 22 °C or about 72 h at 15 °C gave strong results [1][2]. (2–4 days)
  10. Score at least 40 animals per condition [3] and run each RNAi in triplicate [1]. Compare with the L4440 control plate and the positive control [1][2].
  11. Confirm knockdown with a reporter, a qRT-PCR for the target mRNA or an antibody. Interpret weak or absent phenotypes with the troubleshooting table.

Pause points

  • Seeded plates can be stored at 4 °C for 1–2 weeks and warmed to room temperature before use [1][3].
  • Bacteria can be stored as glycerol stocks at −80 °C [1].

Expected results

  • Empty-vector control worms look and behave as normal.
  • unc-22 RNAi causes twitching in nearly 100% of progeny [1][2].
  • Embryonic-lethal controls such as gpb-1 give close to 100% lethal progeny on IPTG plates after overnight induction [2].
  • Target gene knockdown gives the expected loss-of-function phenotype, usually in the progeny of adults fed for 24 h or more.

Controls

  • Empty-vector L4440 HT115(DE3) (negative)
  • unc-22 RNAi (twitching), or a reporter RNAi such as gfp (positive)
  • An uninduced plate (without IPTG) for the same clone
  • Sequence-verified insert for each clone
  • A second, non-overlapping RNAi fragment or a mutant allele to confirm specificity

Troubleshooting

ProblemLikely causeSolution
No phenotype with the positive controlIPTG or carbenicillin degraded, poor induction, or HT115 lostMake fresh plates; use new IPTG; restreak the clone and verify the insert
Weak or mixed phenotypeFeeding too short, old plates, or low dsRNA expressionFeed 36–72 h, use second-generation plates and 25 °C if the phenotype allows [2][5]
Neuronal gene not silencedNeurons resist feeding RNAiUse a neuronal-RNAi-enhanced strain (e.g., sid-1 expressed in neurons, eri-1 or lin-15) [1]
Contamination on platesNon-sterile technique or old cultureUse sterile reagents and prepare plates in a hood; add antibiotic
Worms starve or crowdToo many worms or too little foodReduce worm number or use larger plates
Phenotype after prior injectionInjected dsRNA suppresses later feeding RNAiDo not inject worms before feeding [1]

Safety and waste

  • E. coli HT115(DE3) is a BSL-1 laboratory strain; dispose of cultures and plates by autoclaving.
  • Ampicillin and carbenicillin can sensitize; wear gloves and avoid inhaling powder.
  • Bleach (hypochlorite) is corrosive; use in a hood with gloves and eye protection.
  • Genetically modified worms and bacteria follow your institution's biosafety rules.
