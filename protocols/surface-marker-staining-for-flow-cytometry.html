Surface Marker Staining for Flow Cytometry

1.5–2 h · 2026-11-23

Stain cell-surface antigens on live mammalian cells for flow cytometry: antibody titration, Fc block, 30 min cold staining in the dark, washes, viability dye, optional paraformaldehyde fixation, with unstained, FMO and compensation controls.

⬇ Word (.docx)⬇ PowerPoint (.pptx)

Purpose

To label cell-surface proteins on live mammalian cells with fluorochrome-conjugated antibodies so that cell populations can be identified and quantified by flow cytometry.

Principle

Antibodies bind surface antigens on intact cells in a protein-containing buffer that limits non-specific binding. Fc receptors on myeloid and B cells can bind antibodies through their Fc portion, so an Fc-blocking step is added before the specific antibodies [1][2]. Staining is done cold and in the dark to protect the fluorochromes and to keep antigens from being internalized, and unbound antibody is removed by washing [1][2][4]. The antibody amount is set by titration so that the separation between positive and negative cells is maximal [3]. Dead cells bind antibodies non-specifically, so a viability dye is used to exclude them from the analysis [1][2].

Materials and reagents

ItemDetails
Single-cell suspensionViability about 95% and not below 90%; 10⁵–10⁶ cells per test; Yale uses 1–5 × 10⁶ cells/mL in 100 µL [1][2].
Staining (FACS) bufferPBS with 0.5–1% BSA or 2–10% FBS; 0.1% sodium azide may be added, but leave it out if cells will be recovered or used live [2][4].
Fc blockMouse: anti-CD16/CD32, 0.5–1 µg per 100 µL. Human: Fc receptor binding inhibitor (for example, 20 µL per 100 µL) or normal serum from the species of the secondary antibody [1][2].
Fluorochrome-conjugated antibodiesTitrated amounts per test; store at 2–8 °C in the dark, do not freeze, spin briefly before use [1][3].
Brilliant-dye staining buffer (only for panels that need it)For example 50 µL per sample, as the supplier specifies [1].
Viability dyeNon-fixable: 7-AAD, propidium iodide or SYTOX, added just before acquisition. Fixable amine-reactive dye if cells will be fixed [1].
Fixative (optional)1–4% paraformaldehyde in PBS [2][4].
Consumables12 × 75 mm polystyrene FACS tubes or 96-well round-bottom plates, 35–40 µm cell-strainer cap tubes, 2 mL wash volume per tube.

Equipment

  • Flow cytometer with the lasers and filters needed for the chosen fluorochromes
  • Refrigerated centrifuge with plate or tube rotor
  • Ice bucket or 4 °C cold room
  • Cell counter or hemocytometer with trypan blue
  • Biosafety cabinet

Procedure

  1. Titrate each new antibody lot first: start at 10 µg/mL and make eight 2-fold dilutions, using the same cells, volume, time and temperature as the real experiment [3]. (1–2 h (once per lot))
  2. Acquire at least 1,000 live positive cells per dilution and choose the concentration with the highest separation index between positive and negative populations [3]. (30 min)
  3. Prepare cells as a single-cell suspension, strain if clumpy, count and check viability; keep on ice [1][2]. (15 min)
  4. Adjust cells to 2–20 × 10⁶/mL in cold staining buffer and aliquot 50 µL (10⁵–10⁶ cells) into each tube or well. Prepare a tube for every control [1][2]. (10 min)
  5. Add Fc block directly to the 50 µL of cells (mouse anti-CD16/CD32 at 0.5–1 µg per 100 µL of final volume, or the human reagent). Incubate 10–20 min at 2–8 °C [1][2]. (15 min)
  6. Prepare the antibody master mix in staining buffer (plus Brilliant staining buffer if the panel uses polymer dyes) at the titrated concentrations, 50 µL per test [1][3]. (5 min)
  7. Add 50 µL of antibody mix to the 50 µL of cells without washing (100 µL total) and mix gently. Do not vortex [1]. (2 min)
  8. Incubate for 30 min at 2–8 °C in the dark. Purified or biotinylated primary antibodies need about 60 min [1][2]. (30–60 min)
  9. If a secondary reagent is needed, wash once (see the wash step below), then incubate with it in 100 µL for at least 30 min at 2–8 °C in the dark [1][2]. (30 min)
  10. Wash by adding 2 mL staining buffer, centrifuging at 400–600 × g for 5 min and discarding the supernatant. Repeat once [1][4]. (15 min)
  11. Resuspend in 200–500 µL cold staining buffer. For non-fixed cells add the non-fixable viability dye (for example, 7-AAD) just before acquisition [1]. (5 min)
  12. If fixing: resuspend the pellet in 100 µL of 1–4% paraformaldehyde for 10–15 min at room temperature, spin, and resuspend in 200 µL–1 mL PBS [2][4]. (25 min)
  13. Acquire on the flow cytometer as soon as possible; keep tubes cold and dark and record at least 10,000 events in the gate of interest, or more for rare populations [2][3]. (20–40 min)

Pause points

  • After the washes cells may sit on ice for a short time in the dark.
  • After fixation and washing, stained fixed cells may be stored at 2–8 °C in the dark: acquire the same day or next day (the supplier allows up to 3 days with its own fixation buffer) [1][2].

Expected results

  • A clearly separated positive population and a negative population, with the live singlet gate containing most of the events [3].
  • Stained cells are brighter than the unstained control, and the antibody titration shows a plateau of the staining index [3].

Controls

  • Unstained cells, to set autofluorescence and voltages.
  • Viability-dye only, plus a heat-killed sample for gating dead cells.
  • Single-stained compensation controls for every fluorochrome (cells or beads) in a multicolor panel.
  • Fluorescence-minus-one (FMO) controls to set positive gates in multicolor panels.
  • Isotype or biological negative controls, used with titration and blocking checks [4].

Troubleshooting

ProblemLikely causeSolution
Weak signalToo little antibody, low antigen, or cells stained at the wrong temperatureRe-titrate; check antibody storage; try a brighter fluorochrome [1][3]
High backgroundToo much antibody, Fc binding, or dead cellsTitrate down, add Fc block, add a viability dye and include more washes [2][3]
Cell lossAggressive aspiration or low-speed spinLeave a little liquid; spin at 400–600 × g [1]
ClumpingDNA from dead cellsStrain through a 35–40 µm filter; use a DNase or EDTA if the cells allow it
Signal drifts after fixationTandem dye degradation or delayProtect from light and acquire promptly [1][2]

Safety and waste

  • Sodium azide is highly toxic and can form explosive metal azides in plumbing; label buffers, avoid pouring concentrated azide down the drain and follow your waste rules.
  • Paraformaldehyde is toxic and a suspected carcinogen; handle it in a fume hood or ventilated cabinet and dispose of it as chemical waste.
  • Human, primate or other primary cells may carry pathogens: handle at the biosafety level set by your institution, with universal precautions; sort or acquire only after fixation if the biosafety office requires it.
  • 7-AAD and propidium iodide bind DNA; wear gloves and dispose of them as hazardous waste.
