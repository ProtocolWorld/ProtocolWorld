DAPI and Hoechst Nuclear Staining

Estimated time: About 1.5 h (fixed cells) or 45 min (live cells) | Protocol World

To label cell nuclei with blue fluorescence for localization, counting, morphology and counterstaining, using DAPI in fixed samples and Hoechst 33342 in living cells.

DAPI and Hoechst 33342 bind the minor groove of double-stranded DNA, preferring AT-rich sequences, and fluoresce much more strongly when bound (DAPI about 20-fold) [1][3]. Both are excited by UV (about 350–360 nm) and emit blue light (about 460 nm) [1][2]. Hoechst 33342 crosses intact membranes, so it can stain live cells; DAPI is usually applied to fixed cells and is more photostable [2][3][6]. Because the dyes can damage DNA, are mutagenic, and UV excitation is phototoxic, keep doses and light exposure low in live imaging [2][5].

DAPI stock, 5 mg/mLDissolve 10 mg DAPI (dihydrochloride or dilactate) in 2 mL distilled water (sonicate if needed); do not use PBS; aliquot, store at −20 °C, protected from light [1]
Hoechst 33342 stock, 10 mg/mLDissolve 100 mg in 10 mL deionized water (sonicate if needed); store at 2–6 °C for up to 6 months or at −20 °C for longer, protected from light [2]
DAPI working solution300 nM DAPI in PBS (about 0.1 µg/mL); prepare fresh from the stock [1]
Hoechst working solution (live cells)About 1–2 µM (≈0.6–1.1 µg/mL) in the cells' own culture medium; alternatively about 5 µg/mL in PBS (5 µL of 10 mg/mL stock in 10 mL) for 5–15 min [2][4]
1× PBS, pH 7.4137 mM NaCl, 2.7 mM KCl, 10 mM Na₂HPO₄, 1.8 mM KH₂PO₄; autoclave or filter-sterilize
PBS+ for live cellsPBS with added CaCl₂ and MgCl₂ so cells stay attached [3]
Fixative4% paraformaldehyde in PBS, freshly prepared or thawed; avoid glutaraldehyde because it raises blue autofluorescence [3]
Permeabilization buffer (optional)0.1–0.25% Triton X-100 in PBS, only if combined with antibody staining
Antifade mounting mediumFor example ProLong Gold or SlowFade Gold; or seal with nail polish or wax [1]
SamplesCells on glass coverslips, or cryosections/paraffin sections (deparaffinized and rehydrated) on slides
Glass slides and coverslipsNo. 1.5 coverslips for high-resolution imaging
  • Fluorescence microscope with DAPI/UV filter set (excitation about 350–360 nm, emission about 460 nm)
  • Optional confocal with 405 nm laser or UV line
  • Humidified staining chamber
  • Micropipettes (10–1000 µL)
  • Fume hood for paraformaldehyde
  • 37 °C CO₂ incubator (live cells)
  • Forceps and absorbent tissue
  • Dark box or foil for light protection
  • Fixed cells: rinse coverslip cultures briefly in PBS, then fix in 4% paraformaldehyde for 10–15 min at room temperature in a fume hood. (20 min)
  • Wash 3 × 5 min in PBS. Pause point: fixed samples can be stored in PBS at 4 °C, protected from light, for a few days before staining.
  • Optional: permeabilize in 0.1–0.25% Triton X-100 in PBS for 5–10 min if antibodies are also used; DAPI itself does not need permeabilization [1]. (10 min)
  • Complete any antibody or other staining first; apply DAPI last, because it is the final counterstain [1].
  • Prepare 300 nM DAPI in PBS from the 5 mg/mL stock (14.3 mM for the dihydrochloride): dilute about 1:47,000 in two steps [1].
  • Equilibrate in PBS, then cover the cells with about 300 µL of 300 nM DAPI for 1–5 min at room temperature in the dark [1]. (1–5 min)
  • Rinse several times in PBS to remove unbound dye; drain excess buffer from the edge with absorbent tissue [1]. (5 min)
  • Tissue sections: after the last antibody wash, cover each section with the same DAPI solution for 5–10 min in a humid dark chamber, then wash 3 × 5 min (adapted, optimize per tissue) [1]. (25 min)
  • Mount in antifade medium (ProLong Gold or SlowFade Gold), lower the coverslip without bubbles, or seal edges with nail polish [1].
  • Pause point: cure per mountant instructions, then store slides flat at 4 °C in the dark; image within days to weeks.
  • Image with a DAPI filter set (about 358 nm excitation, 461 nm emission); nuclei should be bright blue with little cytoplasmic signal [1]. (10 min)
  • Live cells: grow cells on coverslips or in a glass-bottom dish, remove the medium and rinse 3 × with PBS+ or warm medium; never let cells dry out [3].
  • Dilute 10 mg/mL Hoechst 33342 to about 1–2 µM in the cells' own medium (e.g., 1:10,000 of a 20 mM stock) and add it; use 50 µL per well of a 96-well plate [4]. (5 min)
  • Incubate 20 min at 37 °C protected from light; shorter (5–15 min) or room-temperature incubations also work, and 10–30 min at room temperature was used on coverslips [2][3][4]. (20 min)
  • Replace the dye solution with fresh warm medium or PBS+ (wash 2–3 ×); you may also image with dye present at low concentration [2][4]. (5 min)
  • Mount the coverslip in a live-cell chamber or in medium on a slide, image at 37 °C if possible, using a UV or 405 nm source (about 353 nm excitation, 461–483 nm emission) [2][3]. (10 min)
  • Minimize phototoxicity: use the lowest dye concentration and light dose, long intervals between frames, and neutral-density filters [5].
  • Dispose of dye solutions and stained materials as chemical waste per institutional rules; wipe benches and wear gloves throughout [1][2].
  • The protocol can be paused after sterilization and labeling; store solutions as described.
  • Fixed samples show uniformly bright blue nuclei, brighter condensed chromatin in mitotic cells, and minimal cytoplasmic signal.
  • Live cells show blue nuclei within 5–30 min; mitotic chromosomes and apoptotic condensed or fragmented nuclei are readily recognized.
  • Blue nuclear signal separates cleanly from green, yellow, or red probes in multicolor imaging [1].
  • Unstained sample imaged with the same settings to assess autofluorescence [3]
  • Secondary-only or no-primary control when DAPI is used as a counterstain
  • Untreated, unstained live cells grown alongside to check that Hoechst and light exposure do not alter growth or division
  • Known positive: fixed HeLa or other cell line stained at 300 nM DAPI

  • DAPI and Hoechst bind DNA and are known mutagens: wear nitrile gloves, lab coat and eye protection; avoid skin contact and inhaling powder (weigh in a fume hood) [1][2].
  • Dispose of stock, working solutions, and contaminated tips, slides and gels as hazardous chemical waste according to institutional and local regulations; do not pour down the drain [1][2].
  • Paraformaldehyde is toxic and a suspected carcinogen: prepare and use in a fume hood and collect waste separately.
  • DMF, if used for DAPI stock, is a hazardous solvent: use in a fume hood.
  • UV light damages eyes and skin: use shielded microscope sources and never look into the beam. Consult the safety data sheet (SDS) before use [3].
