Biofilm Crystal Violet Assay

3 days (about 2 h hands-on) · 2026-11-25

Static 96-well biofilm assay: grow E. coli or other bacteria 24 h, wash away planktonic cells, stain with 0.1% crystal violet, dissolve in 30% acetic acid and read A550 to quantify biofilm biomass.

⬇ Word (.docx)⬇ PowerPoint (.pptx)

Purpose

To quantify the biomass of surface-attached bacterial biofilm (for example E. coli) grown statically in 96-well plates by staining with crystal violet and measuring absorbance.

Principle

Bacteria grown without shaking in a polystyrene microtiter plate attach to the well wall and form a biofilm. Planktonic cells are washed away with water, and the remaining cells and matrix are stained with 0.1% crystal violet, which binds negatively charged biomass [1][2]. After rinsing, the dye is dissolved in 30% acetic acid and its absorbance at 550 nm is proportional to the amount of attached biomass [1]. Optimal medium, temperature and time must be determined empirically for each strain [1][2].

Materials and reagents

ItemDetails
Bacterial strainsTest strain(s), a biofilm-forming positive control and a non-attaching or sterile-medium negative control [1]
LB broth (per liter)10 g tryptone, 5 g yeast extract, 10 g NaCl; autoclave. Other media such as M63 with glucose and casamino acids are also used for biofilm work [1]
96-well polystyrene platesSterile, round- or flat-bottom, not tissue-culture treated, with lid, for growth [2]; optically clear flat-bottom plate for reading [1][2]
0.1% (w/v) crystal violet0.1 g crystal violet in 100 mL water; wear gloves because the powder stains skin and clothes [1]
30% (v/v) acetic acid in water30 mL glacial acetic acid plus 70 mL water; also used as the blank [1]
WaterTap or distilled water in a tub or tray for washing [1][2]
Alternative solubilizer (optional)95% ethanol, as used by [3], or 80% ethanol/20% acetone, which [2] lists for E. coli

Equipment

  • 37 °C incubator for static growth (an incubator at 25–30 °C may suit some E. coli strains) [2]
  • Multichannel pipette and sterile reservoirs
  • Plate reader for 550 nm absorbance
  • Plastic tubs or trays for washing and a paper towel pad
  • Shaking incubator for overnight cultures

Procedure

  1. Inoculate a 3–5 mL LB culture from a single colony of each strain and grow overnight with shaking at 37 °C [1][2]. (16–18 h)
  2. Dilute each overnight culture 1:100 in fresh medium (for example, 50 µL of culture in 4.95 mL of LB) [1][2]. (5 min)
  3. Pipet 100 µL of each dilution into 4–8 replicate wells of a 96-well plate. Add 100 µL of sterile medium to blank wells [1][2]. If evaporation is a concern, fill the outer wells with sterile water and do not use them for data. (15 min)
  4. Cover the plate and incubate it without shaking at 37 °C for 24 h. Many organisms form a biofilm within 48 h; test several times to choose the best [1][2][3]. (24 h)
  5. Optional: before washing, read OD600 of the culture to check that strains grew equally, since a growth defect can look like poor attachment [2]. (5 min)
  6. Flick out the liquid by turning the plate over a waste tray and discard into bleach. Do not pipet from the biofilm [1][3]. (2 min)
  7. Submerge the plate in a tub of water and shake out the water. Repeat once. This removes planktonic cells without disturbing the biofilm [1][3]. (3 min)
  8. Add 125 µL of 0.1% crystal violet to every well, including blanks, and stain at room temperature for 10–15 min [1]. (15 min)
  9. Shake out the stain into a waste container. Rinse the plate 3–4 times by submerging it in clean water, shaking out the water each time [1][3]. (5 min)
  10. Invert the plate and tap on a paper towel pad. Dry it upside down for a few hours or overnight; dried stained plates keep for weeks [1][2]. (2 h – overnight)
  11. Add 125 µL of 30% acetic acid to each well and incubate at room temperature for 10–15 min so that the dye dissolves completely [1]. (15 min)
  12. Transfer 125 µL of the dissolved dye into a new optically clear flat-bottom plate. Use 30% acetic acid as the blank [1]. (5 min)
  13. Read absorbance at 550 nm (500–600 nm is acceptable) [1][2]. Subtract the sterile-medium blank wells from each replicate. (5 min)

Pause points

  • After step 10: dry stained plates can be stored at room temperature for several weeks before solubilization [2].
  • After the overnight culture: dilute and plate the next day if more convenient.

Expected results

  • Biofilm-forming strains give clearly higher A550 than sterile-medium blanks; the positive control gives a strong signal.
  • Replicates should agree closely; report the mean ± standard deviation of 4–8 wells per strain [1].
  • A reading above about 1.0–1.5 may be outside the linear range; dilute an aliquot of dissolved dye in 30% acetic acid and re-read.

Controls

  • Sterile medium wells (no bacteria) to correct for dye binding to the plastic
  • Positive control strain known to form biofilm on every plate, and a negative control strain if available [1]
  • Same medium, temperature and plate type for all strains
  • Acetic acid blank for the plate reader

Troubleshooting

ProblemLikely causeSolution
High background in blank wellsInsufficient washing or crystal violet binding to plasticRinse 3–4 times and use fresh water; verify wells were sterile [1][3]
Variable replicatesPipetting into the biofilm, uneven washing or edge effectsFlick liquid gently, wash all wells equally and use 4–8 replicates [1][2]
Weak signal for strains that should form biofilmToo short incubation, wrong medium or temperatureExtend time toward 48 h or optimize medium and temperature for the strain [1][2]
Low signal because of growth defectStrain grows slowly, not poor attachmentMeasure OD600 before washing and compare growth [2]
Crystal violet does not dissolve completelyToo little solvent or short timeIncrease incubation or pipet up and down; consider 95% ethanol or 80% ethanol/20% acetone [2][3]

Safety and waste

  • Crystal violet powder is hygroscopic and stains skin and clothing: wear gloves and a lab coat [1]. It is harmful if swallowed or inhaled; check the safety data sheet.
  • Biofilm cultures can contain pathogens; follow the biosafety level of the organism and use appropriate containment [2].
  • Collect liquid waste with bleach and dispose of stained waste and plates as chemical or biohazard waste per institutional rules. Acetic acid is corrosive; handle it in a fume hood.
