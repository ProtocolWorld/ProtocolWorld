Fluorescent Western Blotting

1.5 days · 2026-11-27

Detect and quantify one or two proteins per blot with near-infrared fluorescent secondaries: low-fluorescence membrane, detergent-free blocking, 1 h dark secondary incubation, two-channel scanning, and normalization to total protein.

⬇ Word (.docx)⬇ PowerPoint (.pptx)

Purpose

To detect and quantify one or two proteins on the same membrane with fluorescently labeled secondary antibodies, giving a wide linear range and reliable multiplexing without film or enzymatic substrates.

Principle

After SDS-PAGE and transfer, a membrane-bound target is recognized by a primary antibody and then by a secondary antibody carrying a near-infrared dye (for example, emission near 700 nm or 800 nm). Because the signal is emitted directly by the dye and does not depend on an enzyme reaction, it stays proportional to the amount of protein over a broad range, and two targets can be read in separate channels from one blot [1][3]. Low-autofluorescence membranes, fluorescence-compatible blocker, detergent kept out of the blocking step, and protection from light keep background low [1][2][3]. Normalizing the target to total protein or to a validated loading control converts band intensities into comparable quantities.

Materials and reagents

ItemDetails
Protein lysateCells or tissue lysed in RIPA buffer with protease and phosphatase inhibitors; clarified at about 15,000 × g for 5 min at 4 °C; protein measured by BCA or Bradford [3]
Sample buffer4× LDS or Laemmli buffer with reducing agent (for example, 50 mM DTT final); heat at 70 °C for 10 min for LDS buffer [3]
Precast or hand-cast gelPercentage chosen for the target size; load 1–10 µg of lysate per lane for abundant targets and up to about 20–30 µg for low-abundance targets [2][3]
MembraneLow-fluorescence PVDF (activate 30 s in 100% methanol, then 5 min in TBS) or nitrocellulose (hydrate 5 min in TBS) [1]
Transfer bufferYour standard wet or semi-dry buffer; keep the membrane and filter paper free of ink and dust
TBS and TBS-T20 mM Tris-HCl pH 7.6, 150 mM NaCl; TBS-T is TBS with 0.1–0.2% Tween-20 [1][3]
Blocking bufferFluorescence-validated blocker (for example, Intercept/Odyssey blocking buffer in TBS) or 3–5% BSA in TBS; avoid milk with phospho-targets or goat antibodies [1][2][3]. No Tween-20 during blocking
Primary antibodiesTwo from different host species for multiplexing (for example, rabbit and mouse); dilutions per the supplier, typically 1:200–1:5,000 [1]
Fluorescent secondary antibodiesCross-adsorbed, one 700 nm-channel and one 800 nm-channel conjugate, raised in the same host species; start at 1:20,000 (700 nm) and 1:6,000–1:20,000 (800 nm) [1][3]
SDS, 10% stock (optional)Add to secondary diluent to 0.01–0.02% to reduce 700 nm background, especially on PVDF [1][3]
Total protein stain (optional)Reversible or fluorescent membrane total-protein stain compatible with your imager, used for normalization [1][2]

Equipment

  • Gel and transfer system
  • Orbital shaker
  • Infrared fluorescence imager
  • Clean trays for fluorescent blots
  • Blunt clean forceps
  • Pencil for labeling
  • Foil or light-proof box
  • Image analysis software

Procedure

  1. Prepare lysate, quantify protein, and mix with sample buffer and reducing agent; heat as required (70 °C, 10 min for LDS) and spin briefly [3]. (1 h)
  2. Load 1–10 µg of lysate per lane (more for rare targets) plus prestained marker. Use an orange or neutral tracking dye, since some blue dyes fluoresce in the far red [2]. (10 min)
  3. Run the gel at constant voltage per the manufacturer (for example, 200 V for about 35 min for a Bis-Tris mini gel with MES buffer) [3]. (35–90 min)
  4. Transfer to the membrane. Activate PVDF 30 s in methanol, then equilibrate in TBS; hydrate nitrocellulose in TBS. Handle by the edges with clean forceps [1]. (7 min–1 h)
  5. Optional: rinse in water and image or stain for total protein now, then destain according to the stain instructions [1][2]. (15 min)
  6. Block in fluorescence-compatible blocker, at least 0.4–0.8 mL per cm² of membrane, for 1 h at room temperature with gentle shaking; keep Tween-20 out of this step [1][3]. (1 h)
  7. Dilute primary antibodies in blocker plus 0.1–0.2% Tween-20 and incubate overnight at 4 °C with gentle shaking (or 1–4 h at room temperature) [1][3]. (Overnight)
  8. Wash 4 × 5 min (or 3 × 10 min) in TBS-T with vigorous shaking, using enough buffer to cover the membrane [1][3]. (20–30 min)
  9. Dilute the two secondary antibodies in blocker plus 0.1–0.2% Tween-20 (add 0.01–0.02% SDS for PVDF). Incubate 1 h at room temperature in the dark [1][3]. (1 h)
  10. Do not exceed 1 h; longer exposure raises background. Keep the tray covered with foil during the incubation [1][3].
  11. Wash 4 × 5 min in TBS-T in the dark, then rinse 2 × 5 min in TBS without Tween-20 to remove detergent before imaging [1][2]. (30 min)
  12. Scan the wet or air-dried membrane on a two-channel infrared imager. Drying often increases signal but can also increase background [1]. (10 min)
  13. Quantify integrated band intensity, subtract local background, and normalize to total protein or a validated loading control [1][3].
  14. Store dried membranes between clean paper at room temperature in the dark (or wet in TBS at 4 °C if reprobing) [1][3].

Pause points

  • After transfer: membranes can be dried and stored in the dark at room temperature.
  • After the primary antibody incubation: leave overnight at 4 °C.
  • After the final wash and scan: dried, light-protected blots can be rescanned for months.

Expected results

  • Sharp bands in the 700 nm and 800 nm channels at the expected molecular weights, on a low, even background.
  • Signal that scales with the amount loaded over a wide range, allowing relative quantification.
  • Two proteins (for example, a phospho-protein and its total form) visible on one membrane.

Controls

  • Secondary-only membrane strip or lane to reveal non-specific secondary binding
  • Knockout, knockdown, or untreated lysate to confirm antibody specificity
  • Dilution series of one lysate (for example, 2.5–40 µg) to confirm the linear range
  • Total protein stain or validated loading control for normalization
  • Single-color blots before the multiplex blot to check for cross-reactivity

Troubleshooting

ProblemLikely causeSolution
High, speckled backgroundDust or fluorescent contaminants, ink on membrane, dirty traysUse clean trays, forceps, and pencil; filter buffers; rinse equipment with water then 70% ethanol [1]
High uniform 700 nm backgroundToo much secondary, PVDF membrane, detergent in blockingDilute the secondary further, add 0.01–0.02% SDS to the secondary diluent, and remove Tween-20 from the blocking step [1][3]
Weak or no signalPoor primary antibody, low target abundance, short transferOptimize primary concentration; use the 800 nm channel for rare targets; check transfer with a total protein stain [1][3]
Extra bandsNon-specific primary or excess secondaryTry another blocker such as casein or BSA, reduce secondary concentration, and verify with controls [1][3]
Signal in both channelsCross-reactive secondaries or same-species primariesUse primaries from different species and cross-adsorbed secondaries; run single-color blots first [1]
Signal fadesLight exposure, long storage wetProtect from light and store dry in the dark [1][3]

Safety and waste

  • Acrylamide gels (unpolymerized) are neurotoxic; wear gloves and dispose of gel waste as chemical waste.
  • Methanol is flammable and toxic; use it in a ventilated area.
  • Dispose of RIPA lysates, buffer waste, and used blocking solutions according to institutional chemical and biohazard rules; treat human or animal lysates as potentially infectious.
  • Some preservatives (for example, sodium azide in antibody stocks) are toxic and can inhibit peroxidase-based methods; handle with gloves and follow disposal rules.
