cDNA Synthesis by Reverse Transcription

About 1.5 h · 2026-11-25

A two-step, 20 µL first-strand cDNA synthesis from purified total RNA using oligo(dT), random hexamer or gene-specific priming, a thermostable reverse transcriptase and an RNase inhibitor. The cDNA is ready for qPCR or PCR, with no-RT and no-template controls.

⬇ Word (.docx)⬇ PowerPoint (.pptx)

Purpose

To convert purified RNA into single-stranded cDNA that can be used as a template for qPCR, PCR or cloning, while monitoring genomic DNA carryover and contamination.

Principle

Reverse transcriptase is a DNA polymerase that copies RNA into complementary DNA from a primer annealed to the template. Oligo(dT) primers bind the poly(A) tail of mRNA, random primers anneal along all RNA types and tolerate degraded RNA, and gene-specific primers give the most sensitive copy of a single target [1][3][4]. A brief 65 °C denaturation followed by rapid cooling opens RNA secondary structure so primers can bind [3]. Engineered enzymes work at 50–55 °C, which further reduces structure-related stalling, and enzymes with RNase H activity are favored for qPCR [2][3][5]. Heat then inactivates the enzyme so it does not interfere with downstream amplification [3][5]. A reaction without reverse transcriptase (no-RT) detects contaminating genomic DNA, and a reaction without RNA detects reagent contamination [1][2][4].

Materials and reagents

ItemDetails

Equipment

  • Thermal cycler with heated lid
  • Ice bucket or cold block
  • Micropipettes (0.5–10 µL, 10–100 µL) with filter tips
  • Mini centrifuge for PCR tubes
  • Spectrophotometer or fluorometer for RNA quantification
  • Dedicated clean bench or PCR hood
  • Freezer (−20 °C) and ice for storing cDNA

Procedure

  1. Wipe the bench and pipettes with an RNase decontamination solution; wear gloves and use only RNase-free tips and tubes. (5 min)
  2. Thaw RNA, primers, dNTPs, buffer and DTT on ice, then mix by flicking and spin briefly. Keep the enzyme and RNase inhibitor at −20 °C until use [4][5]. (10 min)
  3. Measure RNA concentration and purity (A260/A280 near 2.0) and confirm integrity; poor RNA quality lowers yield and shifts Ct values [3][4].
  4. Choose a primer strategy: oligo(dT) for intact poly(A) mRNA, random hexamers for degraded or non-polyadenylated RNA, or a gene-specific primer for one target [1][3].
  5. Optional gDNA removal: treat up to 1 µg RNA with DNase per its manual (commonly 2–15 min at 37 °C), then inactivate as directed; skip if qPCR primers span exon junctions [2]. (15–30 min)
  6. Dilute RNA with nuclease-free water so the volume to add is no more than 11 µL; the same amount is used in every reaction and the no-RT control [3][4].
  7. On ice, label four tube types: sample (+RT), no-RT, no-template control (NTC) and, if used, a positive-control RNA.
  8. Assemble each 13 µL annealing mix: RNA (10 ng–1 µg), 1 µL primer (50 µM oligo(dT) or random hexamers), 1 µL 10 mM dNTPs, and water to 13 µL [1][4].
  9. For the NTC, replace RNA with nuclease-free water. For the no-RT control, prepare an identical annealing mix with the same RNA [1][2].
  10. Mix gently and spin down. Heat at 65 °C for 5 min with the heated lid on, then snap-cool on ice for at least 1 min to hold the primers on the RNA [3]. (7 min)
  11. Prepare the RT master mix on ice (add 10% overage): per reaction 4 µL 5× RT buffer, 1 µL 100 mM DTT, 1 µL RNase inhibitor (40 U/µL) and 1 µL reverse transcriptase (200 U/µL) = 7 µL [1][4][5]. (5 min)
  12. Prepare a no-RT master mix by replacing the 1 µL of enzyme with 1 µL of nuclease-free water. Mix by flicking and spin briefly; do not vortex the enzyme [1][2].
  13. Add 7 µL of RT master mix to each sample and NTC tube, and 7 µL of no-RT mix to each no-RT tube, giving 20 µL per reaction. Mix gently and spin down [1][4].
  14. For random hexamers, hold at 25 °C for 10 min so short primers can anneal and begin extension; skip this for oligo(dT) or gene-specific primers [1][4]. (10 min)
  15. Incubate at 50–55 °C for 10–50 min for thermostable enzymes (about 55 °C for 10 min is common for fast kits); use 37 °C for 50 min for standard MMLV [1][3][4][5]. (10–50 min)
  16. For difficult, structured RNA, use the top of the enzyme's temperature range (about 55 °C) and consider a gene-specific primer [3][5].
  17. Inactivate the enzyme at 80–85 °C for 5–10 min, then hold at 4 °C [3][4][5]. (5–10 min)
  18. If the enzyme lacks RNase H activity, add 1 µL RNase H (2 U) and incubate at 37 °C for 20 min to remove RNA from the cDNA:RNA hybrid [2][5]. (20 min)
  19. Spin tubes briefly. Keep cDNA on ice for immediate use, or dilute it 1:5 to 1:10 in nuclease-free TE and aliquot into single-use tubes [3].
  20. Store at −20 °C for short-term use (weeks) or −80 °C for longer, avoiding repeated freeze–thaw cycles [1][3].
  21. Use the cDNA in qPCR or PCR at no more than 10% of the final reaction volume (for example 2 µL in 20 µL) [1][4].
  22. Run qPCR on sample, no-RT, NTC and a no-template qPCR control in at least technical duplicate, and inspect melt curves for specific products [3][4].
  23. Record the primer type, RNA input, enzyme, temperatures and times so the RT step can be reported to MIQE standards [3][4].

Pause points

  • After RNA quantification (step 3): keep RNA at −80 °C.
  • After the 65 °C denaturation (step 10): keep tubes on ice for no more than about 10 min before adding the RT mix.
  • After enzyme inactivation (step 17): cDNA can be stored at 4 °C overnight or at −20 °C for weeks [1][3].

Expected results

  • Successful reactions give a clear qPCR amplification curve for abundant targets, with Ct values that fall as RNA input increases and a single melt-curve peak.
  • The no-RT control should show no amplification or a Ct many cycles later than the +RT sample (at least about 5 cycles); the NTC should show none [2][3].
  • Yield and Ct are not guaranteed to be linear with RNA input across a wide range, so keep input constant across compared samples [4].

Controls

  • No-RT control: all components except reverse transcriptase, detecting genomic DNA carryover; signal can also come from primer-dimers or contamination, so check the melt curve [1][2][3].
  • No-template control (NTC): all reagents but no RNA, detecting contamination of reagents [1].
  • Positive control RNA: a known RNA, for example a reference RNA, to confirm enzyme and primer function.
  • Constant RNA input and primer strategy across all compared samples [3][4].

Troubleshooting

ProblemLikely causeSolution
No or late amplification in the +RT sampleDegraded RNA, inhibitors, inactive enzyme, or RNase contaminationCheck RNA integrity and purity, re-purify if needed, use fresh enzyme and inhibitor, and clean the workspace with RNase decontamination solution [3][4].
Amplification in the no-RT controlGenomic DNA carryover or contamination, or primer-dimersTreat RNA with DNase, redesign primers across exon junctions, replace contaminated reagents, and check the melt curve [2][3].
Amplification in the NTCContaminated water, primers, or mastermix, or aerosol carryoverUse fresh aliquots, filter tips and separate pre- and post-PCR areas, then repeat [1].
Poor representation of 5′ regions or long transcriptsOligo(dT) 3′ bias, RNA secondary structure, or degraded RNAUse random hexamers or an oligo(dT) plus hexamer blend, use a higher RT temperature (up to 55 °C), or use a gene-specific primer [3][5].
Low cDNA yield for mRNA targets with random primersrRNA is most of total RNA, so hexamers prime all RNA typesUse oligo(dT) or a hexamer/oligo(dT) blend, or raise the primer concentration moderately [2][3].
High Ct values or poor replicates at high RNA inputEnzyme saturation by too much RNA, or carried-over inhibitorsStay within the kit's range (usually up to 1 µg per 20 µL); dilute cDNA 1:5 to 1:10 before qPCR [3][4].
qPCR inhibited by the RT productToo much RT reaction in the PCR, or enzyme not inactivatedKeep cDNA at ≤10% of PCR volume, confirm 80–85 °C inactivation, and dilute the cDNA [1][4][5].

Safety and waste

  • Wear gloves, lab coat and safety glasses; RNase decontamination solutions can irritate skin and eyes.
  • DTT is harmful if swallowed and has a strong odor; handle stock solutions with gloves.
  • The thermal cycler block, heated lid and tubes can cause burns at 65–85 °C.
  • If ethidium bromide or other stains are used downstream, follow local rules for mutagens.
  • Dispose of used tips, tubes and kit reagents as chemical or biohazardous waste according to institutional rules; treat human or animal-derived RNA samples as potentially infectious.
