Plasmid Miniprep by Alkaline Lysis

1 h + overnight culture · 2026-11-17

Isolate plasmid DNA from 2 mL of E. coli overnight culture with homemade P1, P2 and P3 buffers, isopropanol precipitation and an ethanol wash, then check yield, purity and identity.

⬇ Word (.docx)⬇ PowerPoint (.pptx)

Purpose

To isolate plasmid DNA from 1–5 mL of an overnight E. coli culture using homemade alkaline lysis buffers (P1, P2, P3), isopropanol precipitation and an ethanol wash, giving plasmid DNA suitable for restriction digests, PCR and Sanger sequencing.

Principle

Cells are resuspended in Tris–EDTA buffer containing RNase A, then lysed with NaOH and SDS, which dissolves membranes and denatures both chromosomal and plasmid DNA [1][2][3]. Potassium acetate at acidic pH neutralizes the lysate: the small, covalently closed plasmid re-anneals quickly and stays in solution, while chromosomal DNA, proteins and SDS precipitate and are removed by centrifugation [1][2][3]. The plasmid is concentrated from the cleared lysate by isopropanol precipitation, washed with 70% ethanol and dissolved in TE or Tris buffer. Lysis must not exceed about 5 min, and lysates are mixed by gentle inversion, because longer exposure to alkali irreversibly denatures plasmid DNA and vigorous mixing shears genomic DNA into the prep [2][3][4].

Materials and reagents

ItemDetails
LB medium with the correct antibioticFor example 100 µg/mL ampicillin for an ampR plasmid. Use a freshly streaked single colony or a glycerol stock [3].
Buffer P1 (resuspension)50 mM Tris-Cl pH 8.0, 10 mM EDTA. Per 100 mL: 5 mL of 1 M Tris-Cl pH 8.0 + 2 mL of 0.5 M EDTA pH 8.0 + water to 100 mL. Add RNase A to 100 µg/mL (0.5 mL of 20 mg/mL per 100 mL) and store at 4 °C [2][3][4].
Buffer P2 (lysis)200 mM NaOH, 1% (w/v) SDS. Per 100 mL: dissolve 0.8 g NaOH in about 90 mL water, then add 5 mL of 20% SDS and bring to 100 mL. Store at room temperature; do not shake [2][3][4].
Buffer P3 (neutralization)3 M potassium acetate, pH 5.5. Per 100 mL: dissolve 29.45 g potassium acetate in about 50 mL water, adjust to pH 5.5 with glacial acetic acid (about 11 mL), then bring to 100 mL. Store at 4 °C [2].
IsopropanolRoom temperature, 100%
70% ethanolFor washing the DNA pellet [2][3]
TE buffer10 mM Tris-Cl pH 8.0, 1 mM EDTA pH 8.0 (or 5 mM Tris-Cl pH 8.0, or water) for resuspension [1][2][3]

Equipment

  • 37 °C shaking incubator
  • Benchtop microcentrifuge (≥11,000 × g)
  • 1.5 mL and 2 mL microcentrifuge tubes
  • Micropipettes
  • Vacuum aspirator or pipette for removing supernatants
  • Spectrophotometer (NanoDrop or similar)
  • Agarose gel electrophoresis equipment for checking the plasmid

Procedure

  1. Inoculate 2–5 mL LB plus antibiotic with a single colony. Grow overnight, 12–16 h, at 37 °C with shaking to stationary phase [3][4]. (12–16 h)
  2. Transfer 2 mL of culture to a 2 mL tube. Pellet cells at 11,000 × g for 1 min. Discard the supernatant and remove residual medium with a pipette tip [1][3]. (3 min)
  3. Resuspend the pellet completely in 200 µL Buffer P1 (with RNase A) by pipetting or brief vortexing. No clumps should remain [1][2]. (2 min)
  4. Add 200 µL Buffer P2. Mix gently by inverting the tube 6–10 times. Do not vortex. The lysate becomes clear and viscous. Incubate at room temperature for no more than 5 min [1][2][3]. (≤5 min)
  5. Add 250 µL Buffer P3 and mix immediately by inverting 6–10 times. A white precipitate forms. Do not vortex [1][2][3]. (2 min)
  6. Centrifuge at maximum speed (at least 11,000 × g) for 10 min at room temperature. Repeat the spin if the supernatant is still cloudy [1][2][3]. (10 min)
  7. Transfer about 600 µL of the cleared supernatant to a fresh 1.5 mL tube without touching the pellet or the white precipitate [2][3]. (2 min)
  8. Add 0.7 volume isopropanol (about 420 µL), mix by inversion and centrifuge at maximum speed for 10 min. Mark the tube hinge outward so the pellet can be located [2][3]. (12 min)
  9. Carefully remove the supernatant. Add 500–650 µL 70% ethanol without disturbing the pellet and centrifuge at maximum speed for 1–5 min [2][3]. (5 min)
  10. Remove all ethanol with a pipette. Air-dry the open tube for 5–15 min at room temperature, or 5 min at 50 °C, until no ethanol smell remains [2][3]. (5–15 min)
  11. Dissolve the pellet in 30–50 µL TE or 5 mM Tris-Cl pH 8.0. Tap or flick gently and wait several minutes; warming to 50–70 °C improves dissolving [2][3]. (5 min)
  12. Measure A260, A280 and A230 to estimate concentration (A260 = 1.0 corresponds to about 50 ng/µL dsDNA) and purity [3]. (5 min)
  13. Check the plasmid on an agarose gel (5 µL gives a clear band) or digest 10 µL with a suitable restriction enzyme to confirm identity [2][3]. (1 h)
  14. Store the DNA at −20 °C. Cell pellets that are not processed immediately can be frozen at −20 °C [3]. (1 min)

Pause points

  • Pelleted cells can be stored at −20 °C before starting lysis [3].
  • The DNA pellet in TE can be left overnight at 4 °C before use [1].
  • Finished plasmid DNA is stored at −20 °C [3].

Expected results

  • Typical yield from a few mL of high-copy-plasmid culture is about 5–20 µg of plasmid DNA [3].
  • A260/A280 of 1.8–1.9 and A260/A230 of 2.0–2.2 indicate clean double-stranded DNA; RNA raises the A260/A280 ratio, and protein or phenol lowers it [3].
  • On an agarose gel, supercoiled plasmid is the main band; open-circular and linear forms may appear faintly, and a smear or high-molecular-weight band suggests genomic DNA [4].

Controls

  • A culture of untransformed host strain (no plasmid) carried through the protocol to confirm that the bands come from the plasmid
  • A known plasmid as a positive control for yield and quality
  • Uncut versus restriction-digested plasmid on a gel to confirm size [2][3]
  • TE or water blank for the spectrophotometer

Safety and waste

  • Buffer P2 contains NaOH and SDS and is corrosive: wear gloves and eye protection and collect it as hazardous waste instead of pouring it down the drain [1][3].
  • Ethanol and isopropanol are flammable; keep them away from flames and collect as solvent waste.
  • Treat bacterial cultures, pellets and used tubes as biological waste. Decontaminate with bleach or autoclave before disposal, following institutional rules.
  • If you switch to a silica column kit, some buffers contain guanidine salts: never mix them with bleach or strong acids [3].
