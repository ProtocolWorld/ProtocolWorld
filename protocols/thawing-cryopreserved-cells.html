Thawing Cryopreserved Cells

· 45 minutes plus 24 h recovery · 2026-11-14

Rapid 37 °C thaw, aseptic dilution to remove DMSO, viability count and seeding of cryopreserved mammalian cells for reliable recovery.

Estimated time: 45 minutes plus 24 h recovery | Protocol World

To recover cryopreserved mammalian cell lines with high viability while minimizing DMSO toxicity and contamination risk.

Cells frozen in a cryoprotectant such as DMSO are damaged by slow warming and by prolonged DMSO exposure at 37 °C. Rapid warming, immediate dilution in pre-warmed medium and removal of DMSO by gentle centrifugation maximize survival [1][2][3]. Cells are then seeded at the recommended density and checked after 24 h [1][3].

A. Prepare

  • Pre-warm complete medium to 37 °C, disinfect the water bath and label flasks and tubes [2].
  • Place 5–9 mL of medium in a sterile 15 mL tube and pre-equilibrate the culture vessel [1][3].

B. Thaw

  • Wearing a face shield and cryo gloves, remove the vial from liquid nitrogen storage and check the label [1].
  • Hold the lower part of the vial in the 37 °C water bath with gentle swirling, keeping the cap above the water line; thaw for roughly 1–2 min, until only a small ice crystal remains [1][2][3]. Do not over-thaw, because DMSO is toxic to cells at 37 °C [3][4].
  • Wipe or spray the vial with 70% ethanol and move to the biosafety cabinet [1][3].

C. Dilute and wash

  • Transfer the contents slowly (for sensitive cells, dropwise to limit osmotic shock) into the tube of pre-warmed medium [2].
  • Centrifuge gently to remove DMSO: 125 × g for 10 min (ATCC), or 200 × g for 3–5 min [1][2][3]. Fragile primary cells may need lower speeds [4].
  • Aspirate the supernatant carefully and resuspend the pellet in fresh pre-warmed medium.

D. Count and seed

  • Count cells and assess viability by trypan blue or an automated counter [2][3].
  • Seed at the density recommended for the cell line; if none is listed, thaw the whole vial into a T-25 flask [3]. Typical medium volumes: 6-well 3 mL/well, T-25 7 mL, T-75 20 mL, 100 mm dish 10 mL [3].
  • Incubate at 37 °C, 5% CO₂ and examine cells under the microscope after about 24 h for attachment and morphology; subculture when appropriate [1][2]. Some labs add extra serum (10–20% FBS) for sensitive cells during the first 24 h [2].
  • Viability typically well above 70% after thaw for robust cell lines (a 70% threshold is cited for fragile tumor-derived cells) [4].
  • Cells attach and show normal morphology within 24 h; full recovery and a first passage after one to several days. Hybridomas can need several days [1].

  • Liquid nitrogen causes severe cold burns; wear cryo gloves, face shield and lab coat. Sealed vials that took up liquid nitrogen can burst on warming, so follow your institution's rules for handling vials and use a face shield (standard laboratory practice; not stated in the consulted pages).
  • Store cells in vapor phase below −130 °C; viability falls rapidly at higher temperatures [1].
  • DMSO penetrates skin; wear gloves. Cells may carry pathogens, so follow local biosafety rules [4].
