SDS-PAGE

About 3–4 h plus staining · 2026-11-18

Discontinuous Laemmli SDS-PAGE: gel recipes for 8–15% resolving and 5% stacking gels, sample preparation, running conditions, Coomassie staining, controls and troubleshooting.

⬇ Word (.docx)⬇ PowerPoint (.pptx)

Purpose

To separate denatured proteins by molecular weight on a discontinuous Tris-glycine polyacrylamide gel and visualize them by Coomassie staining.

Principle

SDS binds proteins at a roughly constant ratio and, with a reducing agent and heat, unfolds them into negatively charged rods, so migration in a gel depends mainly on size [1]. In the Laemmli system a low-percentage stacking gel at pH 6.8 concentrates the sample into a sharp band before it enters the resolving gel at pH 8.8, where glycine ions overtake proteins and sieving separates them [1][2]. Lower acrylamide percentages resolve large proteins; higher percentages resolve small ones [3].

Materials and reagents

ItemDetails
30% acrylamide/bis-acrylamide solution29:1 to 37.5:1; neurotoxic; ready-made solution preferred
Resolving buffer1.5 M Tris-HCl pH 8.8 (18.15 g Tris in 100 mL, adjusted with HCl) [1]
Stacking buffer0.5 M Tris-HCl pH 6.8 (6 g Tris in 100 mL) [1]
10% (w/v) SDS and 10% (w/v) ammonium persulfate (APS)Make APS fresh or keep at 4 °C for up to 1 month [2]
TEMEDAdded last to start polymerization; use in a fume hood
1× running buffer25 mM Tris, 192 mM glycine, 0.1% SDS (10×: 30 g Tris, 144 g glycine, 10 g SDS per liter) [2][3]
2× Laemmli sample buffer0.125 M Tris-HCl pH 6.8, 4% SDS, 20% glycerol, 10% 2-mercaptoethanol (or 100 mM DTT), 0.004% bromophenol blue [1]
Protein standardPre-stained or unstained molecular weight ladder
Coomassie Brilliant Blue R-250 stain0.1% in 40% methanol, 10% acetic acid; destain 10–15% methanol, 10% acetic acid [1][4]
Isopropanol or water-saturated butanolTo overlay the resolving gel

Equipment

  • Mini gel casting stand, glass plates, 1.0 mm spacers and combs
  • Vertical electrophoresis tank and power supply
  • Heating block at 95 °C
  • Microcentrifuge
  • Orbital shaker
  • Gel imager or scanner

Procedure

  1. Assemble clean glass plates and spacers in the casting stand and test for leaks with water. Mark a line about 1 cm below the comb teeth [2]. (10 min)
  2. For two mini gels mix 10 mL resolving gel. 10%: water 3.8 mL, 30% acrylamide 3.4 mL, 1.5 M Tris pH 8.8 2.6 mL, 10% SDS 100 µL, 10% APS 100 µL, TEMED 10 µL (TEMED last) [1]. (5 min)
  3. Resolving mixes for other gels (per 10 mL): 8% water 4.6, acrylamide 2.6 mL; 12% water 3.2, acrylamide 4.0 mL; 15% water 2.2, acrylamide 5.0 mL; other volumes unchanged [1].
  4. Pour to the mark, overlay gently with isopropanol or water, and let polymerize for 20–30 min at room temperature [1][2]. (30 min)
  5. Pour off the overlay, rinse with water, and dry with filter paper. Mix 4 mL of 5% stacking gel: water 2.25 mL, 30% acrylamide 0.67 mL, 0.5 M Tris pH 6.8 1.0 mL, 10% SDS and APS 40 µL each, TEMED 4 µL [1][2]. (5 min)
  6. Pour the stacking gel to the top, insert the comb without trapping bubbles, and polymerize for 20–30 min [1][2]. (30 min)
  7. Prepare samples: mix protein 1:1 with 2× sample buffer, heat at 95 °C for 5 min, then spin 5 min at 13,000–16,000 × g [1][3]. (15 min)
  8. Place the gel in the tank, fill with 1× running buffer, remove the comb, and rinse wells with running buffer. Remove bubbles under the gel [2][3]. (5 min)
  9. Load 5–10 µL of ladder and 10–20 µL of sample per well (about 5–20 µg of a complex lysate for Coomassie, or ≥0.2 µg of a pure protein) [2][3]. (10 min)
  10. Run at 80 V until the dye passes the stacking gel, then 150–180 V until the dye front reaches the bottom of the gel. Allow about 1–1.5 h total [3]. (1–1.5 h)
  11. Disassemble, trim the stacking gel, and fix in 50% methanol, 10% acetic acid for 30 min to 2 h [1]. (30–120 min)
  12. Stain in 0.1% Coomassie R-250 for 2–4 h (or overnight) with gentle shaking [1][4]. (2 h–overnight)
  13. Destain with 10–15% methanol, 10% acetic acid, changing the solution until the background is clear. Image or scan the gel [1][4]. (2–4 h)

Pause points

  • Casted gels with the comb in place keep 1–2 weeks at 4 °C wrapped in moist paper towels and plastic [2][4].
  • Prepared samples keep at −20 °C; reheat at 95 °C for 5 min before loading [2].
  • A fixed gel can be left in water or 1% acetic acid at 4 °C before imaging.

Expected results

  • Sharp, horizontal bands in each lane; the stacking line is flat and the dye front is straight.
  • Ladder bands resolve in the expected positions; molecular weight can be estimated from a log-linear plot of distance versus size.
  • Coomassie detects about 0.2 µg or more per band; silver staining reaches about 5 ng [2].

Controls

  • Molecular weight ladder in every gel
  • Positive-control protein (for example BSA, 66 kDa)
  • Blank lane with sample buffer only
  • Reduced and non-reduced samples if disulfide bonds matter

Troubleshooting

ProblemLikely causeSolution
Smiling or distorted bandsOverheating or uneven runLower the voltage; use fresh buffer; run in a cold room if needed
Smeared lanesOverloaded sample, protein aggregation or incomplete denaturationLoad less; heat fully; spin before loading [2]
Poorly polymerized gelOld APS or TEMEDUse fresh 10% APS and TEMED [2]
Bands compressed at the top or bottomWrong percentage for protein sizeUse 8% for 25–200 kDa, 12–15% for 4–70 kDa, or a gradient gel [3]
No bands after stainingToo little protein or over-destainingLoad more or switch to silver stain [2]
LeakingPoorly seated plates or spacersReassemble and retest with water [2]

Safety and waste

  • Unpolymerized acrylamide and bis-acrylamide are neurotoxic: wear gloves and weigh powder in a hood; polymerized gel is much safer but still handle with gloves [1].
  • TEMED, 2-mercaptoethanol, methanol and acetic acid are toxic or flammable; work in a fume hood.
  • Dispose of unpolymerized gel solution as hazardous chemical waste, stain and destain solutions as methanol/acetic-acid waste, and gels per institutional rules.
