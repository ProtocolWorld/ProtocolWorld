Phalloidin Staining of F-Actin

Estimated time: About 2–3 h | Protocol World

To visualize filamentous actin (F-actin) in fixed, permeabilized cells or thin tissue sections by fluorescence microscopy, using a fluorescent phalloidin conjugate with an optional nuclear counterstain.

Phalloidin is a bicyclic peptide from the death cap mushroom (Amanita phalloides) that binds tightly and specifically to F-actin, but not to monomeric G-actin, and does not form a covalent bond. Coupled to a fluorophore, it outlines stress fibers, cortical actin and other filamentous structures at high contrast [1][2][4]. Phalloidin cannot cross intact membranes, so cells must be fixed and permeabilized first. Fixation must use methanol-free formaldehyde, because methanol and other alcohol or acetone fixatives disrupt the actin cytoskeleton and abolish staining [1][2][3][4].

ItemDetails
Phosphate-buffered saline (PBS), pH 7.41×, room temperature and cold
Fixative3.7–4% methanol-free formaldehyde (or paraformaldehyde) in PBS, freshly diluted. Do not use methanol-containing fixatives [1][2][3][4]
Optional quenching solution0.1 M glycine or 10 mM ethanolamine in PBS, to neutralize leftover formaldehyde [1]
Permeabilization solution0.1% (v/v) Triton X-100 in PBS (0.2% in some protocols) [1][2][3]
Blocking and staining diluent1% (w/v) bovine serum albumin (BSA) in PBS. Some protocols use 3–5% BSA [1][2][3]
Fluorescent phalloidin conjugateFor example Alexa Fluor, iFluor or similar dye. Prepare the stock as the supplier directs: about 6.6 µM in methanol (40×) or about 66 µM in DMSO (400×) [2][4]
Nuclear counterstain (optional)DAPI or Hoechst 33342, with a color that does not overlap the phalloidin dye. Use the supplier's recommended dilution; the St Andrews SOP uses Hoechst 33342 at 1:2,000 [3][4]
Aqueous antifade mounting mediumFor example a glycerol-based or curing antifade medium. Avoid organic-solvent mountants where possible [1][4]
Cells on coverslipsAdherent cells grown to 50–80% confluence on sterile glass coverslips (or in glass-bottom dishes)
Slides, forceps, nail polishTo mount and seal coverslips
  • Biosafety cabinet or fume hood for handling formaldehyde
  • Humid, light-tight staining chamber (a covered box with wet paper towels and foil)
  • Forceps and fine-tipped needle to lift coverslips
  • Fluorescence or confocal microscope with suitable filters
  • Pipettes and 12-well or 24-well culture plates
  • Fridge and −20 °C freezer for storing phalloidin stock and slides
  • Wash the cells on coverslips twice with warm PBS, 5 min each, to remove medium and serum [3]. (10 min)
  • Fix in 3.7–4% methanol-free formaldehyde in PBS for 10 min at room temperature. Longer fixation (up to 30 min) is acceptable [1][2][3]. (10 min)
  • Wash the cells 2–3 times with PBS, 5 min each, to remove the fixative. Collect formaldehyde waste in a labeled container [2][3]. (15 min)
  • Optional: quench with 0.1 M glycine in PBS for 5 min, then wash again with PBS [1]. (5 min)
  • Permeabilize with 0.1% Triton X-100 in PBS for 3–5 min at room temperature (up to 15 min for thick samples or tissue), then wash 2 × 5 min in PBS [1][2][4]. (5–15 min)
  • Block with 1% BSA in PBS for 20–30 min at room temperature to lower background [1][2][4]. (20–30 min)
  • Dilute the phalloidin conjugate in PBS with 1% BSA. Common dilutions are 1:100 to 1:1,000, or 5 µL of methanol stock (or 0.5 µL of DMSO stock) in 200 µL per coverslip [1][2][4]. (5 min)
  • Place a drop of staining solution on a piece of Parafilm in the staining chamber and lay the coverslip cell side down on it. Protect from light [1][2]. (2 min)
  • Incubate for 20–60 min at room temperature in the dark. Dilute counterstain into the same solution if desired [1][2][3][4]. (20–60 min)
  • Return the coverslip to a well and wash 3 times with PBS, 5 min each, keeping the plate covered with foil [1][2][3]. (15 min)
  • If you did not add counterstain earlier, incubate with DAPI or Hoechst 33342 for 5–10 min, then wash once more with PBS [3][4]. (10 min)
  • Rinse briefly in deionized water to remove salts, blot the edge, and mount cell side down in a drop of aqueous antifade medium on a slide [1][4]. (5 min)
  • Let the mountant cure as directed (often overnight at room temperature in the dark), then seal the edges with nail polish [2][4]. (10 min + curing)
  • Image soon after staining, or store at 2–8 °C in the dark. Stained slides in a suitable mountant keep actin staining for months [2][4]. (30 min+)
  • After fixation and PBS washes, cells can be kept in PBS at 4 °C for a day or so before permeabilization.
  • After mounting and curing, slides can be stored at 2–8 °C in the dark [2].
  • Bright, sharp filaments: stress fibers, cortical actin rings and lamellipodial networks, as appropriate for the cell type.
  • Low background in the cytoplasm and on the glass; nuclei, if counterstained, appear in a separate color channel.
  • The signal appears in the channel of the phalloidin dye only (for example Ex/Em about 493/517 nm for a green conjugate [1]).
  • Unstained, fixed, permeabilized cells imaged with the same settings (autofluorescence control)
  • A sample fixed with methanol-free formaldehyde alongside a known positive cell line such as HeLa, U2OS or fibroblasts
  • A coverslip treated with a known actin-disrupting drug (for example cytochalasin D, per your institution's guidance) as a biological control
  • Staining without counterstain, to check for bleed-through
ProblemLikely causeSolution
Weak or no signalMethanol-containing fixative, inadequate permeabilization, too little dye, old stockUse fresh methanol-free formaldehyde, permeabilize adequately, use a higher dye concentration or a fresh aliquot [1][4]
High backgroundDye too concentrated or sticky, insufficient washingAdd 1% BSA to the staining solution, lower the dye concentration, wash more [1][2][4]
Fragmented or collapsed actinMethanol or acetone fixation, rough handling, unhealthy cellsUse methanol-free formaldehyde at room temperature and handle coverslips gently [1][4]
Nuclear dye overpowers the actin signalToo much DAPI or HoechstLower the concentration or exposure time [1]
Signal fades quicklyPhotobleaching, no antifadeUse an antifade mountant and limit light exposure [1][4]
Weak signal in tissue or organoidsPoor penetrationLengthen permeabilization, raise dye concentration and extend incubation [4]
  • Formaldehyde is toxic and a suspected carcinogen. Work in a ventilated area with gloves and eye protection and collect waste for chemical disposal.
  • Phalloidin is highly toxic if injected (a reported LD50 of about 2 mg/kg in mice) but is poorly absorbed by mouth and the amounts used are small. Use gloves, avoid skin contact and handle it as a hazardous reagent [2].
  • Triton X-100, DAPI and Hoechst are irritants and may be harmful. Wear gloves and a lab coat, and clean spills promptly [3].
  • Methanol and DMSO stock solutions are flammable and DMSO can carry other chemicals through skin. Dispose of all stained waste according to local rules.
