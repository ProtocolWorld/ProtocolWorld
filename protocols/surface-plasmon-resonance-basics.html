Surface Plasmon Resonance Basics

Estimated time: ≈ 1 day (surface preparation 2–3 h, kinetic run 3–5 h) | Protocol World

To measure the association rate (ka), dissociation rate (kd) and equilibrium dissociation constant (KD) of a protein–protein interaction in real time without labels, using a Biacore-type SPR instrument and amine-coupled CM5 chip.

Polarized light reflected from a thin gold film under total internal reflection excites surface plasmons. The angle of minimum reflected intensity changes with the refractive index near the surface, so binding of analyte to ligand immobilized on a dextran matrix is seen as a change in response units (RU) [1][2]. Flowing a concentration series of analyte over a ligand surface and a reference surface gives sensorgrams that are fitted to a 1:1 Langmuir model to obtain ka, kd and KD [2][3][4]. Good data need a low-density ligand surface, a sufficiently high flow rate, matched buffers, and a regeneration step that restores the surface without damaging the ligand [1][4].

ItemDetails
LigandPurified protein, preferably > 90% pure (at least > 50%), free of Tris, glycerol and amine-containing additives; 5–10 µg needed for pH scouting plus immobilization; pI above about 3.5 [1][2]
AnalytePurified partner of known molecular weight and concentration; dialyzed or exchanged into the running buffer
CM5 sensor chipCarboxymethylated dextran; warm to room temperature before use [2]
Running buffer HBS-EP+10 mM HEPES pH 7.4, 150 mM NaCl, 3 mM EDTA, 0.05% (v/v) surfactant P20; filter (0.22 µm) and degas [4]
Coupling reagents0.4 M EDC and 0.1 M NHS, mixed 1:1 immediately before use; 1 M ethanolamine-HCl pH 8.5 [1][2][3]
Immobilization (scouting) buffers10 mM sodium acetate at pH 4.0, 4.5, 5.0 and 5.5 (10 mM sodium borate or MES for pH above 6) [1][2]
Regeneration candidates10 mM glycine-HCl at pH 3.0, 2.5, 2.0 and 1.5; 1–2 M NaCl; 10–50 mM NaOH; 3 mM EDTA for metal-dependent complexes [1][3][4]
Normalization / solvent solutionsIf the analyte is in DMSO: running buffer with the same DMSO percentage and a solvent-correction series spanning the expected range [4]
Low-bind tubes or platesSample volumes of at least 100–150 µL per cycle
  • Biacore-type SPR instrument (for example Biacore T200, 3000 or equivalent) with evaluation software
  • Microcentrifuge (to clear samples at 18,000 × g)
  • 0.22 µm filter units and vacuum degasser
  • Pipettes and low-bind tubes or plates
  • Computer with the instrument’s evaluation software
  • Prepare 1 L HBS-EP+, filter (0.22 µm), degas and dock it. Dock the CM5 chip and prime the system three times; leave flow at 10 µL/min for 30 min to stabilize the baseline [4]. (≈ 1 h)
  • Spin the ligand and analyte at 18,000 × g for 10 min at 4 °C to remove aggregates and bubbles; measure concentrations accurately [2].
  • Immobilization pH scouting: dilute ligand to 20–50 µg/mL in 100 µL acetate buffer at pH 4.0, 4.5, 5.0 and 5.5 [1][2]. (30 min)
  • Inject each dilution for 2 min at 5–10 µL/min over an unactivated flow cell, starting with the highest pH. Choose the highest pH that gives strong electrostatic pre-concentration [1][2]. (30 min)
  • Calculate the target ligand level: theoretical Rmax = (analyte MW ÷ ligand MW) × ligand RU. For kinetics aim for an Rmax of about 30–50 RU, which usually means 100–1,000 RU of ligand [4].
  • Activate flow cells 2 (ligand) and 1 (reference) with a fresh 1:1 EDC/NHS mix: 70 µL at 10 µL/min (7 min) [1][3]. (7 min)
  • Inject ligand in the chosen scouting buffer over flow cell 2 at 5–10 µL/min until the target RU is reached; shorter contact times give proportionally lower levels [1][2]. (5–10 min)
  • Block both flow cells with 1 M ethanolamine-HCl pH 8.5 for 7 min at 10 µL/min. Record the final RU and check for ligand loss over the next 10 min [1][3]. (7 min)
  • Regeneration scouting: inject analyte near its expected top concentration, then test one regeneration solution for 30–60 s at 30 µL/min. Start with mild solutions (glycine pH 3.0) [1][4]. (≈ 1 h)
  • Judge each regeneration: it should remove more than 90% of bound analyte and restore the baseline. If under 30% is removed, change class (high salt, low pH, high pH) [1]. (≈ 30 min)
  • Repeat the same analyte injection and regeneration 3 times; binding must stay within ≈ 5–10% of the first cycle, otherwise try a milder or different solution [1][4].
  • Design the series: 5–8 analyte concentrations as 2-fold or 3-fold dilutions around the expected KD (about 0.1–10 × KD), plus a zero sample [2][4]. (30 min)
  • Repeat one concentration in the series to check surface stability and reproducibility [4].
  • Set method parameters: flow 30 µL/min or higher to limit mass transport, association 120–180 s, dissociation 300–600 s, regeneration as chosen, 3–5 start-up cycles with buffer [3][4].
  • Run the series with random or ascending concentration order, with a buffer blank every 4–5 cycles. Keep samples at 4–10 °C in the sample compartment [4]. (2–4 h)
  • If the analyte contains DMSO, match DMSO in running buffer and samples (for example 1–2%) and run a solvent-correction series; 1% DMSO shifts the response by about 1,200 RU [4].
  • Evaluate: subtract reference flow cell 1 and buffer blanks (double referencing), zero baseline and align injections [2][3][4]. (30 min)
  • Fit globally to a 1:1 Langmuir model. Accept the fit only if residuals scatter randomly around zero within about ±1–2 RU and parameter U-values are below 25 [4].
  • Check mass transport: repeat the series with a surface of about half the ligand density or a higher flow rate. Rate constants should be unchanged; otherwise report them as limits [1][3].
  • Cross-check: kinetic KD (kd ÷ ka) should agree with KD from a steady-state fit of equilibrium responses [1]. (15 min)
  • Clean the instrument (desorb or sanitize per the manual), undock the chip, store it at 4 °C in its original conditions per vendor instructions and save all methods and raw files.
  • The protocol can be paused after sterilization and labeling; store solutions as described.
  • Sensorgrams show concentration-dependent association curves that reach a plateau (steady state) or curve to it, followed by dissociation back toward baseline.
  • A global 1:1 fit gives ka, kd and KD with residuals within about ±1–2 RU; kinetic and steady-state KD agree within about 2-fold.
  • Reference-subtracted blanks are flat and carry-over is near zero.
  • Interactions with ka above about 10⁶ M⁻¹ s⁻¹ are hard to measure accurately because of mass transport; kd slower than about 10⁻⁵ s⁻¹ or faster than about 1 s⁻¹ is also difficult to measure [1].
  • Reference flow cell (activated and blocked without ligand, or with an irrelevant protein) subtracted from every cycle
  • Buffer blank injections (zero concentration) for double referencing
  • Non-binding control analyte injected over the ligand surface
  • Repeated analyte concentration to confirm surface stability
  • Same analyte tested over two ligand densities to exclude mass-transport artifacts
  • Reverse orientation (swap ligand and analyte) for important interactions [1]

  • Treat protein samples according to their biosafety level.
  • Dispose of instrument waste and regeneration solutions according to local chemical waste rules.
