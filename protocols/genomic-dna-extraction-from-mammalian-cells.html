Genomic DNA Extraction from Mammalian Cells

1 day (overnight lysis optional) · 2026-11-14

Proteinase K lysis followed by phenol-chloroform extraction and ethanol precipitation to recover high-molecular-weight genomic DNA from cultured mammalian cells.

⬇ Word (.docx)⬇ PowerPoint (.pptx)

Purpose

To isolate high-molecular-weight genomic DNA from cultured mammalian cells (about 1–5 × 10⁶ cells) suitable for PCR, restriction digestion and Southern blotting.

Principle

Cells are lysed with SDS in the presence of EDTA, which inhibits nucleases, and proteinase K digests proteins [2][3]. Phenol:chloroform:isoamyl alcohol partitions denatured protein into the organic phase and interphase while DNA stays in the upper aqueous phase [1]. DNA is then concentrated by salt-assisted ethanol precipitation, washed with 70% ethanol to remove salt, SDS and phenol, and redissolved in TE [1][2]. Volumes below are scaled from published tissue and generic cell protocols; treat them as a starting point to optimize.

Materials and Reagents

Reagent / itemConcentration / details
Lysis buffer50 mM Tris-HCl pH 8.0, 100 mM EDTA, 0.5% (w/v) SDS [2]
Proteinase K10 mg/mL stock, added fresh to about 0.5 mg/mL final [2]
RNase A (optional)DNase-free, about 5 µg per sample [2]
Phenol:chloroform:isoamyl alcohol25:24:1, Tris-equilibrated pH 8.0 [1]
Sodium acetate3 M, pH 6.0 (do not go below pH 6.0) [2]
Ethanol100% (room temperature) and 70% [1][2]
TE buffer10 mM Tris-HCl, 1 mM EDTA, pH 8.0 [1]
PBS1x, cold, for washing cells
Glycogen (optional)20 µg/µL, carrier for small samples [1]

Equipment

  • Microcentrifuge (16,000 × g capable, refrigerated)
  • Heat block or water bath at 55 °C
  • Rocking platform
  • Fume hood
  • Wide-bore pipette tips
  • Spectrophotometer (e.g., NanoDrop) and agarose gel system

Procedure

A. Harvest and lyse (Day 1)

  • Harvest 1–5 × 10⁶ cells, pellet at 300 × g for 5 min and wash once with cold PBS. Remove all PBS.
  • Resuspend the pellet in 0.7 mL lysis buffer by gentle pipetting with a wide-bore tip [2].
  • Add 35 µL of fresh 10 mg/mL proteinase K and mix by gentle inversion [2].
  • Incubate at 55 °C for 1–2 h, or overnight on a gently rocking platform, until the lysate is clear and viscous [1][2].

B. Organic extraction (in a fume hood)

  • Optional: add RNase A and incubate 30 min at 37 °C before extraction [2].
  • Add 0.7 mL phenol:chloroform:isoamyl alcohol (25:24:1). Mix by inversion for 2–3 min; avoid vortexing to limit shearing [2][3].
  • Centrifuge 5 min at 16,000 × g at room temperature [1].
  • Transfer the upper aqueous phase to a new tube with a wide-bore tip, leaving the interphase behind [1][2].
  • For cleaner DNA, repeat the extraction once more, then optionally extract with chloroform:isoamyl alcohol (24:1) to remove residual phenol [3].

C. Precipitate and wash

  • Add 1/10 volume of 3 M sodium acetate pH 6.0 and mix [2].
  • Add 1 volume (about 0.7 mL) of room-temperature 100% ethanol (or 2.5 volumes if working from a small sample) and mix gently. High-molecular-weight DNA may appear as a stringy precipitate [2][3].
  • Pellet by centrifugation (30 s to 30 min; use 30 min at 4 °C and 16,000 × g for small or dilute samples) and remove the supernatant [1][2].
  • Wash with 1 mL 70% ethanol, mixing well; this step removes SDS, salt and phenol [2]. Spin 2 min and remove all liquid. Repeat once.
  • Air-dry the pellet 5–10 min. Do not over-dry, which makes DNA hard to dissolve [1].

D. Resuspend and quantify

  • Dissolve the pellet in 50–100 µL TE or nuclease-free water. Leave at room temperature overnight, or heat 5–10 min at 65 °C [1][2].
  • Measure A260/A280 (target above 1.7–1.8; 50 µg/mL corresponds to an A260 of 1.0) [2][3].
  • Check integrity on a 0.8% agarose gel; intact gDNA runs as a high-molecular-weight band. Store at 4 °C short term or −20 °C long term.

Expected Results

  • Yield of roughly 10–30 µg gDNA per 10⁶ to 5 × 10⁶ cells is common for rapidly dividing lines, but depends on cell type and ploidy (typical range, not a value stated in the sources).
  • A260/A280 of 1.7–2.0; a dominant high-molecular-weight band (>20 kb) on a gel with little smearing.
  • Phenol-chloroform preparations contain some RNA, which does not block restriction digestion or Southern analysis [2].

Troubleshooting

ProblemPossible causeSolution
Low yieldIncomplete lysis or too few cells; pellet lostExtend proteinase K digestion; use glycogen carrier; keep the pellet visible with a longer 4 °C spin [1]
Low A260/A280 (<1.7)Phenol or protein carry-overRe-extract; avoid the interphase; do the 70% ethanol wash twice [2]
Smeared gDNAShearing or nuclease activityAvoid vortexing, use wide-bore tips, keep EDTA in the lysis buffer [2][3]
Pellet will not dissolveOver-drying or too much DNAHeat at 65 °C for 5–10 min and rest overnight at room temperature [2]
Precipitate is cloudy or whiteEDTA salting out at pH below 6.0Use sodium acetate at pH 6.0 [2]

Safety Notes

  • Phenol and chloroform are toxic and corrosive. Work in a fume hood with nitrile or other chemical-resistant gloves, eye protection and a lab coat [1][3].
  • Dispose of phenol-chloroform waste as hazardous chemical waste; do not pour it down the drain.
  • Treat human-derived cell lines at the biosafety level required by your institution.
