Calcium Phosphate Transfection

Estimated time: ≈ 1 h hands-on + 24–72 h expression | Protocol World

To introduce plasmid DNA into adherent mammalian cells, mainly HEK293 or 293T, for transient expression or virus production using an inexpensive calcium phosphate–DNA coprecipitate.

DNA mixed with calcium chloride and then with phosphate in HEPES-buffered saline forms a fine calcium phosphate–DNA coprecipitate that settles on cells and is taken up by endocytosis [3][5]. Efficiency depends on a narrow window of calcium, phosphate, pH, temperature and reaction time: DNA binds within about 1 min, and long reaction times cause aggregation and lower expression [1]. Buffer pH is the most critical variable [3]. Circular plasmid works well; linear DNA is nearly inactive [2].

ItemDetails
2× HEPES-buffered saline (2× HBS), 500 mL8.18 g NaCl (280 mM), 0.37 g KCl (10 mM), 0.106 g Na₂HPO₄ anhydrous (1.5 mM), 1.08 g dextrose (12 mM), 5.96 g HEPES free acid (50 mM). Adjust to pH 7.05 with NaOH using a freshly calibrated meter; filter 0.22 µm; aliquot, store at −20 °C [4][5]
2 M CaCl₂2.94 g CaCl₂ (anhydrous) in 10 mL water, filter-sterilized; store at 4 °C or −20 °C; make fresh weekly if kept at 4 °C [4][5]
Plasmid DNAEndotoxin-low, ≥ 1 µg/µL in water or TE; 10–20 µg per 10-cm dish, 3–5 µg per 6-well well [4]
Sterile waterCell-culture grade
CellsHEK293 or 293T in DMEM + 10% FBS (avoid RPMI) [4][6]
Chloroquine (optional)25 mM stock in PBS, filtered, −20 °C; 25 µM final (1:1000) [5]
Culture vessels10-cm dishes or 6-well plates
Reporter plasmidGFP expression vector for efficiency control
  • Biosafety cabinet
  • 37 °C, 5% CO₂ incubator
  • Calibrated pH meter (two-point, pH 4.01/7.00)
  • 0.22 µm filter units
  • Pipette aid and sterile 1.5–15 mL tubes
  • Inverted microscope (fluorescence for GFP)
  • Plate cells 4–24 h earlier so they are 60–80% confluent at transfection: about 5 × 10⁶ 293T per 10-cm dish or 5 × 10⁵ per 6-well well [3][4][5]. (4–24 h before)
  • Warm 2× HBS and water to room temperature. Check precipitate quality first with a no-DNA mix; it should look faintly cloudy, not clumped [3].
  • 10-cm dish: combine 10–20 µg DNA, 62 µL 2 M CaCl₂ and water to 500 µL in a tube [4][5]. For 6-well: 3–5 µg DNA, 16 µL 2 M CaCl₂, water to 125 µL [4].
  • Pipette 500 µL (10-cm) or 125 µL (6-well) 2× HBS into a separate tube. Add the DNA–CaCl₂ mix dropwise while bubbling air through the HBS or mixing gently [3][5][6].
  • Let the mixture stand 2–5 min at room temperature; a fine, slightly opalescent precipitate forms. Longer times risk aggregation [1][5]. (2–5 min)
  • Optional: pre-treat cells 1 h with 25 µM chloroquine in fresh medium before adding precipitate [5][6].
  • Add the suspension dropwise over the whole dish (9–10 mL medium; 2.25–3 mL per 6-well well). Rock gently once; the medium turns orange [4][6].
  • Incubate at 37 °C, 5% CO₂. Fine, dust-like particles should be visible on cells after a few hours [3][6]. (8–16 h)
  • Remove medium (and chloroquine, after ≤ 10–11 h), wash once with warm PBS, and add fresh complete medium [5][6].
  • Check GFP-control cells at 24 h and harvest or assay other samples at 48 h (secreted protein up to 72 h or longer) [3][5][6]. (24–72 h)
  • After step 9 the cells can remain in fresh medium until assay at 24–72 h.
  • 2× HBS and CaCl₂ stocks can be stored frozen in aliquots; do not refreeze HBS.
  • Fine precipitate visible within hours; no heavy cell lifting.
  • GFP-positive cells within 24 h; optimized lab protocols report up to 70–90% in HEK293, but 30–60% is more typical for routine work [1][5].
  • Efficiency is usually lower than with lipid reagents but is very cost-effective [6].
  • GFP reporter plasmid for efficiency
  • Mock transfection (CaCl₂ + HBS, no DNA)
  • Empty vector to equalize total DNA mass [4]
  • Untransfected cells
