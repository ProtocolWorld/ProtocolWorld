Mouse Splenocyte Isolation

About 1 h · 2026-11-26

Preparation of a single-cell suspension from a mouse spleen by mechanical dissociation through a 70 µm strainer, ACK red blood cell lysis, 40 µm filtration, counting and adjustment for staining or culture.

⬇ Word (.docx)⬇ PowerPoint (.pptx)

Purpose

To prepare a viable single-cell suspension of splenocytes from a mouse spleen, free of red blood cells and clumps, for flow cytometry, cell culture, ELISpot or cell sorting.

Principle

The spleen is gently pressed through a cell strainer to release lymphocytes and other leukocytes from the capsule. Red blood cells are then lysed with an ammonium chloride–potassium bicarbonate (ACK) buffer: they take up ammonium, swell and burst, while nucleated cells survive a short exposure. Cold buffers and short lysis times protect viability, and a second filtration removes fat and aggregates [1][2][4]. A typical mouse spleen yields about 50–150 million cells [4].

Materials and reagents

ItemDetails

Cold PBSWithout Ca²⁺/Mg²⁺ (or HBSS), kept on ice [1]

Equipment

  • Biosafety cabinet
  • Refrigerated centrifuge with swinging-bucket rotor
  • Dissection tools (sterile scissors, forceps)
  • Hemocytometer or automated cell counter
  • Ice bucket
  • 37 °C incubator (only if using enzymes)

Procedure

  1. Prepare 1X ACK buffer, chill complete RPMI and PBS on ice, and label a 15 mL and a 50 mL tube per spleen [4][5]. (10 min)
  2. Euthanize the mouse, spray the left flank with 70% ethanol, open the abdomen and remove the spleen with sterile forceps; trim fat and connective tissue [3][4]. (5 min)
  3. Place the spleen in 2 mL of cold complete medium in a 15 mL tube on ice for transport to the hood [4].
  4. Place a 70 µm strainer on a 50 mL tube and wet it with 2 mL of cold medium; put the spleen on the strainer [3]. (2 min)
  5. Mash the spleen with the flat end of a 3 mL syringe plunger in gentle circles until only the pale capsule remains; rinse the strainer with 3 mL of medium and repeat once [3][4]. (3 min)
  6. Bring the volume to about 10 mL with cold medium or PBS and centrifuge at 400 × g for 5 min at 4 °C; discard the supernatant [1][2]. (8 min)
  7. Loosen the pellet by tapping or flicking the tube so that no clumps remain before adding any lysis buffer [4].
  8. Add 2 mL of cold 1X ACK buffer, mix by gentle inversion and incubate 3 min on ice; do not exceed 5 min [1][2][4][6]. (3 min)
  9. Stop the lysis with 10 mL of cold complete medium (or PBS) and mix [2][4].
  10. Centrifuge at 400 × g for 5 min at 4 °C and discard the supernatant; the pellet should be white to pale pink [1][2]. (8 min)
  11. Resuspend the cells in 5 mL of medium and pass them through a 40 µm strainer to remove fat and aggregates; rinse with 3 mL of medium [4]. (3 min)
  12. Mix 10 µL of cells with 10 µL of trypan blue, count and record total cells and viability [4]. (5 min)
  13. Adjust to the working concentration, for example 2 × 10⁶ cells/mL in complete medium for culture [4] or 2–3 × 10⁶ cells/mL in PBS for staining [1]; keep on ice. (3 min)

Pause points

  • Cells can be held on ice in medium for a short time after the 40 µm filtration step; process them for staining or culture the same day.
  • Avoid storing dense cell slurries in a cold room for long periods, since they can freeze [4].

Expected results

  • A single-cell suspension of about 50–150 million splenocytes per mouse [4].
  • Little or no red color in the pellet and high viability on trypan blue counting.
  • Mostly lymphocytes by flow cytometry (T and B cells), with fewer myeloid cells unless enzymes are used.

Controls

  • Count an unlysed aliquot to estimate how many red cells remain
  • Keep an unstained sample of the final suspension for flow cytometry compensation
  • Include a media-only well if the cells go on for stimulation assays [4]

Troubleshooting

ProblemLikely causeSolution
Pellet remains redIncomplete lysis or clumped pelletResuspend the pellet well; repeat ACK for 1–2 min
Low viabilityOver-lysis, warm buffers or rough mashingKeep everything cold; limit lysis to 3–5 min; mash gently
Low yieldCells left in the strainer or the capsuleRinse the strainer again with 3 mL of medium
Clumps or fat in suspensionConnective tissue or DNA from dead cellsTrim the spleen; filter through 40 µm; add DNase I
Few dendritic or myeloid cellsMechanical dissociation onlyUse collagenase IV and DNase I digestion [1]

Safety and waste

  • Follow the approved animal protocol and local biosafety rules; wear a lab coat, gloves and eye protection.
  • Dispose of carcasses and sharps in the designated animal and sharps waste.
  • Treat cell suspensions and liquid waste as biohazardous; add bleach (10% final) or follow institutional rules before disposal. ACK buffer has no special waste requirements beyond institutional policy.
