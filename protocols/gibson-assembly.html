Gibson Assembly

1 day + 1–2 days screening · 2026-11-19

Seamless, one-pot joining of overlapping DNA fragments into a plasmid using T5 exonuclease, a high-fidelity polymerase and Taq ligase at 50 °C, followed by transformation into E. coli.

⬇ Word (.docx)⬇ PowerPoint (.pptx)

Purpose

To join two or more PCR-generated or linearized DNA fragments with matching end overlaps into one circular plasmid without restriction sites or scars, then recover it in E. coli.

Principle

At 50 °C, T5 exonuclease exposes complementary overlaps, a DNA polymerase fills the gaps and Taq ligase seals the nicks in one reaction [1][4]. Neighbors need identical end overlaps (about 40 bp originally; 15–40 bp in commercial formats), which primers can add [1][2][3]. The product is used to transform E. coli [1][2]; verify junctions by colony PCR and sequencing [3][5].

Equipment

  • Thermocycler or 50 °C heat block
  • Ice bucket
  • Water bath at 42 °C or electroporator with 1 mm cuvettes
  • 37 °C incubator and shaking incubator
  • Microcentrifuge
  • Gel electrophoresis system and imager
  • Spectrophotometer or fluorometer (for DNA concentration)
  • Biosafety cabinet or clean bench (recommended)

Procedure

  1. Design fragments so each neighbor shares an identical end overlap; use about 15–40 bp (up to 40 bp or more in the original protocol) [1][2][3]; check overlap Tm with an assembly design tool. (1–2 h)
  2. Add the overlap to PCR primers (for example about 20–30 bp overlap tail plus 18–25 bp template binding) and check for hairpins [3]. (30 min)
  3. Amplify inserts and vector with a high-fidelity polymerase. Alternatively linearize the vector with one or two restriction enzymes [2][3]. (1.5–3 h)
  4. Add DpnI to PCR reactions that used plasmid template and incubate at 37 °C for 1 h to remove parental DNA [4]. (1 h)
  5. Run a gel to confirm size and yield. Purify by column or gel extraction, especially if extra bands are present [3]. (1 h)
  6. Measure concentrations. Convert to moles: pmol = ng × 1,000 / (bp × 650) [2]. (10 min)
  7. Plan amounts: 0.02–0.5 pmol total DNA for 2–3 fragments (vector 50–100 ng) [2].
  8. Use a 2–3-fold molar excess of each insert over vector (1:2 or 1:3). For inserts under 200 bp, use a 5-fold excess [1][2].
  9. On ice, mix DNA with 10 µL 2× master mix and water to 20 µL. Keep unpurified PCR product to 20% of the volume or less [2].
  10. Set up controls: vector-only, no-assembly (DNA without master mix), and the kit positive control (10 µL control DNA plus 10 µL master mix) [2][4].
  11. Incubate at 50 °C: 15 min for 2–3 fragments, 60 min for 4–6 fragments or when yield is low [2]. Homemade mix: 1 h [1]. (15–60 min)
  12. Chill on ice. Dilute homemade reactions 1:5 in water before transformation [1]. Store at −20 °C if not transforming immediately [2]. (5 min)
  13. Transform: add 2 µL assembly to NEB 5-alpha cells per the cell supplier's protocol [2], or electroporate 3 µL into 30 µL cells (1 mm cuvette, 1200 V) [1].
  14. For electroporation, recover in 1 mL SOC. For chemical cells, recover per the supplier's protocol, usually about 1 h at 37 °C with shaking [1][2]. (1 h)
  15. Plate several volumes on pre-warmed LB agar with the correct antibiotic and incubate at 37 °C overnight [1][3]. (16 h)
  16. Count colonies on all plates. Pick 4–8 colonies from the assembly plate for colony PCR with flanking primers or a restriction digest [3]. (2 h)
  17. Grow positive colonies in selective LB, miniprep and sequence across every junction (Sanger or whole-plasmid sequencing) [3][5]. (1–2 days)

Pause points

  • Grow positive colonies in selective LB, miniprep and sequence across every junction (Sanger or whole-plasmid sequencing) [3][5] (1–2 days).

Expected results

  • Assembly plates should show many more colonies than vector-only and no-assembly controls (often tens to hundreds for a successful 2–3 fragment build; depends on fragments and cells).
  • The positive control should give antibiotic-resistant colonies [4].
  • Most picked colonies should give an insert-sized colony PCR band; sequencing confirms seamless junctions [3][5].
  • Efficiency falls as fragment number rises, and more than five fragments often needs optimization [2][5].

Controls

  • Vector-only reaction (with master mix): estimates carry-over of uncut or unpurified template and self-ligation background
  • No-assembly control (DNA without master mix or without incubation): shows background from parental plasmid [4]
  • Positive control DNA from the kit (or a known two-fragment pUC19 assembly) [2][4]
  • Competent-cell control (no DNA) on antibiotic plate and a plain-LB viability plate for transformation efficiency

Troubleshooting

ProblemLikely causeSolution
No or few coloniesLow or wrong DNA amount, degraded fragments, salt or PCR-mix inhibitors, poor competent cellsCheck molar amounts; purify DNA; limit PCR product to ≤20% of the reaction; test positive control and cell efficiency [2]
Many colonies, none correctResidual template plasmid, vector self-closure, wrong antibiotic selectionDigest with DpnI; gel-purify vector; use vector-only control; consider splitting the resistance gene between fragments [3][4][5]
Wrong-size inserts or rearrangementsRepeats or hairpins in overlaps, nonspecific PCR productsRedesign overlaps; avoid hairpins; gel-purify; sequence the junctions [3][5]
Small inserts lostShort pieces are digested or under-representedUse 5-fold molar excess for inserts under 200 bp, or assemble them as stitched oligos [1][2][5]
Multi-fragment assembly failsToo many pieces or unequal molar ratiosUse 0.2–1.0 pmol total, 1:1 ratio, 60 min at 50 °C; or assemble in two rounds [2][5]
Poor transformation efficiencySalts in the reaction, aged cellsDilute reaction 1:5 for homemade mix; use fresh high-efficiency cells; try electroporation [1][2]

Safety and waste

  • Antibiotics: wear gloves; some people are allergic to ampicillin and other beta-lactams. Prepare stock solutions carefully.
  • Gel stains: use safer alternatives when possible; ethidium bromide is a mutagen, so use gloves and dispose of gels and buffer as hazardous waste.
  • DTT and PEG powders: avoid inhaling dust. Electroporators generate high voltage; follow manufacturer rules.
  • Recombinant E. coli are GMOs: work at the biosafety level approved by your institution, disinfect surfaces with 70% ethanol or bleach.
  • Waste: autoclave or chemically inactivate cultures, plates and tips before disposal as GMO waste; dispose of antibiotic solutions as chemical waste.
