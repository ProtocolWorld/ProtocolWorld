Automated Cell Counting

About 15 min · 2026-11-20

Count mammalian cells and measure viability with an image-based automated counter and 0.4% trypan blue: 1:1 sample mixing, chamber loading, working range, viability and cell-number calculations, plus a hemocytometer cross-check.

⬇ Word (.docx)⬇ PowerPoint (.pptx)

Purpose

To measure the concentration and viability of a mammalian cell suspension quickly and reproducibly with an image-based automated counter and trypan blue, so that cultures can be passaged and plated at defined densities.

Principle

Trypan blue is excluded by cells with intact membranes, so live cells stay clear while dead cells take up the dye and look blue. An automated counter loads a small volume into a chamber slide, focuses and images the cells, and reports live, dead and total concentrations plus the percentage of cell aggregates [1][3]. Results are only as good as the sample: the suspension must be single cells, well mixed and within the instrument's working range, and the dye dilution must be accounted for [2][3][4]. The same numbers can be checked by hand on a hemocytometer, where cells/mL = average count per large square × 10⁴ × dilution factor [3].

Materials and reagents

ItemDetails
Single-cell suspensionAdherent cells detached with trypsin or TrypLE and resuspended in medium; suspension cells mixed well. About 1 × 10⁶ cells/mL is ideal [3]
0.4% trypan blue solutionIn saline; filter (0.2 µm) if lab-made or old to remove particles [3][4]
Counting chamber slidesDisposable slides for your counter (for example, two-chamber slides with a half-moon loading port) [1]
PBS or serum-free mediumFor dilution or washing; serum proteins also stain with trypan blue [4]
Microcentrifuge tubes or a 96-well plateFor mixing cells 1:1 with dye
70% ethanolTo wipe the instrument and, if used, the hemocytometer and coverslip [3]

Equipment

  • Automated cell counter (for example, a Countess-type image-based counter)
  • P20 and P200 pipettes with filter tips
  • Biosafety cabinet
  • Vortex mixer or pipette for mixing
  • Hemocytometer and microscope (for cross-checking)

Procedure

  1. Detach adherent cells, then neutralize and resuspend in 2–5 mL of medium. Pipette up and down to break clumps into single cells [1]. (5 min)
  2. Mix the suspension thoroughly just before sampling; cells settle quickly, and an unmixed sample is the commonest cause of error [3]. (30 s)
  3. Pipette 10 µL of cell suspension into a microcentrifuge tube. If the cell density is above about 1 × 10⁷ cells/mL, dilute it first in PBS [3]. (1 min)
  4. Add 10 µL of 0.4% trypan blue (a 1:1 mix). Mix gently by pipetting up and down without making bubbles [1][2][4]. (1 min)
  5. Wait 1–2 min, then count promptly. Do not leave cells in trypan blue longer than 15 min, because the dye becomes toxic and inflates dead counts [3][4]. (1–2 min)
  6. Load 10 µL of the mixture into the half-moon port of the chamber slide, and fill both chambers if replicates are wanted [1]. (1 min)
  7. Let the cells settle for about 30 s, then insert the slide until you hear a soft click. The instrument sets the illumination and focuses automatically [1]. (30 s)
  8. Press Count. Check the image: live cells should be bright and round, dead cells blue, with no bubbles or debris counted as cells [1][3]. (1 min)
  9. Record total, live and dead concentration, percent viability and percent aggregates. Confirm the 1:1 dye dilution is entered in the settings (factor 2) [1][3]. (1 min)
  10. Calculate cells to seed: volume (mL) = cells needed ÷ live cells/mL. Eject the slide and wipe the instrument with 70% ethanol [3].
  11. Cross-check occasionally by hand: average four large-square counts × 10⁴ × 2 (dye) × any prior dilution = cells/mL [3]. (10 min)

Pause points

  • The cell suspension can sit on ice for a few minutes before dye is added; count within 1–5 min of adding trypan blue, and no later than 15 min.

Expected results

  • Healthy log-phase cultures show viability of at least 95% [2].
  • The count falls inside the instrument's validated range (for example, 5 × 10⁴ to 1 × 10⁷ cells/mL for one validated analyzer; check your own model's manual) and duplicate chambers agree closely [3].
  • Percent aggregates is low (clusters of three or more touching cells are flagged) [1].

Controls

  • Count two chambers or two separate dye mixes and compare.
  • Occasionally cross-check one sample against a hemocytometer count.
  • Include a heat-killed aliquot (for example, 65 °C for 10 min) to confirm the dye stains dead cells.
  • Run a blank of PBS plus trypan blue to check for debris and dye precipitate.

Troubleshooting

ProblemLikely causeSolution
Count too low or too highConcentration outside the working rangeDilute above about 1 × 10⁷ cells/mL; concentrate by centrifugation below about 1 × 10⁵ cells/mL [3]
High variability between countsUnmixed suspension, clumps, inconsistent loadingMix well before each sample, break clumps, load the same volume each time [3]
Viability appears falsely lowLong dye incubation or serum proteins stainedCount within 1–5 min; wash cells into PBS or serum-free medium [3][4]
Debris or bubbles counted as cellsDirty dye, bubbles in chamberFilter trypan blue; reload the slide slowly without bubbles [1][3]
Aggregate percentage is highIncomplete dissociationTriturate gently or use a cell strainer, then recount [1]

Safety and waste

  • Trypan blue is a suspected carcinogen: wear gloves and avoid contact or inhalation.
  • Treat all cell lines as potentially biohazardous; work in a biosafety cabinet and follow local biosafety rules.
  • Discard used slides and tips in biohazard waste and dye-containing liquid waste per institutional rules.
