Polyethylenimine (PEI) Transfection

3 days (about 1.5 h hands-on) · 2026-11-27

Transfect adherent HEK293T cells with linear 25 kDa PEI: stock preparation at pH 7.0, DNA:PEI ratio titration (1:1 to 1:6), complex formation, medium change and troubleshooting.

⬇ Word (.docx)⬇ PowerPoint (.pptx)

Purpose

To introduce plasmid DNA into adherent HEK293T cells with linear 25 kDa polyethylenimine (PEI), a low-cost reagent that gives high transient expression for protein production and viral vector packaging.

Principle

The positively charged, protonatable amines of linear PEI condense negatively charged plasmid DNA into nanoparticles that bind the cell surface, are taken up by endocytosis, and escape the endosome by buffering protons (the “proton sponge” effect). The DNA:PEI mass ratio (commonly 1:2 to 1:3 for 25 kDa linear PEI) and cell density control efficiency and toxicity, and the best ratio should be tested for each new PEI batch [1][3]. Endotoxin-free DNA and healthy, sub-confluent cells are essential [1].

Materials and reagents

ItemDetails
HEK293T cellsLow passage (below passage 20 for virus production), split three times a week, free of mycoplasma [1]
Complete DMEMDMEM high glucose + 10% heat-inactivated FBS + 4 mM stable glutamine, no antibiotics during transfection [1]
Linear PEI, 25,000 Da (for example, Polysciences 23966)1 mg/mL stock: dissolve 100 mg in 100 mL deionized water (stirring, may take hours), add HCl until clear, adjust to pH 7.0, filter 0.22 µm, aliquot 0.5–1 mL, store at −80 °C; thawed aliquots at 4 °C for up to 2 months [1][2]
Plasmid DNAEndotoxin-free, A260/A280 about 1.8–1.95, mostly supercoiled; 1 µg/µL or more [1][3]
Serum-free mediumOpti-MEM or OptiPro SFM for diluting DNA and PEI [1][2]
Chloroquine (optional)25 mM stock: 0.129 g chloroquine diphosphate in 10 mL sterile water, 0.22 µm filter, 50–100 µL aliquots at −20 °C; use at 25 µM [1]
Cell culture dishes10 cm tissue culture dishes (scale volumes by surface area for other formats)
Reporter plasmidGFP plasmid to test efficiency (5% of total DNA in a test) [3]
Trypan blue or cell counterFor seeding density

Equipment

  • Biosafety cabinet
  • CO₂ incubator (37 °C, 5% CO₂)
  • Cell counter or hemocytometer
  • 0.22 µm syringe filters
  • pH meter
  • Magnetic stirrer
  • Fluorescence microscope

Procedure

  1. Seed 3.8 × 10⁶ HEK293T cells per 10 cm dish in complete DMEM and grow about 20 h at 37 °C, 5% CO₂ to roughly 50% confluence. Higher density lowers efficiency [1][2]. (Day 0)
  2. Optional: replace the medium with 10 mL fresh complete DMEM containing 25 µM chloroquine (10 µL of 25 mM stock) and incubate for about 5 h before adding complexes [1]. (5 h)
  3. In tube A, dilute 18.9 µg plasmid DNA in 500 µL serum-free medium (OptiPro SFM or Opti-MEM) [1]. (5 min)
  4. In tube B, mix 56.7 µL of 1 mg/mL PEI with 443.3 µL serum-free medium (1:3 µg DNA:µg PEI). For other ratios see troubleshooting [1]. (5 min)
  5. Add the diluted PEI dropwise to the diluted DNA while flicking the tube. Do not add DNA to PEI. Mix gently; do not vortex vigorously [1]. (2 min)
  6. Incubate the mixture at room temperature for 15–20 min to let complexes form [1]. (15–20 min)
  7. Add the 1 mL complex dropwise to the cells, swirling gently to distribute evenly [1][2]. (2 min)
  8. Return the dish to the incubator and incubate overnight (about 18 h) at 37 °C, 5% CO₂ [1]. (18 h)
  9. Next morning, replace the medium with 15 mL fresh complete DMEM to remove complexes and reduce toxicity [1]. (Day 2, 10 min)
  10. Check reporter expression by fluorescence 24–48 h after the medium change. Harvest cells or supernatant when expression peaks (day 3–5 for most plasmids) [1][2]. (Day 3–4)
  11. Small-scale alternative (Vanderbilt method): per 10 cm dish use 4 µg DNA in 100 µL Opti-MEM plus 24 µL of 1 mg/mL PEI (1:6), incubate 10–15 min, add to cells in 10 mL fresh medium [2]. (Option)

Pause points

  • PEI stock can be stored at −80 °C for months, and at 4 °C after thawing for up to 2 months [1].
  • Complexes should be added promptly after the 15–20 min incubation; do not store them.

Expected results

  • With HEK293T and 1:2 to 1:3 DNA:PEI, a GFP reporter is expressed in a high percentage of cells at 24–48 h, with limited effect on cell growth [1].
  • Cells stay adherent and healthy after the medium change; heavy rounding or detachment indicates toxicity.
  • Protein or virus yield rises over 48–72 h; an extra day often increases yield [2].

Controls

  • Mock transfection (PEI without DNA) for toxicity
  • Reporter plasmid (GFP) in a parallel dish for efficiency by fluorescence or flow cytometry
  • Ratio titration with 18.9 µg DNA: 18.9, 37.8, 56.7, 75.6, 94.5 or 113.4 µL of 1 mg/mL PEI (1:1 to 1:6) [1]
  • Untransfected cells for background

Troubleshooting

ProblemLikely causeSolution
Low efficiencyCells too confluent, wrong ratio or poor DNA qualitySeed at about 50% confluence, titrate the ratio for the PEI batch, and use endotoxin-free supercoiled DNA [1][2][3]
High toxicity or cell detachmentToo much PEI or prolonged exposureLower the ratio (for example, 1:2) and change medium at 18 h [1]
Variable results between batchesPEI batch, pH or serum lotTest each new PEI preparation and FBS lot; keep stock at pH 7.0 [1]
Precipitate in complexesMixed in salt-rich or serum-containing medium, or PEI added too fastDilute both components in serum-free medium and add PEI to DNA dropwise [1][3]
No expressionWrong plasmid or promoter, or old PEI stockInclude a GFP control, use fresh aliquots, and check the vector

Safety and waste

  • PEI is cytotoxic; wear gloves and avoid skin and eye contact.
  • HEK293T cells and viral vectors require biosafety level 2 practices: work in a biosafety cabinet and decontaminate with 10% bleach or autoclave.
  • Chloroquine is toxic if swallowed; dispose of as chemical waste.
  • Dispose of PEI-containing liquid and plasmid waste according to institutional rules.
