Bacterial Conjugation

2–5 days · 2026-11-26

Transfer a mobilizable plasmid from an E. coli donor (WM3064/MFDpir DAP auxotroph, or a triparental helper format) into a recipient by plate mating, then select, counter-select and confirm exconjugants by PCR.

⬇ Word (.docx)⬇ PowerPoint (.pptx)

Purpose

To transfer a mobilizable plasmid from an E. coli donor into a recipient bacterium by plate mating, then select and confirm the exconjugants while eliminating the donor.

Principle

A plasmid that carries an origin of transfer (oriT) can be moved from donor to recipient through cell-to-cell contact when the transfer (tra) functions are supplied. In diaminopimelic acid (DAP) auxotroph donors such as E. coli WM3064, MFDpir or B2155, RP4-derived tra genes sit on the chromosome; in a triparental mating a helper strain (for example pRK2013 or pRK600) supplies them instead [1][2][3][4]. Donor and recipient are mixed on a non-selective plate, and the cells are then plated on medium that selects for the plasmid marker and against the donor, either by leaving out DAP, by using a carbon source the donor cannot use, or by adding an antibiotic to which only the recipient is resistant [1][2][4]. Exconjugants are then confirmed by colony PCR.

Materials and reagents

Equipment

  • Shaking incubator (28–30 °C for environmental recipients, 37 °C for E. coli)
  • Spectrophotometer for OD₆₀₀
  • Microcentrifuge (about 3,000 rpm or 2,000–9,400 × g, depending on the source)
  • 90 mm Petri dishes, inoculation loops and spreaders
  • Biosafety cabinet or clean bench for drying plates
  • Thermal cycler for colony PCR

Procedure

  1. Grow the donor overnight in 5 mL LB with 0.3 mM DAP and the plasmid antibiotic. Grow the recipient in its own medium. Slow recipients may need longer [1]. (overnight)
  2. Wash 1 mL of each culture twice: spin at about 3,000 rpm for 5 min, discard the supernatant, and resuspend in 500 µL PBS. This removes carry-over antibiotic [1][2]. (20 min)
  3. Measure OD₆₀₀ and mix donor and recipient to 100 µL total. Use 1:1 (50 µL each) for fast growers; use 10:1 or 100:1 recipient to donor for slow or non-model recipients [1][2]. (10 min)
  4. For a triparental mating, mix recipient, donor and helper at about 1:1:1 (50 µL of each, from cells washed in 0.8% NaCl) instead [3][4]. (10 min)
  5. Spot the 100 µL mixture on a non-selective plate containing 0.3 mM DAP and no antibiotic. Do not spread. Let the spot dry with the lid ajar in a clean bench [1][2]. (15 min)
  6. Incubate at the recipient's preferred temperature, usually 28–30 °C. Robust E. coli pairs can conjugate in under 1 h; non-model recipients may need 24 h or more [1][2][3]. (overnight to 2 days)
  7. Scrape the whole spot into 1 mL PBS and vortex. Spin at about 3,000 rpm for 5 min, resuspend in 1 mL PBS, spin again, and resuspend the pellet in 100 µL PBS [1]. (15 min)
  8. Plate on selective plates (plasmid antibiotic, no DAP): spread the full 100 µL, and also 100 µL of a 1:10 dilution, giving 3–4 plates per mixture [1][2]. (15 min)
  9. Incubate at the recipient's temperature until colonies appear. Slow growers can take several days to weeks. (1–3 days)
  10. Restreak single colonies on fresh selective plates at least three times, because early colonies can be mixtures of donor and recipient [1][2]. (1–2 days)
  11. Confirm exconjugants by colony PCR for the transferred plasmid or insert, and run a donor-specific PCR (for example uidA) that should be negative [1][2]. (3 h)
  12. Store verified clones as glycerol stocks in medium with the selecting antibiotic. (10 min)

Pause points

  • After the mating spot is incubated, the scraped suspension can be plated the same day or the next morning.
  • Selective plates can be held at the incubation temperature or moved to 4 °C briefly once colonies appear.
  • Verified exconjugants can be frozen as glycerol stocks at −80 °C.

Expected results

  • Colonies appear on plasmid-antibiotic plates without DAP only where donor and recipient were mixed; frequency depends strongly on the strain pair, plasmid and mating time, and no single typical value applies [2][5].
  • With DAP counter-selection, small colonies are expected because donors can conjugate on the selective plate before dying; antibiotic counter-selection gives fewer of them [2].
  • Verified clones carry the plasmid or insert by PCR and are negative for the donor marker.

Controls

  • Donor alone and recipient alone on the selective plate: the donor should not grow without DAP, and the recipient shows spontaneous resistance
  • Donor plus recipient without helper in a triparental mating: no exconjugants expected [4]
  • Non-selective plate counts to estimate conjugation efficiency [1][2]
  • Known working mating pair as a positive control

Troubleshooting

ProblemLikely causeSolution
No exconjugantsPlasmid lacks oriT or the donor lacks tra genesConfirm oriT on the plasmid and RP4 transfer genes in the donor or helper [1]
Few exconjugants with a slow recipientDonor outcompetes the recipientRaise the recipient to donor ratio to 10:1 or 100:1; adjust time and temperature [1][2]
Donor growth on selective platesIncomplete counter-selection or carry-over DAPOmit DAP, add a donor-killing antibiotic, restreak several times, check a donor marker such as uidA by PCR [1][2]
Poor transferPili sheared or poor cell contactMix gently, try 1:3, 1:1 and 3:1 ratios, and let the spot dry before incubating [1][2]
No recipient growth at standard antibiotic levelsNon-model recipients are often more sensitiveTest a concentration series and confirm resistance by restreaking [2]
Mixed or impure coloniesDonor and recipient grow togetherRestreak several times and re-check by PCR [1][2]
Skewed frequenciesLong matings let early exconjugants multiplyShorten the mating or report counts as overdispersed [2]

Safety and waste

  • Treat donors and recipients at the biosafety level required for the recipient; some environmental isolates and Agrobacterium need institutional approval.
  • Autoclave or bleach-treat plates, cultures and washes before disposal.
  • Handle antibiotic stocks as chemical waste; tetracycline is light-sensitive.
  • Do not release strains carrying antibiotic resistance genes.
