Bacterial Conjugation

Estimated time: 2–5 days | Protocol World

To transfer a mobilizable plasmid from an E. coli donor into a recipient bacterium by plate mating, then select and confirm the exconjugants while eliminating the donor.

A plasmid that carries an origin of transfer (oriT) can be moved from donor to recipient through cell-to-cell contact when the transfer (tra) functions are supplied. In diaminopimelic acid (DAP) auxotroph donors such as E. coli WM3064, MFDpir or B2155, RP4-derived tra genes sit on the chromosome; in a triparental mating a helper strain (for example pRK2013 or pRK600) supplies them instead [1][2][3][4]. Donor and recipient are mixed on a non-selective plate, and the cells are then plated on medium that selects for the plasmid marker and against the donor, either by leaving out DAP, by using a carbon source the donor cannot use, or by adding an antibiotic to which only the recipient is resistant [1][2][4]. Exconjugants are then confirmed by colony PCR.

ItemDetails
Donor strainE. coli WM3064 (or MFDpir, B2155) carrying the oriT plasmid; for triparental mating, a plain donor plus a helper strain such as HB101/pRK2013 [1][2][3]
Recipient strainYour target bacterium, with a known chromosomal resistance marker (for example rifampicin or streptomycin) if you plan antibiotic counter-selection [3][4]
LB broth and LB agarMiller LB, 25 g/L; add 15 g/L agar for plates; autoclave. Use the recipient's preferred medium if it grows poorly in LB
DAP (2,6-diaminopimelic acid)0.3 mM final in donor cultures and in the non-selective mating plate; omit from selective plates [1][2]
AntibioticsPlasmid marker: for example carbenicillin or ampicillin 100 µg/mL, kanamycin 50 µg/mL, chloramphenicol 20–33 µg/mL, tetracycline 5–15 µg/mL, streptomycin 25–50 µg/mL [1][3][5]. Test a concentration series for non-model recipients [2]
1× PBS or 0.8% NaClPBS per liter: 8 g NaCl, 0.24 g KCl, 1.42 g Na₂HPO₄, 0.24 g KH₂PO₄; autoclave. 0.8% NaCl is used for the triparental wash [3]
Selective platesPlasmid antibiotic without DAP; optionally add a second antibiotic that kills the donor but not the recipient [1][2]
Colony PCR reagentsPrimers for the plasmid or insert, and for a donor-specific gene such as uidA to exclude donor carry-over [1][2]
  • Shaking incubator (28–30 °C for environmental recipients, 37 °C for E. coli)
  • Spectrophotometer for OD₆₀₀
  • Microcentrifuge (about 3,000 rpm or 2,000–9,400 × g, depending on the source)
  • 90 mm Petri dishes, inoculation loops and spreaders
  • Biosafety cabinet or clean bench for drying plates
  • Thermal cycler for colony PCR
  • Grow the donor overnight in 5 mL LB with 0.3 mM DAP and the plasmid antibiotic. Grow the recipient in its own medium. Slow recipients may need longer [1]. (overnight)
  • Wash 1 mL of each culture twice: spin at about 3,000 rpm for 5 min, discard the supernatant, and resuspend in 500 µL PBS. This removes carry-over antibiotic [1][2]. (20 min)
  • Measure OD₆₀₀ and mix donor and recipient to 100 µL total. Use 1:1 (50 µL each) for fast growers; use 10:1 or 100:1 recipient to donor for slow or non-model recipients [1][2]. (10 min)
  • For a triparental mating, mix recipient, donor and helper at about 1:1:1 (50 µL of each, from cells washed in 0.8% NaCl) instead [3][4]. (10 min)
  • Spot the 100 µL mixture on a non-selective plate containing 0.3 mM DAP and no antibiotic. Do not spread. Let the spot dry with the lid ajar in a clean bench [1][2]. (15 min)
  • Incubate at the recipient's preferred temperature, usually 28–30 °C. Robust E. coli pairs can conjugate in under 1 h; non-model recipients may need 24 h or more [1][2][3]. (overnight to 2 days)
  • Scrape the whole spot into 1 mL PBS and vortex. Spin at about 3,000 rpm for 5 min, resuspend in 1 mL PBS, spin again, and resuspend the pellet in 100 µL PBS [1]. (15 min)
  • Plate on selective plates (plasmid antibiotic, no DAP): spread the full 100 µL, and also 100 µL of a 1:10 dilution, giving 3–4 plates per mixture [1][2]. (15 min)
  • Incubate at the recipient's temperature until colonies appear. Slow growers can take several days to weeks. (1–3 days)
  • Restreak single colonies on fresh selective plates at least three times, because early colonies can be mixtures of donor and recipient [1][2]. (1–2 days)
  • Confirm exconjugants by colony PCR for the transferred plasmid or insert, and run a donor-specific PCR (for example uidA) that should be negative [1][2]. (3 h)
  • Store verified clones as glycerol stocks in medium with the selecting antibiotic. (10 min)
  • After the mating spot is incubated, the scraped suspension can be plated the same day or the next morning.
  • Selective plates can be held at the incubation temperature or moved to 4 °C briefly once colonies appear.
  • Verified exconjugants can be frozen as glycerol stocks at −80 °C.
  • Colonies appear on plasmid-antibiotic plates without DAP only where donor and recipient were mixed; frequency depends strongly on the strain pair, plasmid and mating time, and no single typical value applies [2][5].
  • With DAP counter-selection, small colonies are expected because donors can conjugate on the selective plate before dying; antibiotic counter-selection gives fewer of them [2].
  • Verified clones carry the plasmid or insert by PCR and are negative for the donor marker.
  • Donor alone and recipient alone on the selective plate: the donor should not grow without DAP, and the recipient shows spontaneous resistance
  • Donor plus recipient without helper in a triparental mating: no exconjugants expected [4]
  • Non-selective plate counts to estimate conjugation efficiency [1][2]
  • Known working mating pair as a positive control

  • Treat donors and recipients at the biosafety level required for the recipient; some environmental isolates and Agrobacterium need institutional approval.
  • Autoclave or bleach-treat plates, cultures and washes before disposal.
  • Handle antibiotic stocks as chemical waste; tetracycline is light-sensitive.
  • Do not release strains carrying antibiotic resistance genes.
