Single-Nucleus RNA-seq Nuclei Isolation

Estimated time: 1.5–2 h | Protocol World

To isolate intact, clean nuclei from flash-frozen tissue with RNA preserved, at a concentration and quality suitable for droplet-based single-nucleus RNA-seq (for example, 10x Genomics Chromium 3′ gene expression).

Frozen tissue cannot yield intact cells, but its nuclei keep most of the RNA that is needed to profile gene expression. Tissue is homogenized in a cold, isotonic sucrose buffer containing RNase inhibitor, which keeps nuclei round and RNA intact, and a mild detergent releases nuclei from the cytoplasm [1][4]. Low-speed spins, filtration and a BSA cushion remove debris, and a final count with a membrane-impermeant dye confirms that nuclei are intact, unclumped and at the target concentration [2][3][5]. Everything stays at 0–4 °C and the work is done quickly, because ambient RNases and long handling times are the main causes of poor data.

ItemDetails
Flash-frozen tissueUp to 50 mg per isolation; keep on dry ice and do not thaw before homogenization [4][5]. Quality gate: a separate piece of the same tissue should give RNA with RIN ≥ 5 [1].
NIM1 (nuclei isolation medium 1), 50 mL10 mM Tris-HCl pH 8.0 (500 µL of 1 M), 250 mM sucrose (4.26 g), 50 mM KCl (0.186 g), 5 mM MgCl₂ (250 µL of 1 M), water to 50 mL. Keep at 4 °C for up to 1 month [4]. A 25 mM KCl version is also reported [1].
NIM2, 5 mL (make fresh)4.9 mL NIM1 + 5 µL of 100 mM DTT (0.1 mM) + 100 µL of 50× protease inhibitor (1×) + 50 µL of 10% BSA (0.1%) [4].
NIM3, 1 mL per sample (make fresh)1 mL NIM2 + 5 µL RNase inhibitor (0.2 U/µL final, such as Protector RNase Inhibitor or RNasin Plus) [4].
BSA cushion, 1 mL per sample100 µL of 10% BSA + 900 µL NIM3 (1% BSA) [4].
10% Triton X-10010 µL per 1 mL NIM3 gives 0.1% [4].
Resuspension buffer, 2.5 mL1× PBS (nuclease-free), 1% BSA, 0.2 U/µL RNase inhibitor [2][3][4].
Counting dyeAO/PI (acridine orange/propidium iodide) or ethidium homodimer-1; the 10x guide advises against trypan blue because it can overestimate counts [5].
ConsumablesRNase-free 2 mL Dounce homogenizer (loose and tight pestles), 1.5 mL and 5 mL low-bind tubes, 70 µm and 40 µm strainers, wide-bore tips, ice and dry ice.
  • Refrigerated microcentrifuge or swinging-bucket centrifuge at 4 °C
  • Dounce homogenizer, 2 mL, with loose and tight pestles
  • Automated fluorescent cell counter or hemocytometer with a fluorescence microscope
  • Dry-ice box and ice bucket
  • Biosafety cabinet or clean bench (optional, for human tissue)
  • Pre-chill the Dounce, tubes, strainers and centrifuge to 4 °C. Wipe the bench and pipettes with an RNase-decontamination solution, then wear gloves throughout [3][4]. (15 min)
  • Make NIM2 and NIM3 fresh and keep both on ice. Add DTT, protease inhibitor and RNase inhibitor only just before use [4]. (10 min)
  • Prepare a 5 mL tube with 1 mL of 1% BSA cushion (100 µL 10% BSA + 900 µL NIM3) and keep it on ice [4]. (2 min)
  • On dry ice, cut or fragment no more than 50 mg of frozen tissue into pieces of about 2 mm, without letting it thaw [4][5]. (5 min)
  • Transfer the tissue into the pre-chilled Dounce containing 1 mL NIM3 on ice [4]. (1 min)
  • Homogenize with 10 strokes of the loose pestle, then 5 strokes of the tight pestle. Optimize stroke number on a spare piece the first time [4]. (3 min)
  • Transfer the homogenate to a 1.5 mL tube and centrifuge at 400 × g for 5 min at 4 °C. Remove and discard the supernatant (check it for nuclei during optimization) [1][4]. (7 min)
  • Add 10 µL of 10% Triton X-100 to 1 mL NIM3. Resuspend the pellet in it by pipetting gently 3 times, then leave on ice for 1 min [4]. (2 min)
  • Filter the lysate through a 70 µm strainer, then a 40 µm strainer, to remove tissue clumps [2][4]. (2 min)
  • Layer the filtered lysate slowly on top of the 1 mL BSA cushion in the 5 mL tube. Do not mix the layers [4]. (2 min)
  • Centrifuge at 400 × g for 5 min at 4 °C. Carefully remove the supernatant and cushion, leaving the nuclei pellet undisturbed [4]. (7 min)
  • Resuspend the pellet in 500 µL resuspension buffer (PBS, 1% BSA, 0.2 U/µL RNase inhibitor) using a wide-bore tip. Keep on ice [3][4]. (2 min)
  • Filter through a 40 µm strainer (35 µm is also used) into a fresh low-bind tube to remove aggregates [1][2]. (2 min)
  • Mix 10 µL of nuclei with the counting dye as the dye supplier specifies. Count two or three replicates and calculate the mean (CV should be below 25%) [5]. (10 min)
  • Inspect under the microscope: nuclei should be round with smooth outlines, with little debris and no blebbing or clumps [5]. (5 min)
  • Adjust to the target concentration: 700–1,200 nuclei/µL for 10x 3′ gene expression. If too dilute, spin 500 × g for 5 min and resuspend in a smaller volume, but not below 50 µL [5]. (10 min)
  • Keep on ice and load onto the single-cell platform immediately, following its user guide [3][5]. (5 min)
  • Frozen tissue may stay at −80 °C until the day of isolation; do not thaw it before homogenization [5].
  • After the final count, do not pause: proceed to droplet partitioning or fixation straight away. Freezing nuclei can cause substantial loss [1][5].
  • A clean, single-nucleus suspension of round nuclei with smooth outlines, little debris and few clumps; healthy tissue typically yields about 5,000–15,000 nuclei per mg with the 10x kit [5].
  • Most nuclei stain with membrane-impermeant dyes such as PI, because nuclei are not alive; this is expected for frozen tissue [1][4].
  • A concentration of about 700–1,200 nuclei/µL for 10x 3′ gene expression [5].
  • Test a spare piece of the same tissue first to tune Dounce stroke number and lysis time before processing precious samples [4].
  • Check the first supernatant (step 7) under the microscope on pilot runs to see whether nuclei are being lost [1].
  • Check tissue RNA integrity on a separate piece; the Current Protocols method proceeds only if RIN is ≥ 5 [1].
  • Count each sample in 2–3 technical replicates and record counts and nuclear morphology [5].
ProblemLikely causeSolution
Low nuclei yieldToo much supernatant removed, under-homogenization, or nuclei stuck in debrisLeave a little liquid above the pellet, check the supernatant for nuclei, and add Dounce strokes in small steps [1][4]
Blebbing or ruptured nuclei, high background RNAOver-lysis or over-homogenization, or detergent too longShorten the Triton incubation or reduce strokes; work faster on ice [5]
Clumps or aggregatesFree DNA from broken nuclei, or insufficient filteringFilter again through 40 µm, resuspend gently with a wide-bore tip, and vortex briefly if the platform guide allows [5]
Heavy debrisTissue-specific lipids or myelinAdd a density step such as the iodixanol gradient in the Imperial SOP [3], or use a debris-removal reagent from the kit [5]
Overestimated countDebris counted as nuclei, or trypan blue usedUse AO/PI or ethidium homodimer-1 and check the image of the count [5]
Low RNA quality in dataSlow handling, tissue thawed, or RNase contaminationKeep samples frozen until the lysis buffer, add RNase inhibitor fresh, and use RNase-free consumables [1][4]
  • Dry ice and liquid nitrogen can cause cold burns; wear gloves and eye protection.
  • Human or other primary tissue can carry pathogens: handle at the biosafety level set by your institution, and decontaminate waste, strainers and tubes accordingly.
  • DTT and Triton X-100 are irritants; consult the safety data sheet. Dispose of buffers, dyes (PI, ethidium homodimer) and tissue waste following institutional rules.
  • Propidium iodide and ethidium compounds bind DNA; treat them as mutagenic hazards.
