rRNA Depletion for RNA-seq

About 2.5 hours · 2026-11-18

Probe hybridization, RNase H and DNase I digestion and bead cleanup to remove ribosomal RNA from total RNA before RNA-seq library preparation (NEBNext workflow).

⬇ Word (.docx)⬇ PowerPoint (.pptx)

Purpose

To remove abundant ribosomal RNA (rRNA) from total RNA before library preparation so that sequencing reads are spent on mRNA and other informative transcripts. The example uses the NEBNext rRNA Depletion Kit (Human/Mouse/Rat) workflow, and the same logic applies to other probe-based kits.

Principle

Single-stranded DNA probes complementary to cytoplasmic 5S, 5.8S, 18S and 28S rRNA and mitochondrial 12S and 16S rRNA are hybridized to total RNA. RNase H then digests the RNA strand of each RNA:DNA hybrid, and DNase I removes the leftover probes. A bead cleanup recovers the depleted RNA [1][2][4]. Because the method removes rRNA rather than selecting a poly(A) tail, it can be used with partially degraded RNA, although the probes are designed for human, mouse and rat sequences and other species need a species-matched kit [2]. All volumes below are kit-specific; follow the manual of the kit you actually use.

Equipment

  • Thermocycler with heated lid (105 °C) and ramp control
  • Magnetic rack for PCR tubes or plates
  • Fluorometer and Bioanalyzer or TapeStation
  • Filter tips; RNase-free workspace
  • Ice bucket

Procedure

  1. Quantify the RNA by fluorometry and check integrity (RIN). Confirm it is DNA-free and dissolved in nuclease-free water, with no EDTA, EGTA or citrate [2][4]. (20 min)
  2. If genomic DNA may be present, treat with DNase I and then remove the DNase by phenol/chloroform extraction and ethanol precipitation, because residual DNase I degrades the probes [2]. (Optional)
  3. Dilute 5 ng–1 µg total RNA to 12 µL in nuclease-free water in a PCR tube [1][2]. (5 min)
  4. Make the hybridization mix (1 µL rRNA Depletion Solution + 2 µL Probe Hybridization Buffer per sample). Add 3 µL to each 12 µL RNA (15 µL total) and mix thoroughly [1][2]. (5 min)
  5. Run the thermocycler with the lid at 105 °C: 95 °C for 2 min, ramp down to 22 °C at 0.1 °C/s, then hold at 22 °C for 5 min [1][2]. (15–20 min)
  6. Prepare the RNase H mix (2 µL RNase H, 2 µL RNase H Reaction Buffer, 1 µL water per sample). Add 5 µL to each 15 µL hybridized RNA (20 µL total) and mix well [1][2]. (5 min)
  7. Incubate at 37 °C for 30 min with the lid at 40 °C or off [1][2]. (30 min)
  8. Prepare the DNase I mix (5 µL DNase I Reaction Buffer, 2.5 µL DNase I, 22.5 µL water per sample). Add 30 µL to each 20 µL reaction (50 µL total) and mix [1][2]. (5 min)
  9. Incubate at 37 °C for 30 min with the lid at 40 °C or off [1][2]. (30 min)
  10. Add 110 µL (2.2×) room-temperature beads to each 50 µL reaction, mix by pipetting at least 10 times, and incubate on ice for 15 min [1][2]. (15 min)
  11. Place the tubes on the magnet for 5 min until the liquid clears, then remove and discard the supernatant without disturbing the beads [1][2]. (5 min)
  12. With the tubes on the magnet, add 200 µL fresh 80% ethanol, wait 30 s at room temperature and remove it. Repeat once for two washes in total [1][2]. (5 min)
  13. Air-dry the beads on the magnet for up to 5 min. Stop when they are dark brown and glossy; light brown, cracked beads are over-dried [1]. (5 min)
  14. Remove from the magnet, resuspend the beads in 8 µL nuclease-free water, return to the magnet for 5 min and transfer 6 µL of depleted RNA to a fresh tube [1][2]. (10 min)
  15. Proceed straight to library preparation, keeping the RNA on ice. If you must pause, freeze at −80 °C [2]. (Pause point)

Pause points

  • After elution (step 14): store the depleted RNA at −80 °C (the instruction manual) or at −20 °C (the NEB web protocol) and continue with library construction later [1][2].
  • Do not pause in the middle of the hybridization, RNase H or DNase I steps; run them back to back.

Expected results

  • Typical recovery is only 3–10% of the input RNA, so the eluate is too dilute to quantify by standard methods and is used whole in the next step [2].
  • Fragment traces of depleted RNA show loss of the 18S and 28S ribosomal peaks. For a quantitative check, use RT-qPCR primers for rRNA and a housekeeping gene on equal input before and after depletion [2].
  • A library made from depleted intact RNA (RIN above 7) shows a narrow peak near 300 bp on a DNA 1000 chip after the kit's downstream protocol [2][4].

Controls

  • Include a no-RNA (nuclease-free water) tube through the whole procedure to detect carry-over contamination
  • Keep an undepleted aliquot of the same RNA for the before-and-after rRNA RT-qPCR or electropherogram comparison [2]
  • Process a reference RNA of known quality (such as commercial human reference RNA) in each batch when validating the method

Troubleshooting

ProblemLikely causeSolution
rRNA still abundant in readsGenomic DNA or residual DNase I degraded the probes; poor mixing at hybridization; wrong speciesRemove DNA before depletion and clean up DNase I; mix thoroughly; use a species-matched kit [2]
Very low RNA recoveryBeads over-dried or lost during washes; input below the kit minimumStop drying when beads are glossy brown; stay at or above 5 ng input [1][2]
Carry-over of ethanol or saltsIncomplete ethanol removal; EDTA, EGTA or citrate in the RNARemove all ethanol and re-dissolve the RNA in plain nuclease-free water [4]
Library has adaptor dimersToo little depleted RNA for the adaptor dilution usedDilute the adaptor according to the input, and re-clean the library with beads [2]

Safety and waste

  • Wear gloves and use RNase-free technique; avoid skin and breath contact with samples.
  • Phenol/chloroform, if used for DNase removal, is toxic and corrosive; work in a fume hood and dispose of it as hazardous waste.
  • Ethanol is flammable; keep away from ignition sources.
  • Dispose of tips, tubes and bead waste as laboratory biological or chemical waste according to local rules; treat human-derived RNA as potentially infectious.
