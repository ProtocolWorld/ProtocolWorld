Bacterial Growth Curves

Estimated time: 1 day + overnight culture | Protocol World

To record the lag, exponential, stationary and decline phases of an E. coli culture and to calculate its maximum specific growth rate (µ) and doubling time (g) in a chosen medium and temperature.

Cells scatter light, so turbidity at 600 nm (OD600) rises with cell density. Readings are reliable only within the linear range of the instrument, and each instrument differs, so dense samples are diluted in the same medium and the reading is multiplied by the dilution factor [3][4][5]. In exponential growth ln(OD) rises linearly with time; the slope is µ and the doubling time is g = ln 2/µ [6]. Plate readers use short, volume-dependent path lengths and need blank wells, shaking and a lid to limit evaporation [2][5].

ItemDetails
E. coli strainFresh colony from a streak plate (e.g., K-12 MG1655) [1]
LB medium10 g/L tryptone, 5 g/L yeast extract, 5–10 g/L NaCl; autoclaved [1]. M9 minimal medium with glucose may be used instead
Antibiotic (optional)Ampicillin 50 µg/mL from a 50 mg/mL stock stored at −20 °C, only if the strain carries a plasmid [1]
Overnight cultureOne colony in 5–7 mL LB, 37 °C, 250 rpm, 14–18 h [1]
Sterile baffled or plain flasks250 mL flask with 50 mL medium keeps the flask-to-medium volume ratio at 5:1 or more for aeration
CuvettesDisposable, 1 cm path length, or 13–18 mm tubes for a Spectronic-type instrument [3][4]
96-well plateFlat-bottom, clear (or black-walled clear-bottom) with lid [1][2]
Medium-only blankSame sterile medium (and antibiotic) as used for the culture [3][4]
Lid anti-condensation (optional)A Triton X-100 in ethanol pre-treatment of the lid limits condensation; follow the concentrations in [1]
  • Shaking incubator (37 °C, 200–250 rpm)
  • Spectrophotometer set to 600 nm
  • Temperature-controlled microplate reader with shaking and 600 nm filter
  • Biosafety cabinet or Bunsen burner
  • Pipettes and sterile tips
  • Spreadsheet or R/Python for analysis
  • Timer
  • Streak E. coli on an LB plate and incubate at 37 °C overnight to obtain single colonies. (16 h)
  • Inoculate one colony into 5–7 mL LB (plus antibiotic if needed) and grow at 37 °C, 250 rpm to saturation [1]. (14–18 h)
  • Pre-warm fresh medium and the flasks or plate to 37 °C overnight or for at least 1 h so the culture does not start in a cold medium [2]. (1 h)
  • Flask format: measure the OD600 of the overnight culture (dilute 1:10 in medium if above the linear range) and calculate the volume needed for a starting OD600 of about 0.02–0.05 [1][6].
  • Inoculate 50 mL pre-warmed LB in a 250 mL flask (5:1 volume ratio) with the calculated volume, about 0.1–0.5 mL; swirl to mix. (5 min)
  • Immediately take 1 mL for the t = 0 reading, then shake at 37 °C and 200–250 rpm. Mix the flask well before each sampling [1].
  • Blank the spectrophotometer at 600 nm with sterile medium in the same cuvette type; wipe the cuvette and seat it fully [3][4].
  • Every 30 min, withdraw 1 mL aseptically, read OD600 in a 1 cm cuvette and record time and value [1][2]. (6–8 h)
  • If a reading exceeds about 0.4–0.6, dilute the sample in sterile medium (for example 1:5 or 1:10), re-read, and multiply by the dilution factor [3][4][5].
  • Sample every 15 min during the steep rise (usually 1.5–4 h after inoculation) for at least 6–8 points in exponential phase. Keep the shaker running between samples. (15 min intervals)
  • Continue until three consecutive readings change by less than about 5%, indicating stationary phase. (to plateau)
  • Plate format: dilute overnight culture 100- to 500-fold in pre-warmed medium to an OD600 below about 0.05 [1].
  • Pipette 195 µL medium plus 5 µL overnight culture per well, or 200 µL diluted culture; use at least 3 replicates placed randomly [1][2].
  • Fill at least 3 blank wells with 200 µL sterile medium only; leave the outer ring empty or filled with water to limit evaporation [1][2].
  • Cover with a lid (pre-treated with Triton X-100 in ethanol if condensation occurs) and warm the reader to 37 °C before loading [1][2][5].
  • Program the reader: 37 °C, absorbance at 600 nm every 10 min, orbital shaking between reads, total 16–24 h [1][2]. (16–24 h)
  • Export the data to a spreadsheet and average the blank wells at each time point. Pause point: save the raw file and the plate map before analysis.
  • Subtract the blank, then plot OD600 against time on linear and semi-log axes to identify lag, exponential and stationary phases [2][6].
  • For plate data, remember that path length depends on fill volume; correct to a 1 cm equivalent only with a measured volume-based factor, otherwise compare plates run at the same volume [5].
  • Calculate ln(OD) and fit a straight line through the steepest consecutive points (at least 5) in the exponential phase; the slope is µ [6].
  • Calculate the doubling time as g = ln 2/µ (0.693/µ), for example µ = 0.9 h⁻¹ gives g ≈ 0.77 h (46 min) [6].
  • Check the fit (R² ≥ 0.95) and compare replicates; repeat if the standard error exceeds about 5% of the mean rate [6].
  • The protocol can be paused after sterilization and labeling; store solutions as described.
  • A sigmoid OD600 curve with a lag phase, an exponential phase, a plateau and, in long runs, a decline.
  • E. coli K-12 in LB at 37 °C with good aeration typically shows µ near 1.5–2 h⁻¹ (doubling time about 20–30 min); in M9 glucose it is slower, about 50–60 min.
  • The semi-log plot shows a straight line in exponential phase; flask and plate readings can differ in absolute OD because of path length.
  • Medium-only blank wells or tube to detect contamination and to subtract background [1][3]
  • Uninoculated flask incubated alongside the culture
  • Include a reference strain or untreated condition in every plate
  • Use at least 3 replicate wells or 3 independent flasks per condition [2]

  • Treat E. coli K-12 as biosafety level 1 and work with gloves, lab coat and eye protection; follow local biosafety rules.
  • Autoclave or bleach (10%, 30 min) all cultures, plates and cuvettes before disposal.
  • Shaking incubators and hot media cause burns; secure flasks and loosen caps before autoclaving.
  • Ethanol is flammable: keep it away from open flames when pre-treating plates.
