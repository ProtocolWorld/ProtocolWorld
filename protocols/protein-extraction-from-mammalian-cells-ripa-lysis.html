Protein Extraction from Mammalian Cells (RIPA Lysis)

1.5 hours · 2026-11-14

Ice-cold RIPA lysis with protease and phosphatase inhibitors, clarification by centrifugation and BCA quantification to prepare lysates for Western blotting and immunoassays.

⬇ Word (.docx)⬇ PowerPoint (.pptx)

Purpose

To prepare whole-cell protein lysates from adherent or suspension mammalian cells for Western blotting, immunoprecipitation and immunoassays.

Principle

RIPA buffer combines a non-ionic detergent (NP-40), an ionic detergent (sodium deoxycholate) and a low level of SDS to solubilize membrane, cytoplasmic and nuclear proteins [1][3]. Protease and phosphatase inhibitors preserve protein integrity and phosphorylation, and all steps are kept cold [1][2]. Centrifugation removes insoluble debris, and a BCA assay sets equal loading [1][3].

Materials and Reagents

Reagent / itemConcentration / details
RIPA buffer (Fortis recipe)50 mM Tris pH 8.0, 150 mM NaCl, 5 mM EDTA pH 8.0, 1% NP-40, 0.5% sodium deoxycholate, 0.1% SDS; store 4 °C up to 1 month [1]
RIPA buffer (commercial alternative)50 mM Tris-HCl pH 7.6, 150 mM NaCl, 1% NP-40, 0.5% deoxycholate, 0.1% SDS [3]
Protease and phosphatase inhibitor cocktail100× stock; add 1:100 (e.g., 100 µL to 10 mL) fresh before use [1][2]
PBSIce-cold, 1x
BCA protein assay kitWith BSA standards [1][3]
OptionalPMSF and sodium orthovanadate (about 10 µL per mL RIPA each) as used in a lab protocol [4]

Equipment

  • Ice bucket
  • Cell scraper
  • Refrigerated microcentrifuge (13,000–14,000 × g)
  • Probe sonicator (optional)
  • Plate reader for BCA
  • Pre-chilled 1.5 mL tubes

Procedure

A. Prepare

  • Chill PBS, tubes and the centrifuge to 4 °C. Add inhibitor cocktail to RIPA immediately before use and keep on ice [1][2].
  • Grow cells to about 80% confluence (adherent) or 1–2 × 10⁶ cells/mL (suspension) [1][2].

B. Lyse adherent cells

  • Place the dish on ice, aspirate medium and wash once or twice with 10 mL ice-cold PBS [1][4].
  • Add RIPA directly to the dish: about 200–500 µL for a 100 mm dish at 80% confluence (Fortis), up to 500–1000 µL per 100 mm dish (Boster); 250–500 µL for 60 mm; 200–400 µL per well of a 6-well plate [1][3]. Optimize volume per cell type.
  • Scrape cells and pipette the lysate several times (about 20 passes), then transfer to a pre-chilled tube [1][4].

C. Lyse suspension cells

  • Pellet cells at 300 × g for 5 min at room temperature, wash the pellet once with 5–10 mL ice-cold PBS and spin again [1][2].
  • Resuspend in 10–100 µL RIPA per 10⁶ cells [1][2].

D. Incubate and clarify

  • Incubate on ice 15–30 min with occasional mixing [1][3].
  • If the lysate is viscous from genomic DNA, sonicate (for example three 2 s pulses at low-moderate power with at least 1 min on ice between pulses) and incubate 15 min more [1][2][3].
  • Centrifuge at 13,000–14,000 × g for 5–15 min at 4 °C [1][3][4].
  • Transfer the supernatant to fresh pre-chilled tubes, leaving the pellet.

E. Quantify and store

  • Measure protein by BCA; the vendor of one RIPA product advises against the Bradford assay with RIPA detergents [1][3].
  • Typical working concentrations are 1–5 mg/mL for Western blotting [2].
  • Aliquot and store at −20 °C (or −80 °C for long term); avoid repeated freeze-thaw [1][2]. Mix with Laemmli sample buffer and heat when ready to run gels.

Expected Results

  • Clear supernatant with protein at roughly 1–5 mg/mL (adjust volume to cell number) [2].
  • Strong, even bands for abundant proteins (e.g., loading controls) on a Western blot and preserved phospho-signal when phosphatase inhibitors are used.

Troubleshooting

ProblemPossible causeSolution
Viscous, gel-like lysateGenomic DNA releasedSonicate briefly or pass through a needle, then re-spin [1][3]
Low protein concentrationToo much RIPA for the cell numberReduce volume (e.g., pool across plates) and re-quantify [1]
Protein degradation or lost phospho-signalInhibitors omitted or lysate warmAdd fresh inhibitors; keep everything at 4 °C [1][2]
Poor recovery of enzyme activity or interacting partnersDeoxycholate and SDS are denaturingUse a milder buffer without deoxycholate/SDS for kinase or co-IP work [3]
Inconsistent protein assayDetergent interferenceUse BCA and make standards in the same RIPA diluent [3]

Safety Notes

  • RIPA contains SDS and NP-40 (irritants); PMSF and sodium orthovanadate are toxic, so handle with gloves and in a fume hood where indicated [4].
  • Treat cell lysates from human or primate cells as potentially infectious at the biosafety level your institution requires.
  • Dispose of inhibitor-containing waste as chemical waste.
