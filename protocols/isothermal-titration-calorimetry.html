Isothermal Titration Calorimetry

About 3 h of run time plus overnight dialysis · 2026-11-26

Step-by-step ITC on a MicroCal ITC200: designing concentrations from the c value, buffer matching by dialysis, degassing, loading, a 19-injection titration with a buffer control, and One Set of Sites fitting for Kd, n, ΔH and ΔS.

⬇ Word (.docx)⬇ PowerPoint (.pptx)

Purpose

To measure the binding thermodynamics of two molecules in solution (for example a protein and a peptide or small molecule) by ITC, obtaining the stoichiometry (n), association constant (Ka), enthalpy (ΔH) and entropy (ΔS) in a single label-free experiment.

Principle

An ITC instrument keeps a sample cell and a reference cell at the same temperature. Each time ligand from the syringe is injected into the macromolecule in the cell, binding releases or absorbs heat, and the instrument changes the power it supplies to keep the temperature difference at zero. Integrating each power peak gives the heat per injection; as the macromolecule becomes saturated, the peaks shrink to the heat of dilution. Fitting the binding isotherm gives n, Ka and ΔH, from which ΔG = −RT ln Ka and ΔS = (ΔH − ΔG)/T follow [1][2][3]. Quality depends on accurate concentrations, exactly matched buffers and bubble-free loading [1][4]. The c value (c = n × [cell]/Kd) should be about 10–100 for a well-defined curve [1].

Materials and reagents

ItemDetails
Macromolecule (cell sample)Typically 5–50 µM, at least 300 µL for an ITC200 (about 200 µL cell volume plus loading volume) [1]
Ligand (syringe sample)Typically 50–500 µM and at least 10 × the cell concentration for a 1:1 interaction; at least 100–120 µL [1]
Dialysis bufferExample: 20 mM Tris pH 8.0, 150 mM NaCl, 1 mM DTT [3]; prefer buffers with a low ionization enthalpy (phosphate, HEPES-type) and TCEP at ≤ 1 mM in place of DTT or β-mercaptoethanol where possible [1][2][4]
Dialysis tubing or cassettes12 kDa MWCO for proteins; 1 kDa MWCO for peptides [3]
DMSO (only if needed)Final DMSO at most 5% and identical in cell and syringe [4]
Cleaning solutionsUltrapure water; 10–20% Contrad 70 detergent for a suspected precipitate [1]
Concentration standardsExtinction coefficient (for example from ProtParam) for A280; a second method such as Bradford is advised [2]

Equipment

  • MicroCal ITC200 (or VP-ITC) with its control and Origin-based analysis software
  • Hamilton syringe for cell loading
  • Vacuum degasser or ThermoVac
  • Refrigerated microcentrifuge (up to 16,000 × g)
  • UV spectrophotometer
  • Magnetic stirrer and 4 °C space for dialysis

Procedure

  1. Estimate Kd and choose concentrations so that c = n[cell]/Kd is about 10–100; use ligand at ≥ 10 × the cell concentration [1][4]. (30 min)
  2. Dialyze both partners in the same beaker of buffer for at least 2 h, or overnight at 4 °C, stirring at 100–200 rpm; keep the dialysate for all dilutions and rinses [3]. (2 h to overnight)
  3. If a ligand stock contains DMSO, add the same final DMSO (≤ 5%) to the protein; check that the two pH values differ by no more than 0.1 [1][4].
  4. Measure both concentrations accurately by A280 with the right extinction coefficient and, if possible, a second method; errors change n and Kd directly [1][2]. (20 min)
  5. Dilute samples to final concentrations with dialysate. Degas about 5 min under vacuum with gentle stirring, then spin 3–5 min at 12,300–16,000 × g (4 °C) to remove aggregates [2][3]. (20 min)
  6. Clean the cell by washing at least three times with matched buffer, then confirm a quiet baseline with a water-into-water test (noise below about 1.5 µcal/s) [1][3]. (30 min)
  7. Load about 300 µL of cell sample with a Hamilton syringe without bubbles, then aspirate and dispense about 50 µL to dislodge any; fill the syringe with the ligand [1]. (10 min)
  8. Set 20–25 °C, reference power 5–7 µcal/s, stirring 750 rpm, initial delay 60 s, 19 injections of 2 µL over 4 s (first 0.4 µL), spacing 150–180 s [1][3]. (5 min)
  9. Let the instrument equilibrate; the differential power should settle within ± 1 µcal/s of the reference power in 5–10 min. If not, reload or reclean [1].
  10. Run the titration of ligand into protein and watch that the peaks shrink to a flat plateau by the last injections [1][4]. (About 1 h)
  11. Repeat the identical program with ligand injected into buffer (or into the dialysate) to measure the heat of dilution [1][2][4]. (About 1 h)
  12. Delete the first injection, subtract the control heats, and fit a One Set of Sites model (at least 200 iterations) to obtain n, Ka and ΔH; calculate Kd = 1/Ka, ΔG and ΔS [1][2]. (30 min)
  13. Clean the cell and syringe after every run; use a 10–20% Contrad 70 soak if precipitate is suspected, and rinse thoroughly with water [1].

Pause points

  • Samples can wait at 4 °C after dialysis and concentration measurement; run them within the same day after degassing and spinning to limit bubbles and aggregation.
  • Fitted data and raw thermograms can be saved at any time after the run.

Expected results

  • A sigmoidal binding isotherm with a clear plateau for c values of about 10–100, with n close to the expected stoichiometry.
  • Fitted values of Kd, n, ΔH, ΔG and ΔS with fit errors; the ligand-into-buffer control should be small, flat and uniform.
  • Weak binding (c below about 10) still gives a Kd but not a reliable n; very tight binding (c above about 1,000) gives n but not Kd [1].

Controls

  • Ligand injected into buffer (heat of dilution) with identical settings [1][2][4]
  • Water-into-water noise test before sample runs [1][3]
  • Repeat each measurement at least three times and, if possible, vary temperature or buffer to check ΔH assumptions [2][4]

Troubleshooting

ProblemLikely causeSolution
Large, noisy or drifting peaksBubbles, dirty cell or poor degassingReclean the cell; degas; reload without bubbles
Large heats in the controlBuffer, pH or DMSO mismatchDialyze together; match DMSO; keep pH within 0.1
Flat curve or no heatConcentrations too low or enthalpy near zeroRaise concentrations; change temperature or buffer
Curve is a step with no inflectionc too highLower the cell concentration
Odd stoichiometry (n far from 1)Inaccurate concentration or inactive proteinRe-measure concentration; check protein activity
Precipitate in cellAggregation during titrationSpin or filter samples; soak in 10–20% Contrad 70

Safety and waste

  • Wear gloves and eye protection; DMSO carries other chemicals through skin, and Contrad 70 is a strongly alkaline detergent.
  • Treat protein samples according to their biosafety level and discard in the appropriate liquid waste.
  • Collect used buffers containing DTT, TCEP or organic solvents according to institutional chemical waste rules.
