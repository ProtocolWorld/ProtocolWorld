Bacterial Glycerol Stocks

≈ 20 min · 2026-11-27

Prepare long-term −80 °C glycerol stocks of E. coli strains and plasmid-carrying clones, label and record them, and revive them correctly by scraping without thawing.

⬇ Word (.docx)⬇ PowerPoint (.pptx)

Purpose

To preserve a bacterial strain or plasmid-bearing clone for years in a form that can be revived reliably and without genetic drift.

Principle

Glycerol is a cryoprotectant that reduces ice-crystal damage to cells during freezing. Mixing a fresh overnight culture with sterile glycerol to a final concentration of about 15–25% and storing at −80 °C keeps most cells viable for years [1][3][4]. Survival depends on the strain: 15% worked best for some E. coli strains and 20–25% for the K-12 strain MG1655 in a systematic test, so the commonly used range is 10–25% [2]. Freeze–thaw cycles lower viability and can shift the population, so the stock is never thawed; a small amount of frozen material is scraped off for each use [1][2].

Materials and reagents

ItemDetails

Equipment

  • −80 °C freezer
  • Shaking incubator (37 °C)
  • Autoclave
  • Biosafety cabinet or clean bench near a flame
  • Vortex mixer
  • Pipettors (1 mL) and sterile tips

Procedure

  1. Streak the strain on a selective plate and grow it overnight. Inoculate a single colony into 3–5 mL of LB with the appropriate antibiotic and grow 14–18 h at 37 °C with shaking [1][3]. (overnight)
  2. Label two cryovials for each strain (name, plasmid, date, initials) on the tube and the lid [1][3].
  3. Add 500 µL of sterile 50% glycerol to each cryovial [1][5]. (2 min)
  4. Add 500 µL of the overnight culture to each vial. The final glycerol concentration is 25% [1][5]. (2 min)
  5. Close the cap tightly and mix by gentle inversion or brief vortexing until the glycerol is uniform and no streaks remain [1][3].
  6. For a 15% stock instead, use 150 µL sterile 100% glycerol with 850 µL culture and vortex well [3][4]. The best concentration depends on the strain [2].
  7. Freeze the vials directly at −80 °C. Cells survive poorly at −20 °C, so never store stocks there [5]. (immediately)
  8. Record the box, position and date in the freezer inventory. Place the duplicate vial in a different box or freezer if the strain is valuable [3][4].
  9. To revive, keep the vial on dry ice, scrape a small amount of frozen material with a sterile loop, tip or toothpick, and streak it on a selective plate. Do not thaw the stock [1][5]. (2 min)
  10. Return the vial to −80 °C immediately and incubate the plate overnight at 37 °C (or the temperature the plasmid requires) [1]. (overnight)
  11. Verify a revived clone before important experiments: check colony appearance, antibiotic resistance, and, if needed, a restriction digest or sequencing.

Pause points

  • Make the stock on the morning the overnight culture is finished; plasmids degrade in liquid culture [1][3].
  • Stocks stay stable at −80 °C for years; avoid temperature fluctuation.

Expected results

  • The revived stock gives growth of single colonies within 16–20 h of plating.
  • Colonies show the expected morphology and antibiotic resistance.
  • Freezer inventory lists every stock with an exact location.

Controls

  • Plate a loopful of a new stock the same week to confirm viability
  • Streak the revived stock on a plate without antibiotic and with antibiotic to check plasmid retention
  • Make duplicate stocks for important strains [4]

Troubleshooting

ProblemLikely causeSolution
No growth after revivingStock thawed repeatedly or too few cells frozenUse the duplicate vial; remake the stock from a healthy overnight culture [1][2]
Contamination on platesNon-sterile glycerol or tubesAutoclave glycerol and use sterile tubes; work near a flame
Plasmid lost after revivalCulture grown without antibiotic or stored too long in liquidAdd the antibiotic to overnight cultures; freeze the culture promptly because plasmids degrade in liquid culture [3]
Label unreadable at −80 °CInk or label failed in the coldUse cryo labels or marker, label both tube and lid [1]
Cap popped openSnap-cap tubeUse screw-cap cryovials with a gasket [1]

Safety and waste

  • Wear gloves and a lab coat. Work with BSL-1 strains under standard microbiological practice; follow institutional rules for higher-risk strains.
  • Dry ice and −80 °C vials can cause cold burns: use insulated gloves and eye protection.
  • Autoclave or bleach contaminated tubes, tips and plates before disposal as biohazardous waste.
