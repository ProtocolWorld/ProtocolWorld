Beta-Galactosidase Activity Assay

Estimated time: About 2 h (plus culture growth) | Protocol World

To measure β-galactosidase (lacZ) activity in permeabilized E. coli cells with the chromogenic substrate ONPG and to express the result in Miller units, so that promoter or reporter-fusion activity can be compared between strains and conditions.

β-Galactosidase hydrolyzes o-nitrophenyl-β-D-galactopyranoside (ONPG, colorless) to galactose and o-nitrophenol, which is yellow under alkaline conditions and absorbs at 420 nm. Cells are permeabilized with chloroform and SDS so that ONPG enters, the reaction is timed at a fixed temperature and stopped with sodium carbonate, which raises the pH to about 11 and inactivates the enzyme. Light scattering by cell debris is corrected with the A550 reading, and activity is normalized to reaction time, culture volume and cell density (OD600) [1][2][3].

ItemDetails
Z buffer (1 L)16.1 g Na₂HPO₄·7H₂O (60 mM), 5.5 g NaH₂PO₄·H₂O (40 mM), 0.75 g KCl (10 mM), 0.246 g MgSO₄·7H₂O (1 mM); adjust to pH 7.0; add 2.7 mL β-mercaptoethanol (about 38 mM) only on the day of use [2][3]
0.1% SDS0.1% (w/v) sodium dodecyl sulfate in water [1]
ChloroformMolecular-biology grade; handle in a fume hood [1]
ONPG solution4 mg/mL o-nitrophenyl-β-D-galactopyranoside in 0.1 M sodium phosphate, pH 7.0 (1.61 g Na₂HPO₄·7H₂O + 0.55 g NaH₂PO₄·H₂O per 100 mL); prepare fresh and warm to the reaction temperature [1][2]
Stop solution1 M Na₂CO₃ (53 g per 500 mL water) [1][5]
Culture mediumMedium appropriate for your E. coli strain, with antibiotic and inducer (for example IPTG) as needed
StrainsReporter strain(s), a lacZ-null negative control and, if available, a strain with known high lacZ activity [1]
  • Spectrophotometer or plate reader for OD600, A420 and A550
  • 28 °C or 30 °C water bath
  • Shaking incubator (37 °C or 30 °C)
  • Fume hood
  • Microcentrifuge
  • Timer (stopwatch)
  • Vortex mixer, glass test tubes or 2 mL tubes
  • Inoculate an overnight culture. Dilute 1:100 into fresh medium (with inducer if needed) and grow at 37 °C with shaking to mid-log phase, OD600 0.2–0.7 [1][2]. (3–4 h)
  • Stop growth by chilling the culture on ice for 20 min, then record the OD600 of each culture against a medium blank [2]. (20 min)
  • Prepare and label tubes in duplicate: reporter samples, lacZ-negative control and a Z-buffer blank. Pre-warm the water bath to 28 °C [1]. (10 min)
  • For each sample, add 0.5 mL of culture and 0.5 mL of Z buffer (1 mL total). For highly active strains (over about 500 Miller units) use 0.1 mL culture and 0.9 mL Z buffer [1]. (10 min)
  • Add 100 µL chloroform and 50 µL of 0.1% SDS to each tube in the fume hood. Vortex for 10 s and equilibrate for 5 min at 28 °C [1]. (10 min)
  • Start the reaction by adding 0.2 mL of ONPG, vortex briefly and record the time. Stagger tubes by 15–30 s so that each can be stopped on time [1][2]. (1 min)
  • Incubate at 28 °C until a light yellow color appears (aim for about 15 min; 8–30 min is typical). Weak fusions may need hours [1][3]. (8–30 min)
  • Stop each tube by adding 0.5 mL of 1 M Na₂CO₃ and record the stop time. The time between ONPG addition and Na₂CO₃ is t in minutes [1][2]. (1 min)
  • Spin the tubes at 13,000 × g for 5 min to pellet cell debris, then carefully transfer 1 mL of supernatant, avoiding chloroform, to a clean cuvette [1][5]. (10 min)
  • Read A420 and A550 against the Z-buffer blank. Repeat with a smaller culture volume or longer time if A420 is outside 0.1–1.2 (ideal range 0.6–0.9) [1]. (10 min)
  • Calculate Miller units = 1000 × (A420 − 1.75 × A550) / (t × V × OD600), where t is in minutes and V is the culture volume in mL [1][2]. (10 min)
  • Average the duplicates, subtract the negative-control value if it is above the blank, and repeat on at least three independent cultures to report mean ± SD. (10 min)
  • Z buffer without β-mercaptoethanol can be stored at 4 °C; add β-mercaptoethanol on the day of use, because it oxidizes in a few days [1][2].
  • Cultures can be chilled on ice and kept for a short time before assay [2].
  • Stopped reactions are stable for a short time but should be read the same day.
  • The negative control gives near-background values, typically only a few Miller units (about 1.5–3 for uninduced lac⁺ cells in one report) [1].
  • Fully induced lac⁺ strains give on the order of 1,500 Miller units [1].
  • A420 for each sample should fall within the linear range (0.1–1.2, ideally 0.6–0.9) so that the calculation is reliable [1].
  • lacZ-null (negative) strain to define background [1]
  • Strain with high or known lacZ activity (positive control) [1]
  • Z-buffer plus ONPG blank (no cells) to correct for spontaneous ONPG hydrolysis [1][3]
  • Uninduced versus induced cultures of the reporter strain
  • Biological replicates from independent colonies
ProblemLikely causeSolution
A420 too lowLow activity or too few cellsUse more culture, extend the reaction time or switch to a more sensitive reporter [1]
A420 too high (over 1.2)Too much enzyme or too long incubationUse less culture (0.1 mL) or stop earlier [1]
Negative control turns yellowSpontaneous ONPG hydrolysis or contaminated bufferPrepare fresh ONPG and Z buffer; subtract the A420 and A550 of the negative control [1]
Variable results between replicatesDifferent growth phase or imprecise timingHarvest cultures at the same OD600 and stagger tubes carefully [2]
Many samples or chloroform not wantedTube assay is slow and uses hazardous solventUse the single-step 96-well microplate method with a lysis reagent and lysozyme and read OD420 kinetically [4]
Weak signal from fresh Z bufferOxidized β-mercaptoethanolMake Z buffer with β-mercaptoethanol only a few days before use [1]
  • Chloroform is toxic and a suspected carcinogen: dispense it only in a fume hood, wear gloves and collect waste in a labeled halogenated-solvent container.
  • β-Mercaptoethanol is toxic and has a strong odor: handle it in a fume hood.
  • ONPG powder and SDS can irritate the eyes and respiratory tract: wear gloves and avoid generating dust.
  • 1 M Na₂CO₃ is an irritant. E. coli K-12 strains are handled at biosafety level 1; disinfect cultures and tubes before disposal.
