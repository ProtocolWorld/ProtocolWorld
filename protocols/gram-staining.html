Gram Staining

Estimated time: About 20 min | Protocol World

To classify bacteria as Gram-positive or Gram-negative and to view cell shape and arrangement by light microscopy using the differential Gram stain.

Cells are stained with crystal violet and treated with Gram's iodine, which forms a large crystal violet–iodine complex. Decolorizer (ethanol or an ethanol–acetone mixture) dehydrates the thick, cross-linked peptidoglycan wall of Gram-positive bacteria, which retain the complex and look purple, whereas the thin peptidoglycan layer and outer membrane of Gram-negative bacteria let it wash out. A safranin counterstain then colors the decolorized cells pink to red [1][2][3]. Decolorization time is the most critical step [2].

Sterile inoculating loop or needleFlame-sterilized or disposable

QC strainsStaphylococcus aureus (Gram-positive) and Escherichia coli (Gram-negative) [5]

  • Bunsen burner or slide warmer (about 37 °C)
  • Staining rack or tray with a sink or wash bottle
  • Bright-field microscope with 100× oil-immersion objective
  • Timer
  • Slide marker pencil
  • Label a slide. Place a small drop (5–10 µL) of water or saline on it and emulsify a pinpoint amount of colony in it. Avoid thick smears [2][3]. (2 min)
  • For a broth culture, spread 10–50 µL thinly without adding water. Prepare a mixed QC smear of S. aureus and E. coli on a second slide [3][5]. (2 min)
  • Air-dry the smear completely. Fixing a wet smear can aerosolize organisms and distort cells [1][3]. (5 min)
  • Fix the dry smear by passing it briefly through a flame (about 1 s, smear side up), by warming on a slide warmer, or by flooding with methanol for 60 s [1][2][3].
  • Flood the slide with crystal violet for 1 min, then rinse gently with water at an angle [1][2][3]. (1 min)
  • Flood with Gram's iodine for 1 min, then rinse gently with water [1][2][3]. (1 min)
  • Decolorize: tilt the slide about 45° and drip decolorizer on until the purple stops running off (often only 2–5 s). Rinse with water immediately [1][2][5]. (5–10 s)
  • Flood with safranin for 30–60 s, then rinse gently with water [1][2][3]. (1 min)
  • Blot gently with bibulous paper without rubbing and let the slide air-dry completely [1][2][4].
  • Scan at 10×, then 40×. Add one drop of immersion oil and examine with the 100× oil-immersion objective (1000× total magnification) [1][2][4].
  • Record cell shape, arrangement and color: purple is Gram-positive and pink to red is Gram-negative [1][2][4]. Check that the QC slide gave the expected result.
  • Discard slides into a sharps or biohazard container and clean the stage and objective with lens paper as directed.
  • After fixing (step 4), slides can be held dry until staining.
  • Stained, dried slides may be stored for later review or documentation.
  • Gram-positive bacteria such as Staphylococcus aureus appear purple (cocci in clusters); Gram-negative bacteria such as Escherichia coli appear pink to red rods [2][5].
  • Clean background, intact cells and a QC slide that gives the expected colors for both strains.
  • QC slide with S. aureus (purple cocci) and E. coli (pink rods), stained at the same time as unknowns [5]
  • Check reagents and technique if the QC results are out of range, and re-stain [5]
  • Record culture age and medium for every smear
ProblemLikely causeSolution
Gram-positive cells look pinkOver-decolorization, old culture or heat damageShorten decolorizing, use a fresh culture, avoid overheating [1][2][5]
Gram-negative cells look purpleUnder-decolorization or thick smearDecolorize until the runoff is clear and make thinner smears [1][2]
Stain precipitate or debrisOld or unfiltered crystal violetFilter the stain or use a fresh bottle [1]
Smear washes offSmear not fully dried or fixed, or greasy slideDry and fix properly; use clean slides [2][3]
Mixed or uneven colorsGram-variable organisms or inconsistent rinsingRepeat with a younger culture and uniform timing [1][5]
  • Wear a lab coat, gloves and eye protection. Some stains and decolorizers are harmful or flammable; check the SDS and keep acetone and ethanol away from the flame [3].
  • Allow smears to dry completely before heat fixation to avoid aerosols, and handle unknown cultures in a biosafety cabinet when required [1][5].
  • Discard stain waste into liquid chemical waste and used slides into a biohazard or sharps container according to local rules [5].
