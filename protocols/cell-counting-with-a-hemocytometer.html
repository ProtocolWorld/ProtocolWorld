Cell Counting with a Hemocytometer

Estimated time: About 15 min | Protocol World

To determine the concentration and viability of a mammalian cell suspension by manual counting in an improved Neubauer hemocytometer with trypan blue exclusion.

The hemocytometer is a thick glass slide with a ruled grid. Under a coverslip, each 1 mm × 1 mm large square holds a fixed volume of 0.1 mm³ (0.0001 mL), because the chamber depth is 0.1 mm [1][2][3]. Counting cells in several squares and multiplying by the dilution factor and 10⁴ gives cells per milliliter. Trypan blue is excluded by cells with intact membranes, so live cells stay clear while dead cells stain blue; the dye is toxic over time, so counting must be done promptly [2][3].

  • Clean the chamber and coverslip with 70% ethanol and dry them with lint-free tissue. Press the coverslip on its supports until Newton's rings appear [1][3]. (2 min)
  • Resuspend the cells thoroughly to single cells by gentle pipetting. Clumps and uneven mixing are the main sources of error [2][3]. (1 min)
  • Mix equal volumes of cell suspension and 0.4% trypan blue (for example, 20 µL + 20 µL). The dilution factor is 2 [1][2][3]. (1 min)
  • Wait 1–2 min after mixing. Trypan blue is itself cytotoxic, so complete all counting within 3–5 min of mixing; remix and start again if you run over [2][3]. (1–2 min)
  • Load about 10 µL at the edge of the coverslip so that capillary action fills the chamber. Do not overfill or create bubbles [1][2][3]. (1 min)
  • Let the cells settle for about 1 min. Focus on the grid with the 10× objective and move to one of the large corner squares [3]. (1 min)
  • Count unstained (live) and blue (dead) cells separately in the four large corner squares and optionally the central square [1][2][3]. (2–3 min)
  • Use one boundary rule consistently: count cells touching the top and left lines and skip those on the bottom and right (some sources use top and right) [1][2][3].
  • Aim for roughly 20–100 live cells per large square and at least 100 in total; dilute or concentrate and recount if far outside this range [2][3].
  • Load the second chamber side with fresh mixture and count it. If the two sides differ by more than about 10–15%, remix and repeat [2][3].
  • Calculate cells/mL = (mean live cells per large square) × dilution factor × 10⁴. Viability (%) = live ÷ (live + dead) × 100 [1][2][3].
  • Clean the chamber with 70% ethanol and dry it. Record the count, viability and dilution in your notebook.
  • The cell suspension may sit on ice briefly before counting, but once trypan blue has been added, count within 3–5 min [2][3].
  • Example: 325 live cells counted in 5 large squares with a 1:1 trypan blue dilution gives (325 ÷ 5) × 2 × 10⁴ = 1.3 × 10⁶ cells/mL [2].
  • Healthy log-phase cultures typically show about 95% viability or more; 80–95% is usually acceptable and below 80% suggests a problem [2].
  • Count both chamber sides and compare them
  • Include the trypan blue dilution factor in every calculation
  • Occasionally cross-check with an automated counter or a second operator

  • Trypan blue is a suspected carcinogen; wear gloves and a lab coat and avoid skin contact and aerosol exposure.
  • Treat all cell material according to its biosafety level and handle human or primate cells as potentially infectious.
  • Dispose of trypan blue waste as chemical waste, and of cells in biohazard waste per institutional rules. Disinfect the chamber with 70% ethanol after use.
